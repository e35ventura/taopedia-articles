# Drand Time-Lock Encryption

Drand time-lock encryption helps Bittensor Commit Reveal keep newly submitted validator weights
concealed until a later reveal round. In the commit-then-reveal flow, validators commit weight
information before it becomes readable, reducing the usefulness of copied public weights
([Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal),
[Glossary: Drand/time-lock encryption](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#drandtime-lock-encryption)).

The term belongs to timing and visibility. It explains when committed validator-weight information
can be read, while validator weights and Yuma Consensus explain how revealed evaluations are used
after the delay
([Glossary: Validator Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-weights),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).

## Delayed Visibility

Delayed visibility matters because validator weights are valuable consensus inputs. A validator that
can simply reuse public weight signals adds less independent evaluation to a subnet
([The Weight Copying Problem](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/weight-copying-in-bittensor)).

Commit Reveal reduces that copying window by separating submission from later readability. The
validator commits information first, and the readable weight information appears later in the reveal
part of the process ([Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal)).

That delay is useful because the valuable signal is the fresh validator evaluation, not a public
copy of another validator's recent weights. Drand time-lock encryption is the timing tool that helps
preserve that delay.

## Drand Round Timing

Time-lock encryption binds access to a later round instead of immediate decryption. Encrypted data
becomes decryptable only after a future Drand round
([Glossary: Drand/time-lock encryption](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#drandtime-lock-encryption)).

The Drand round supplies the timing reference. Before that round, the committed weight information
stays concealed; after the relevant round, the reveal process can make the information readable.

This makes the phrase more specific than generic encryption. The important feature is delayed
readability tied to a future Drand round, not merely that the data was encrypted.

## Commit Reveal

Commit Reveal uses concealed weight submission before later reveal. Drand time-lock encryption fits
that process as the timing layer: committed weight data remains unreadable until the target round,
then the revealed information can enter the consensus flow
([Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal),
[Glossary: Drand/time-lock encryption](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#drandtime-lock-encryption)).

That placement keeps the concept narrow. Commit Reveal is the broader commit-then-reveal process;
Drand time-lock encryption is the delayed-readability tool used inside that process.

## Consensus Role

Drand time-lock encryption controls when concealed weight information becomes readable; it does not
score miners or allocate emissions. After reveal, validator evaluations enter the Yuma Consensus
aggregation process for miner incentives and validator dividends
([Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal)).

The delayed-visibility layer protects inputs before that aggregation step. Once the weights are
revealed, nearby terms such as validator weights, rank, incentives, and dividends describe the
consensus and emission path.

## Reading the Term

The term names a timing and visibility tool, not a separate consensus algorithm. It is closest to
Commit Reveal, weight-copying protection, and validator-weight handling
([Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal),
[The Weight Copying Problem](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/weight-copying-in-bittensor)).

The concise reading is: Drand time-lock encryption delays readability of committed validator-weight
information until a future Drand round, helping Commit Reveal reduce copied-weight behavior before
Yuma Consensus uses the revealed weights.

## Early Decryption Is Blocked for Everyone

Official [Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal) documentation
states that encrypted validator weights cannot be decrypted by anyone until the designated
[Glossary: Drand/time-lock encryption](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#drandtime-lock-encryption)
round is reached. That restriction includes the validator who submitted the concealed weights.

The point is stronger than ordinary private-key secrecy. A validator cannot treat the commit step as
a personal preview of readable weights, because the time-lock condition binds decryption to future
beacon output rather than to the submitter's signing keys
([Glossary: Validator Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-weights)).

That property keeps the concealment interval meaningful for observers as well as for the committing
validator. Until the unlock round arrives, the committed weight information stays unreadable across
the network, which is what makes the delay useful against immediate copying of fresh evaluations
([The Weight Copying Problem](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/weight-copying-in-bittensor)).

References: [Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal),
[Glossary: Drand/time-lock encryption](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#drandtime-lock-encryption)

## Chain Automation Replaces Manual Reveal Steps

The same Commit Reveal overview explains that time-lock encryption lets the chain automatically
reveal validator weights after the concealment interval. That automation removes the need for manual
reveal steps and helps prevent selective revelation attacks, where a validator might otherwise
choose which committed weights to disclose
([Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal)).

Validators still set weights through the ordinary submission path, while the concealed commit and
later reveal handling run on chain around those signals. Drand time-lock encryption supplies the
timed unlock condition that makes that automatic path possible without trusting each validator to
self-disclose on schedule
([Glossary: Drand/time-lock encryption](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#drandtime-lock-encryption)).

The distinction matters for how the term should be read. Drand time-lock encryption is not a
separate validator workflow; it is the shared timing layer that lets concealed weights move from
unreadable commit to readable reveal without optional manual disclosure in between
([The Weight Copying Problem](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/weight-copying-in-bittensor)).

References: [Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal),
[The Weight Copying Problem](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/weight-copying-in-bittensor)

## Public Beacon Randomness Sets the Unlock Point

The
[Glossary: Drand/time-lock encryption](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#drandtime-lock-encryption)
entry describes Drand as a distributed randomness beacon that publishes publicly verifiable,
unpredictable random numbers through independent operators in the League of Entropy consortium.
Time-lock encryption in that setting encrypts data so it can be decrypted only after a specific
time, using randomness the beacon produces on a regular schedule.

Bittensor uses that external beacon as the unlock reference inside Commit Reveal rather than relying
on a subnet-local clock or a validator-held decryption key. The committed weight data may already
exist on chain, but readability waits for the beacon output tied to the target round
([Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal)).

That external anchor is what separates Drand time-lock encryption from generic encryption labels.
The important feature is delayed readability tied to public randomness anyone can verify, not merely
that the weight payload was encrypted before reveal
([Glossary: Drand/time-lock encryption](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#drandtime-lock-encryption)).
