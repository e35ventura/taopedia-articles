Green Compute is Bittensor Subnet 110. Its on-chain identity describes the subnet as enterprise
inference hardware powered by 100% green energy and lists
[green-compute.com](https://www.green-compute.com/) as the subnet URL. The live identity does not
currently publish a GitHub repository.

## What Green Compute Provides

Green Compute presents itself as a decentralized GPU-compute marketplace on Bittensor. The website
describes users renting GPUs, running OpenAI-compatible inference, and using verified-green compute
capacity. It also names Bittensor subnet 110 as the network layer for the marketplace.

The public product framing is specific to inference and hardware supply. The site references RTX
4090 and RTX 5090 hardware, metered inference, enterprise buyers, and compute providers that
contribute renewable-powered GPU sites. It also describes hardware-location proofs and green-energy
verification as requirements for miner participation.

## Marketplace Boundary Context

Green Compute's public site describes three connected audiences: buyers who want inference or GPU
runtime, providers who contribute verified hardware, and stakers who are exposed to subnet demand.
That matters because the subnet is framed as a market for usable compute rather than as a one-model
competition. The buyer-facing claim is access to GPU capacity; the provider-facing claim is earning
for hardware that satisfies the subnet's verification expectations.

The same source also separates the runtime examples from the underlying subnet role. It mentions
OpenAI-compatible inference, language-model serving, image models, fine-tuning, training pods, and
long-running agent workloads. Those examples describe what customers may run on the supplied GPUs,
not separate miner categories. At the article level, they are best read as demand-side use cases for
the same compute marketplace.

The energy requirement is the other boundary. The website's provider path describes green-energy
attestation and hardware-location proofs before a miner is eligible for rewards. That keeps the
subnet's public identity tied to both compute capacity and source-of-power verification. A miner is
not only offering a machine; the public materials say the machine has to be part of a verified
green-energy site.

For readers comparing compute subnets, this makes Green Compute narrower than a general distributed
GPU network. The article should treat hardware class, availability, energy source, and customer
runtime demand as connected parts of the same claim. The subnet's published positioning is not just
"more GPUs on Bittensor"; it is verified renewable-powered GPUs made available for metered inference
and adjacent AI workloads.

This boundary also limits what the public source proves. It supports describing Green Compute as a
verified-green GPU marketplace for inference and related workloads. It does not publish a GitHub
repository or a complete validator scoring implementation through the live identity, so deeper
claims about exact reward formulas should be checked against current subnet operations rather than
inferred from the marketing page alone.

Reference: [Green Compute website](https://www.green-compute.com/)

## Miner and Validator Roles

Miners are hardware providers. In Green Compute's public materials, they contribute GPU capacity
from renewable-powered sites and are evaluated around whether their hardware and power source meet
the subnet's verification requirements. The site states that verified green nodes receive emission
priority and that hardware-location proofs are part of the participation model.

Validators score miner-provided compute. The website describes validators as checking miner work
against inference demand and verification requirements before payouts. Those scores become subnet
weights that feed into [[Yuma Consensus]], which converts validator agreement into Bittensor
incentives.

## On-Chain Identity

Live SN110 data is available on [TaoStats](https://taostats.io/subnets/110). The live Finney
identity for netuid 110 reports the subnet name as Green Compute, the subnet URL as
[green-compute.com](https://www.green-compute.com/), and the description as "Enterprise Inference
Hardware, 100% Green Energy." The GitHub, contact, and Discord fields are currently blank.
