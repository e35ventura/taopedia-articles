Green Compute is Bittensor Subnet 110. Its on-chain identity describes the subnet as enterprise
inference hardware powered by 100% green energy and lists
[green-compute.com](https://www.green-compute.com/) as the subnet URL. The live identity does not
currently publish a GitHub repository.

## What Green Compute Provides

Green Compute presents itself as a decentralized GPU-compute marketplace on Bittensor. The website
describes users renting GPUs, running OpenAI-compatible inference, and using verified-green compute
capacity. It also names Bittensor subnet 110 as the network layer for the marketplace.

The public product framing is specific to inference and hardware supply. The site references RTX
4090 and RTX 5090 hardware, metered inference, enterprise buyers, and compute providers that
contribute renewable-powered GPU sites. Its provider page also presents hardware-location proofs and
green-energy verification as part of the provider path.

## Marketplace Boundary Context

Green Compute's homepage and provider page describe a two-sided compute market. The customer side is
metered GPU access for inference and related AI workloads. The provider side is GPU capacity from
sites whose power source can be verified as low-carbon. Reading those sources together keeps the
subnet description centered on usable compute supply and demand rather than on one named model.

The homepage lists demand-side examples such as OpenAI-compatible inference, language-model serving,
image models, fine-tuning, training pods, and long-running agent workloads. Those examples show the
range of workloads the public site connects to rented GPU capacity. They also explain why the live
identity describes Green Compute as enterprise inference hardware instead of as a general-purpose
article about every compute task on Bittensor.

The provider page gives the supply-side boundary. It says Subnet 110 miners provide inference
capacity from clean-energy sites, and it describes carbon-credit-style attestation plus hardware
proof-of-location as the verification frame. That source supports saying the provider path combines
compute service, energy provenance, and hardware location.

For readers, the useful interpretation is the connection between compute buyers, verified providers,
and Bittensor settlement. Enterprise demand is described through metered inference and GPU runtime.
Provider participation is described through green-energy proof and hardware registration. The subnet
identity ties those two sides together under Green Compute's public "Enterprise Inference Hardware"
description.

This keeps the article's factual scope narrow. The public pages support the marketplace framing, GPU
classes, workload examples, and provider-verification language. The live identity confirms the
registered subnet name, website, and green-energy inference description, while leaving the GitHub,
contact, and Discord fields blank.

## Miner and Validator Roles

Miners are hardware providers. In Green Compute's public materials, they contribute GPU capacity
from renewable-powered sites and are described around whether their hardware and power source fit
the subnet's provider-verification model. The site states that verified green nodes receive emission
priority and that hardware-location proofs are part of the participation model.

Validators score miner-provided compute. The website describes validators as checking miner work
against inference demand and verification requirements before payouts. Those scores become subnet
weights that feed into [[Yuma Consensus]], which converts validator agreement into Bittensor
incentives.

## On-Chain Identity

Live SN110 data is available on [TaoStats](https://taostats.io/subnets/110). The live Finney
identity for netuid 110 reports the subnet name as Green Compute, the subnet URL as
[green-compute.com](https://www.green-compute.com/), and the description as "Enterprise Inference
Hardware, 100% Green Energy." The GitHub, contact, and Discord fields are currently blank.
