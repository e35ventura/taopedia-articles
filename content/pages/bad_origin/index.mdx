# Bad Origin

`BadOrigin` means a privileged extrinsic was signed by an account that does not have the permission
level the call requires. The standard-errors reference describes it as "Wallet not authorized.
Ensure that the account has the correct root or subnet owner permissions"
([Subtensor Standard Errors](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/errors/subtensor#badorigin)).

Many Subtensor actions are open to ordinary coldkeys and hotkeys, but subnet hyperparameter changes,
governance actions, and other owner-scoped calls require a specific signing role. `BadOrigin` is the
broad authorization rejection when that role check fails at the origin boundary
([Bittensor CLI: Permissions Guide](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/btcli/btcli-permissions),
[Subtensor Error Codes](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/errors)).

## What It Means

An extrinsic's origin is the account that signed the call. `BadOrigin` means the runtime rejected
the dispatch because that origin did not satisfy the permission filter for the extrinsic family
being attempted
([Subtensor Standard Errors](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/errors/subtensor#badorigin),
[Subtensor Error Codes](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/errors)).

On owner-scoped subnet paths, the required origin is typically the coldkey that owns the targeted
subnet. On root-scoped governance or sudo paths, the required origin is a privileged network account
instead of an ordinary participant wallet
([Bittensor CLI: Permissions Guide](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/btcli/btcli-permissions),
[Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)).

This is an authorization gate. The chain is rejecting who signed the call, not whether the submitted
parameter values, balances, or subnet state would otherwise pass later validation.

## Why It Appears

This error appears when a wallet signs a privileged extrinsic without the required root or
subnet-owner role. That can happen when automation uses a participant coldkey for an owner-only
hyperparameter change, when the wrong wallet file is loaded in tooling, or when a copied extrinsic
still references an account that is not the subnet owner on the targeted netuid
([Bittensor CLI: Permissions Guide](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/btcli/btcli-permissions),
[Subtensor Standard Errors](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/errors/subtensor#badorigin)).

When debugging owner-scoped subnet calls on netuid 1, confirm the signing coldkey is the owner of
that subnet before reading balance, timing, or parameter-value errors. Live ownership for registered
subnets can be checked through explorers such as [TaoStats](https://taostats.io/subnets/1)
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

When debugging, rule out other authority failures on the same extrinsic family. `NotSubnetOwner`
means the signer is not the owner of the targeted subnet on an owner-scoped path.
`NonAssociatedColdKey` means the signing coldkey does not own the hotkey named in a coldkey-hotkey
operation
([Subtensor Standard Errors: NotSubnetOwner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/errors/subtensor#notsubnetowner),
[Subtensor Standard Errors: NonAssociatedColdKey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/errors/subtensor#nonassociatedcoldkey)).

The practical meaning is narrow: the signing account lacked the privileged permission required for
the call.

## Distinction from Not Subnet Owner

`BadOrigin` and `NotSubnetOwner` both involve authorization on privileged subnet paths, but at
different layers. `BadOrigin` is the broad origin rejection when the signer does not meet the
required permission filter. `NotSubnetOwner` is the narrower Subtensor error returned when an
owner-scoped extrinsic reaches the subnet-owner check and the signer is not the owner of the named
subnet
([Subtensor Standard Errors: NotSubnetOwner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/errors/subtensor#notsubnetowner)).

- **BadOrigin** — the signing account lacks the required privileged permission.
- **NotSubnetOwner** — the signer is not the owner of the targeted subnet.

## Distinction from Non Associated Cold Key

`BadOrigin` and `NonAssociatedColdKey` both mean the wrong account signed a call, but they test
different relationships. `BadOrigin` concerns privileged roles such as root or subnet-owner
permission. `NonAssociatedColdKey` concerns whether the signing coldkey owns the hotkey named in a
staking-style operation
([Non Associated Cold Key](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/errors/subtensor#nonassociatedcoldkey)).

- **BadOrigin** — missing root or subnet-owner permission for a privileged call.
- **NonAssociatedColdKey** — coldkey does not own the targeted hotkey.

## Distinction from Admin Action Prohibited During Weights Window

`BadOrigin` and `AdminActionProhibitedDuringWeightsWindow` can both appear on subnet hyperparameter
changes, but they reject different problems. `BadOrigin` means authorization failed before the call
could proceed. `AdminActionProhibitedDuringWeightsWindow` means the signer was otherwise permitted,
but the admin freeze window around epoch boundaries was active
([Admin Action Prohibited During Weights Window](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/errors/subtensor#adminactionprohibitedduringweightswindow)).

- **BadOrigin** — the signer lacks required privileged permission.
- **AdminActionProhibitedDuringWeightsWindow** — authorized change arrived during the freeze window.

## Distinction from Not Senate Member

`BadOrigin` and `NotSenateMember` both involve governance authority, but on different paths.
`BadOrigin` is the generic privileged-origin rejection on calls that require root or subnet-owner
permission. `NotSenateMember` is the governance-specific rejection when a Senate-only action is
signed by an account that is not a Senate member
([Subtensor Standard Errors: NotSenateMember](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/errors/subtensor#notsenatemember)).

- **BadOrigin** — signer lacks the required privileged permission for the call.
- **NotSenateMember** — signer is not a Senate member on a Senate-scoped path.

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the meaning of the error label and the privileged-origin gate it represents. It
does not describe how subnet ownership is assigned, which coldkey owns a live subnet, or every
extrinsic that requires root permission; those are documented separately
([Bittensor CLI: Permissions Guide](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/btcli/btcli-permissions),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).
