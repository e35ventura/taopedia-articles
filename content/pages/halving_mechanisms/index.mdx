# Halving Mechanisms

A halving reduces a token emission rate by 50%. In Bittensor, halving mechanisms apply to both
network-level TAO emissions and subnet-level alpha-token emissions
([Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

## What It Represents

TAO halving reduces network-level TAO emission after total TAO issuance reaches a supply-based
threshold. Alpha halving reduces a subnet alpha token's emission rate after that subnet's alpha
issuance reaches its own threshold
([Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving),
[Glossary: Issuance](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#issuance)).

Halvings are supply-threshold events, not fixed calendar or block-count schedules. The relevant
question is how much supply has been issued, not what date appears on a schedule
([Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving)).

During a TAO halving, alpha rewards to miners, validators, and subnet owners do not halve and remain
constant. Only the reserve-side alpha injected alongside TAO pool movement is cut proportionally
([Effect on Alpha Emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving#effect-on-alpha-emissions)).

During an alpha halving, protocol alpha distributed to miners, validators, and subnet owners each
block is reduced by half uniformly within the affected subnet
([Alpha Halvings](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving#alpha-halvings)).

Examples tied to netuid 1 illustrate one subnet's alpha halving path. They should not be read as
representing production halving behavior on another network
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).

## Distinction from Alpha Halving

Halving mechanisms name the supply-triggered rate-cut rules for TAO and subnet alpha. Alpha halving
is the subnet-scoped instance on one subnet's alpha token
([Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving),
[Alpha Halvings](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving#alpha-halvings)).

- **Halving mechanisms** — supply-triggered 50% rate-cut rules for TAO and subnet alpha.
- **Alpha halving** — subnet milestone that cuts that subnet's alpha emission rate.

## Distinction from Issuance

Issuance names total circulating TAO. Halving mechanisms are the rules that cut emission rates when
relevant issuance thresholds are reached
([Glossary: Issuance](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#issuance),
[Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving)).

- **Issuance** — the circulating TAO supply measure.
- **Halving mechanisms** — rate-change rules activated at supply thresholds.

## Distinction from Block Reward

The block reward is the amount of new TAO emitted per block. Halving mechanisms cut that per-block
emission in half at supply milestones
([Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

- **Block reward** — new TAO emitted per block.
- **Halving mechanisms** — supply-triggered cuts to that emission rate.

## Distinction from Maximum Supply

Maximum supply names the network-wide 21 million TAO ceiling. Halving mechanisms are the
supply-triggered rate cuts that slow minting as issuance approaches that ceiling
([Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving)).

- **Maximum supply** — the fixed lifetime ceiling on total TAO issuance.
- **Halving mechanisms** — rate cuts that slow new issuance toward that ceiling.

## Distinction from Recycling

Halving mechanisms cut the per-block emission rate at supply thresholds. Recycling returns deducted
TAO toward future protocol distribution, which can change how quickly the next halving threshold is
reached ([Glossary: Halving](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#halving),
[Glossary: Recycling](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling)).

- **Halving mechanism** — a supply-threshold cut to the per-block emission rate.
- **Recycling** — returning deducted TAO toward future protocol distribution.

## Distinction from Coinbase

Coinbase is the per-block process that advances emission accounting and applies halving schedules.
Halving mechanisms name when rates drop
([Coinbase Implementation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/navigating-subtensor/emissions-coinbase),
[Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving)).

- **Halving mechanisms** — supply thresholds and emission-rate reductions.
- **Coinbase** — the per-block process that carries those rates through emission paths.

## Distinction from ADR

ADR compares outside-pool alpha emissions with pool injections using TAO and alpha halving indexes.
Halving mechanisms name when emission rates step down
([Glossary: ADR](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#adr-alpha-distribution-ratio),
[Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving)).

- **Halving mechanisms** — when emission rates drop after supply thresholds.
- **ADR** — how outside-pool versus pool-injection split responds to halving index divergence.

## Distinction from Yuma Consensus

Halving mechanisms reduce token emission rates at supply thresholds. Yuma Consensus converts
validator weight submissions into emission shares each tempo
([Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving)).

- **Halving mechanisms** — supply-based cuts to TAO and subnet alpha emission rates.
- **Yuma Consensus** — on-chain settlement that turns validator weights into emission shares.

## Reader Boundary

Halving mechanisms should not be read as reward tables, exact timing reports, or advice about token
movement. They name supply-based rules that reduce TAO and subnet alpha emission rates when relevant
thresholds are reached ([Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).
