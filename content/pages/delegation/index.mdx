# Delegation

Delegation is the process of staking TAO to a validator. The official
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation) describes delegation as
staking TAO to a validator, increasing that validator's stake and helping secure a validator permit.

## Validator Support

Delegation is a support relationship between a TAO holder and a validator. The
[Staking/delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
explains that staking, also known as delegation, supports validators because their total stake in a
subnet includes stake delegated by others.

This makes delegation different from the validator role itself. A delegate is the validator
receiving delegated stake, while delegation is the process that attaches staking support to that
validator.

## Stake Context

Delegated TAO contributes to the validator's stake context. The
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake) entry defines stake as
the currency-token amount delegated to a validator UID in a subnet, including both the validator's
own stake and stake delegated from others.

For a reader, delegation should therefore be read as part of the stake picture, not as a separate
validation task. It changes the support associated with a validator position; it does not make the
delegator perform validation work.

## Consensus and Emissions Context

Delegation matters because validator stake affects subnet consensus and emission context. The
[Staking/delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
states that a validator's total stake helps determine consensus power and share of emissions. The
[Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit)
also places stake weight in the context of validation rights.

This article keeps the concept at the reference level. It does not calculate rewards, rank
validators, or describe how to carry out staking actions.

## Nomination Handoff

Delegation names the support relationship between a TAO holder and a validator. The official
[Glossary: Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate) describes
nomination as staking TAO on a validator hotkey so token holders can participate through active
validators. That action vocabulary is the operational side of the delegation relationship defined
here.

For readers, delegation is the relationship concept; nominate is the action term used when
describing how nominators attach TAO to validators before stake amounts and emissions context are
interpreted.

References: [Glossary: Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Relationship to Effective Stake

Effective stake names the delegate-side total of own and nominated TAO, while delegation names the
support relationship that produces that total. The
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake)
defines effective stake as combined self and nominated TAO for a delegate. Delegation is the process
vocabulary; effective stake is the resulting amount vocabulary on the delegate side.

References:
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake),
[Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation)

## Relationship to Delegate Stake

Delegate stake is the self-staked TAO portion for a delegate, while delegation describes nominators
attaching additional TAO. The
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake) isolates
the delegate's own contribution. Delegation therefore explains how external support arrives; delegate
stake explains what the validator supplied personally.

References:
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake),
[Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate)

## Relationship to Incentives

Incentives name miner-side emission results after Yuma Consensus, while delegation names how TAO
holders support validators through staking. Delegation does not make a nominator perform miner work;
it changes validator stake context that later interacts with consensus and emissions. The
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus) documentation separates
miner incentives from validator-side outcomes.

References: [Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)

## Relationship to Dividends

Dividends are validator-side consensus outcomes derived from bonds and miner incentives. Delegation
feeds the stake context validators carry, but dividends remain a consensus result term rather than a
direct delegation payout label. Readers should consult
[Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions)
for validator-side emission language.

References:
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights)

## Relationship to Validator Trust

Validator trust is a consensus-side trust measure in subnet evaluation vocabulary. Delegation is a
staking support process and should not be confused with trust scores displayed in explorer or
consensus contexts. Official
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus) material is the right
reference for trust-like consensus measures.

References: [Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Glossary: Validator Trust](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-trust)

## Relationship to Staking into Subnets

Staking into subnets converts TAO support into subnet-local alpha context. The
[Staking into Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation#staking-into-subnets)
section explains that stake is local to a subnet. Delegation should be read with that boundary: a
nominator delegates TAO, and the resulting stake context is subnet-specific rather than a single
network-wide balance label.

References:
[Staking into Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation#staking-into-subnets),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## Relationship to Liquidity Positions

Liquidity positions describe how participants interact with subnet pool mechanics in tokenomics
articles. Delegation belongs to validator support vocabulary. The two terms can appear near each
other when discussing subnet liquidity, but delegation is not itself a pool position label.

References:
[Understanding Subnets: Liquidity pools](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets#liquidity-pools),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Relationship to Wallets, Coldkeys, and Hotkeys

Delegation actions are initiated from wallet contexts using coldkey and hotkey material described in
Bittensor wallet articles. Delegation names the support relationship; wallet articles name key
custody and signing boundaries. Readers should not treat delegation as a wallet type.

References:
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey),
[Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey)

## Relationship to Transaction Fees

Staking and unstaking transactions can incur fees described in tokenomics references. Delegation is
the support concept; fees are an operational cost layer on the transactions that implement staking
actions. Consult official fee documentation for current values rather than inferring fees from the
delegation definition alone.

References:
[Glossary: Transaction Fees](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#transaction-fees),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Relationship to Flow-Based Emissions

Flow-based emissions compare subnets using net TAO staking flows. Delegation contributes to the flow
story because it moves TAO into validator-associated subnet contexts, but delegation itself is not the
subnet weight or injection formula. See
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution-across-subnets) for the
injection-stage model.

References:
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Exponential Moving Averages](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/ema)

## Relationship to Validator Miner Bonds

Validator-miner bonds are consensus smoothing relationships between validators and miners. Delegation
is a staking relationship between nominators and validators. Bonds affect dividend-like validator
outcomes; delegation affects stake totals that precede consensus participation.

References:
[Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)

## Relationship to Root Subnet

The root subnet has specialized staking and validation context in Bittensor documentation. Delegation
articles on generic validator support should be read with subnet context in mind: delegation to a
subnet validator is not automatically the same as root-subnet participation without checking the
official root-subnet references.

References:
[Root subnet documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/root),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Relationship to Immunity Period

Immunity period language describes registration timing protections for miners in subnet lifecycle
articles. Delegation belongs to validator staking support and should not be read as miner immunity.
Keep subnet lifecycle terms separate from nominator-to-validator support terms.

References:
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## Relationship to Alpha Distribution Ratio

Alpha distribution ratio articles discuss how alpha-related emission shares are described in
tokenomics references. Delegation moves TAO into subnet staking contexts that later interact with
alpha mechanics, but delegation itself is not the ratio formula.

References:
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## Reader Boundary

Delegation should not be read as a recommendation to support any particular validator or as a status
report on current stake. Exact balances, fees, slippage, validator take, and current subnet state
belong in current official staking references.
