# Delegation

Delegation is the process of staking TAO to a validator. It names the support relationship between a
TAO holder and the validator receiving that stake
([Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

The term should be read separately from the validator role and from stake as an amount. Delegation
is relationship vocabulary inside the staking model.

## Validator Support

Delegation attaches staking support to a validator. A validator's total stake can include stake
delegated by others, so delegated TAO contributes to the validator's broader staking context
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake)).

This makes delegation different from performing validation work. The delegator supplies support,
while the validator performs the validator role.

## Stake and Nomination

Stake names the amount associated with a validator position after support exists. Delegation is one
path by which that support becomes attached
([Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation)).

Nominate is nearby action vocabulary. Delegation names the support relationship, while nominate
names the staking action that attaches TAO to a validator
([Glossary: Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate),
[Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation)).

## Consensus and Dividends

Delegation matters because validator stake is part of Bittensor consensus and emission context. It
helps explain why stake attached to a validator can matter for consensus power and emission outcomes
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit)).

Dividends are a related but distinct outcome term. Delegation establishes the support position, and
dividends describe an emission share that can follow from delegated-stake relationships
([Glossary: Dividends](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#dividends),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

## Scope Boundaries

Delegation is not a validator recommendation, balance report, fee explanation, or staking guide. It
does not rank validators or calculate dividends. It names staking TAO to a validator as support
inside Bittensor's staking model
([Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

Keeping that boundary visible prevents delegation from being mistaken for the validator role, the
stake amount, or a reward outcome.

## Environment Scope

Bittensor separates localnet, testnet, and mainnet environments. Delegation examples from one
environment should not be treated as evidence for another because validators, balances, emissions,
and subnet state are environment-specific
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).

Localnet examples are isolated development examples. Testnet examples are shared non-production
examples. Mainnet delegation concerns production validator relationships and production TAO.

## Autostaking and Emission Context

Delegation can also occur through autostaking. When a miner configures autostaking, mining emissions
are automatically routed as new delegated stake to the configured validator. That automated routing
is a form of delegation: the miner's emission-derived stake is attached to a validator without a
separate manual staking transaction
([Auto Staking for Miners](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners/autostaking),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

For readers, autostaking shows that delegation can come from multiple paths. Manual delegation
through the nominate action is one path; automatic emission routing through autostaking is another.
Both result in stake attached to a validator without the delegator performing the validator role.

The Dynamic TAO model also introduces alpha-token delegation contexts. In alpha-enabled subnets,
staking can operate through subnet-specific alpha, so delegation vocabulary extends to alpha-token
stake that is attached to a validator inside that subnet context
([Dynamic TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#dynamic-tao),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

The delegation concept — staking support attached to a validator — stays the same across TAO and
alpha contexts. The asset being delegated follows the relevant subnet's tokenomics, but the
relationship vocabulary and the distinction between delegator and validator role remain constant
([Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation)).
