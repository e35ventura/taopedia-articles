# Delegation

Delegation is the process of staking TAO to a validator. It names the support relationship between a
TAO holder and the validator receiving that stake
([Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

The term should be read separately from the validator role and from stake as an amount. Delegation
is relationship vocabulary inside the staking model.

## Validator Support

Delegation attaches staking support to a validator. A validator's total stake can include stake
delegated by others, so delegated TAO contributes to the validator's broader staking context
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake)).

This makes delegation different from performing validation work. The delegator supplies support,
while the validator performs the validator role.

The direction of the relationship matters. A nominator or delegator supplies TAO support, while the
delegate is the validator-side role receiving that support
([Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator),
[Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate)).

That makes delegation relationship vocabulary rather than a validator-status label. The same staking
relationship can involve a holder, a validator, and a stake amount, but delegation names the support
connection between those pieces.

## Stake and Nomination

Stake names the amount associated with a validator position after support exists. Delegation is one
path by which that support becomes attached
([Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation)).

Nominate is nearby action vocabulary. Delegation names the support relationship, while nominate
names the staking action that attaches TAO to a validator
([Glossary: Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate),
[Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation)).

Delegate stake and effective stake are amount-side terms near the same relationship. Delegate stake
identifies the delegate's own supplied TAO, while effective stake summarizes the broader total
associated with a delegate after nominated support is counted
([Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake),
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake)).

Those distinctions keep delegation from being used as a catch-all amount. Delegation explains how
support is attached; stake, delegate stake, and effective stake describe different amount views
around that support.

## Consensus and Dividends

Delegation matters because validator stake is part of Bittensor consensus and emission context. It
helps explain why stake attached to a validator can matter for consensus power and emission outcomes
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit)).

Dividends are a related but distinct outcome term. Delegation establishes the support position, and
dividends describe an emission share that can follow from delegated-stake relationships
([Glossary: Dividends](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#dividends),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

Stake weight is the consensus-side interpretation of stake context. Delegation can help explain why
stake is attached to a validator, but stake weight describes how the resulting stake context is used
for validator consensus power and emissions
([Glossary: Stake Weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight),
[Understanding Subnets: Validator stake weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets#validator-stake-weight)).

This separates the relationship from the outcome. Delegation can position support behind a
validator; dividends and stake weight describe neighboring result and interpretation vocabulary.

## Scope Boundaries

Delegation is not a validator ranking, balance report, fee explanation, or staking guide. It does
not rank validators or calculate dividends. It names staking TAO to a validator as support inside
Bittensor's staking model
([Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

Keeping that boundary visible prevents delegation from being mistaken for the validator role, the
stake amount, or a reward outcome.

Delegation should also stay separate from wallet custody and address vocabulary. Wallet terms
identify where keys and accounts are represented, while delegation identifies a staking support
relationship involving TAO and a validator
([Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

## Environment Scope

Bittensor separates localnet, testnet, and mainnet environments. Delegation examples from one
environment should not be treated as evidence for another because validators, balances, emissions,
and subnet state are environment-specific
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).

Localnet examples are isolated development examples. Testnet examples are shared non-production
examples. Mainnet delegation concerns production validator relationships and production TAO.

The concept can be explained across environments, but concrete validator relationships and stake
amounts belong to the network where they were observed. A localnet delegation example can show the
relationship vocabulary, while a mainnet statement needs production context
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation)).
