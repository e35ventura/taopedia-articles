# Hotkey Swap On Subnet Interval

Hotkey swap on subnet interval is the per-subnet rate limit a hotkey swap must satisfy, named in the
documentation as `HotkeySwapOnSubnetInterval`. The rate-limits reference lists it as the per-subnet
portion of the hotkey swap rate limit, at roughly 36,000 blocks or about five days
([Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits)).

## What It Controls

A [hotkey swap](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey-swap) replaces the hotkey
tied to an account's stake and registrations. The documentation states a swap must satisfy two
separate rate limits: a short network-wide transaction limit and this longer per-subnet interval.
This interval sets how long must pass on a subnet before a hotkey can be swapped there again
([Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits)).

## When It Matters

The common misconception is that clearing the fast network-wide
[tx rate limit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits), which lifts after about a
block, is enough to swap again. On a subnet it is not: this per-subnet interval still blocks a
repeat swap for roughly five days
([Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits)).

Concretely, an operator who swaps a validator's hotkey on netuid 1, then realizes the new hotkey is
also wrong, cannot immediately swap it again on netuid 1. The network-wide limit clears within a
block, but netuid 1's swap interval must elapse first, so the corrected swap waits out the roughly
five-day window. That waiting cost is the point: it makes rapid hotkey rotation on a subnet
expensive enough to discourage churning hotkeys to game participation
([Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits)).

## Documented Nature

The [Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits) reference describes
`HotkeySwapOnSubnetInterval` as a global interval constant rather than a per-subnet owner setting,
and notes it is not queryable from chain state the way mutable hyperparameters are. The same fixed
interval governs the per-subnet swap cooldown across subnets, evaluated against whichever netuid a
swap touches ([Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits),
[Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).

## Distinction from Tx Rate Limit

Tx rate limit is the short network-wide transaction limit that also applies to hotkey swaps. Hotkey
swap on subnet interval is the longer per-subnet cooldown, and a swap must clear both
([Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits)).

- **Tx rate limit** — short network-wide transaction cooldown.
- **Hotkey swap on subnet interval** — longer per-subnet hotkey-swap cooldown.

## Distinction from Coldkey Swap

Hotkey swap on subnet interval paces repeated rotation of a hotkey on one subnet. Coldkey swap is
the separate staged recovery path that migrates an entire wallet's on-chain identity to a new
coldkey, including balances, subnet ownership, and associated hotkeys
([Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits),
[Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)).

That scope difference is the practical boundary. A hotkey swap changes the operational key under the
same coldkey owner and is blocked by this per-subnet interval if repeated too soon. A coldkey swap
changes the wallet's controlling coldkey through its own announcement, lock, and dispute process
rather than through this hotkey-specific cooldown
([Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits),
[Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)).

- **Hotkey swap on subnet interval** — cooldown on repeating a hotkey rotation for one subnet.
- **Coldkey swap** — staged migration of the full wallet identity to a new coldkey.
