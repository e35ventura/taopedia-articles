# Front Running

Front running is the practice of detecting a pending transaction and submitting a competing
transaction ahead of it, typically to extract value from the price movement the first transaction
would cause
([Glossary: Front running](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#front-running)).

In Bittensor, front running is most relevant in subnet pool operations such as staking and
unstaking, where large transactions can move the alpha price. Price protection is the mechanism
designed to limit the value that front running can extract from these operations.

## Pool Context

Front running risk arises when a pending staking or unstaking transaction is visible before it is
included in a block. A front runner observing the pending transaction can act first and then profit
from the subsequent price movement
([Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)).

This makes front running a pool-liquidity concern in Bittensor. It is not a general network-security
attack on consensus or validator behavior.

## Price Protection Response

Price protection allows a caller to set a minimum acceptable rate for their staking or unstaking
operation. If front running shifts the price beyond that minimum, the operation reverts rather than
completing at the degraded rate
([Price Protection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/price-protection)).

That revert mechanism limits what a front runner can extract. The front runner may move the price,
but the original transaction will not settle at a rate the caller did not accept.

## Slippage Relationship

Slippage and front running are related but distinct concepts. Slippage is the price movement that
occurs naturally from pool size and transaction size. Front running is deliberate action by another
party who profits from the slippage a pending transaction will cause
([Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)).

Price protection addresses both: it gives callers a floor that limits the combined impact of natural
slippage and adversarial front running.

## Reader Boundary

Front running is concept vocabulary for transaction-ordering exploitation in pool-based operations.
The term should not be read as a validator scoring issue, a consensus attack, or a general network
safety concern
([Glossary: Front running](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#front-running)).

The risk is specific to operations that interact with subnet pool pricing. Transfers, registration,
and consensus participation are outside the front-running vocabulary as used in Bittensor.
