# Front-Running

Front-running is acting on the knowledge of someone else's pending transaction by getting your own
transaction in first. In Bittensor it appears around a subnet's automated market maker pool: a swap
that is waiting to execute is visible before it lands, and another trader can try to order their own
swap ahead of it to profit from the price move the pending swap will cause.

References: [Front-Running](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#front-running)

## Why It Is Possible

A transaction does not execute the instant it is submitted. It first sits in the mempool, the
holding area of pending transactions waiting to be included in a block, where its contents can be
observed. Because a subnet prices alpha through a pool whose price moves with every swap, seeing a
large pending swap tells an observer which way the price is about to move and by roughly how much.
That visibility is the raw material front-running depends on.

References: [Mempool](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#mempool),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## The Sandwich Pattern

The best-known front-running pattern is the sandwich attack. An attacker who sees a pending buy
places their own buy first, lets the victim's order push the price up, then sells into that higher
price. The victim's trade is wrapped, or sandwiched, between the attacker's two trades, and the
extra slippage the victim pays is the attacker's gain. Front-running is the front half of that
pattern, and it can also stand alone whenever ordering ahead of a known trade is profitable on its
own.

References: [Sandwich Attack](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#sandwich-attack),
[Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)

## What Defends Against It

Two mechanisms reduce the payoff. Price protection lets a trader set a limit on the price they will
accept, so a swap that would execute worse than that limit fails instead of filling at the
front-runner's inflated price, capping how much can be extracted. The MEV shield works earlier in
the pipeline by limiting the predatory ordering of transactions itself, so a pending swap is harder
to profitably trade ahead of in the first place. Together they attack both the payoff and the
opportunity.

References: [Price Protection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/price-protection),
[MEV Shield](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/mev-shield/)

## Beyond Trading: Weight Copying

Front-running also has a non-trading form. A validator can watch the weights other validators submit
and copy or trade ahead of them rather than do its own evaluation, free-riding on their work.
Commit-reveal answers this by having validators first publish only a hidden commitment to their
weights and reveal the actual values later, so there is nothing readable to copy or run ahead of at
submission time. The shape is the same as a pool sandwich, even though the thing being front-run is
an evaluation rather than a swap.

References: [Weight Copying](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/weight-copying-in-bittensor),
[Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept at a high level and is not trading advice or a claim that any specific
trade was front-run. Whether front-running is profitable in a given moment depends on live pool
depth, pending order flow, and the protections in force, all of which change continuously. The
durable point is the relationship: visible pending transactions plus price-moving pools create the
opening, and price protection and the MEV shield are what narrow it.

References: [Price Protection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/price-protection)
