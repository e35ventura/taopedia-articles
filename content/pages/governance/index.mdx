# Bittensor Governance

Bittensor governance is the network process for reviewing and executing privileged changes. The
current protocol moves administrative authority away from a single `sudo` key and toward a bicameral
structure: the Triumvirate creates proposals, and the Senate approves or rejects them.

References: [Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance),
[Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate)

## Why governance matters

Privileged network changes can affect subnet creation, hyperparameters, chain upgrades, and other
configuration that shapes how Bittensor operates. Before governance, these actions depended on
single-key `sudo` authority. The governance model raises the control threshold: an attacker would
need both Triumvirate access and control over a Senate majority to execute a malicious proposal.

Reference: [Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance)

## Proposal flow

A proposal wraps the call data for a privileged action. It does not execute immediately when it is
created. Execution requires two things:

- A majority of the Senate must approve it.
- A Triumvirate member must close the proposal after approval.

When a proposal is closed successfully, the proposal call data is executed in the same block as the
close action. This gives the Senate a review gate while leaving final execution with the
Triumvirate-controlled proposal process.

Reference: [Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance)

## The Senate

The Senate is made up of delegates who have elected to participate in governance and who control a
significant share of total network stake. TAO holders are represented indirectly through the
delegates they choose to stake with, so Senate membership connects governance participation to
delegated stake.

To participate, a coldkey-hotkey pair must be registered, the hotkey must hold more than two percent
of total network stake through self-stake or delegation, and the participant must elect into the
Senate. If all Senate seats are filled, a higher-stake delegate can replace the lowest-stake member.

Reference: [Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate)

## Viewing and voting

Proposal visibility is public. Anyone can inspect proposals currently before the Senate without
special permissions. Voting is restricted to senators, and senators use the proposal hash to cast an
approval or disapproval vote.

The Bittensor CLI permissions guide treats governance participation as a privileged action: coldkey
authority is required for participating as a senator, while hotkeys are also part of governance
identity and registration.

References: [Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate),
[BTCLI Permissions Guide](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/btcli/btcli-permissions)

## Practical implications

- **For stakers:** Delegation is also a governance signal because Senate members represent the stake
  delegated to them.
- **For senators:** Proposal review requires understanding the call data and the operational impact
  of the proposed change.
- **For subnet creators and operators:** Governance can affect network parameters, subnet
  configuration, and the rules that determine who can participate.
- **For readers:** Governance should be evaluated from official docs, proposal details, and
  implementation behavior rather than social claims alone.
