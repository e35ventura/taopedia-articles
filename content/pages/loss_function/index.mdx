# Loss Function

A loss function is a way of measuring how far a result is from the desired answer: the closer the
result, the lower the loss. In Bittensor, a subnet's incentive mechanism plays that role. It sets
the standard for what good work looks like and rewards miners according to how closely their work
meets it, so the incentive mechanism acts as the subnet's loss function.

References: [Loss Function](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#loss-function),
[Incentive Mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentive-mechanism)

## From One Model to a Whole Network

The idea comes from machine learning, where a loss tells a single system how well it is doing and in
which direction to improve. A subnet applies the same pressure, but across the network rather than
inside one program: the incentive mechanism defines the desired outcome, and every miner in the
subnet is pushed toward it at once. The subnet as a whole becomes the thing being improved.

References:
[Anatomy of an Incentive Mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## The Goal It Measures Against

A loss only means something relative to a goal. The goal a subnet keeps working toward, through its
incentive mechanism, is its objective function. The loss describes how far current work sits from
that goal, and the objective function is the goal itself. The two are a pair: the objective says
what the subnet wants, and the loss says how close it currently is.

References:
[Objective Function](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#objective-function),
[Loss Function](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#loss-function)

## Each Subnet Defines Its Own

There is no single network-wide loss. Each subnet decides what it is asking miners to do and how it
rates their answers, so the standard is specific to that subnet's purpose. A standard that suits a
language subnet says nothing about what a prediction or storage subnet should reward. A claim about
how miners are scored should therefore name the subnet it describes rather than generalize across
Bittensor.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Incentive Mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentive-mechanism)

## How the Standard Becomes Reward

The standard does not pay miners on its own. Validators judge miner work against the subnet's
incentive mechanism and submit weights that express those judgments, and Yuma Consensus combines the
validators' weights into the consensus that drives emissions. The loss shapes what validators are
looking for; consensus turns the resulting signals into the rewards miners actually receive.

References: [Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Anatomy of an Incentive Mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)

## Reader Boundary

Read a loss function as the standard a subnet measures miner work against, expressed through its
incentive mechanism, not as one rule shared by the whole network or as the payout step itself. It
describes how close work sits to the subnet's goal; consensus and emissions are separate stages that
act on the signals it produces.

References: [Loss Function](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#loss-function),
[Objective Function](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#objective-function)
