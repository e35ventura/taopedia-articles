# Issuance

Issuance is the circulating amount of TAO counted in Bittensor tokenomics. It includes TAO held
directly and TAO held in subnet liquidity pool contexts:
[Glossary: Issuance](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#issuance),
[Emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions).

The term is a supply-state concept. It names the TAO amount being counted, not a reward type, wallet
balance, or subnet-specific alpha-token category.

## Circulating Supply Context

Issuance is useful when a reader needs the circulating TAO supply frame. It keeps direct holdings
and pool-held TAO within one network-level accounting concept:
[Glossary: Issuance](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#issuance),
[Glossary: RAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rao).

That makes issuance broader than any one role's rewards. A token amount can belong to circulating
issuance even when the article topic is not a personal balance or payout path.

## Pool-Held TAO

Subnet liquidity pools matter for issuance because pool-held TAO remains part of the circulating TAO
picture. It is not a separate asset class just because it sits inside a subnet reserve context:
[Glossary: Issuance](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#issuance),
[Emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions).

This boundary helps distinguish issuance from wallet vocabulary. Issuance asks whether TAO is part
of the counted network supply, not whether one wallet directly controls the amount.

## Emission Boundary

Emission and issuance answer different questions. Emission is the process that creates and
distributes token amounts over time, while issuance is the circulating TAO supply state affected by
those processes: [Emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Glossary: Issuance](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#issuance).

This distinction keeps process language separate from accounting language. A statement about rewards
reaching miners, validators, stakers, or subnet creators is not the same as a statement about
circulating TAO supply.

## Halving Context

TAO halving uses issuance as a supply milestone. In that context, issuance supplies the measured TAO
amount, while halving names the emission-rate change tied to reaching supply thresholds:
[Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving),
[Emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions).

The terms should not be merged. Issuance is the supply measure; halving is the rate-change rule that
refers to that measure.

## Recycling and Burning Context

Recycling and burning are supply-flow concepts around issuance. Recycling can subtract token amounts
from the issuance record so they can be emitted again, while burning removes tokens from ordinary
circulation:
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning),
[Glossary: Issuance](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#issuance).

Issuance remains the counted supply context around those flows. It is not the name of either flow
and should not be used as a substitute for recycling, burning, or emission.

## Network Context

Issuance examples should be read in their network environment. Localnet, testnet, and mainnet are
different contexts, so a supply figure from one environment should not be treated as the supply
state of another: [Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development).

This boundary keeps examples from carrying more weight than their environment supports. The stable
concept is circulating TAO supply; the environment determines which network the example belongs to.
