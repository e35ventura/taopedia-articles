# Crowdloans

A crowdloan is a Bittensor funding structure that lets multiple contributors pool TAO toward subnet
creation. When the campaign succeeds, resulting emissions are shared pro-rata according to
contribution share ([Crowdloans](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)).

The term belongs to subnet bootstrapping. It explains how a group can fund the cost side of bringing
a subnet into existence, rather than describing a general staking strategy or a guaranteed emission
outcome
([Create a Subnet: Burn cost](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/create-a-subnet#burn-cost),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

## Collective Funding Context

Subnet creation has a TAO cost. A crowdloan spreads that cost across contributors by collecting
funds toward a campaign goal instead of requiring one sponsor to cover the full amount alone
([Crowdloans](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans),
[Glossary: Burn Cost](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burn-cost)).

That makes crowdloan a funding-context term. It names the pooled contribution structure around
subnet creation, while burn cost names the TAO amount associated with creation itself
([Create a Subnet: Burn cost](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/create-a-subnet#burn-cost)).

## Campaign Outcome Context

A crowdloan campaign can succeed or fail to finalize. Success creates the leased-subnet outcome
described by the crowdloan model; failure leaves the campaign in a refund path rather than turning
the funding effort into an operating subnet
([Crowdloans](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)).

This boundary is important for static reference prose. A crowdloan is conditional until its campaign
outcome is known. The article-level concept is pooled funding toward a specified subnet outcome, not
a claim that every campaign becomes a subnet
([Crowdloans](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)).

## Emission Sharing Context

When a crowdloan creates a leased subnet, emissions are shared according to contribution share. That
pro-rata framing is the crowdloan-specific tokenomics relationship between funding and later
emission flow ([Crowdloans](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans),
[Emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

Emission sharing should be read as a rule of the crowdloan structure, not as a prediction about
subnet performance. The actual emission context still depends on the subnet, consensus outcomes, and
network environment ([Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).

## Subnet Lifecycle Context

Crowdloans sit near subnet creation because they can fund a subnet entering the network. They also
remain distinct from later lifecycle events such as subnet removal or dissolution
([Crowdloans](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans),
[Subnet Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-deregistration)).

This keeps the lifecycle vocabulary separate. Crowdloan names a collective funding path at the start
of a leased-subnet path, while deregistration names a removal process that can occur under separate
network rules
([Subnet Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-deregistration)).

## Network Context

Network environment changes how crowdloan examples should be read. Localnet, testnet, and mainnet
are separate contexts, so a crowdloan example from one environment is evidence about that
environment rather than a universal statement for every Bittensor network
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).

The same boundary applies to campaign details. A cap, end block, contribution set, or emission
sharing example belongs to the campaign and network environment being discussed
([Crowdloans](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)).

## Reader Boundary

Crowdloans are reference vocabulary for collective subnet funding. They are not staking advice,
yield estimates, campaign recommendations, or step-by-step funding instructions. Concrete examples
require the selected campaign, subnet context, network environment, and relevant official source
([Crowdloans](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans),
[Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).
