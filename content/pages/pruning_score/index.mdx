# Pruning Score

A pruning score is the emission-based metric Bittensor uses when a subnet must remove or displace a
low-performing neuron from a UID slot. Official mining and validating documentation describe pruning
score as the chain-side signal tied to a neuron's emissions when a full subnet accepts a new
registration or when a subnet owner trims excess UIDs.

## Emission Context

The official
[Mining: Miner deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-deregistration) and
[Validating: Validator deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators#validator-deregistration)
documentation state that the subnet does not distinguish between miners and validators for
deregistration selection at this step. The chain looks at emissions represented as pruning score,
and the neuron with the lowest score becomes the displacement candidate when replacement rules
apply.

For readers, pruning score is therefore a participation-performance metric in subnet lifecycle
vocabulary, not a consensus rank label or a manual editorial judgment.

References:
[Mining: Miner deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-deregistration),
[Validating: Validator deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators#validator-deregistration)

## UID Slot Displacement

Pruning score matters when subnet UID capacity is constrained. The
[Glossary: UID Slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot) entry defines a
slot as a position occupied by a subnet miner or subnet validator, and subnet hyperparameters such
as `max_allowed_uids` set how many slots a subnet can hold. When a subnet is full and a new
registration arrives, official documentation identifies the participant with the lowest pruning
score as the displacement candidate.

This article does not quote live subnet counts or current displacement outcomes. Those values belong
in the [Subnet Metagraph](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/metagraph) and live chain data.

References: [Glossary: UID Slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot),
[Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters),
[Subnet Metagraph](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/metagraph)

## Immunity Period Boundary

Newly registered neurons are not immediately exposed to ordinary low-performance removal. The
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)
describes a grace window for newly registered participants, and official deregistration
documentation notes that immunity status can prevent an otherwise low pruning score from leading to
immediate displacement.

If all neurons are still immune when replacement is required, official documentation states that the
one with the lowest pruning score can still be deregistered by the next incoming registration. When
two or more neurons share the lowest score, the older node is displaced first.

References:
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period),
[Mining: Miner deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-deregistration)

## Relationship to Deregistration

Pruning score and deregistration address related but different parts of subnet lifecycle vocabulary.
[[Deregistration]] names the removal of a low-performing subnet participant from a UID slot, while
pruning score names the emission-based metric used to identify which participant is most eligible
for that removal when subnet capacity and replacement rules require displacement.

For readers, deregistration is the removal process, while pruning score is the selection signal tied
to emissions inside that process.

References:
[Glossary: Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#deregistration),
[Mining: Miner deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-deregistration)

## Relationship to UID Trimming

Pruning score also appears in subnet-management contexts beyond ordinary registration displacement.
The [UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming) documentation describes
subnet owners reducing excess neuron UIDs by removing lower-emission participants while preserving
immune and owner-protected slots. That process uses emission-based selection in the same broad
family as pruning-score displacement rules.

For readers, pruning score belongs to low-emission displacement vocabulary, while UID trimming names
an owner-controlled subnet-management action that applies similar emission ordering at subnet scope.

References: [UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming),
[Subnet Metagraph](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/metagraph)

## Relationship to Multiple Mechanisms

Subnets can run more than one incentive mechanism in parallel. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms) notes
that miner performance in one mechanism does not affect their rating in another.

Pruning score still names subnet-level emission displacement context for a neuron occupying one UID
slot. Mechanism count belongs with subnet incentive design rather than with the pruning score label
itself.

References:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms)

## Reader Boundary

Pruning score should not be read as a live ranking table, a staking recommendation, or a substitute
for rank or trust in Yuma Consensus vocabulary. It is the emission-based displacement metric used in
subnet registration and trimming context. Exact values and eligibility outcomes belong in official
subnet references and live chain data.
