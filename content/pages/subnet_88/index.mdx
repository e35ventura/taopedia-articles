Investing is Bittensor Subnet 88, a subnet that turns portfolio selection into a competitive network
task. Its public [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mobiusfund/investing/main/README.md)
describes the subnet as decentralized assets under management: miners provide competing investment
strategies, validators score their performance, and the project combines strong strategies into
portfolio intelligence that can be used for managed assets or offered to third parties.

## What Investing Provides

The Investing subnet produces ranked portfolio strategies for use in asset management. Participants
submit allocation strategies across supported asset classes — beginning with staking strategies in
the Tao/Alpha ecosystem at launch, then extending to US equities in a later phase — and the network
evaluates how those strategies perform over time. The result is a continuously ranked set of
strategies that represent the subnet's output: assessed investment approaches across multiple asset
classes. The project overview for
[Subnet 88](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mobiusfund/investing/main/Investing/doc/Subnet88.md)
frames the final output as an ensemble of top strategies rather than a single miner's portfolio:
miners contribute the raw strategies, and the subnet operator can combine the strongest approaches
into one or more meta-strategies for each asset class.

That design makes the article's subject different from a trading bot or a signal feed. The subnet is
not just publishing a model; it is maintaining a competition in which independent strategies are
measured over time and the resulting rankings become the network output.

## Strategy and Asset-Class Model

The source documentation defines a miner strategy as an allocation table within an asset class.
Miners can revise or rebalance their strategies as conditions change, so the competition is
continuous rather than a one-time submission. The README says the subnet launched with Tao/Alpha
staking strategies and then added US stocks, while the overview notes that the same design is meant
to support other asset classes over time.

Asset classes matter because Subnet 88 partitions UID space and emissions between them. The overview
describes this as an adjustable ratio: if a class receives a particular share of emissions, miners
in that class compete with one another for that portion instead of being scored directly against
unrelated markets. That lets a staking strategy and an equity strategy remain part of the same
subnet while still being evaluated inside market contexts that make sense for each.

## Miner and Validator Roles

Miners on netuid 88 submit investment strategies — allocations across supported assets — and update
them over time as conditions change. Validators evaluate submitted strategies by tracking their
performance and ranking them against others competing in the same asset class. Validator assessments
feed into [[Yuma Consensus]], which translates those rankings into emissions for the strongest
performers.

The validator role is therefore closer to portfolio evaluation than to ordinary request-response
inference scoring. Validators gather miner strategies, calculate how they performed, and assign
scores that account for return alongside risk and volatility. The source documentation also notes
that overly similar strategies can receive reduced scores, which makes duplication less attractive
than contributing an independent allocation view.
