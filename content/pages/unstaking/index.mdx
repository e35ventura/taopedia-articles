# Unstaking

Unstaking describes removing staked TAO from a staking position. Bittensor staking and delegation
material uses the term when explaining how TAO can be withdrawn from validator support and returned
to the coldkey ([Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking),
[Staking and Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

In plain language, unstaking answers what changes when a staker withdraws: the staked amount leaves
the delegation position and the balance becomes available under the coldkey again. It is not the
same as moving stake to a different hotkey or subnet, and it is not reducing a conviction lock while
keeping the position active
([Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking)).

For a reader, the distinction from moving stake matters most when the goal is different: unstaking
ends the staking relationship and frees the TAO for other use, while moving stake keeps the position
active under a new hotkey or subnet target.

## Staking Context

The term is the counterpart to
[staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking). Staking places TAO into a
staking context, while unstaking describes taking TAO out of that context. The important distinction
is conceptual: unstaking is not mining, validating, or a subnet task; it is part of wallet and
staking management ([Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation)).

A [nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator) supplies delegated
support before any removal occurs. Unstaking is exit-action vocabulary for that holder role;
nomination names the earlier attachment step that created the delegated relationship on the subnet
([Glossary: Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate)).

## Alpha Conversion Context

The [Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking) describes
unstaking as withdrawing staked TAO from a validator hotkey and converting subnet-specific alpha
tokens back to TAO through the subnet's automated market maker. That conversion can involve
[slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage), because pool reserves participate in the
exchange. The same glossary entry notes that Bittensor provides price-protection mechanisms for
unstaking operations and that unstaking incurs blockchain transaction fees.

## Development Stage Context

The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)
describes subnet development as moving from localnet to testnet and then mainnet. For unstaking on a
subnet such as netuid 1, that sequence changes how readers should interpret evidence about stake
withdrawal and pool conversion behavior.

In localnet, unstaking can be tested in an isolated environment. Localnet slippage and pool
conversion outcomes reflect local chain configuration and do not represent production subnet pool
state.

On testnet, unstaking can be observed in a shared, non-production network. Testnet unstaking
outcomes and pool interactions are separate from mainnet staking state.

On mainnet, unstaking is a live Subtensor operation that withdraws actual TAO or alpha from a
validator position back to the coldkey. The
[Staking and Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
documentation describes the withdrawal mechanics that apply on the production network.

The [Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks) reference
separates mainnet, testnet, and localnet. An unstaking example from one environment should not be
read as representing pool conversion behavior or stake amounts in another environment.

## Distinction from Moving Stake

The
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
lists unstaking and moving stake as different ways to rearrange an existing staked position.
Unstaking removes support from delegation, while
[moving stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation#moving-stake)
repoints that position between hotkeys and/or subnets in one atomic step without first withdrawing
to the coldkey
([Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking)).

- **Unstaking** — exit delegated support on the selected subnet.
- **Moving stake** — keep support delegated under a new hotkey or subnet target.

## Distinction from Slippage

The [Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking) describes
unstaking as withdrawing from a validator hotkey and converting subnet-specific alpha back to TAO
through the subnet automated market maker.
[Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage) names the gap between
expected and received amounts when that reserve-based conversion runs
([Glossary: Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#slippage)).

On a subnet such as netuid 1, that withdrawal uses paired reserves rather than a fixed one-for-one
exchange, so the received TAO amount can differ from a static pre-trade estimate
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

- **Unstaking** — withdrawal action that triggers alpha-to-TAO conversion through pool reserves.
- **Slippage** — gap between expected and received amounts during that conversion.

## Distinction from Price Protection

[Price protection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/price-protection) is the tolerance
mechanism that can govern how an unstaking conversion proceeds once pool movement is measured.
Unstaking is the withdrawal action that enters that conversion path
([Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking),
[Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)).

Price-protection vocabulary answers what happens after movement is measured. Unstaking vocabulary
names the withdrawal step itself on the selected subnet.

- **Unstaking** — withdrawal action that enters the conversion path.
- **Price protection** — tolerance mechanism governing how conversion proceeds once movement is
  measured.

## Distinction from Staking

[Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking) attaches TAO support to a
validator hotkey. Unstaking removes that support from the delegated position on the selected subnet
([Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking)).

- **Staking** — add support behind a validator.
- **Unstaking** — remove support through the documented withdrawal path.

## Distinction from Autostaking

[Autostaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners/autostaking) routes miner emission proceeds
into a staking position over time. Unstaking is the exit path that removes from an accumulated
delegated position through the ordinary withdrawal process
([Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking)).

Autostaking adds to a position as emissions arrive. Unstaking removes from that position when the
holder exits through the documented withdrawal flow.

- **Unstaking** — exit path that removes from an accumulated delegated position.
- **Autostaking** — routes miner emission proceeds into stake over time.

## Distinction from Nominate

[Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate) is the action that attaches
TAO support to a validator hotkey. Unstaking is the withdrawal action that removes that support from
the delegated relationship on the selected subnet
([Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation),
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking)).

Nominate vocabulary belongs at support attachment. Unstaking vocabulary belongs at support removal.

- **Nominate** — action that attaches TAO support to a validator hotkey.
- **Unstaking** — withdrawal action that removes that support from the delegated relationship.

## Distinction from Effective Stake

Unstaking and effective stake describe a position as withdrawal and resulting total. Unstaking is
the operation that removes staked TAO from a validator and returns it to the coldkey, while
effective stake is that validator's combined self-and-delegated total, so unstaking lowers the
effective stake of the validator the TAO leaves and with it that validator's stake-weighted
influence ([Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking),
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

- **Unstaking** — operation that removes staked TAO and returns it to the coldkey.
- **Effective stake** — combined self-and-delegated total behind the validator being exited.

## Distinction from Yuma Consensus

Unstaking names removing staked TAO from a validator delegation position. Yuma Consensus is the
separate subnet process that converts validator weight submissions into emission shares each tempo
and does not by itself move stake back to a coldkey
([Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

- **Unstaking** — withdrawing TAO from a delegation position.
- **Yuma Consensus** — mechanism that turns validator weights into emission shares.

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the term. It does not report balances, timing, fees, validator choices, or a
specific staking action. Readers should use current staking documentation for security-sensitive
staking actions
([Staking and Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking)).
