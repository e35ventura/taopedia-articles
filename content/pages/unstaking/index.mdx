# Unstaking

Unstaking describes removing staked TAO from a staking position. Official Bittensor documentation
uses staking and delegation terms when explaining how TAO can be associated with validators and
staking context.

References: [Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking),
[Staking and Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Staking Context

The term is the counterpart to staking. Staking places TAO into a staking context, while unstaking
describes taking TAO out of that context. The important distinction is conceptual: unstaking is not
mining, validating, or a subnet task; it is part of wallet and staking management.

References: [Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation)

## Alpha Conversion Context

The [Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking) describes
unstaking as withdrawing staked TAO from a validator hotkey and converting subnet-specific alpha
tokens back to TAO through the subnet's automated market maker. That conversion can involve
[slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage), because pool reserves participate in the
exchange. The same glossary entry notes that Bittensor provides price-protection mechanisms for
unstaking operations and that unstaking incurs blockchain transaction fees.

References: [Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking),
[Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)

## Relationship to Multiple Mechanisms

Unstaking removes staked TAO from a validator position inside a subnet. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms) notes
that each mechanism operates with its own bond pool for Yuma Consensus calculations.

For readers, unstaking still names withdrawal from validator support in one subnet market.

References:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms)

## Relationship to Stake

Unstaking and stake are related but different parts of Bittensor staking vocabulary. Stake names TAO
held in a staking context, while unstaking names the process of removing staked TAO from that
context. The [Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake) describes
TAO associated with validators or subnets through staking, and the
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking) describes
withdrawing staked TAO from a validator hotkey.

For readers, stake names TAO in a staking position, while unstaking names the exit action that
removes TAO from that position.

References: [Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking)

## Relationship to Delegation

Unstaking and delegation address related but different parts of Bittensor staking vocabulary.
Delegation names assigning stake to a validator for shared reward context, while unstaking names
removing staked TAO from that delegated position. The
[Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation) describes
stake assignment to validators, and the
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking) describes
withdrawal from a validator hotkey with alpha-to-TAO conversion through the subnet pool.

For readers, delegation names how stake is assigned to a validator, while unstaking names how that
staked TAO is withdrawn from the validator context.

References: [Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation),
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking)

## Relationship to Netuid

Unstaking and a netuid are related but different parts of Bittensor subnet vocabulary. A netuid
identifies which subnet context is in scope, while unstaking describes removing staked TAO from a
validator position inside that subnet's staking and pool context. The
[Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid) places netuid at the
subnet level, and the
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking) describes
withdrawal that can convert subnet-specific alpha back to TAO through the subnet pool.

For readers, netuid answers which subnet market the withdrawal happens in, while unstaking answers
what action removes staked TAO from validator support in that context.

References: [Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid),
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking)

## Relationship to Slippage

Unstaking and slippage address related but different parts of subnet pool vocabulary. Unstaking
names the withdrawal action that can convert alpha-denominated stake back to TAO, while slippage
names the difference between an expected exchange amount and the amount produced by pool reserves at
execution time. The
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking) describes
withdrawal through the subnet automated market maker, and the
[Glossary: Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#slippage) describes exchange
differences caused by pool state.

For readers, unstaking names the withdrawal action, while slippage names a pool-context effect that
can change how much TAO is received during that conversion.

References: [Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking),
[Glossary: Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#slippage)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the term. It does not report balances, timing, fees, validator choices, or a
specific staking action. Readers should use current official staking documentation for
security-sensitive staking actions.
