# Unstaking

Unstaking describes removing staked TAO from a staking position. Bittensor staking and delegation
material uses the term when explaining how TAO can be withdrawn from validator support and returned
to the coldkey ([Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking),
[Staking and Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

## What It Represents

Unstaking ends the staking relationship and frees TAO for other use. On alpha subnets, withdrawal
passes through subnet pool reserves, converting subnet-specific alpha back toward TAO rather than a
fixed one-for-one exchange
([Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage),
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking)).

The Root Subnet differs here. Root stake is TAO-denominated, so unstaking from root returns TAO
directly without a subnet-alpha-to-TAO pool conversion; the pool-conversion path applies to ordinary
alpha subnets rather than Root Subnet staking
([Glossary: Root Subnet/Subnet Zero](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero),
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking)).

Readers should separate four stake-management operations that sound similar:

- **Staking** — adds new TAO support to a validator hotkey on a subnet.
- **Unstaking** — withdraws delegated stake back toward the coldkey.
- **Moving stake** — re-points an existing delegated position without withdrawing first.
- **Transferring stake** — changes which coldkey owns a staked position while keeping it delegated.

Unstaking is not the same as
[moving stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation#moving-stake) to a
different hotkey or subnet, and it is not reducing a conviction lock while keeping the position
active. Moving stake keeps delegated exposure active under a new target; unstaking exits delegation
and returns value toward free balance.

[Transfer](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#transfer) sends free, unstaked TAO
between coldkey accounts. Unstaking withdraws from a validator delegation position first; transfer
is a separate step on liquid balance afterward.

[Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#slippage) names the gap between
expected and received amounts when reserve-based conversion runs.
[Price protection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/price-protection) is the tolerance
mechanism that can govern how an unstaking conversion proceeds once pool movement is measured.

Unstaking can also involve a separate liquidity fee on the pool conversion. Fee documentation
describes the staking and unstaking liquidity fee as subnet-dependent, with a default or typical
level around 0.05% of transacted liquidity, so that charge should be kept separate from slippage
([Transaction Fees in Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/fees),
[Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)).

[Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate) attaches TAO support to a
validator hotkey. Unstaking removes that support from the delegated relationship on the selected
subnet. Nomination and unstaking move support in opposite directions on the same delegation path.

Unstaking lowers the
[effective stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake) behind the
validator the TAO leaves. It changes how much stake backs a validator over time; it does not by
itself describe how consensus settles weight signals at the epoch boundary
([Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).

Unstaking is also part of the staking-operation rate limit on each netuid. The rate-limit
documentation groups add stake, remove stake, and move stake together, allowing one successful
operation per block on a particular subnet. Repeated unstaking attempts on netuid 1 or another
selected netuid are therefore paced by the chain rather than being an unrestricted sequence of
withdrawals
([Rate Limits: Staking operations rate limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits#staking-operations-rate-limits)).

Unstaking examples tied to netuid 1 illustrate one subnet's withdrawal path. They should not be read
as representing production pool conversion behavior on another network
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).

## Locked Stake Cannot Be Unstaked Immediately

Not all staked alpha is freely withdrawable. The conviction staking documentation explains that
locking stake binds an amount of a coldkey's staked alpha on a subnet so the total staked balance
cannot drop below the locked amount, while everything above that locked amount stays freely
unstakable. Unwinding a locked position is not an on-demand unstake: the holder must put the lock in
decaying mode and wait through an exponential decay period, during which the locked amount falls
over time until it reaches zero and frees the stake
([Conviction and locked stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/conviction-staking),
[Staking and Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

## Distinction from Moving Stake

- **Unstaking** — exit delegated support and return TAO to the coldkey.
- **Moving stake** — keep support delegated under a new hotkey or subnet target.

## Distinction from Slippage

- **Unstaking** — withdrawal action that enters the conversion path.
- **Slippage** — gap between expected and received conversion amounts.

## Distinction from Price Protection

- **Unstaking** — withdrawal step on the selected subnet.
- **Price protection** — tolerance mechanism after pool movement is measured.

## Distinction from Staking

- **Staking** — add support behind a validator.
- **Unstaking** — remove support through the documented withdrawal path.

## Distinction from Transfer

- **Unstaking** — withdraw staked support back to the owner's balance.
- **Transfer** — send TAO from one wallet address to another.

## Distinction from Autostaking

- **Autostaking** — accumulation mechanism that adds to a staked position.
- **Unstaking** — exit mechanism that removes from that position.

## Distinction from Nominate

- **Nominate** — attach TAO support to a validator hotkey.
- **Unstaking** — remove that support from the delegated relationship.

## Distinction from Effective Stake

- **Unstaking** — withdrawal operation that reduces delegated support.
- **Effective stake** — combined staking total behind a delegate hotkey.

## Distinction from Yuma Consensus

- **Unstaking** — withdrawing TAO from a validator delegation position.
- **Yuma Consensus** — epoch-boundary settlement from validator weights to emission shares.

## Distinction from Transferring Stake

- **Unstaking** — withdrawing a position back into free TAO balance.
- **Transferring stake** — reassigning a staked position to another coldkey intact.

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the term. It does not report balances, timing, fees, validator choices, or a
specific staking action
([Staking and Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking)).
