# Unstaking

Unstaking is the process of withdrawing staked liquidity from a validator position. The
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking) entry defines it
as withdrawing staked TAO from a validator hotkey by converting subnet-specific alpha tokens back to
TAO through the subnet's automated market maker.

## Staking Context

Unstaking is the reverse side of staking into a Bittensor subnet. The official
[Staking/delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation#staking-into-subnets)
explains that staking into a subnet converts TAO into subnet-specific alpha held in the validator
position. The
[Unstaking back to TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation#unstaking-back-to-tao)
section describes the reverse movement: alpha leaves the validator position, enters the subnet pool,
and TAO is returned according to the pool's current exchange rate.

This makes unstaking a staking-state change and an AMM conversion, not merely a label for stopping
participation.

## AMM Price Impact

Each subnet has its own automated market maker for the TAO and alpha pair. Because unstaking trades
alpha back into the pool for TAO, the amount received depends on pool reserves and the transaction's
own price impact. The official
[Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage) page explains that staking
and unstaking can receive less than a static-price estimate because the transaction itself changes
the price.

For readers, the important boundary is that unstaking value is not determined by a fixed table or a
universal exchange rate. It depends on the relevant subnet pool at the time of execution.

## Price Protection and Fees

The staking overview lists slippage, price protection, and transaction fees as key unstaking
considerations.
[Understand Price Protection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/price-protection) describes
controls that can reject or partially execute staking and unstaking transactions when price movement
would exceed a tolerance threshold. The overview also notes that unstaking operations incur
blockchain transaction fees, which are recycled back into the TAO emission pool.

These considerations do not change the definition of unstaking. They explain why the final result of
an unstaking action can differ from a simple static-price expectation.

## Reader Boundary

Unstaking should not be read as a guarantee of immediate full-value withdrawal, a recommendation to
exit a validator, or a live estimate of received TAO. Actual outcomes depend on the subnet's pool,
the amount being unstaked, price-protection settings, transaction fees, and any staking-state
constraints described in current official documentation.
