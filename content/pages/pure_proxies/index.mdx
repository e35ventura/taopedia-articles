# Pure Proxies

Pure proxies are Bittensor accounts with no private key. The official
[Understanding Pure Proxies](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies/pure-proxies)
documentation describes them as keyless, non-deterministic accounts created fresh through the
chain's pure-proxy mechanism. Because a pure proxy cannot sign transactions by itself, control flows
through a delegator account that signs and authorizes actions involving the pure proxy.

The important reader boundary is that pure proxies are not just ordinary proxy wallets under a
different name. Regular proxy wallets are mainly a way to let one account act for a more sensitive
account. Pure proxies are mainly an account-indirection tool, especially for multisig designs where
the stable account identity matters.

Reference: [Understanding Pure Proxies](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies/pure-proxies)

## Keyless Account Model

A pure proxy has an address and can hold funds, but it does not have its own private key. The
official guide explains that the proxy account cannot sign transactions independently and can act
only through its delegator. This makes the pure proxy a stand-in account rather than an account that
someone logs into or operates directly.

That distinction affects how readers should think about custody. The controlling account signs the
action, while the pure proxy is treated as the account being acted through. The pure proxy must hold
the funds needed for actions that spend from it.

References: [Understanding Pure Proxies](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies/pure-proxies),
[Proxies overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies)

## Difference From Regular Proxy Wallets

Regular proxy wallets protect a valuable account by allowing a separate delegated account to perform
permitted actions on its behalf. The official
[Proxies overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies) presents that pattern as a security
tool for leaving a safe wallet or real account in a more protected environment.

Pure proxies invert the reader's mental model. The pure proxy is the stable account, and another
account controls it through proxy authority. The official pure-proxy guide explicitly says pure
proxies do not offer the same security advantage as regular proxy wallets for ordinary wallet
delegation. For most wallet indirection, regular proxy wallets are the simpler and intended concept.

References: [Proxies overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies),
[Understanding Pure Proxies](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies/pure-proxies)

## Multisig Member Continuity

The primary use case in the official pure-proxy documentation is multisig wallet construction with
swappable members. A conventional multisig address is derived from its member set. If the members
change, the multisig address changes too, which can require moving funds and updating references to
the old account.

Pure proxies solve that operational problem by making the pure proxy addresses the multisig members.
If a person or team member changes, control of one pure proxy can move to a new controller while the
pure proxy address itself remains the multisig member. The multisig can keep the same address
because its member accounts did not change, only the controller behind one member changed.

Reference: [Understanding Pure Proxies](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies/pure-proxies)

## Control Transfer Boundary

The official guide describes changing control of a pure proxy by adding a new account as an `Any`
proxy for the pure proxy and removing the previous controller. In article terms, this is the
mechanism behind member replacement: the durable pure-proxy address remains in place while the human
or operational controller changes.

That control-transfer pattern should not be read as automatic safety. The controller relationship
still matters, and broad authority such as `Any` should be treated carefully. Pure proxies are most
useful when their role in the account structure is clear and when operators understand which account
can authorize actions for them.

References: [Understanding Pure Proxies](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies/pure-proxies),
[Working with Proxies](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies/working-with-proxies)

## Security Boundaries

A pure proxy being keyless does not mean it is risk-free. If the controller account has the relevant
authority, that controller can authorize actions involving the pure proxy. If the pure proxy holds
funds, mistakes in controller setup, proxy scope, or lifecycle management can still affect those
funds.

For readers, the safest summary is narrow: a pure proxy is a keyless stand-in account controlled
through delegation. It is valuable when a stable account identity is needed, especially around
multisig member continuity. It is not a replacement for normal key security, and it is not the
default proxy pattern for day-to-day wallet protection.
