# Emission Split

Emission split is how a subnet's alpha emission is divided among the participants who share it.
Documentation gives explicit proportions: of the alpha a subnet distributes at the end of each
tempo, for example on netuid 1, 41% goes to miners, 41% goes to validators and their stakers, and
18% goes to the subnet owner ([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

## The Three Shares

Each share lines up with a role. Miners take 41% for producing the subnet's work. Validators and
their stakers take another 41% for evaluating that work. The subnet owner takes 18% for creating and
running the subnet. Together these account for the whole of a subnet's alpha emission, splitting it
across the groups that make the subnet function
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

## Distributed Each Tempo

The split is not applied block by block in isolation; the documentation describes the alpha being
distributed at the end of each tempo, the subnet's recurring reward interval. On a subnet such as
netuid 1, emission accumulates over the tempo and is then divided according to these proportions, so
the split is best read as the outcome of a tempo rather than of a single block
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Glossary: Tempo](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tempo)).

## Validators Share With Stakers

The 41% for validators is not kept whole by the validators. The documentation describes validators
first receiving their share according to Yuma Consensus scoring, after which the remaining alpha is
distributed to stakers in proportion to their holdings. So this portion is really a
validators-and-stakers share, which is why delegating stake to a validator earns a part of it
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).

## Root/Subnet Emission Split, Dividends, and Yuma Reward Pipeline

[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions) documentation separates subnet rewards
into injection and distribution. Injection adds
[alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha) liquidity to each subnet's pools
every [block](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#block), while distribution waits
until the end of each [tempo](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tempo). The
documented 41%, 41%, and 18% shares on a subnet such as netuid 1 therefore describe how alpha is
divided when distribution runs at the tempo boundary, not how much alpha enters the subnet on every
individual block
([Glossary: Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#emission)).

Network-level TAO reserve injection and subnet-level alpha distribution are related but distinct
stages. Emission split vocabulary names the within-subnet role partition at tempo end; cross-subnet
TAO flow vocabulary names upstream injection comparison before that partition applies
([Emission: Distribution Across Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution-across-subnets)).

[Yuma Consensus: Miner emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#miner-emissions)
documentation explains that the miner-side allocation on a subnet such as netuid 1 is a proportional
split based on each miner's share of total aggregate
[rank](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rank). The 41% miner portion is shared
among miners according to consensus results, not assigned as one equal amount to every miner.
Emission split names how alpha is partitioned among roles at tempo end; Yuma Consensus names how the
miner partition is subdivided among miners after validator weights are aggregated
([Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)).

Validator-side outcomes follow a parallel path inside the same tempo split.
[Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions)
documentation ties validator dividends to bond-weighted miner outcomes, so the
validators-and-stakers 41% share is not a flat per-validator payout before consensus runs.

[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution) documentation
places [validator take](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-) inside
the stage that follows consensus on a subnet such as netuid 1. The 41% validators-and-stakers share
may also be split when validators retain a documented take percentage from the delegated portion
before the remainder flows to stakers. Emission split reading should keep those layers in order. The
three headline percentages describe the subnet alpha partition at tempo end, while validator take
vocabulary describes a later split inside the validator-and-staker portion
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

For readers on netuid 1, the pipeline order is: per-block alpha injection into subnet pools, tempo
accumulation, Yuma Consensus aggregation of validator weights into miner incentives and validator
dividends, the 41/41/18 role partition at tempo end, and validator take on the delegated portion
before staker distribution. Emission split vocabulary names the role-level partition; Yuma
vocabulary names how the miner and validator shares are calculated inside that partition.

## Development Stage Context

The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)
describes subnet development as moving from localnet to testnet and then mainnet. For emission split
on a subnet such as netuid 1, that sequence changes how readers should interpret miner, validator,
and subnet-owner share examples.

In localnet, tempo-bound emission splits can be tested in an isolated environment. Localnet
distribution shares on netuid 1 reflect local configuration rather than production subnet rewards.

On testnet, emission split flows can be exercised in a shared non-production network. Testnet tempo
outcomes on a selected netuid are separate from mainnet subnet state
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

On mainnet, emission split describes live production alpha distribution at tempo boundaries on
subnets such as netuid 1. Observed shares depend on the selected subnet's consensus and emission
context ([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

The [Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks) reference
separates mainnet, testnet, and localnet. An emission-split example from one environment should not
be read as representing production reward shares in another environment.

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the split at a high level. The 41, 41, and 18 percent figures are the documented
proportions, but how much total alpha a subnet emits varies over time, and the exact mechanics
within each share are described in the fuller emission and consensus references. Live amounts should
be checked from current Bittensor sources rather than inferred from the proportions alone
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

Emission split names the role-level partition at tempo end. Yuma Consensus and validator take
vocabulary explain later subdivisions inside the miner and validator portions; those layers should
not be collapsed into the headline percentages alone
([Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-)).
