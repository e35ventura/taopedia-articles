# RAO

RAO is a small-denomination unit for TAO amounts in Bittensor. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rao) defines RAO as one billionth of a
TAO, while [Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction) describes
TAO as the token used to incentivize subnet activity.

## Denomination Role

RAO is useful when Bittensor material needs to discuss small TAO-denominated amounts without losing
precision. Instead of changing what the token is, RAO changes the scale used to express the amount.
The [Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rao) gives the base conversion,
and [Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction) places TAO in
the broader incentive context of miners, validators, subnet creators, and stakers.

## Transfer Amount Precision

When Bittensor documentation discusses moving TAO between wallet addresses, amount precision can
matter as much as the transfer action itself. The
[Glossary: Transfer](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#transfer) describes
transferring TAO between coldkeys, while the
[Glossary: RAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rao) gives the fine-grained unit
often used when a TAO-denominated figure would otherwise be rounded away in prose.

Readers should keep transfer vocabulary and unit vocabulary separate. A transfer names the movement
between addresses; RAO names how precisely a TAO-denominated quantity can be written when that
movement or its resulting balance is described.

References: [Glossary: Transfer](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#transfer),
[Glossary: RAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rao)

## Emission Reporting Versus Token Identity

[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions) describes newly created TAO and
subnet-specific alpha tokens being distributed across network roles. RAO helps with the reporting
side of TAO figures in that picture: it does not introduce a second incentive token, but it can make
small TAO-denominated distribution amounts easier to read without changing what is being
distributed.

That reporting role is easy to misread if RAO is treated as its own reward class. The emission frame
still centers on TAO and subnet alpha tokens; RAO only adjusts the numeric scale for TAO-side
amounts when finer wording is useful.

References: [Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Glossary: RAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rao)

## Unit Language Versus Role Labels

[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction) introduces miners,
validators, subnet creators, and stakers as participant roles incentivized by TAO. RAO belongs
beside those role descriptions only when an amount needs finer TAO precision. It does not rename the
role and does not substitute for subnet-specific alpha vocabulary used in staking and subnet
markets.

Keeping that boundary clear prevents RAO from sounding like a participant category. Roles describe
who acts in the network; RAO describes how finely a TAO amount can be stated when those actors move
or receive TAO-denominated values.

References: [Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction),
[Glossary: RAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rao)

## Relationship to TAO

RAO and TAO are not separate token systems. RAO is a denomination of TAO, so the concept belongs
where Bittensor documentation discusses TAO-denominated incentives, emissions, staking context, or
subnet activity. [Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction)
describes TAO as the incentive token for subnet activity, and
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions) describes emission as distributing newly
created TAO and subnet-specific alpha tokens across network roles.

## Emission Context

Emission articles and references sometimes need to distinguish the token being distributed from the
unit used to express an amount. RAO helps with that second part: it is a smaller unit for
TAO-denominated quantities, not a different reward category. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rao) links RAO to emission context,
and [Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions) provides the broader distribution
frame for TAO and subnet-specific alpha tokens.

## Reader Cautions

RAO should be read as a denomination, not as a separate token or subnet role. It also should not be
confused with subnet-specific alpha tokens: those are separate subnet-context tokens, while RAO is a
denomination of TAO. This distinction follows the
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rao) definition and the
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions) distinction between TAO and
subnet-specific alpha tokens.

## Development Stage Context

The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)
describes subnet development as moving from localnet to testnet and then mainnet. For RAO, that
sequence changes how readers should interpret evidence about TAO amounts and on-chain balances.

In localnet, RAO values in examples reflect local chain state. Test balances in localnet do not
represent production TAO holdings.

On testnet, RAO amounts are in a shared, non-production environment with testnet TAO that is
separate from mainnet supply and real token value.

On mainnet, RAO values correspond to actual TAO on the live production network. The
[Glossary: RAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rao) defines RAO as the smallest
indivisible unit of TAO on the production chain.

The [Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks) reference
separates mainnet, testnet, and localnet. A RAO amount from one environment should not be read as
representing a balance or supply figure in another environment.
