# Rug Pull

A rug pull is the general risk that an insider extracts value at everyone else's expense, for
example by abandoning a project or dumping its token. In Bittensor the concern attaches to a subnet
and its owner. Several protocol mechanics shape how much an owner can actually do, which is what
makes the Bittensor version of this risk worth understanding specifically.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## Owners Cannot Mint at Will

A common rug-pull move elsewhere is an insider inflating a token's supply. Bittensor does not leave
that lever in an owner's hands. Subnet emissions are split by a protocol-set rule, not owner
discretion: a fixed share goes to the subnet owner while the rest is allocated to miners and
validators through Yuma Consensus. An owner's share of emission follows that network-wide rule, not
a private control over how much the subnet creates.

References: [Emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## Abandonment Triggers a Payout, Not a Guarantee

The protocol does have a mechanic for abandonment. When a subnet is deregistered, all alpha tokens
in it are swapped back to TAO and distributed to that subnet's alpha holders in proportion to their
stake, rather than leaving holders with a token that has no further protocol-level exit path
([Subnet Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-deregistration)).

That payout is not a capital guarantee. Each holder's share is calculated from the subnet's TAO
reserve at the time of deregistration, proportional to their stake, so it reflects the pool's TAO at
that moment rather than what holders originally put in. If alpha had already lost most of its value
against TAO before deregistration, the payout can be far smaller than the original stake.

References: [Subnet Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-deregistration)

## Risk Is Reduced, Not Erased

These mechanics narrow the classic rug-pull paths, but they do not turn every subnet into a safe
bet. Protocol rules constrain emission and give holders a defined exit mechanic on deregistration,
yet judgment about a subnet's quality, activity, and the people behind it remains the participant's
own.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept and the relevant protocol limits at a high level. It is not financial
advice and makes no claim that any particular subnet is safe or unsafe. The behavior of any owner
and the live state of any subnet change over time and should be assessed directly rather than
assumed from this overview.

References: [Subnet Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-deregistration)
