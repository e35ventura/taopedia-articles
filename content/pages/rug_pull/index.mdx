# Rug Pull

A rug pull is the general risk that an insider extracts value at everyone else's expense, for
example by abandoning a project or dumping its token. In Bittensor the concern attaches to a subnet,
for example netuid 1, and its owner. Several protocol mechanics shape how much an owner can actually
do, which is what makes the Bittensor version of this risk worth understanding specifically.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## Owners Cannot Mint at Will

A common rug-pull move elsewhere is an insider inflating a token's supply. Bittensor does not leave
that lever in an owner's hands. The documentation states that emissions follow consistent protocol
rules across all subnets, so a subnet founder cannot arbitrarily print tokens. An owner's share of
emission is set by the same network-wide logic as everyone else's, not by a private control.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)

## Abandonment Returns Capital

The sharper protection concerns abandonment. The documentation describes that when a subnet is
deregistered, the alpha held in it is swapped back to TAO and distributed to that subnet's alpha
holders. So even if an owner walks away and the subnet is removed, holders are not simply left with
a dead token; the design routes their capital back to TAO rather than stranding it.

References: [Subnet Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-deregistration)

## Risk Is Reduced, Not Erased

These mechanics narrow the classic rug-pull paths, but they do not turn every subnet into a safe
bet. The documentation still encourages participants to evaluate subnet projects and the people
behind them. Protocol rules constrain supply and protect capital on exit, yet judgment about a
subnet's quality and intentions remains the participant's own.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept and the relevant protocol limits at a high level. It is not financial
advice and makes no claim that any particular subnet is safe or unsafe. The behavior of any owner
and the live state of any subnet change over time and should be assessed directly rather than
assumed from this overview.

References: [Subnet Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-deregistration)
