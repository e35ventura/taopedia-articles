# Coldkeys (Bittensor)

A coldkey is the long-term, security-sensitive key in Bittensor. It controls your TAO and stake and
is used to sign financial operations.

References:
[Wallets (learnbittensor.org)](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/getting-started/wallets/),
[Working with Keys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/working-with-keys/),
[BTCLI Reference](https://docs.bittensor.com/btcli)

## Responsibilities

- Hold and secure TAO balances and delegated stake.
- Authorize staking, unstaking, moving stake, and transfers.
- Own one or more hotkeys used for operational roles.

## Security practices

- Keep the coldkey and its seed phrase offline when possible.
- Never place the coldkey on internet-exposed nodes used for mining/validating.
- Verify official binaries and documentation before performing key operations.

## Operational relationship

- Coldkey provides ownership and control; hotkeys derive authority for operational actions.
- If a hotkey is compromised, the coldkey can rotate or revoke without exposing funds (assuming best
  practices).

## Tooling

- `btcli` and the Wallet SDK support creating, importing, and managing coldkeys.
- Use minimal on-chain interactions from environments you fully trust.

## Relationship to Hotkeys

Coldkey and hotkey are related but different parts of Bittensor wallet-key vocabulary. A coldkey is
the security-sensitive key that controls TAO and stake, while a hotkey is the operational key used
for routine on-chain actions such as registration and weight submission. The
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) describes the
coldkey as the key that controls TAO and stake, and the
[Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey) describes the hotkey
as the operational key used by miners, validators, and subnet actors.

For readers, coldkey names fund and stake control, while hotkey names the operational authority
derived under that control.

References: [Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey),
[Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey)

## Relationship to Subtensor

Coldkey and Subtensor are related but different parts of Bittensor chain vocabulary. Subtensor names
Bittensor's layer 1 blockchain and system of record, while coldkey names the wallet key that
authorizes financial operations recorded on that chain layer. The
[Glossary: Subtensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subtensor) describes the
blockchain layer, and the
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) describes the
coldkey as the key that controls TAO and stake.

For readers, Subtensor names the chain layer, while coldkey names the protected signing key used for
TAO and stake operations on that layer.

References: [Glossary: Subtensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subtensor),
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey)

## Relationship to Extrinsics

Coldkey and extrinsics are related but different parts of Bittensor chain action vocabulary.
Extrinsics name signed transactions and calls submitted to Subtensor, while coldkey names the wallet
key that can authorize staking, unstaking, transfer, and other financial extrinsics. The
[Glossary: Extrinsics](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#extrinsics) describes
signed transactions submitted to the chain, and the
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) describes the
coldkey as the key that controls TAO and stake.

For readers, extrinsics name submitted chain actions, while coldkey names the protected key material
used to authorize financial actions among those submissions.

References: [Glossary: Extrinsics](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#extrinsics),
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey)

## Relationship to TAO

Coldkey and TAO are related but different parts of Bittensor token vocabulary. TAO is the network
incentive token, while coldkey names the wallet key that controls TAO balances and delegated stake.
The [Glossary: TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao) describes TAO as the
token used to incentivize subnet activity, and the
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) describes the
coldkey as the key that controls TAO and stake.

For readers, TAO names the network token, while coldkey names the protected key that controls that
token and related stake context.

References: [Glossary: TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao),
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey)
