# Coldkeys (Bittensor)

A coldkey is the long-term, security-sensitive key in Bittensor. It controls your TAO and stake and
is used to sign financial operations.

References:
[Wallets (learnbittensor.org)](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/getting-started/wallets/),
[Working with Keys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/working-with-keys/),
[BTCLI Reference](https://docs.bittensor.com/btcli)

## Responsibilities

- Hold and secure TAO balances and delegated stake.
- Authorize staking, unstaking, moving stake, and transfers.
- Own one or more hotkeys used for operational roles.

## Security practices

- Keep the coldkey and its seed phrase offline when possible.
- Never place the coldkey on internet-exposed nodes used for mining/validating.
- Verify official binaries and documentation before performing key operations.

## Operational relationship

- Coldkey provides ownership and control; hotkeys derive authority for operational actions.
- If a hotkey is compromised, the coldkey can rotate or revoke without exposing funds (assuming best
  practices).

## Tooling

- `btcli` and the Wallet SDK support creating, importing, and managing coldkeys.
- Use minimal on-chain interactions from environments you fully trust.

## Relationship to Hotkey

Coldkeys and hotkeys address related but different parts of the Bittensor wallet model. A coldkey
controls TAO, stake, and ownership authority, while a hotkey is the operational key used for subnet
roles such as registration and weight submission. The
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) describes the
ownership key, and the [Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey)
describes the operational key controlled by that coldkey.

For readers, a coldkey answers who owns funds and stake, while a hotkey answers which operational
key performs day-to-day subnet actions under that ownership.

References: [Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey),
[Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey)

## Relationship to Hotkey-Coldkey Pair

Coldkeys and a hotkey-coldkey pair address related but different parts of Bittensor key vocabulary.
A coldkey names the ownership key that controls funds and stake, while a hotkey-coldkey pair names
the authentication binding between that coldkey and an operational hotkey. The
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) describes ownership
authority, and the
[Glossary: Hotkey-Coldkey Pair](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey-coldkey-pair)
describes the pairing used in staking and governance authentication.

For readers, a coldkey names ownership authority, while a hotkey-coldkey pair names how an
operational hotkey is bound to that authority.

References: [Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey),
[Glossary: Hotkey-Coldkey Pair](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey-coldkey-pair)

## Relationship to Neuron

Coldkeys and a neuron address related but different parts of Bittensor participation vocabulary. A
coldkey controls ownership and stake, while a neuron names a registered participant record inside a
subnet. The [Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey)
describes the ownership key, and the
[Glossary: Neuron](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#neuron) describes a registered
subnet participant whose operational hotkey acts under a coldkey.

For readers, a coldkey names ownership authority, while a neuron names the participant record that
subnet registration creates inside a selected netuid.

References: [Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey),
[Glossary: Neuron](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#neuron)

## Relationship to Netuid

Coldkeys and a netuid address related but different parts of Bittensor subnet vocabulary. A netuid
selects which subnet context is in scope, while a coldkey names the ownership key that controls the
stake and authority behind subnet participation. The
[Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid) places netuid at the
subnet level, and the
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) describes the key
that controls TAO and stake used in subnet participation.

For readers, a netuid answers which subnet market is being discussed, while a coldkey answers which
ownership key controls the stake context behind participation in that subnet.

References: [Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid),
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey)
