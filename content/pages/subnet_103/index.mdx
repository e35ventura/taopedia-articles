Djinn is Bittensor Subnet 103, the trust layer for a marketplace that separates market information
from acting on it. Its on-chain description is "Information × Execution," and the codebase for the
subnet is the `Djinn-Inc/djinn` repository. On Djinn, analysts sell predictions to buyers with an
unusual twist: the predictions themselves stay encrypted, while each analyst's long-run accuracy is
provable to anyone.

## What Djinn Provides

The protocol is built around two promises. First, a prediction's contents stay secret forever — not
even Djinn can read the signal a buyer has paid for, so an analyst never risks their edge being
copied or leaked. Second, an analyst's track record is verifiable forever: cryptographic proof can
confirm how well their past predictions performed without revealing the individual picks. Together
these let buyers judge an analyst on a provable history rather than on marketing, while sellers keep
their methods private.

Making those promises hold requires checking real-world facts and keeping secrets that no single
party controls, and that is the job the Bittensor subnet does. The network independently verifies
that the information behind a prediction was genuinely available at the time, and it can produce
tamper-evident proof that a given website showed specific content at a specific moment — a public
attestation service with uses well beyond the marketplace, from journalism to legal evidence.
Settlement and escrow are handled by accompanying smart contracts, so payments follow the verified
outcomes.

## Miner and Validator Roles

Miners do the verification legwork. They confirm that real-world information — such as a live
betting line — was actually available when claimed, and they generate the cryptographic attestations
that prove what a website served at a point in time.

Validators coordinate the trust guarantees and the scoring. They jointly hold the pieces of the keys
that keep analysts' signals encrypted, so that no single validator can decrypt a signal on its own,
and they reach consensus on the real outcomes used to settle predictions and confirm track records.
Validators judge miners on the quality of their verification work and set weights on the network
accordingly, which is how reward flows through [[Yuma Consensus]]. At the article level the split is
straightforward: miners verify facts and produce proofs, while validators safeguard the encrypted
signals, agree on outcomes, and weight the network.

## On-Chain Identity

Live SN103 subnet data is available on [TaoStats](https://taostats.io/subnets/103), which identifies
the subnet as Djinn. Readers can reproduce the subnet identity fields directly with the Bittensor
CLI command documented in the
[btcli reference](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/btcli/#btcli-subnets-get-identity):
`btcli subnets get-identity --netuid 103 --network finney`. That Finney identity is the source of
truth for the subnet name and the GitHub repository associated with the subnet.
