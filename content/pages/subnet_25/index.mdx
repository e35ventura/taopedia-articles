Mainframe is Bittensor Subnet 25, described by its operator as the first decentralized science
subnet on Bittensor. It was originally launched as Protein Folding and is run by Macrocosmos, the
team behind several other Bittensor subnets. The subnet turns scientific compute into a rewarded
service: miners are paid emissions for producing valid solutions to molecular simulation jobs rather
than for serving a model or a dataset.

## What Mainframe Computes

The subnet's core workloads come from drug-discovery pipelines. Two are central: protein molecular
dynamics simulation, run with the OpenMM engine, and protein-ligand docking, run with DiffDock. Both
are standard steps in evaluating how a candidate molecule behaves, and both are expensive to run at
scale, which is what makes them a good fit for a paid, distributed compute market.

Mainframe is expanding from pure protein folding toward general life-sciences compute. The
repository describes additional workloads such as Density Functional Theory calculations and a
research partnership with Rowan Scientific, positioning the subnet as a broader scientific-computing
resource rather than a single-task network.

## Miner and Validator Roles

Miners pull challenges from a shared Global Job Pool and compete to find the lowest free-energy
solution for each configuration, posting their results to shared storage. Rewards for a molecular
dynamics challenge are concentrated: the top-ranked miner in a batch receives about 80% of the
reward for that challenge, and the remaining 20% is split across the next several ranked miners (the
top-K, currently K = 5).

Validators evaluate submissions by recomputing the energy of a configuration, which lets them
independently check a miner's claimed solution before assigning a score. Those validator scores
become weights that feed into [[Yuma Consensus]], which converts them into the incentive split
across miners and validators. Because correctness can be re-derived from the configuration, the
mechanism rewards genuinely better solutions rather than unverifiable claims.

## On-Chain Identity

Live SN25 data, including metagraph state and alpha token pool information, is available on
[TaoStats](https://taostats.io/subnets/25), which lists the subnet under the name Mainframe. Readers
can reproduce the subnet's registered identity fields with the Bittensor CLI command documented in
the [btcli reference](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/btcli/#btcli-subnets-get-identity):
`btcli subnets get-identity --netuid 25 --network finney`.

The subnet's open-source codebase (macrocosm-os/mainframe) is the source of truth for the workloads
and reward mechanism described above. As a Macrocosmos subnet, Mainframe sits alongside the team's
other networks such as [[Subnet 1: Apex]] and [[Subnet 13: Data Universe]].
