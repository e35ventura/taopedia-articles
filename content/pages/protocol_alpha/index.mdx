# Protocol Alpha

Protocol alpha is subnet alpha accumulated by the Bittensor protocol during reserve injection. It is
cached per subnet instead of being immediately recycled, and it becomes important during pro-rata
settlement when a subnet dissolves
([Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)).

The term belongs to subnet tokenomics. It names protocol-held alpha rather than staker-held alpha,
ordinary staking position, or the alpha reserve side of the liquidity pool
([Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

## Reserve Injection Origin

Protocol alpha arises from reserve injection. Coinbase runs per block and advances emission
processing, including TAO and alpha movement around subnet liquidity pools. During that process,
chain-side TAO buys can leave alpha attributed to the protocol
([Coinbase Implementation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/navigating-subtensor/emissions-coinbase),
[Emission: TAO reserve injection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#tao-reserve-injection)).

This makes protocol alpha an accounting result of the protocol process. It is related to emission
and reserve injection, but it is not the same as miner incentives, validator dividends, or ordinary
staker alpha ([Emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)).

The important point is attribution. Protocol alpha is alpha attributed to the protocol during
reserve-injection activity, not alpha distributed through the ordinary emission path.

That attribution makes protocol alpha useful accounting vocabulary. It gives a name to alpha that
arises near reserve injection but stays associated with the protocol for the relevant subnet
([Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)).

The coinbase context also keeps the timing clear. Protocol alpha is tied to emission processing and
reserve injection, so it belongs with tokenomics accounting
([Coinbase Implementation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/navigating-subtensor/emissions-coinbase)).

## Protocol-Held Alpha

Staker alpha and protocol alpha sit on different sides of subnet accounting. Stake refers to tokens
delegated to a validator in a subnet, while protocol alpha is alpha held by the protocol itself
([Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)).

Protocol alpha is cached for the subnet and handled by protocol rules. Staker alpha belongs to
staking context and is read through stake vocabulary
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

Protocol-held alpha also differs from alpha outstanding. Alpha outstanding names alpha outside the
reserve pool in subnet accounting, while protocol alpha names a protocol-attributed amount within
that broader outside-pool vocabulary
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)).

The glossary describes protocol alpha as cached per subnet. That makes the subnet label part of the
term: protocol alpha is not a single cross-subnet bucket, and it should not be folded into every
other alpha amount without preserving its protocol-held context
([Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)).

This per-subnet cache is the main reader-facing detail. A protocol-alpha statement needs the subnet
context because the amount is tracked for a particular subnet rather than for all subnet alpha at
once.

The cache also explains why the term appears with subnet lifecycle language. It records
protocol-attributed alpha while the subnet exists and gives settlement a protocol-side amount to
account for
([Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)).

## Pool Vocabulary

Protocol alpha is different from the alpha reserve. The alpha reserve is the subnet-token side of a
subnet liquidity pool, paired with the TAO reserve. Protocol alpha is alpha attributed to the
protocol during reserve-injection activity
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Emission: Alpha reserve injection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#alpha-reserve-injection)).

The two terms can appear near the same pool activity, but they answer different questions. Alpha
reserve names where subnet alpha sits in the pool pair; protocol alpha names the protocol-held alpha
tracked for the subnet
([Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)).

Alpha reserve is pool-side vocabulary. Protocol alpha is protocol-side accounting. Keeping those
terms separate prevents reserve-injection prose from treating every alpha amount near the pool as
alpha reserve.

That distinction is practical in pool explanations. A subnet liquidity pool uses TAO and alpha
reserves, while protocol alpha is alpha attributed to the protocol during the surrounding emission
and reserve-injection process
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

## Dissolution and Recycling

Protocol alpha matters when a subnet dissolves. In that settlement context, protocol alpha
participates in pro-rata TAO settlement, reducing staker payouts proportionally while the protocol's
corresponding TAO share returns to the chain
([Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha),
[Subnet Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-deregistration)).

This makes dissolution the main lifecycle boundary for the term. During ordinary subnet operation,
protocol alpha is best read as cached protocol-side accounting; at dissolution, it becomes part of
the settlement process
([Subnet Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-deregistration)).

The pro-rata detail gives the term its lifecycle meaning. Protocol alpha can sit quietly as cached
accounting during subnet operation, then matter directly when dissolution settlement calculates how
the subnet's value is divided.

Protocol alpha is also distinct from recycling. Recycling is supply-accounting language for tokens
removed from circulation, while protocol alpha is now cached per subnet rather than immediately
recycled
([Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning),
[Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)).

Both terms concern protocol accounting. Recycling names a supply outcome; protocol alpha names a
protocol-held alpha amount retained for subnet settlement context
([Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)).

## Reading the Term

Protocol alpha is reference vocabulary for subnet alpha held by the protocol. It identifies the
protocol-attributed amount created around reserve injection and relevant to subnet settlement
([Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)).

A protocol-alpha statement needs the subnet and lifecycle context attached. During reserve
injection, the term explains protocol-side alpha attribution. During dissolution, it explains how
that cached amount participates in settlement.

The narrow reading is protocol-held alpha, cached by subnet, and relevant to settlement. Alpha
reserve, alpha outstanding, stake, and Dynamic TAO remain separate tokenomics terms for other alpha
contexts.

## Attribution During Reserve Injection

Official [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)
documentation describes how protocol alpha is specifically attributed to the protocol during the
[Glossary: Reserve Injection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#reserve-injection)
process. When the protocol injects TAO into a subnet's liquidity pool to support its alpha supply, a
distinct portion of the newly generated alpha is designated as protocol-held, rather than entering
the subnet's immediately tradable or circulating supply.

This mechanism ensures that a certain amount of alpha remains under the protocol's direct
accounting, separate from the active
[Glossary: Alpha Reserve](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-reserve) used by
the subnet's liquidity pool. This attribution is a deliberate part of the
[Glossary: Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#emission) process, where
alpha tokens are generated. It establishes the protocol's claim on a share of the subnet's value
from its inception.

The attribution means that not all alpha generated for a subnet becomes part of its tradable
reserves. Instead, a specific portion is reserved by the protocol, influencing the total amount of
alpha available for market dynamics versus what is held for system-level accounting.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Glossary: Reserve Injection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#reserve-injection)

## Protocol Retention for Subnet Solvency

Official
[Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)
defines it as the alpha attributed to the protocol and retained for subnet settlement. This
retention serves to manage the long-term solvency and orderly winding down of subnets,
differentiating it from tokens that are immediately recycled or those that flow into active
circulation.

The protocol holds this designated alpha amount to ensure that, upon a subnet's
[Glossary: Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#deregistration) or
dissolution, there are sufficient resources for the established pro-rata settlement mechanism. This
strategic retention prevents these tokens from being spent or circulated, preserving them for their
specific purpose of balancing outstanding claims and returning value to the main chain when a
subnet's lifecycle concludes.

By retaining protocol alpha, the network maintains a systemic safeguard for subnet lifecycles,
ensuring that the economic implications of dissolution are managed within predefined parameters.
This makes protocol alpha a crucial component for the overall economic stability of the Bittensor
network, providing a buffer that supports the integrity of subnet operations and their eventual
settlement.

References:
[Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha),
[Subnet Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-deregistration)

## Impact on Subnet Available Supply

While generated in relation to a specific
[Glossary: Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet), protocol alpha is
explicitly _not_ part of that subnet's immediately available circulating supply or its operational
liquidity pool. Official documentation describes it as alpha attributed to the protocol during the
surrounding emission and reserve-injection process, distinguishing it from the alpha available for
market interactions.

This distinction is vital for understanding a subnet's true liquid market capacity. The presence of
protocol alpha indicates an amount that, while linked to the subnet's value, is not intended for
active trading, staking, or other on-chain operations within that subnet's economy. Instead, it is a
held amount, influencing the overall balance of alpha tokens without directly participating in the
subnet's daily economic activity.

Consequently, when assessing the total alpha supply pertinent to a subnet's active economy, protocol
alpha is accounted for separately. It reflects a strategic hold by the protocol, balancing the need
to support subnet value with the requirement to manage system-level settlement responsibilities
without inflating the perceived liquid supply within a subnet.
