# Coldkey Swap

A coldkey swap is the operation that rotates the coldkey securing a Bittensor wallet, moving the
wallet's identity and assets — its TAO balance, its stake across subnets, control of its hotkeys,
and any subnet ownership — to a new cryptographic key pair. The official docs describe it as the
secure way to switch to a new coldkey when the secrecy of the existing one can no longer be trusted,
and note that swapping is important when a coldkey may have been leaked or compromised.

Reference: [Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)

## When a swap is needed

The docs draw a clear line between swapping and simply moving funds. A coldkey swap is described as
necessary when the coldkey carries subnet ownership or registrations that must move with it; a
holder who only keeps or stakes TAO does not need to swap and can instead transfer balances to a new
coldkey. The practical takeaway is that swapping is the heavier operation reserved for migrating an
established identity, not routine fund movement.

Reference: [Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)

## Why it matters for security

Because a coldkey secures a wallet's identity and assets, anyone who obtains its secret key can take
everything the wallet controls. The docs therefore treat any possibility of compromise as serious
and present the swap as the way to move to a fresh key. This places coldkey swap alongside other
key-hygiene topics: the
[Coldkey and Hotkey Workstation Security](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-hotkey-security)
guidance explains how keys can be exposed in the first place, while the swap is the response once
exposure is suspected.

References: [Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap),
[Coldkey and Hotkey Workstation Security](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-hotkey-security)

## A staged, disputable process

The docs describe the swap as unfolding in careful stages rather than a single action. The owner
first announces the swap, which begins a mandatory waiting period and locks the wallet against
operations such as transfers or staking; only a hash of the destination is published, so the new
address stays private. A pending period must then elapse, during which the swap can be disputed but
not yet finalized. If no dispute is raised, the owner finalizes the swap by supplying the
destination coldkey, which is checked against the announced hash before the migration completes.
Raising a dispute instead blocks the swap, and the dispute is resolved through Bittensor's
governance.

Reference: [Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)

## What moves and what is preserved

On a successful swap, the docs state that the source wallet's assets migrate to the destination: its
TAO, its stake in subnets, control of its hotkeys, and any subnet ownership. If the destination
coldkey already has its own identity, that identity is preserved and the source wallet's assets are
merged into it rather than overwriting it. The operation also carries a cost that must be available
in the source coldkey when the swap is announced.

Reference: [Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)

## Careful interpretation

A coldkey swap is a recovery and migration tool, not a guarantee. It moves an identity to a new key,
but it does not undo a theft that has already drained a wallet, and its waiting and dispute stages
mean it is deliberately not instant. The article-level point is that the swap protects the future
control of a wallet by changing the key that secures it; it does not replace careful key handling,
and the staged process — announcement, waiting period, and finalization or dispute — is what gives
it its safety margin.

References: [Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap),
[Coldkey and Hotkey Workstation Security](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-hotkey-security)
