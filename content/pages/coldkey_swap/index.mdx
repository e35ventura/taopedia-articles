# Coldkey Swap

A coldkey swap migrates a Bittensor wallet's entire on-chain identity to a new coldkey key pair. The
[Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap) documentation
describes it as an operation that moves on-chain identity, including TAO balances and subnet
ownership, to a new cryptographic key pair, and recommends it when a coldkey may have been leaked or
compromised.

## What It Represents

A coldkey secures a wallet's identity and assets, so a leaked coldkey threatens everything that
wallet controls. Coldkey swap is the recovery path that re-homes the full on-chain identity — TAO
balances, subnet ownership, and associated hotkeys — under a fresh coldkey instead of abandoning
registrations ([Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap),
[Coldkey and Hotkey Workstation Security](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-hotkey-security)).

The operation is deliberately staged rather than instant. The owner announces the swap on chain with
the destination address published as a hash, which locks the wallet against transfers and staking. A
pending window — currently 36,000 blocks, roughly five days — must elapse before finalization.
During that window the swap can be disputed but not completed. If the pending period expires without
dispute, the owner must finalize by supplying the destination coldkey, which is checked against the
on-chain hash from announcement
([Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)).

The lock is part of the design. It prevents a compromised key from continuing to move balances or
stake during recovery and gives observers time to notice an unexpected swap announcement before the
migration completes.

If a swap was not authorized, the legitimate coldkey owner can dispute the pending migration using
the coldkey private key while the announcement is active. A disputed coldkey is then blocked from
performing any operation until the
[Triumvirate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#triumvirate) resolves the matter
rather than reverting automatically to normal use
([Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)).

The documentation states a 0.1 TAO announcement fee must be available in the source coldkey when the
swap is announced. The destination coldkey must also be unused on chain, with no existing hotkey
associations such as stake, registrations, or child hotkeys, or the swap fails even after the
pending period ([Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)).

Holders who only keep and stake TAO without subnet ownerships or registrations may instead move
assets through ordinary [transfers](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#transfer)
rather than using the swap path
([Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)).

Incident response should separate leak from loss. A deleted coldkey file may call for
[regenerating a key](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#regenerating-a-key) from its
mnemonic when local files are missing. Regeneration reproduces the same on-chain coldkey identity
from backup words. Exposed recovery material may require coldkey swap rather than continued reliance
on the compromised words. A suspected coldkey leak calls for coldkey swap to migrate identity to a
new coldkey; that is a heavier recovery path than
[hotkey swap](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-hotkey-security), which rotates only the
operational key under an unchanged coldkey owner
([Handle your Seed Phrase Securely](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/handle-seed-phrase),
[Coldkey and Hotkey Workstation Security](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-hotkey-security)).

Readers should separate four recovery and relocation ideas that sound similar:

- **Coldkey swap** — staged migration of the full wallet identity to a new coldkey pair.
- **Hotkey swap** — operational-key rotation under an unchanged coldkey owner.
- **Regenerating a key** — offline recovery that rebuilds the same key material from a mnemonic.
- **Moving stake** — atomic reassignment of delegated stake between hotkeys or subnets without
  changing coldkey ownership
  ([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

[Transferring stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation#transferring-stake)
changes which coldkey owns one staked position. Coldkey swap migrates the entire wallet identity in
one staged operation.
[Toggle transfer](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters) is a per-subnet
permission that gates coldkey-to-coldkey reassignment on that subnet, not the full identity
migration path.

Auto-staking configuration follows the broader identity migration. Autostaking documentation states
that when a coldkey is swapped, the configured auto-stake destination automatically transfers to the
new coldkey, so the destination does not need to be recreated after the swap
([Auto Staking for Miners](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners/autostaking)).

A [proxy wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies) grants scoped authority to act for a
coldkey without changing which coldkey controls the identity. Coldkey swap permanently re-homes
ownership.

Coldkey swap examples from one network environment should not be read as representing production
identity migration on another
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).

## Conviction Locks Affect Whether a Swap Can Run

A coldkey swap also interacts with conviction locks, in two directions worth planning around. The
documentation states that the swap fails if the destination coldkey already holds active locked mass
on any subnet, succeeding only when that destination's locks are expired or zero-mass, so the
receiving key has to be clear of live locks first. On the source side, the locked mass and its
conviction are preserved through the swap and follow the coldkey identity to the new key pair, so a
migration does not forfeit an existing lock
([Conviction Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/conviction-staking),
[Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)).

## Distinction from Moving Stake

- **Moving stake** — re-points delegated stake while the controlling coldkey stays the same.
- **Coldkey swap** — migrates the full wallet identity to a new coldkey pair.

## Distinction from SS58 Encoded

- **SS58 encoded** — address format used to identify wallets on the network.
- **Coldkey swap** — on-chain identity migration to a new coldkey address.

## Distinction from Wallets, Coldkeys, and Hotkeys

- **Wallets, coldkeys, and hotkeys** — two-layer key model for custody and subnet participation.
- **Coldkey swap** — operation that re-homes that identity under a new coldkey.

## Distinction from Hotkey Swap

- **Coldkey swap** — full on-chain identity migration to a new coldkey pair.
- **Hotkey swap** — operational-key rotation under an unchanged coldkey owner.

## Distinction from Proxy Wallets

- **Coldkey swap** — permanent change of the controlling coldkey identity.
- **Proxy wallet** — revocable, scoped permission without changing ownership.

## Distinction from Transfer

- **Transfer** — movement of TAO or stake value between accounts.
- **Coldkey swap** — staged migration of the wallet's on-chain identity to a new coldkey.

## Distinction from Coldkey

- **Coldkey** — ownership key in the wallet model.
- **Coldkey swap** — staged operation that moves that ownership role to a new coldkey address.

## Distinction from Hotkey-Coldkey Pair

- **Hotkey-coldkey pair** — standing ownership structure between operational and custody keys.
- **Coldkey swap** — migration event that re-homes that ownership to a new coldkey.

## Distinction from Coinbase

- **Coldkey swap** — identity-ownership migration at the wallet layer.
- **Coinbase** — per-block emission accumulation and epoch distribution mechanism.

## Distinction from Transferring Stake

- **Coldkey swap** — full wallet identity migration across balances, ownerships, and registrations.
- **Transferring stake** — targeted reassignment of one staked position's owning coldkey.

## Distinction from Seed Phrase

- **Coldkey swap** — on-chain migration action after compromised recovery material.
- **Seed phrase** — off-chain recovery secret whose exposure can trigger that migration.

## Distinction from Toggle Transfer

- **Toggle transfer** — per-subnet permission for coldkey-to-coldkey stake reassignment.
- **Coldkey swap** — full identity migration of all hotkeys and registrations to a new coldkey.

## Distinction from Regenerating a Key

- **Coldkey swap** — on-chain identity migration to a new coldkey while files may still exist.
- **Regenerating a key** — offline recovery that reproduces the same key material from a mnemonic.

## Reader Boundary

Coldkey swap should not be read as a step-by-step command guide, a live parameter quote, or a
recommendation to swap any specific wallet. The lock-out length, announcement fee, and other values
can change on chain, so current figures belong in references and live chain state. This article
describes the concept of migrating a wallet's on-chain identity to a new coldkey
([Rotate or Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)).
