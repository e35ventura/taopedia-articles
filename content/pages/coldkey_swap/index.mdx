# Coldkey Swap

A coldkey swap is a scheduled operation that moves control from a source coldkey to a destination
coldkey. The official documentation presents it as a recovery path: if an owner believes a coldkey
is potentially compromised, they can schedule a swap so a new coldkey takes over what the old one
controlled.

References: [Rotate/Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/schedule-coldkey-swap)

## Why Schedule a Swap

The coldkey is the wallet's owning key, so losing control of it puts the associated funds and roles
at risk. The documentation frames the scheduled swap as the response to a possibly compromised
coldkey: rather than abandoning the holdings, the owner schedules a move to a destination coldkey
they still control. It is a deliberate, owner-initiated action, not an automatic process.

References: [Rotate/Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/schedule-coldkey-swap),
[Wallets, Coldkeys and Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets)

## How It Executes

The swap does not take effect immediately. The documentation states that a scheduled coldkey swap
executes on mainnet exactly five days after it is successfully scheduled. The transaction has a cost
of 0.1 TAO, which must be available in the source coldkey at scheduling time. The delay is a
built-in safeguard around such a sensitive change.

Reference: [Rotate/Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/schedule-coldkey-swap)

## What Transfers

When the swap executes, the destination coldkey takes over the roles tied to the source coldkey. The
documentation notes that subnet ownership held by the source coldkey moves to the destination
coldkey, and that delegated stake is transferred from the source coldkey to the destination coldkey.
The intent is that the new coldkey ends up in the position the old one held.

Reference: [Rotate/Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/schedule-coldkey-swap)

## Arbitration

The swap process includes an arbitration concept tied to an arbitration block, the block at which
the arbitration of a swap takes place. This exists so the chain can resolve the situation where swap
activity is scheduled against the same coldkey, rather than silently applying conflicting changes.
Tooling can read the current arbitration data when a swap is pending.

Reference: [Rotate/Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/schedule-coldkey-swap)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept at a high level. It does not report whether any specific coldkey has a
swap scheduled, the current arbitration state, balances, or ownership of particular wallets. Those
are live chain state and should be checked with the Bittensor CLI for the relevant wallet. The
values described here, such as the five-day delay and the 0.1 TAO cost, are protocol details from
the documentation.

Reference: [Rotate/Swap your Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/schedule-coldkey-swap)
