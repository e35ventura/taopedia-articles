GroundLayer (Subnet 20) is a capital markets protocol built on Bittensor that enables structured
over-the-counter (OTC) deals for subnet alpha tokens. Rather than forcing subnets to sell tokens on
spot markets—which creates immediate price pressure—GroundLayer provides a three-party framework
where subnets raise capital through structured deals, fund managers source and deploy that capital
to earn performance rewards, and investors gain discounted, scheduled access to subnet tokens. All
deal terms are enforced entirely on-chain, eliminating the counterparty risk typical of informal OTC
arrangements. The subnet is operated by [RogueTensor](https://github.com/RogueTensor) and its
website is at [groundlayer.xyz](https://groundlayer.xyz).

## How the Mechanism Works

GroundLayer coordinates three roles: subnet sellers, fund managers, and investors.

**Subnet sellers** (the subnets themselves) define the parameters of a deal upfront: the quantity of
alpha tokens offered, the discount relative to a reference price, a lock schedule governing when
investors can claim tokens, and an expiry date. By setting these terms before any capital is
committed, subnets can raise funds without any spot selling pressure on their token price—the supply
in a deal is committed but not immediately liquidated.

**Fund managers** compete to source investor capital and deploy it into active deals. Their access
to deals scales with their locked assets under management (AUM) and their historical performance.
This creates a merit-based allocation system rather than a first-come, first-served queue. Fund
managers earn rewards proportional to the AUM they have locked into active deals, giving them a
direct incentive to select high-quality deals and maintain investor confidence.

**Investors** commit capital to fund managers rather than directly to deals. They receive
structured, discounted exposure to subnet tokens according to the lock schedules set by the subnet
seller. Because all terms are recorded and auditable on-chain, investors can verify deal parameters
independently before committing capital.

The subnet uses [[Yuma Consensus]] to coordinate the network's participants, with emissions
distributed according to the weights set by validators evaluating miner performance. Under
[[Dynamic TAO]], each subnet's alpha token has its own liquidity pool, and GroundLayer's OTC
mechanism is specifically designed to let subnets raise capital through those tokens without
disrupting the pool price through spot sales.

## Participating as a Miner

Miners on GroundLayer participate in the deal execution layer of the protocol. To register, a miner
must acquire a UID by staking registration cost in TAO against an available slot on the subnet. Once
registered, miners handle the computational and coordination work required to process deal terms,
track lock schedules, and surface deal availability to the fund manager layer.

Because the GitHub repository is listed as pre-release
([RogueTensor/comingsoon](https://github.com/RogueTensor/comingsoon)), the specific task protocol
for miners has not yet been published. Prospective miners should monitor
[groundlayer.xyz](https://groundlayer.xyz) for early-access registration and documentation as the
subnet moves toward full launch.

## Participating as a Validator

Validators on GroundLayer hold a validator permit, which is granted to nodes within the top 64 by
stake-weighted emissions. Validators evaluate miner outputs against the deal parameters defined by
subnet sellers and assign weights accordingly. A miner that correctly tracks deal state, enforces
lock schedules, and accurately reports deal availability receives higher weights; inaccurate or
stale outputs reduce a miner's weight and its share of emissions.

Validators should follow the subnet's forthcoming public documentation for the specific scoring
rubric once the code repository is published.
