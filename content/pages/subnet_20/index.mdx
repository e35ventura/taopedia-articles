# Subnet 20: GroundLayer

GroundLayer is Bittensor Subnet 20 (SN20), developed jointly by [Taoshi](https://x.com/taoshiio) and
[Rizzo AI](https://x.com/teamrizzoai). The subnet builds an institutional-grade, on-chain OTC
marketplace for Bittensor subnet tokens — a structured capital layer designed to let subnet owners
raise funds without selling into spot markets, and let investors gain long-term token exposure on
enforceable, on-chain terms. Fund managers serve as the network's miners, earning Bittensor
emissions in proportion to the capital they lock into verified deals.

References: [groundlayer.xyz](https://groundlayer.xyz),
[GroundLayer on X](https://x.com/groundlayerhq)

## How the Mechanism Works

GroundLayer replaces the conventional miner role — running hardware or serving predictions — with
capital formation. On SN20, miners are fund managers: participants who source, negotiate, and
execute structured OTC deals between investors and subnet token sellers, committing capital into
on-chain contracts for defined lockup periods.

A deal involves three parties. Subnet owners (sellers) post terms specifying a token quantity, a
discount from the prevailing market price, a lockup duration, and any vesting clauses. Investors
supply TAO in exchange for future subnet token allocations at the agreed discount. Fund managers
orchestrate these arrangements, signing and locking the investor capital into verifiable on-chain
contracts and managing the positions through to settlement.

Validators score each miner by reading the total value of capital locked under their management —
assets under management (AUM) — directly from on-chain contract state. Because deal data is public
and verifiable, validators do not rely on self-reported metrics. Each epoch, validators submit
locked-AUM scores as weights, and [[Yuma Consensus]] converts those weights into emissions
distributed from the SN20 alpha pool. The design rewards larger, longer-duration commitments over
short-term activity, since locked positions accumulate score for the full duration of the deal.

The entire deal lifecycle — proposal, acceptance, capital lock, vesting, and settlement — is
enforced on-chain. Neither fund managers nor investors need to trust a counterparty; the contract
terms are immutable once signed. This structure also allows the subnet to scale: as more capital
flows through GroundLayer deals, emissions are redistributed toward the fund managers responsible
for the most productive positions.

## Participating as a Miner

Miners on GroundLayer are fund managers who source structured deals, negotiate terms with subnet
token sellers, and commit investor capital into the resulting on-chain contracts. Performance is
measured entirely by the scale and duration of locked positions, so success depends on identifying
willing sellers and investors and closing deals with terms that maximize AUM over time.

To begin, a participant registers a hotkey on subnet 20 and connects to the GroundLayer marketplace.
Because SN20's 256 UID slots are fully occupied, new registrations require outcompeting existing
participants via the standard Bittensor deregistration process — miners with lower locked-AUM scores
are displaced when a new hotkey registers with enough stake to claim their slot. Early access
registration is open at [groundlayer.xyz](https://groundlayer.xyz).

Source: [groundlayer.xyz/subnet](https://www.groundlayer.xyz/subnet)

## Participating as a Validator

Validators on GroundLayer audit the state of active on-chain deals each epoch and translate locked
AUM into miner weights. Because all deal contracts are on-chain and publicly readable, validators
derive their scores from verified contract state rather than from any off-chain reporting — a fund
manager either has capital locked or does not. These weights are submitted through the Bittensor
axon interface, and [[Yuma Consensus]] handles the conversion to per-epoch emissions.

Running a validator on SN20 requires a registered hotkey with sufficient stake to participate in the
active validator set. As deal volume and network complexity grow, validators must track an expanding
set of on-chain contracts; the incentive to score accurately is strong because inaccurate weight
submissions reduce a validator's own earnings over time.
