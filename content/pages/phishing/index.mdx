# Phishing

Phishing is a scam in which an attacker poses as something trustworthy to trick a person into
revealing a secret. In Bittensor the usual prize is a wallet's recovery material, most often the
seed phrase, which the documentation treats as sensitive wallet access material that should be kept
private.

References:
[Handle your Seed Phrase Securely](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/handle-seed-phrase),
[Wallets, Coldkeys and Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets)

## What It Targets

The center of a Bittensor phishing attempt is the recovery phrase that backs a wallet. The
documentation describes a seed phrase as material that can restore a wallet's keys, so an attacker
who obtains it can rebuild the wallet elsewhere and move its funds. A phishing attempt is, at heart,
an effort to get that phrase out of the owner's sole control by making a fake request look genuine.

Reference:
[Handle your Seed Phrase Securely](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/handle-seed-phrase)

## Why the Seed Phrase Is the Prize

Phishing works for the same reason recovery phrases are powerful: the phrase is key-equivalent.
Because it can regenerate the keys it belongs to, disclosing it is close to handing over the wallet
itself. That is what makes a convincing fake prompt for a recovery phrase so dangerous, and it is
why the documentation frames disclosure of the phrase as a wallet-safety risk rather than a minor
mistake.

Reference:
[Handle your Seed Phrase Securely](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/handle-seed-phrase)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept at a high level. It does not catalog specific scams, sites, or
messages, and the forms phishing takes change constantly. The durable point is the target: a
wallet's recovery phrase is what a Bittensor phishing attempt is ultimately after, which is why that
phrase belongs nowhere but the owner's own secure keeping.

## Coldkey Recovery Material Carries the Broadest Documented Stakes

Official [Wallets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets) documentation describes
[coldkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) as the keys that control TAO,
stake, and subnet ownership, while
[hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey) support day-to-day subnet
operations such as mining and validation. Phishing vocabulary therefore aligns most closely with
recovery material for the coldkey role, because that phrase can regenerate the account tied to
custody and stake movement.

Hotkey recovery phrases remain sensitive wallet material, but they regenerate operational keys
rather than the coldkey that holds the wallet's principal custody functions. The distinction is
role-based: phishing names the social attempt to obtain recovery words, and coldkey vocabulary names
the wallet role whose recovery material the documentation ties to the highest-consequence signing
work ([Handle your Seed Phrase Securely](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/handle-seed-phrase)).

References: [Wallets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets),
[Handle your Seed Phrase Securely](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/handle-seed-phrase)

## Mnemonic Vocabulary Describes the Target Phishing Seeks

The [Glossary: Mnemonic](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#mnemonic) describes the
human-readable recovery words that can regenerate wallet key material.
[Handle your Seed Phrase Securely](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/handle-seed-phrase) frames
those words as sensitive wallet access material whose loss or disclosure are the two main failure
modes. Phishing names the deception that tries to obtain such material through a false request
rather than through legitimate wallet recovery.

The terms sit on opposite sides of the same vocabulary boundary. Mnemonic and seed phrase describe
what the recovery words are; phishing describes how an attacker tries to acquire them. Official
wallet documentation discusses recovery phrases as owner-held secrets without cataloguing specific
scam forms, which keeps phishing here as a concept definition rather than an incident playbook
([Wallets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets)).

References: [Glossary: Mnemonic](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#mnemonic),
[Handle your Seed Phrase Securely](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/handle-seed-phrase)

## Address Poisoning and Phishing Name Different Wallet Risks

[Address poisoning scams](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/address-poisoning-scams)
documentation defines a lookalike-address risk that can mislead destination selection before
signing. Phishing defines a deception aimed at recovery secrets rather than at misrouting a single
transfer. Address poisoning vocabulary stays on destination choice; phishing vocabulary stays on
disclosure of recovery material.

Both topics appear under wallet safety, but the official docs treat them separately. Address
poisoning concerns whether the signed destination is the intended recipient; phishing concerns
whether recovery words leave the owner's control. That boundary keeps destination-address material
distinct from seed-phrase disclosure material in Taopedia reference prose
([Address Poisoning Scams](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/address-poisoning-scams)).

References: [Address Poisoning Scams](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/address-poisoning-scams),
[Wallets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets)
