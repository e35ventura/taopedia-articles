# Phishing

Phishing is a scam in which an attacker poses as something trustworthy to trick a person into
revealing a secret or approving a harmful action. In Bittensor the usual prize is a wallet's secret
material, most often the seed phrase, or a signature that quietly hands over control of TAO or
stake.

References:
[Handle your Seed Phrase Securely](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/handle-seed-phrase)

## What It Targets

The center of a Bittensor phishing attempt is the recovery phrase that backs a wallet. Documentation
treats the seed phrase as material that can recreate the wallet's keys, so an attacker who obtains
it can rebuild the wallet elsewhere and move its funds. A phishing attempt may instead aim to get
the victim to sign a transaction that transfers TAO or stake, reaching the same end through a
different door.

References:
[Handle your Seed Phrase Securely](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/handle-seed-phrase),
[Wallets, Coldkeys and Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets)

## Why the Seed Phrase Is the Prize

Phishing works on Bittensor for the same reason recovery phrases are powerful: the phrase is
key-equivalent. Because it can regenerate the keys it belongs to, disclosing it is close to handing
over the wallet itself. That is what makes a convincing fake prompt for a recovery phrase so
dangerous, and why no legitimate process needs a user to type that phrase into a third-party page.

References:
[Handle your Seed Phrase Securely](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/handle-seed-phrase)

## How It Differs from Address Poisoning

Phishing relies on deception of the user, while a related scam, address poisoning, relies on a
lookalike address slipping into a transaction. The contrast is useful: phishing tries to make a
person volunteer a secret or a signature, whereas address poisoning tries to make a person send
funds to the wrong place without revealing anything. Both end in lost funds, but through different
tricks.

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept at a high level. It does not catalog specific scams, sites, or
messages, and the forms phishing takes change constantly. The durable point is the target: a
wallet's recovery phrase and signing authority are what a Bittensor phishing attempt is ultimately
after.
