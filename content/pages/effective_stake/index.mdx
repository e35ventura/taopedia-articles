# Effective Stake

Effective stake is the total staked TAO associated with a delegate. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake) defines it as the
total staked TAO amount of a delegate, including the delegate's own TAO and TAO delegated by
nominators.

## Delegate Context

A delegate is a subnet validator that receives staked TAO from delegators. Effective stake is read
in that same staking context: it describes the stake associated with the delegate, not a separate
asset type or a standalone participant role.

## Own and Delegated TAO

The term combines two sources of staked TAO for the same delegate: the delegate's own staked TAO and
TAO supplied by nominators. This makes effective stake a combined staking measure rather than only a
description of self-stake.

## Relationship to Stake Weight

Effective stake is an amount of staked TAO. It is distinct from stake weight, which the
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight) defines as the computed
total stake value for a validator that determines their consensus power and emissions in a subnet.

## Delegate Stake Context

The [Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake)
defines delegate stake as the amount of TAO staked by the delegate themselves. Effective stake names
the combined total that includes that self-staked amount plus TAO delegated by nominators, so
delegate stake is the self-staked component within effective stake.

References:
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake),
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake)

## Relationship to Stake

Stake names the currency-token amount associated with a validator UID in a subnet, while effective
stake names the delegate-facing total of own and nominated TAO. The
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake) entry includes both
self-stake and delegated stake in subnet alpha denominations. Effective stake is the delegate-level
reading of that combined support before subnet-specific stake-weight computation.

For readers, stake is the subnet position amount; effective stake is the delegate summary that rolls
up nominators and self-stake for the same validator context.

References: [Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Staking into Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation#staking-into-subnets)

## Relationship to Delegation

Delegation is the process that attaches nominated TAO to a validator, and effective stake is one way
to describe the resulting total on the delegate side. The
[Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation) defines
delegation as staking TAO to a validator. Effective stake does not replace that action vocabulary;
it summarizes how much TAO ends up associated with the delegate after delegation occurs.

Readers should keep delegation as the relationship or action term and effective stake as the
combined amount term for the delegate receiving that support.

References: [Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Relationship to Nominate

Nomination is the action vocabulary for attaching TAO to a validator hotkey, while effective stake
describes the delegate-side total that can result from repeated nominations over time. The
[Glossary: Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate) places nominate in
the staking workflow before stake amounts and emissions context are interpreted.

Effective stake therefore sits downstream of nomination language: nominators act, delegates
accumulate associated TAO, and effective stake names the combined total rather than the button or
transaction label.

References: [Glossary: Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate),
[Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate)

## Relationship to Validator Permit

Validator permit concerns whether a UID may validate in a subnet, and stake weight derived from
stake context feeds that permission picture. The
[Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit)
ties validation rights to stake-weight context. Effective stake is an input-side amount measure for
delegates; permit is the validation-right outcome that depends on broader stake-weight rules rather
than on effective stake alone.

For readers, higher effective stake can support a delegate's staking story, but permit remains a
separate validation-right concept defined in official staking and consensus references.

References:
[Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## Relationship to Dividends

Dividends describe validator-side emission results from Yuma Consensus, while effective stake
describes how much TAO is associated with a delegate in staking vocabulary. The
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions)
documentation connects validator emissions to bonds and miner incentive outcomes. Effective stake
does not calculate dividends; it helps readers situate delegate support before those consensus results
are discussed.

References:
[Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions),
[Dividends](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions)

## Relationship to Emission Distribution

Subnet emissions move through injection and participant distribution stages. Effective stake belongs
to the staking layer that precedes dividend, incentive, and staker payout interpretation. The
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution) docs place
validator take and staker outcomes after consensus resolution.

Readers should not read effective stake as a payout rate. It is a delegate support total; distribution
rules determine how emissions become participant-facing results.

References:
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution),
[Emission overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)

## Relationship to Validator Take

Validator take is a downstream fee-share concept on delegated-stake emissions, while effective stake
is an upstream support total. The
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution) documentation
discusses validator take separately from consensus-side dividend language.

Keeping the boundary clear prevents readers from treating effective stake as validator revenue or
take percentage.

References:
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution),
[Glossary: Validator Take](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take)

## Relationship to Subnet Weights

Subnet weights compare subnets for emission injection using net TAO flow signals. Effective stake is
a within-subnet delegate amount, not a cross-subnet allocation weight. The
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution-across-subnets)
documentation describes subnet weights at the injection stage.

Staking that increases effective stake can influence subnet flow context over time, but effective
stake and subnet weights answer different questions at different layers.

References:
[Glossary: Subnet Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-weights),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)

## Relationship to Flow-Based Emissions

Flow-based emissions read subnet demand through staking and unstaking flows smoothed by EMA. Effective
stake is a position snapshot for a delegate, while flow-based emissions read movement into and out of
subnet liquidity contexts. The
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#tao-reserve-injection) page separates
injection signals from participant distribution.

References:
[Flow-based emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution-across-subnets),
[Exponential Moving Averages](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/ema)

## Relationship to Conviction and Locked Stake

Conviction and locked-stake concepts describe time- or commitment-sensitive staking behavior in
Bittensor articles. Effective stake remains a total associated TAO measure and does not, by itself,
encode lock duration or conviction parameters unless official staking docs attach those fields to the
same delegate context.

Readers comparing articles should treat conviction/lock articles as specialized staking behavior and
effective stake as the broader delegate total vocabulary.

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake)

## Relationship to Yuma Consensus

Yuma Consensus converts validator weight signals into miner incentives and validator-side outcomes.
Effective stake belongs to staking support vocabulary that sits beneath validator weight submission and
bond smoothing. The
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus) overview is the right place
for consensus math; effective stake helps readers understand delegate support totals before entering
that layer.

References: [Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights)

## Relationship to Unstaking

Unstaking removes or reduces TAO attached to validator support contexts, which can lower effective
stake totals over time. The
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking) describes
unstaking as the reverse of staking TAO to a validator. Effective stake should therefore be read as a
state-dependent total that changes when nominators or delegates add or remove support.

References:
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Relationship to Proxy Wallets

Proxy wallets let one account act on behalf of another within defined permissions. Effective stake
still describes delegate-associated TAO totals; proxy mechanics describe who may sign staking actions.
Readers should consult official proxy documentation when an account structure, rather than a stake
total, is the focus.

References:
[Glossary: Proxy](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#proxy),
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake)

## Relationship to Multisig

Multisig arrangements govern how multiple signers approve transactions. Effective stake does not
encode multisig policy; it remains a staking amount summary for delegates. Use multisig articles when
the question is authorization structure rather than how much TAO is associated with a delegate.

References:
[Glossary: Multisig](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multisig),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Reader Boundary

This article defines the term at a high level. It does not list live delegate balances, compare
delegates, recommend staking choices, or describe staking steps. Readers should use official staking
references for staking-specific actions.
