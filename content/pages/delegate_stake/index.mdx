# Delegate Stake

Delegate stake is the TAO a Bittensor delegate has staked themselves. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake) defines it as the
amount of TAO staked by the delegate themselves.

## Staking Context

The glossary describes a [delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate) as
a subnet validator that receives staked TAO from delegators. In that context, delegate stake names
the delegate's own staked TAO rather than TAO supplied by other delegators.

## Relationship to Effective Stake

The [Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake) defines a related
term, effective stake, as the total staked TAO of a delegate — their own TAO plus TAO delegated by
others. Delegate stake is the self-staked portion of that total.

## Nominator Delegation Context

The [Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator) describes a
nominator as an account that stakes TAO on a validator's hotkey. Delegate stake names the delegate's
own TAO in that relationship, distinct from TAO supplied by nominators who nominate to validators or
delegates.

References:
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake),
[Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator)

## Relationship to Multiple Mechanisms

Delegate stake names the TAO a delegate stakes to themselves inside a subnet market. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms) notes
that subnets may run more than one incentive mechanism, each with separate bond pools for Yuma
Consensus, as described in
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets).

Those mechanism rules shape miner evaluation and validator bonds. Delegate stake does not define
them. For readers, delegate stake still names self-staked TAO on a delegate, while
multiple-mechanism context belongs with per-mechanism evaluation when a subnet runs more than one
incentive system.

## Reader Boundary

This article defines the term at a high level. It does not report live stake balances, rank
delegates, or describe transaction steps. Readers should use official staking references for
staking-specific details.

## Relationship to Delegate

Delegate stake and a delegate are related but different scopes in Bittensor staking vocabulary. A
delegate is a subnet validator that receives staked TAO from delegators, while delegate stake names
the TAO that delegate has staked to themselves rather than TAO supplied by others. The
[Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate) describes the
delegate role, and the
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake)
defines delegate stake as the amount of TAO staked by the delegate themselves.

For readers, delegate names the validator role that can receive delegated TAO, while delegate stake
names the delegate's own self-staked TAO inside that role.

References: [Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate),
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake)

## Relationship to Stake

Delegate stake and stake are related but different scopes in Bittensor staking vocabulary. Stake
names the currency-token amount delegated to a validator UID in a subnet, while delegate stake names
the self-staked portion of a delegate's own TAO rather than TAO delegated by nominators. The
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake) defines stake as
delegated currency-token amount in a subnet, and the
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake)
defines delegate stake as TAO the delegate staked to themselves.

For readers, stake names delegated amount in a subnet context, while delegate stake names the
delegate's own TAO contribution inside that staking picture.

References: [Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake)

## Relationship to Nominator

Delegate stake and a nominator are related but different scopes in Bittensor staking vocabulary. A
nominator is an account that stakes TAO on a validator's hotkey, while delegate stake names the
delegate's own TAO rather than TAO supplied by nominators who nominate to validators or delegates.
The [Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator) describes
the nominator role, and the
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake)
defines delegate stake as the delegate's self-staked TAO.

For readers, nominator names the account that delegates TAO to a validator, while delegate stake
names the delegate's own TAO distinct from nominator-supplied stake.

References: [Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator),
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake)

## Relationship to Netuid

Delegate stake and a netuid are related but different parts of Bittensor vocabulary. A netuid
identifies a subnet on the network, while delegate stake names self-staked TAO on a delegate inside
a subnet market context. The
[Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid) places netuid at the
subnet level, and the
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake)
defines delegate stake as TAO the delegate staked to themselves.

For readers, a netuid identifies which subnet staking context is in scope, while delegate stake
names the delegate's own TAO within that subnet market.

References: [Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid),
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake)
