# Delegate Stake

Delegate stake is the TAO a Bittensor delegate stakes themselves. It is the self-staked portion of
the delegate's staking context, not the full amount of TAO that may be associated with that delegate
after nomination
([Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake),
[Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate)).

The term is useful because delegate, nominator, stake, and effective stake all sit close together in
Bittensor staking vocabulary. Delegate stake keeps the focus on who supplied the TAO.

## Delegate and Nominator Boundary

A delegate is a validator that can receive stake from nominators. A nominator stakes TAO to a
validator rather than becoming that validator
([Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate),
[Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator)).

Delegate stake belongs to the delegate side of that relationship. Nominated TAO can add to the
delegate's broader staking context, but it is not delegate stake because it did not come from the
delegate themselves.

## Effective Stake and Stake Weight

Delegate stake and effective stake describe different totals. Effective stake is the total staked
TAO associated with a delegate after both self-stake and nominated support are considered
([Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake)).

Stake weight is another related but distinct term. It is the computed stake value used for validator
consensus power and emissions inside a subnet
([Glossary: Stake Weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight),
[Understanding Subnets: Validator stake weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets#validator-stake-weight)).

These boundaries keep delegate stake from being mistaken for every amount attached to a delegate or
for the consensus measure derived from stake context.

## Staking Context

Delegation places delegates and nominators inside the broader staking model. Delegate stake names
the delegate's self-staked TAO inside that model, while nomination and effective stake describe
other parts of the same support relationship
([Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake)).

Delegate stake is therefore descriptive rather than advisory. It does not rank validators, quote
reward outcomes, or explain how to choose a validator. It identifies the self-supplied part of a
staking relationship.

## Environment Scope

Bittensor separates localnet, testnet, and mainnet environments. Delegate stake examples from one
environment should not be treated as evidence for another because validator sets, balances, and
stake relationships are environment-specific
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).

Localnet examples are isolated development examples. Testnet examples are shared non-production
examples. Mainnet delegate stake concerns production validator relationships and production TAO.

## Autostaked Emissions Route Through Nomination, Not Delegate Stake

Official [Auto Staking for Miners](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners/autostaking) documentation
describes autostaking as a mechanism that routes miner
[emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#emission) proceeds into a configured
validator's position as delegated stake. That routing places the autostaked amount on the nominator
side of the relationship: the miner's emission arrives as externally delegated
[TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao) rather than as TAO supplied by the
validator themselves. The validator's
[effective stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake) total rises
with each autostaked emission, but delegate stake stays unchanged because the source of the new TAO
is a miner's emission, not the delegate's own staking action. Delegate stake records only what the
validator has personally staked, so the autostaking path adds to a different bucket entirely
([Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake),
[Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Auto Staking for Miners](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners/autostaking)).

That sourcing boundary explains a gap that can appear when comparing delegate stake and effective
stake for the same validator. A delegate with a large gap between effective stake and delegate stake
holds a significant share of their staking support from
[nominators](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator) or autostaking miners
rather than from self-stake. Autostaking grows the effective stake total without touching the
delegate stake figure, so a validator popular with autostaking miners may see their effective stake
increase each emission period while their delegate stake remains constant. The gap can widen
indefinitely without the validator taking any staking action of their own, because autostaking
inflows arrive through the nomination channel rather than through the validator's own stake
position. Knowing that autostaked emissions enter through nomination helps explain why the two
figures can show opposite trends at the same time
([Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake),
[Auto Staking for Miners](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners/autostaking),
[Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator)).

Readers describing a validator's staking profile should treat delegate stake and effective stake as
measuring different things about the same validator. Delegate stake names what the validator
committed from their own account; effective stake names the full pool of staking support from all
sources including self-stake, nomination, and autostaking inflows. Neither figure reveals the other:
a low delegate stake figure does not imply low effective stake if nomination or autostaking
contributions are large, and a high delegate stake does not prevent effective stake from being
exactly equal to delegate stake if no nominators or autostaking miners have contributed. The correct
reading is to check both figures independently and look for explanations in nominator activity or
autostaking behavior before concluding anything about a validator's staking relationship with others
([Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake),
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake),
[Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator)).

Validators have no mechanism to prevent autostaking inflows from reaching their effective stake
total. The nomination channel that carries autostaked emissions is open to any miner who has
configured the validator as their autostaking target, and that configuration sits on the miner's
side rather than the validator's side of the relationship. This one-sided inflow path is part of why
effective stake and delegate stake can diverge without any action from the validator: delegate stake
grows only when the validator actively stakes their own TAO, while effective stake can grow each
emission period from miner autostaking decisions that the validator played no part in making. A
validator's delegate stake figure is therefore fully under their control, while their effective
stake figure reflects decisions made by third-party nominators and autostaking miners as well. That
asymmetry in control is a useful reminder that the two figures belong to different parts of the
Bittensor staking vocabulary even when they appear side by side in the same validator profile
([Auto Staking for Miners](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners/autostaking),
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake),
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake),
[Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).
