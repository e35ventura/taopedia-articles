# Delegate

A delegate is a subnet validator that receives staked TAO from delegators and performs validation
tasks in one or more subnets
([Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate),
[Glossary: Subnet Validator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-validator)).

The term sits where Bittensor's validation role and delegated-stake vocabulary meet. It should not
be confused with the nominator role that supplies TAO support.

## Validation and Staking Role

Delegate is a role term for the receiving side of a staking relationship. The delegate receives
staked TAO and performs validation work for subnet consensus
([Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

This makes delegate more specific than a generic staking reference. It names a validator role that
can receive staked TAO from others while carrying out validation tasks.

The role has both staking and validation context. On the staking side, the delegate receives support
from nominators. On the validation side, the delegate participates as a subnet validator rather than
as the TAO holder supplying support
([Glossary: Subnet Validator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-validator),
[Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator)).

That keeps delegate from being used as a general word for any wallet that stakes. The term points to
the receiving validator role in a delegation relationship.

## Nominator Boundary

A delegate is paired with the accounts that supply stake. A nominator is the TAO-holder role that
stakes with validators, while delegate names the validator that receives that stake
([Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator),
[Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate)).

The relationship is directional: nominators supply delegated stake, and delegates receive that stake
while performing validation work. Keeping the two sides separate prevents delegate from being read
as the account role that supplies TAO.

Nominate is the action term on the supplying side. A nominator can nominate by staking TAO with a
validator, while the delegate is the validator-side role that receives that delegated support
([Glossary: Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate),
[Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate)).

This role/action separation helps source reading. Delegate identifies the receiving validator role,
nominator identifies the supporting holder role, and nominate identifies the action that attaches
support.

## Delegate Stake and Effective Stake

Delegate stake and effective stake refine the staking side of the delegate role. Delegate stake is
the TAO the delegate stakes themselves. Effective stake is the broader total associated with the
delegate, including the delegate's own TAO and TAO delegated by nominators
([Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake),
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake)).

These terms keep the role and stake amounts separate. Delegate names the receiving validator role;
delegate stake names the self-staked component; effective stake names the combined total behind that
role.

Stake is the more general amount vocabulary. It can include both self-supplied and delegated support
around a validator position, while delegate stake and effective stake make the amount boundary more
specific ([Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake)).

That prevents a delegate page from turning the role label into one amount. A delegate can have
self-stake, nominated support, and an effective-stake total, but the role term itself names the
validator receiving side.

## Permit Context

Validation rights are separate from the role label. A validator permit is the permission flag for
validation rights in a subnet
([Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit),
[Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate)).

That boundary keeps delegate from carrying every permission detail by itself. Delegate names the
staking-and-validation role; permit vocabulary describes validation rights for the relevant subnet
context.

A validator permit should therefore be read as subnet-specific validation-rights vocabulary, not as
a replacement for the delegate role. A delegate can be discussed as a staking-and-validation role,
while permit material answers whether validation rights are present in the relevant subnet context
([Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit),
[Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate)).

This is a scope boundary, not a ranking statement. Delegate vocabulary identifies the role; permit
vocabulary identifies a rights flag in context.

## Environment Scope

Bittensor separates localnet, testnet, and mainnet environments. Delegate examples from one
environment should not be treated as evidence for another because validators, stake positions, and
nominator relationships are environment-specific
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).

Localnet examples are isolated development examples. Testnet examples are shared non-production
examples. Mainnet delegate examples concern production validators, production stake, and production
nominator relationships.

The role can be explained across environments, but concrete stake positions and validator
relationships belong to the environment being discussed. A localnet example can show the role
boundary, while a mainnet statement needs production context
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).
