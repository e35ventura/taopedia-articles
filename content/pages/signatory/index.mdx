# Signatory

A signatory is one participant in a multisig wallet. Bittensor's
[Glossary: Signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory) and
[multisig wallet guide](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig) describe multisig as shared
coldkey custody distributed across a set of signatories, where any signatory can propose a
transaction but execution still depends on the wallet's threshold of approvals.

The term belongs to shared-custody vocabulary. It names one participant in the proposal and approval
path, not the threshold rule itself and not the protected coldkey being shared
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig),
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey)).

## What It Represents

In a multisig, signatories are the wallets or participants whose approvals count toward the `M` of
`N` threshold. The multisig guide states that any signatory can propose a transaction, but the
action must be agreed by enough of the others before it executes
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

That makes signatory a participant term. It answers who can take part in the approval flow, while
multisig answers how many of those participants must approve before the action proceeds.

## Development Stage Context

The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)
describes subnet development as moving from localnet to testnet and then mainnet. Signatory examples
should be read against that network boundary rather than as proof of live mainnet coldkey custody.

In localnet, multisig approval mechanics can be tested in an isolated environment. Signatory
coordination in localnet does not involve real TAO or represent production coldkey state.

On testnet, threshold signing can be exercised in a shared non-production network. Testnet signatory
roles are separate from mainnet coldkey protection and real wallet control.

On mainnet, each signatory participates in shared custody over an actual coldkey holding real TAO
and subnet positions
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

The [Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks) reference
separates mainnet, testnet, and localnet. A signatory example from one environment should not be
read as representing coldkey control in another environment.

## Proposal and Approval Role

The multisig guide describes two recurring signatory actions: proposing a transaction and approving
an existing proposal. A single signatory can start the process, but threshold approval is still
required before the transaction executes
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

Readers should therefore not treat one signatory's proposal as completed wallet activity by itself.
A signatory is part of the approval path, not a substitute for the threshold rule around that path.

## Distinction from Multisig

A signatory is one participant in a multisig wallet. Multisig is the broader `M`-of-`N` approval
model that requires a threshold of signatories before an action is authorized
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig),
[Glossary: Signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory)).

- **Signatory** — one participant whose approval may count.
- **Multisig** — the threshold custody model those participants operate under.

## Distinction from Coldkey

A signatory is one participant in a shared-custody arrangement. A coldkey is the security-sensitive
wallet key whose authority the arrangement is protecting
([Glossary: Signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory),
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey)).

- **Signatory** — one approver in the multisig path.
- **Coldkey** — the protected ownership key or wallet authority.

## Distinction from Pure Proxies

A signatory is one participant whose approval counts in the multisig workflow. Pure proxies are
keyless stand-in accounts sometimes used to keep multisig member slots stable when membership
changes ([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig),
[Understanding Pure Proxies](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies/pure-proxies)).

- **Signatory** — participant in the approval flow.
- **Pure proxies** — stable stand-in accounts inside some multisig designs.

## Distinction from Proxy Wallets

A signatory is one participant in a multisig approval path. A proxy wallet is a separate account
that acts on behalf of a protected safe wallet under a scoped proxy type such as `Staking` or
`Governance` ([Glossary: Signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory),
[Proxies overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies)).

- **Signatory** — one approver in an M-of-N multisig path.
- **Proxy wallet** — scoped delegation where one account acts for another under a proxy type.

Multisig spreads approval across several signatories on the protected wallet itself. Proxy wallets
place delegated authority on a different account while the safe wallet remains the authority source
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig),
[Proxies overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies)).

## Distinction from Coldkey Swap

A signatory is one participant in shared coldkey custody. Coldkey swap migrates on-chain wallet
identity to a new coldkey address after recovery material is compromised or a controlled migration
is required ([Glossary: Signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory),
[Coldkey Swap](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)).

- **Signatory** — one approver in the multisig path for the current coldkey.
- **Coldkey swap** — on-chain migration of coldkey identity and its hotkey state.

Signatory vocabulary names who may propose or approve under the existing threshold. Coldkey swap
vocabulary names replacing the controlling coldkey rather than adding another approver
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

## Distinction from Hotkeys

A signatory is a multisig participant who may propose or approve coldkey-level actions. A hotkey is
the operational key used for mining, validation, and subnet signing work
([Glossary: Signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory),
[Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey)).

- **Signatory** — participant in shared coldkey approval.
- **Hotkey** — operational signing key for subnet work.

Multisig documentation often protects high-value coldkeys that control validator hotkeys, but
signatory vocabulary stays on the approval path while hotkey vocabulary stays on operational subnet
roles ([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig),
[Wallets, Coldkeys and Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets)).

## Distinction from Delegate

A signatory is one participant in multisig coldkey custody. A delegate is a subnet validator that
receives staked TAO from delegators and performs validation work
([Glossary: Signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory),
[Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate)).

- **Signatory** — one approver in shared wallet custody.
- **Delegate** — receiving validator role in the staking relationship.

The shared word "delegate" in proxy documentation refers to scoped account authority, not the
staking delegate role. Signatory belongs to multisig custody vocabulary; delegate belongs to staking
and subnet validation vocabulary ([Proxies overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

## Distinction from Ledger Hardware Wallet

A signatory is one participant whose approval may count toward a multisig threshold. A Ledger
hardware wallet is one device used to keep coldkey material in offline signing custody
([Glossary: Signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory),
[Using Ledger Hardware Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/using-ledger-hw-wallet)).

- **Signatory** — participant role in the multisig approval path.
- **Ledger hardware wallet** — offline signing device for coldkey custody.

One signatory may use a hardware wallet to approve proposals, but signatory vocabulary names the
approval role while Ledger vocabulary names the custody device
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig),
[Coldkey and Hotkey Workstation Security](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-hotkey-security)).

## Distinction from Coldkey and Hotkey Workstation Security

Coldkey and hotkey workstation security matches each wallet operation to the device tier that fits
its risk. A signatory is one participant in the multisig approval path for a protected coldkey
([Coldkey and Hotkey Workstation Security](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-hotkey-security),
[Glossary: Signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory)).

- **Workstation security** — tiered model of where each key role may sign.
- **Signatory** — one participant in shared coldkey approval.

Workstation security guidance encourages keeping signatory keys on meaningfully separate devices.
Signatory vocabulary names each participant in that shared custody arrangement
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

## Distinction from Staking Proxy Attacks

A signatory is one participant in multisig coldkey custody. Staking proxy attacks name the risk when
a compromised staking proxy misuses delegated staking authority on a protected account
([Glossary: Signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory),
[Avoid Staking Proxy Attacks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/avoid-staking-proxy-attacks)).

- **Signatory** — one approver in an M-of-N multisig path.
- **Staking proxy attacks** — misuse of delegated staking authority on a proxy key.

Multisig raises the bar before coldkey-level actions execute. Staking proxy attack vocabulary
concerns scoped proxy authority that can still move stake even without full transfer control
([Proxies overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies)).

## Distinction from Regenerating a Key

A signatory is one participant in ongoing multisig custody. Regenerating a key is the recovery
process that recreates a lost or deleted coldkey or hotkey from its associated mnemonic
([Glossary: Signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory),
[Glossary: Regenerating a Key](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#regenerating-a-key)).

- **Signatory** — participant in the live approval path.
- **Regenerating a key** — recovery action that rebuilds lost key material from a mnemonic.

Losing one signatory key may be survivable when the multisig threshold can still be met.
Regenerating a key restores a specific lost key file rather than changing how many approvals a group
requires ([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

## Distinction from Yuma Consensus

A signatory is one participant in shared coldkey custody who may propose or approve wallet actions.
Yuma Consensus is the on-chain process that converts submitted validator weights into emission
shares after evaluation completes
([Glossary: Signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).

- **Signatory** — one approver in shared wallet custody.
- **Yuma Consensus** — on-chain weight aggregation that settles emissions.

Signatory vocabulary answers who may take part in coldkey approval. Yuma Consensus answers how
validator weights become emission shares in a subnet
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the role term. It does not identify who the signatories are for a specific wallet
or whether a particular proposal has enough approvals. Those are wallet-specific operational facts
that must be checked in the actual multisig context
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).
