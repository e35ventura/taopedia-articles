# Staking

Staking is the process of attaching TAO support to a validator in Bittensor
([Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).
It is process vocabulary. Stake is the related amount vocabulary after support is attached
([Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake)).

The term should stay tied to validator support. It is not a validator recommendation, balance
report, fee explanation, or full staking guide.

## Validator Support

Staking is read through the relationship between a TAO holder and a validator. Delegation describes
staking TAO to a validator, while staking is the broader process name
([Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

This makes staking different from the validator role itself. The validator performs validation;
staking attaches support to that validator's subnet position.

That distinction also keeps staking separate from the resulting stake amount. The process attaches
support; the amount records the support after the process has happened.

## Stake and Stake Weight

Staking and stake are closely related but not identical. Staking names the process, while stake
names the associated token amount after support is attached to a validator position
([Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking)).

Stake weight is another related term. It is the interpreted stake measure used for validator
consensus power and emissions in a subnet
([Glossary: Stake Weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight),
[Understanding Subnets: Validator stake weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets#validator-stake-weight)).

The useful boundary is process, amount, and weighting: staking attaches support, stake names the
attached amount, and stake weight is the downstream consensus measure.

## Consensus Context

Staking matters because validator stake contributes to consensus and emissions. Bittensor consensus
uses validator signals and stake context when calculating subnet emissions
([Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

That does not turn staking into an emissions calculation. Staking names the support process; reward
outcomes still depend on validator behavior, subnet context, and the broader emission system.

## Autostaking Context

Staking and autostaking describe related paths for attaching support. Staking is the general process
of attaching TAO support to a validator, while autostaking routes miner emissions into a staking
position automatically
([Auto Staking for Miners](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners/autostaking),
[Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking)).

The distinction is trigger rather than destination. Autostaking routes proceeds into a staking
position; it does not create a separate validator-support category.

## Environment Scope

Bittensor separates localnet, testnet, and mainnet environments. Staking examples from one
environment should not be treated as evidence for another because validator sets, balances,
emissions, and subnet state are environment-specific
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).

Localnet examples are isolated development examples. Testnet examples are shared non-production
examples. Mainnet staking concerns production validator relationships and production TAO.

## Unstaking and Moving Stake Name the Position Exit Paths

The [Staking and Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
documentation describes staking as the process of attaching
[TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao) support to a validator through the
delegation model. That attachment is not permanent by design. Bittensor defines two distinct
operations for ending or redirecting a staking position.
[Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking) ends the position and
returns the previously staked TAO to the staker's spendable free balance under their
[coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey).
[Moving stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#moving-stake) transfers the
position to a different validator without first dissolving the staking relationship
([Staking and Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

That distinction matters when describing what happens after a staking position is established.
Unstaking is not a continuation of staking but its reversal. The
[TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao) exits the validator's
[delegated stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake) pool and
returns to a spendable balance rather than remaining in a validator-support position. The validator
loses the corresponding delegated TAO from its support total, and the nominator regains access to
that balance under their own account. Moving stake, by contrast, keeps the TAO inside the staking
model while reassigning which validator receives the delegated support. The TAO does not become
freely spendable during a move; it transitions between validators while remaining in the staking
system and continuing to contribute to a delegated position
([Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking),
[Glossary: Moving Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#moving-stake)).

A staking description that stops at the opening step omits how positions are later closed or
redirected. Both unstaking and moving stake answer follow-on questions that arise naturally from
describing any staking action. Unstaking concerns whether previously staked
[TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao) has been returned to a free balance;
moving stake concerns whether that position has been redirected to a different validator without the
TAO first becoming spendable
([Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking),
[Glossary: Moving Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#moving-stake),
[Staking and Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

The autostaking context reinforces why these exit paths are worth tracking separately. When
[autostaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners/autostaking) routes miner emissions into a
validator position, the resulting staking entry can itself be unstaked or moved later. The staking
entry created by autostaking follows the same rules as manually created entries: unstaking returns
the balance, moving stake redirects it. Treating the autostaked entry as locked or non-transferable
would misread the staking model
([Auto Staking for Miners](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners/autostaking),
[Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking)).

Keeping these three terms in sequence supports accurate staking vocabulary across the full
lifecycle. Staking names how a validator support position is opened; moving stake names how it is
redirected while staying inside the delegation model; unstaking names how it is closed and the
attached [TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao) reclaimed as a free balance.
Each term describes one part of the staking lifecycle without collapsing the others into a single
action. A description of staking that implies the position is permanent misreads the model. A
description that treats moving stake as unstaking followed by re-staking introduces an intermediate
free-balance step that the moving-stake operation deliberately avoids. Keeping each term in its
correct position preserves the vocabulary distinction Bittensor delegation documentation encodes
([Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking),
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking),
[Glossary: Moving Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#moving-stake),
[Staking and Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).
