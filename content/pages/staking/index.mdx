# Staking

Staking is the process of attaching TAO support to a validator in Bittensor
([Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

## What It Represents

Staking is process vocabulary for attaching TAO support to a validator hotkey inside a subnet.
[Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake) is the related amount vocabulary
after support is attached. The term names validator support, not a validator recommendation, balance
report, fee explanation, or full staking guide.

Readers should separate four stake-management operations that sound similar:

- **Staking** — adds new TAO support to a validator hotkey on a subnet.
- **Unstaking** — withdraws delegated stake back toward the coldkey.
- **Moving stake** — re-points an existing delegated position without withdrawing first.
- **Transferring stake** — changes which coldkey owns a staked position while keeping it delegated.

Staking is also a rate-limited action on each netuid. The rate-limit documentation groups add stake,
remove stake, and move stake under the staking-operation limit, allowing one successful operation
per block on a particular subnet. Repeated staking attempts on netuid 1 or another selected netuid
are therefore paced by the chain rather than being an unrestricted sequence of support additions
([Rate Limits: Staking operations rate limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits#staking-operations-rate-limits)).

Staking also carries a documented size requirement. The staking and delegation overview states that
the minimum required stake for nominators is 0.1 TAO, so a holder has to attach at least that amount
behind a validator for the staking position to take effect
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

When nominators earn from staked positions,
[validator take](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-) applies at
tempo distribution on the delegated-stake portion. The staking and delegation overview documents a
default validator take of 18%, configurable by the delegate, so by default a validator keeps 18% of
delegated-stake emissions before the proportional split reaches nominators unless that delegate has
set a different take
([Staking and Delegation Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

Readers should also separate nearby relationship vocabulary:

- **Staking** — general process of attaching TAO support.
- **Delegation** — ongoing support relationship created behind a validator hotkey.
- **Nominate** — on-chain action that attaches that support.
- **Delegate stake** — TAO the validator stakes from its own balance only.
- **Effective stake** — combined delegate-owned and nominated total behind a hotkey.
- **Stake weight** — downstream consensus influence measure derived from that staking context.

On alpha subnets, staking can route TAO through subnet pool reserves into delegated alpha behind a
validator hotkey. [Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage) names the conversion
gap on that pool move. [Liquidity positions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/liquidity-positions)
commit range-based pool depth rather than a direct validator delegation action.
[TAO reserve](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets) is the pool quantity
that staking conversion can shift.

Staking through a subnet pool can also carry a separate liquidity fee. Fee documentation describes
the staking and unstaking liquidity fee as subnet-dependent, with a default or typical level around
0.05% of transacted liquidity, so that fee should be read separately from slippage and validator
take ([Transaction Fees in Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/fees)).

Staking assigns where support sits toward subnet participation. Dividends are the validator-side
emission share credited from smoothed bonds multiplied by miner incentives (`D_i = Σ_j B_ij · I_j`)
when Yuma settles a tempo; staking does not name those outcomes
([Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights),
[Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions)).
[Autostaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners/autostaking) routes miner emission proceeds
into a staking position automatically. Staking is the general support-attachment process that
autostaking can feed over time.

Staking examples depend on which network you connect to. Delegation and stake-weight readings from
one environment should not be read as production behavior on another
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).

## Distinction from Delegation

- **Staking** — action of attaching TAO support.
- **Delegation** — recorded support relationship behind a hotkey.

## Distinction from Stake

- **Staking** — process of attaching support.
- **Stake** — token amount after support is attached.

## Distinction from Effective Stake

- **Staking** — act of adding support behind a hotkey.
- **Effective stake** — combined TAO total behind that hotkey.

## Distinction from Stake Weight

- **Staking** — attaches support behind a validator.
- **Stake weight** — downstream consensus measure derived from stake.

## Distinction from Delegate Stake

- **Staking** — broader act of attaching support behind a hotkey.
- **Delegate stake** — validator-owned TAO staked from their own holdings.

## Distinction from Liquidity Positions

- **Staking** — delegation action through pool conversion.
- **Liquidity positions** — range-based pool depth commitment.

## Distinction from Slippage

- **Staking** — delegation intent behind pool conversion.
- **Slippage** — received-amount difference on that conversion.

## Distinction from Dividends

Staking is the process of attaching TAO support behind a validator hotkey. Dividends are the
validator-side emission share credited from smoothed bonds multiplied by miner incentives
(`D_i = Σ_j B_ij · I_j`) when Yuma settles a tempo
([Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking),
[Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights),
[Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions)).

- **Staking** — support attachment behind a validator hotkey.
- **Dividends** — validator-side share credited from bonds and miner incentives.

## Distinction from Moving Stake

- **Staking** — brings new value into a delegated position.
- **Moving stake** — re-points an existing attachment without adding fresh TAO.

## Distinction from TAO Reserve

- **Staking** — delegation action behind pool conversion.
- **TAO reserve** — pool quantity that action can move.

## Distinction from Yuma Consensus

- **Staking** — committing TAO or alpha to support participation.
- **Yuma Consensus** — on-chain settlement that turns validator weights into emission shares.

## Distinction from Autostaking

- **Staking** — general support-attachment process.
- **Autostaking** — automatic routing of miner proceeds into a staking position.

## Reader Boundary

Staking names the process of attaching TAO support to a validator, not a validator recommendation,
balance report, fee explanation, or complete staking guide
([Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking),
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake)).
