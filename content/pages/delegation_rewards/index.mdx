# Delegation Rewards

Delegation rewards are what a delegator earns for staking TAO to a validator instead of validating
itself. By backing a validator with stake, a delegator shares in the rewards that validator
produces, in proportion to how much stake it contributed
([Staking and Delegation Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

## What It Represents

A delegator's reward is its proportional slice of validator-side returns, scaled by stake relative
to everything else delegated to the same validator. Before nominators are paid, the validator keeps
a take on the delegated portion, so delegation rewards are net of that commission at tempo
distribution
([Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-),
[Glossary: Tempo](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tempo),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

Because delegation rewards are paid net of that take, the default take size matters to a delegator.
The staking and delegation overview documents a default validator take of 18%, configurable by the
delegate, so by default a validator keeps 18% of the delegated-stake emissions before the
proportional split reaches nominators unless that delegate has set a different take
([Staking and Delegation Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-)).

Take increases are not freely repeatable. The rate-limit reference lists `TxDelegateTakeRateLimit`
as a 216,000-block cooldown, roughly 30 days, with `DelegateTxRateLimitExceeded` returned when the
cooldown is breached. That cooldown gates the `increase_take` path specifically, while
`decrease_take` takes effect immediately, so a validator can lower commission at any time but cannot
raise it again until the window clears
([Chain Rate Limits: Delegate take rate limit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits#delegate-take-rate-limit),
[Subtensor Reference: Delegation](https://subtensor.com/reference/mechanisms/delegation)).

Readers should separate outcome vocabulary from action and relationship vocabulary:

- **Delegation rewards** — proportional returns delegated stake earns after distribution and take.
- **Validator take** — commission the validator keeps before the nominator split.
- **Delegation** — ongoing support relationship created when TAO is staked behind a validator
  hotkey.
- **Nominate** — on-chain action that attaches that support.
- **Nominator** — TAO-holder role that performs nomination.
- **Staking** — general process of attaching TAO support to a validator.
- **Dividends** — validator-side emission outcomes consensus produces before the take split.
- **Emission** — broader subnet reward and injection flow.
- **Effective stake** — combined delegate-owned and nominated total behind a validator hotkey.

[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus) produces miner incentives and
validator dividend shares from recorded weights. Delegation rewards describe how nominators share
the delegated-stake portion after validator take—not how weights were aggregated. Settlement and
nominator payout are separate stages on the same emission path
([Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

Nomination and delegation rewards relate as cause and outcome. Nominate attaches support; rewards
follow after distribution and take run at tempo end. Nomination alone does not guarantee a payout
amount, which still depends on validator performance, subnet emission context, take settings, and
live stake distribution behind the hotkey.

## How Delegation Rewards Are Calculated

The Staking and Delegation reference documents a two-step calculation that applies when validator
emissions are distributed at tempo end. First, the validator's configured take percentage is applied
to the emissions earned on delegated stake — the reference gives 18% as the default take, so the
validator keeps 18% of delegated-stake emissions before delegators receive anything
([Staking and Delegation Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

After the take is deducted, the remaining delegated-stake emissions are split proportionally among
all nominators delegating to that validator, in proportion to how much each delegator staked. The
reference expresses this as: a delegator's emission equals their stake divided by the total of all
delegated stakes to that validator, times the available delegated-emission pool
([Staking and Delegation Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

The documentation provides a concrete example: suppose a validator holds 800 TAO in self-stake, and
three nominators have delegated 100 TAO, 70 TAO, and 30 TAO respectively (200 TAO total delegated),
with the validator's take set to 18%. When validator emissions are distributed, the 18% take is
deducted from the delegated-stake emission share; the remainder is then split among the three
delegators in proportion to their stakes. The 100 TAO delegator receives the largest proportional
share (50% of available delegated emissions, since 100 / 200 = 0.5), the 70 TAO delegator receives
35% (70 / 200 = 0.35), and the 30 TAO delegator receives 15% (30 / 200 = 0.15)
([Staking and Delegation Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

Delegation rewards examples tied to netuid 1 illustrate one subnet's delegator return path. They
should not be read as representing production returns on another network
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).

## Distinction from Autostaking

- **Delegation rewards** — proportional return delegated stake earns after distribution and take.
- **Autostaking** — routing path that can grow the delegated position over time.

## Distinction from Validator Take

- **Validator take** — commission kept from delegated returns before the nominator split.
- **Delegation rewards** — proportional share that remains for delegated stake.

## Distinction from Nominator

- **Nominator** — role that supplies delegated support.
- **Delegation rewards** — proportional returns that role earns after distribution.

## Distinction from Nominate

- **Nominate** — action of attaching TAO support to a validator hotkey.
- **Delegation rewards** — returns once distribution and take run at tempo end.

## Distinction from Dividends

- **Dividends** — validator-side emission outcomes from consensus.
- **Delegation rewards** — nominator share after validator take on delegated emissions.

## Distinction from Effective Stake

- **Effective stake** — combined staking total behind a delegate hotkey.
- **Delegation rewards** — outcome vocabulary for what that support earns after take.

## Distinction from Liquidity Provider Rewards

- **Delegation rewards** — share of validator emissions for delegated stake.
- **Liquidity provider rewards** — fee income from trades crossing a liquidity range.

## Distinction from Staking

- **Staking** — process of attaching TAO support to a validator.
- **Delegation rewards** — share earned from that validator's emissions after take.

## Distinction from Emission

- **Emission** — broader subnet reward and injection flow.
- **Delegation rewards** — nominator share inside one validator relationship.

## Distinction from Yuma Consensus

- **Delegation rewards** — nominator returns after validator-side distribution and take.
- **Yuma Consensus** — on-chain mechanism that uses validator weights to determine emission shares.

## Distinction from Delegation

- **Delegation** — act of assigning stake to a validator you do not operate.
- **Delegation rewards** — emissions that delegation pays, net of validator take.

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept at a high level and is not financial advice. What a delegator actually
earns depends on the validator's performance, the take it sets, and the live distribution of stake
([Staking and Delegation Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).
