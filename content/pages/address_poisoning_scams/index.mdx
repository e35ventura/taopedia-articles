# Address Poisoning Scams

Address poisoning scams are a Bittensor wallet safety risk built around lookalike destination
addresses. The official address-poisoning page explains that long wallet addresses are difficult to
read quickly, while the wallet overview explains that wallets are used to sign transactions, access
TAO, and manage stake.

References:
[Address Poisoning Scams: Protect Your Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/address-poisoning-scams),
[Wallets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets)

## Why address poisoning matters

Address poisoning matters because wallet transactions depend on the destination address being the
one the sender actually intended to use. The official source describes the risk as a scam that can
mislead a user into selecting a lookalike address from wallet history. For Taopedia readers, the
important point is not the mechanics of the scam; it is that a familiar-looking address is not the
same thing as a verified destination.

Reference:
[Address Poisoning Scams: Protect Your Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/address-poisoning-scams)

## Wallet context

The wallet overview frames a Bittensor wallet as a tool for signing transactions, accessing TAO, and
managing stake in subnets. That makes address checks part of wallet safety rather than a cosmetic
detail. A mistaken destination can change where value or stake-related actions are directed, so
address-poisoning claims should be read in the context of wallet use.

References: [Wallets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets),
[Address Poisoning Scams: Protect Your Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/address-poisoning-scams)

## Lookalike addresses

The scam relies on confusion between a real destination address and a similar-looking address. The
official source emphasizes that abbreviated address displays can make this confusion easier,
especially when a user is relying on routine wallet history instead of checking the destination.
Taopedia should describe that risk at a concept level without reproducing examples or step-by-step
methods.

Reference:
[Address Poisoning Scams: Protect Your Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/address-poisoning-scams)

## Irreversible outcomes

The official address-poisoning page warns that lost tokens may not be recoverable through
intervention by the foundation or related organizations. This makes address poisoning different from
an ordinary user-interface mistake. Once a wallet transaction is sent to the wrong destination,
readers should not assume that a later correction path exists.

Reference:
[Address Poisoning Scams: Protect Your Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/address-poisoning-scams)

## What this article does not publish

This article does not publish wallet screenshots, example lookalike addresses, address-generation
details, recovery procedures, or incident totals. The goal is to explain why the topic belongs in
Bittensor wallet safety, not to provide a playbook for either carrying out or responding to a scam.

Reference:
[Address Poisoning Scams: Protect Your Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/address-poisoning-scams)

## Reading address-poisoning claims

Address-poisoning claims should be read as claims about destination verification and wallet safety.
A useful claim explains whether it is talking about wallet history, address display, transaction
review, or loss recovery. A weak claim turns the topic into generic security advice without
connecting it back to Bittensor wallets.
