# Consensus-Based Weights

Consensus-based weights, also known as liquid alpha, make validator-miner bond smoothing depend on
how validator weights align with consensus. The mechanism sits inside the Yuma Consensus bond path
between validator-weight signals and validator-side outcomes
([Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).

The core idea is alignment-sensitive smoothing. Stronger consensus alignment can make the related
bond response more direct, while weaker alignment keeps the response more conservative.

That places the mechanism on the bond-response side of Yuma Consensus. Validator weights provide
evaluation signals; consensus-based weights adjust how validator-miner bonds respond after those
signals are compared with consensus
([Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights),
[Glossary: Validator-Miner Bonds](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-miner-bonds)).

## Pair-Specific Response

Consensus alignment is why the bond-smoothing factor can differ across validator-miner pairs. When a
pair is closer to consensus, the bond response can be more direct; weaker alignment can keep the
response more conservative
([Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights)).

That pair scope separates consensus-based weights from fixed-alpha bond smoothing. A fixed setting
gives every bond the same smoothing response, while this mechanism allows the response to vary by
pair inside one subnet without becoming a separate miner-ranking rule.

## Bounds and Hyperparameters

Consensus-based weights operate inside low and high alpha bounds configured as subnet
hyperparameters. Those bounds limit how far the per-pair smoothing factor can move, so the response
stays a bounded EMA parameter rather than an open-ended custom score
([Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights),
[Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)).

The bounds constrain how quickly bond history can react. Consensus and submitted weight signals
still define what is being smoothed; the hyperparameters only cap the smoothing envelope.

## How the Per-Pair Alpha Is Computed

When liquid alpha is enabled, each validator-miner bond gets its own smoothing factor rather than
one subnet-wide value, interpolated between the low and high alpha bounds by a sigmoid. The sigmoid
reads one of two gaps depending on the pair: when the validator's weight is at or above its current
bond for that miner, it uses the gap between the weight and the miner's consensus value; otherwise
it uses the gap between the bond and the weight. The interpolated factor is then clamped back to the
low-to-high interval
([`alpha_sigmoid`](https://github.com/opentensor/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/epoch/run_epoch.rs)).

When liquid alpha is disabled, the same bond EMA instead uses a single subnet-wide factor derived
from the bonds moving average, so the per-pair interpolation between the bounds is what
consensus-based weights add over that baseline
([`compute_disabled_liquid_alpha`](https://github.com/opentensor/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/epoch/run_epoch.rs)).

## Reading Bounds on netuid 1

Alpha low, alpha high, and whether liquid alpha is enabled are per-subnet hyperparameter rows. For
the documented example netuid 1 on Finney mainnet, readers can verify the live bound values with
`btcli subnet hyperparameters --netuid 1 --network finney`
([Subnet Hyperparameters: View hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#view-hyperparameters)).

That read path keeps bound claims tied to a parseable netuid. Other subnets expose their own
hyperparameter tables and may use different configured ranges
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).

## Distinction from Exponential Moving Average

Consensus-based weights make the bond-smoothing alpha parameter vary with consensus alignment. EMA
is the broader smoothing method that updates validator-miner bond history gradually across epochs
([Exponential Moving Averages](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/ema),
[Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights)).

For netuid 1 readers, alpha here names the smoothing parameter in the bond update, not subnet alpha
tokens held in pool reserves
([Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)).

## Distinction from Clipping

Clipping limits outlier validator weights above the consensus benchmark before rank is calculated.
Consensus-based weights adjust how bond history responds after weights are compared with consensus
([Yuma Consensus: Clipping](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#clipping),
[Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights)).

- **Clipping** — filters submitted weights against the benchmark.
- **Consensus-based weights** — shapes bond responsiveness around alignment.

## Distinction from Validator Trust

Validator trust sums clipped weight influence a validator contributed across neurons on netuid 1.
Consensus-based weights adjust how quickly validator-miner bonds respond when alignment changes over
repeated epochs
([Glossary: Validator Trust](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-trust),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).

Validator trust is a clipped-influence reading for one pass. Liquid alpha is the bond-response rule
across epochs
([Glossary: Validator-Miner Bonds](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-miner-bonds)).

## Distinction from Dividends

Dividends are the validator-side emission outcomes paired from bonds and miner incentives.
Consensus-based weights shape how quickly bonds respond; they do not by themselves name a payout
amount
([Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions),
[Subnet Metagraph](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/metagraph)).

Dividend vocabulary belongs at the emission result. Consensus-based-weight vocabulary belongs on the
bond-smoothing step that feeds that result on the selected subnet.

## Distinction from Commit Reveal

Commit Reveal delays visible weight publication to reduce fresh-signal copying. Consensus-based
weights change bond responsiveness when submitted weights align or drift from consensus after they
are processed ([Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal),
[The Weight Copying Problem](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/weight-copying-in-bittensor)).

The two mechanisms address different copying risks in the same Yuma pass on netuid 1
([Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights)).

- **Commit reveal** — delays visible weight publication.
- **Consensus-based weights** — adjusts bond responsiveness around alignment.

## Distinction from Yuma Consensus

Consensus-based weights, also called liquid alpha, make the validator-miner bond-smoothing EMA
coefficient a variable tuned to how a validator's weights align with consensus, rather than a fixed
value ([Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights)).
Yuma Consensus is the broader process that converts validator weights into emission shares
([Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).

- **Consensus-based weights** — the variable bond-smoothing coefficient tuned to consensus
  alignment.
- **Yuma Consensus** — on-chain mechanism that uses validator weights to determine emission shares
  ([Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions)).

Bond-alignment vocabulary and settlement vocabulary answer different consensus layers
([Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights)).

## Distinction from Weight Copying

Consensus-based weights and weight copying relate as a defense and the behavior it counters. Weight
copying is the exploit where a validator mirrors others' weights instead of evaluating miners
([The Weight Copying Problem](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/weight-copying-in-bittensor)),
while consensus-based weights instead tunes the validator-miner bond-smoothing coefficient to
consensus alignment rather than clipping weights directly
([Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights)).

- **Weight copying** — mirroring others' weights without evaluating.
- **Consensus-based weights** — tuning the bond-smoothing coefficient to consensus alignment.

## Reader Boundary

Consensus-based weights are a Yuma Consensus bond-smoothing concept. They are not a separate subnet
token, a new miner task, or a complete explanation of validator rewards
([Consensus-based Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/consensus-based-weights),
[Glossary: Validator-Miner Bonds](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-miner-bonds)).

The mechanism only becomes meaningful with the subnet, incentive mechanism, and consensus process
that interpret validator weights
([Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).
