# Root Staker

A root staker is a TAO holder who stakes on the Root Subnet through validators. Root Subnet, also
called Subnet Zero, is the subnet-agnostic staking venue in Bittensor
([Glossary: Root Staker](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-staker),
[Glossary: Root Subnet/Subnet Zero](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero)).

The term names the holder role in Root Subnet staking. It should not be confused with a validator
role, an alpha staker role, or a root-claim preference.

## Root Subnet Staking

Root Subnet staking lets TAO holders stake through validators in a subnet-agnostic setting. That is
different from subnet-local alpha staking, where staking belongs to a particular subnet's
alpha-token context
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

This asset and venue distinction is the center of the term. A root staker supplies TAO through the
Root Subnet path, while alpha-side staking belongs to a specific subnet's alpha context.

The role also keeps root staking separate from ordinary subnet participation. A root staker may be
exposed to subnet dividend outcomes through Root Subnet mechanics, but the role itself remains the
TAO-holder role on the root side of staking.

That makes root staker a holder-role term, not a validator-role term. The validator is the actor
receiving stake support through the Root Subnet path, while the root staker is the TAO holder
supplying that support
([Glossary: Root Staker](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-staker),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

The asset boundary is part of the role. Root staker refers to TAO staked through Root, while
subnet-local alpha staking belongs to a different subnet-specific token context
([Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution),
[Glossary: Root Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero)).

## Delegation Context

A root staker participates by delegating TAO to a validator on the Root Subnet. Staking and
delegation vocabulary keeps the holder and validator roles separate: the holder supplies stake
support, while the validator receives that support
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Root Staker](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-staker)).

Root staker is therefore descriptive rather than advisory. It names the TAO holder in the Root
Subnet staking relationship without choosing a validator or explaining every staking action.

This is the same role/action distinction used elsewhere in staking vocabulary. Root staker names the
holder role, while delegation names the support relationship and nominate names the staking action
attached to a validator
([Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation),
[Glossary: Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate)).

That distinction prevents the term from becoming a workflow label. A root staker article can explain
who is supplying TAO through Root, but exact operational steps belong to staking and delegation
documentation.

## Dividends and Root Claims

Root-side stake can earn alpha dividends from subnets. Root claims explain how those accumulated
dividends are handled, including Keep and Swap outcomes
([Root Claim Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

That claim layer comes after the staking role. Root staker names who supplied TAO through Root
Subnet staking; root claims describe what can happen to dividends produced through that position.

Root claim type is a separate preference term inside that claim layer. It describes whether
processed dividends are handled through Keep or Swap, while root staker remains the role term for
the TAO holder using Root Subnet staking
([Root Claim Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Glossary: Root Staker](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-staker)).

Keeping this order clear helps with source reading. First comes the Root Subnet staking role, then
dividend context can arise, and then root-claim handling describes how accumulated dividends are
processed.

## Related Measures

Root proportion is nearby vocabulary because it describes how Root-side TAO appears in a subnet's
dividend context. Root staker is the role term, while root proportion is the subnet-level measure
([Glossary: Root Proportion](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-proportion),
[Glossary: Root Staker](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-staker)).

Keeping those terms separate helps prevent a holder role from being read as a subnet metric,
validator ranking, or dividend outcome.

TAO weight is another nearby term with a different scope. TAO weight describes how TAO stake
contributes relative to alpha stake, while root staker identifies the Root-side holder role
([Glossary: TAO Weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao-weight),
[Glossary: Root Staker](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-staker)).

For precise prose, root staker should be used for the TAO holder role, root proportion for the
subnet-level dividend proportion, and TAO weight for the relative contribution parameter.

## Environment Scope

Bittensor separates localnet, testnet, and mainnet environments. Root-staker examples from one
environment should not be treated as evidence for another because validators, balances, dividends,
and root staking state are environment-specific
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).

Localnet examples are isolated development examples. Testnet examples are shared non-production
examples. Mainnet root-staker examples concern production TAO and production Root Subnet staking.

The role vocabulary can be explained across environments, but concrete validator relationships and
dividend results belong to the environment being discussed. A localnet example can illustrate the
role boundary, while a mainnet statement needs production context
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Glossary: Root Staker](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-staker)).
