# Child Hotkeys

A child hotkey is a separate validator key that a
[delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate) authorizes to validate on
its behalf in a selected subnet. The
[Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys) documentation calls the
originating delegate hotkey the parent hotkey, and describes child hotkeys as keys the parent
re-delegates a portion of its stake to so they can validate in its place.

## What It Represents

The parent hotkey is an existing delegate that already holds stake. A child hotkey is a different
key that the parent points a share of that stake toward so the child can validate on behalf of the
parent without transferring ownership of the underlying stake
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).

Child hotkeys are configured for a specific subnet, identified by
[netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid). A parent may have at most five
child hotkeys on a given netuid, and the same parent can hold a separate set of children on a
different netuid. Child-hotkey statements therefore need the netuid attached before readers compare
limits, take settings, or routed stake shares across subnets
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Chain Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits)).

The child-hotkeys guide documents a 150-block cooldown (about 30 minutes) between successful
`set_children` calls on a subnet, including when a parent revokes every child by setting an empty
list. Both set and revoke paths share that limit because revoking is implemented as another
`set_children` call ([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Chain Rate Limits: Child hotkey operations rate limit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits#child-hotkey-operations-rate-limit)).

Before children can be configured, the parent hotkey must meet a documented minimum stake threshold:
1,000 TAO worth of alpha on mainnet or 100 TAO on testnet. The check sums TAO-equivalent alpha stake
across all subnets, not only on the netuid where children are being set. Readers can query the live
threshold with `subtensorModule.stakeThreshold()` on chain
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).

The guide's setting example uses `btcli stake child set --netuid 1` with a parent hotkey, child SS58
list, and proportions. That command form makes the target netuid explicit when routing stake to
children on one subnet ([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).

Readers should separate take vocabulary on the same delegation path:

- **Child hotkey** — authorized worker key validating for a parent delegate on one subnet.
- **Childkey take** — share of parent dividends the child keeps for routed validation on that
  subnet.
- **Validator take** — commission on delegated-stake dividends before nominators receive the
  remainder.
- **Subnet owner take** — fixed protocol share of a subnet's emission paid to its owner.

Childkey take is subnet-specific, ranging from 0 to 0.18 with a default of 0, and is paid from the
dividends the parent's stake would otherwise receive on that subnet. A child hotkey can also hold a
separate validator take, so the two rates are independent settings
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-)).

Childkey-take edits are paced separately from `set_children`. The rate-limit reference documents
`TxChildkeyTakeRateLimit` as a 216,000-block cooldown, roughly 30 days, between successful
childkey-take updates, returning `TxChildkeyTakeRateLimitExceeded` when repeated too soon. That
limit governs how often the take percentage can be changed, not how often children can be assigned
or revoked
([Chain Rate Limits: Child key take rate limit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits#child-key-take-rate-limit),
[Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).

On a subnet such as netuid 1, a subnet owner's hotkey can be authorized as a child hotkey with its
own childkey take on re-delegated stake. That role is separate from subnet owner take, which is the
documented owner share of the subnet's emission paid whether or not any validator re-delegates to
the owner ([Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

The documented rationale is key security. Validating through children lets the parent delegate
hotkey stay in a secure location while each child signs operations for its assigned subnet,
optionally on a different machine. If a child hotkey is compromised, only the subnets where that
child is used are exposed rather than the full parent key.

Child hotkeys route who signs validation work under a parent delegate's stake. They do not by
themselves rename [stake weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight)
influence vocabulary on the selected netuid. Settlement from eligible weight submissions still runs
through [Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus) on that netuid.

Child hotkey examples tied to netuid 1 illustrate one subnet's delegation pattern. They should not
be read as representing production validator configuration on another network
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).

## Distinction from Multiple Incentive Mechanisms

- **Child hotkeys** — delegated validation keys authorized by a parent hotkey.
- **Multiple incentive mechanisms** — parallel evaluation tracks with separate weight matrices.

## Distinction from Coldkey Swap

- **Child hotkeys** — delegated validation keys authorized per subnet.
- **Coldkey swap** — coldkey identity migration preserving hotkey state.

## Distinction from Validator Take

- **Child hotkey** — authorized worker key validating for a parent delegate.
- **Validator take** — commission share kept from delegated validation dividends.

## Distinction from Childkey Take

- **Childkey take** — share of parent dividends kept by the child for routed validation.
- **Validator take** — delegate commission on delegated-stake dividends.

## Distinction from Delegate

- **Delegate** — stake-holding validator that receives nominated support.
- **Child hotkey** — routed validation worker configured under that delegate on a subnet.

## Distinction from Register

- **Register** — subnet-entry process that grants a UID slot.
- **Child hotkey** — registered worker key a parent routes stake and signing authority to on that
  subnet.

## Distinction from Hotkey

- **Hotkey** — operational key tied to a wallet role.
- **Child hotkey** — authorized worker key under a parent delegate on one subnet.

## Distinction from Netuid

- **Netuid** — selector for which subnet market is in scope.
- **Child hotkey** — authorized validation key the parent assigns inside that market.

## Distinction from Chain Rate Limits

- **Child hotkeys** — authorized keys and routed stake shares a parent delegate assigns.
- **Chain rate limits** — pacing rules for how often child sets or take settings may change.

## Distinction from Subnet Owner Take

- **Child hotkey** — optional validating key earning childkey take on re-delegated stake.
- **Subnet owner take** — fixed protocol share of that subnet's emission paid to its owner.

## Distinction from Stake Weight

- **Child hotkeys** — routing vocabulary for which key performs validation with a delegated share.
- **Stake weight** — influence measure applied when validator signals count in consensus.

## Distinction from Yuma Consensus

- **Child hotkeys** — optional validating keys authorized by a parent delegate on one subnet.
- **Yuma Consensus** — epoch-boundary settlement from eligible validator weights to emission shares.

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept at a high level. It does not report which child hotkeys a parent
currently has, current take values, current stake proportions, or wallet ownership. Those are live
chain state and change over time; they should be checked for the relevant netuid with the Bittensor
CLI when current data is needed. The numeric rules described here, such as the five-child limit, the
150-block set/revoke cooldown, the mainnet and testnet minimum-stake thresholds, and the 18% take
ceiling, are protocol limits from the documentation.
