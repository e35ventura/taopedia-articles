# Child Hotkeys

A child hotkey is a separate validator key that a delegate validator authorizes to validate on its
behalf in a specific subnet. The originating delegate hotkey is called the parent hotkey. Instead of
signing every validation operation with the parent key, the validator re-delegates a portion of the
parent's stake to one or more child hotkeys, and those children do the validating for that subnet.

References: [Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)

## Parent and Child

The parent hotkey is an existing delegate that holds stake. A child hotkey is a different key that
the parent points a share of its stake toward. Once configured, the child validates in the subnet on
behalf of the parent, so the parent does not have to be present on that subnet's validation machine.
This is a delegation relationship between two keys, not a transfer of ownership of the underlying
stake.

References: [Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Configured per Subnet

Child hotkeys are set per subnet, identified by netuid. A parent can assign at most five child
hotkeys on a given netuid, and the same parent can assign a different set of children on another
netuid. Because the configuration is independent across subnets, a validator can run a distinct
child key for each subnet it participates in.

References: [Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)

## Why Validators Use Them

The main reason is key security. By validating through children, the parent delegate hotkey can stay
in a secure location while each child signs operations on its assigned subnet, optionally from a
different machine. If an attacker steals a child hotkey, only the subnets where that child is used
are exposed, rather than the full parent key. This isolation limits the blast radius of a
compromised validation key.

References: [Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)

## Child Take

A child hotkey earns a take, which is the share of rewards it keeps for validating on the parent's
behalf. Child take is subnet-specific and ranges from 0 (0%) to 0.18 (18%), with a default of 0. The
take is paid out of the dividends the parent's stake would otherwise receive on that subnet. A child
hotkey can also hold a separate delegate take, so the child take rate and the delegate take rate are
configured independently. Exact current take values are chain state and should be read for the
relevant netuid.

References: [Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Validator Take](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take)

## How They Are Set

Child hotkeys are managed with the Bittensor CLI. A validator assigns children and their stake
proportions with `btcli stake child set`, reviews them with `btcli stake child get`, and adjusts the
reward share with `btcli stake child take`. Each command takes the netuid and the parent hotkey, so
the assignment always applies to one subnet at a time.

References: [Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[btcli Reference](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/btcli)

## Careful Interpretation

Child hotkeys re-delegate validation duty and a share of dividends; they do not move the parent's
stake to a new owner or create new stake. The numeric limits here, such as the five-child cap and
the 18% take ceiling, are protocol rules from the documentation, while the specific children and
take values on any subnet are live chain state. The Taopedia-level meaning is the parent-child
delegation model that lets a validator spread subnet validation across isolated keys.

References: [Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)
