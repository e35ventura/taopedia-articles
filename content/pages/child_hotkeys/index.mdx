# Child Hotkeys

A child hotkey is a separate validator key that a
[delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate) authorizes to validate on
its behalf in a selected subnet. The
[Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys) documentation calls the
originating delegate hotkey the parent hotkey and describes child hotkeys as keys the parent
re-delegates a portion of its stake to so they can validate in its place.

## What It Represents

The parent hotkey is an existing delegate that already holds stake. A child hotkey is a different
key that the parent points a share of that stake toward so the child can validate on the parent's
behalf without the parent signing every validation operation itself. That is a delegation
relationship between two keys, not a transfer of ownership of the underlying stake
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).

Child hotkeys are configured per
[netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid). A parent may have at most five
child hotkeys on a given netuid and can hold a separate child set on another netuid. Any
child-hotkey claim should name both the parent hotkey and the subnet involved before readers compare
limits, take settings, or routed stake shares across subnets
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).

The documented rationale is key security. Validating through children lets the parent delegate
hotkey stay in a secure location while each child signs operations for its assigned subnet,
optionally on a different machine. If a child hotkey is compromised, exposure is limited to the
subnets where that child is used rather than the full parent key
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).

## Three Take Rates Readers Should Not Merge

Child-hotkey vocabulary sits next to several "take" labels that answer different questions:

- **[[Childkey Take]]** — subnet-specific share of dividends the child keeps for validating on the
  parent's behalf, ranging from 0% to 18% with a default of 0%.
- **Validator take** — commission the delegate keeps from delegated-stake dividends before the
  remainder reaches nominators.
- **Subnet owner take** — fixed protocol share of a subnet's alpha emission paid to the subnet
  owner, separate from optional child-hotkey routing.

A child hotkey can therefore encounter both [[Childkey Take]] and validator take on the same subnet
because those rates apply on different relationships within the delegation path
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

A subnet owner's hotkey can also be authorized as a child hotkey, but subnet owner take and
[[Childkey Take]] still arrive through different paths: owner take from the subnet emission split,
and childkey take only when stake is re-delegated to the owner's hotkey for validation on that
subnet ([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

## Per-Netuid Limits and Update Timing

The five-child limit per netuid is a protocol cap on how many worker keys a parent can authorize on
one subnet. [Chain rate limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits) pace how
often the child set or an individual child's take percentage may be changed on chain. Rate-limit
vocabulary belongs to update timing, not to whether a child key exists at all
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).

## Distinction from Multiple Incentive Mechanisms

Child hotkeys name delegated validation keys a parent authorizes inside specific subnets. Multiple
incentive mechanisms name parallel evaluation tracks with separate weight matrices on one subnet
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms)).

- **Child hotkeys** — delegated validation keys authorized by a parent hotkey.
- **Multiple incentive mechanisms** — parallel evaluation tracks with separate weight matrices.

## Distinction from Coldkey Swap

Child hotkeys route validation signing within a delegate's hotkey relationships. Coldkey swap
migrates on-chain identity to a new coldkey while preserving hotkey state
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Coldkey Swap](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-swap)).

- **Child hotkeys** — delegated validation keys authorized per subnet.
- **Coldkey swap** — coldkey identity migration preserving hotkey state.

## Distinction from Validator Take

A child hotkey is the worker key a parent delegate authorizes to validate on its behalf. Validator
take is the commission skimmed from validation dividends before the remainder reaches stakers
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-)).

- **Child hotkey** — authorized worker key validating for a parent delegate.
- **Validator take** — commission share kept from delegated validation dividends.

## Distinction from Childkey Take

[[Childkey Take]] names the share a child keeps from parent dividends for routed validation.
Validator take names the delegate's broader commission on delegated-stake dividends
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).

- **Childkey take** — share of parent dividends kept by the child for routed validation.
- **Validator take** — delegate commission on delegated-stake dividends.

## Distinction from Delegate

A delegate is a validator that can receive nominated stake. A child hotkey is a worker key the
parent delegate assigns so validation can be signed on one subnet without using the parent hotkey
directly ([Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate),
[Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).

- **Delegate** — stake-holding validator that receives nominated support.
- **Child hotkey** — routed validation worker configured under that delegate on a subnet.

## Distinction from Register

Register is the subnet-entry process that grants a UID slot. A child hotkey must already be
registered on the netuid where it validates
([Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register),
[Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).

- **Register** — subnet-entry process that grants a UID slot.
- **Child hotkey** — registered worker key authorized by a parent on that subnet.

## Distinction from Hotkey

A hotkey is the operational wallet key used for subnet actions. A child hotkey is a separate
authorized key a parent delegate assigns so validation can be signed on one subnet without using the
parent hotkey directly
([Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey),
[Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).

- **Hotkey** — operational key tied to a wallet role.
- **Child hotkey** — authorized worker key under a parent delegate on one subnet.

## Distinction from Netuid

Netuid names which subnet market a configuration applies to. Child hotkey names the authorized
validation key the parent assigns inside that market
([Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid),
[Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).

- **Netuid** — subnet market boundary for the configuration.
- **Child hotkey** — operational key working inside that boundary for a parent delegate.

## Distinction from Chain Rate Limits

Child hotkeys name authorized keys and routed stake shares. Chain rate limits pace how often child
assignments or take percentages may change on chain
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Chain Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits)).

- **Child hotkeys** — authorized validation keys and routed stake shares.
- **Chain rate limits** — pacing rules for updating child routing or take settings.

## Distinction from Subnet Owner Take

A child hotkey is an optional validating key on a subnet such as netuid 1. Subnet owner take is the
fixed protocol share of that subnet's emission paid to its owner
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

- **Child hotkey** — optional worker key earning [[Childkey Take]] on re-delegated stake.
- **Subnet owner take** — fixed emission share paid to the subnet owner.

## Distinction from Stake Weight

Child hotkeys route a parent's stake proportion so a child can validate on its behalf. Stake weight
is the computed influence measure used when validator signals count in subnet consensus
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Glossary: Stake Weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight)).

- **Child hotkeys** — routing which key performs validation with a delegated share.
- **Stake weight** — computed influence after consensus processing.

## Distinction from Yuma Consensus

Child hotkeys name key routing inside a delegate's validation setup. Yuma Consensus converts
submitted validator weights into emission shares on a subnet
([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).

- **Child hotkeys** — optional validating keys authorized by a parent delegate on one subnet.
- **Yuma Consensus** — epoch-boundary settlement from eligible validator weights to emission shares.

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept at a high level. It does not report which child hotkeys a parent
currently has, current take values, current stake proportions, or wallet ownership. Those are live
chain state and should be checked for the relevant netuid with the Bittensor CLI when current data
is needed ([Child Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators/child-hotkeys)).
