ChronoLLM is Bittensor Subnet 38. Its on-chain identity registers the subnet for the competitive
training of chronologically consistent large language models.

## What ChronoLLM Provides

The registered identity describes ChronoLLM as a subnet for the "competitive training of
chronologically consistent Large Language Models." Miners compete to train models under that
objective, and the network rewards the strongest results. The codebase is the
[chronollm/sn38](https://github.com/chronollm/sn38) repository, which defines the training task in
more detail than the on-chain identity alone.

## Temporal Consistency Context

The [ChronoLLM README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chronollm/sn38/main/README.md) frames the
subnet around lookahead bias. In historical analysis, a model can appear more capable than it really
is if it uses facts from after the period being analyzed. A backtest or market-history question is
only meaningful when the model is constrained to what could have been known at that time.

ChronoLLM turns that constraint into the center of the training task. The miner contribution is not
just a fluent language model; it is a model that is useful while respecting a time boundary. This
matters because ordinary language-model quality and chronological discipline are different
properties. A model can write well and still fail the subnet's purpose if it leaks later knowledge
into an earlier setting.

The task therefore sits between language-model training and historical reasoning. It rewards models
that can answer within a bounded information horizon, which is useful for domains where future data
would distort the result. Financial backtesting is the clearest example named by the README, but the
same idea also applies to any analysis where the date of available information changes the answer.

## Information Horizon Context

The [ChronoLLM README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chronollm/sn38/main/README.md) makes the
information horizon the center of the subnet. It describes ordinary language models as unsuitable
for some historical tasks because they may have learned facts from after the period being analyzed.
For a backtest or timeline-sensitive question, later knowledge can make an answer look stronger than
it would have been at the time.

ChronoLLM's source materials answer that problem by tying models to cutoff years. The key reader
point is not the exact submission workflow, but the constraint: a model intended for an earlier year
should not rely on later data. That turns chronological consistency into an evaluation target rather
than a branding phrase.

This also explains why the subnet cannot be judged only by ordinary language-model fluency. A model
could produce a persuasive answer while still failing the task if it leaks future events into an
earlier setting. Conversely, a model that avoids future leakage still needs to answer usefully once
the time boundary is enforced.

For readers, ChronoLLM's useful output is therefore a set of language-model capabilities bounded by
time. The miner contribution is valuable when it preserves the information horizon and still gives
strong answers under that constraint. That makes the subnet different from a general chat or
instruction-following contest, even though both involve language models.

The cutoff-year framing also makes the evaluation boundary easier to state. Validators can ask
whether a response uses information that should have been unavailable for that model's period, then
separately compare the usefulness of answers that stay inside the boundary.

The same boundary keeps the article's evaluation language modest. Chronological consistency is not a
promise that a model can predict the future, and it is not a claim about live market performance. It
means the model should reason using only information that belongs inside the selected historical
window, so later facts do not contaminate the result being evaluated.

## Validation Meaning

Validators in ChronoLLM need to evaluate two things at once. First, they need evidence that a model
is not relying on information from after its intended cutoff. Second, they need to compare whether
the answers remain useful once that time boundary is enforced. Those two checks give the subnet its
shape: temporal discipline prevents future leakage, while quality comparison keeps the competition
from rewarding weak but time-bounded models.

For readers, the important point is that ChronoLLM is not a generic chatbot contest. The subnet asks
whether language models can preserve a timeline while still producing strong answers. A model that
uses later events misses the time-bound task, while a model that avoids leakage but cannot answer
well is not enough either. The resulting validator scores become the weights that flow through
[[Yuma Consensus]].

## Miner and Validator Roles

Subnet 38 operates under the standard Bittensor two-role structure. Miners supply a capability —
here trained language models — and validators evaluate those contributions and set weights. Reward
distribution follows [[Yuma Consensus]].
