# Subnet Miner

A subnet miner is the task-performing role inside a Bittensor subnet. Miners perform the work that
subnet validators evaluate
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

The term belongs to subnet incentive vocabulary. It names the work-producing role in the subnet
flow; validator, scoring, consensus, and emission language describe later parts of that flow. On a
selected [netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid), the role applies only
after [miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration) assigns the
hotkey a [UID slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot) on that subnet.

In plain language, subnet miner answers: who is doing the work that validators score in this subnet?
A miner produces the output; a validator evaluates it; consensus aggregates those evaluations into
miner incentives. The miner role is the entry point in that sequence
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner)).

The number of such miner slots is capped. The mining documentation states that a subnet can hold up
to 192 subnet miner UIDs, alongside up to 64 subnet validator UIDs, for 256 UID slots in total, so
the miner side has a documented ceiling on how many participants a subnet can register at once
([Mining in Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners)).

A miner from one subnet does not automatically perform in another subnet; the role is tied to the
netuid where its UID slot was registered
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

## Development Stage Context

The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)
describes subnet development as moving from localnet to testnet and then mainnet. For subnet miners
on a subnet such as netuid 1, that sequence changes how readers should interpret work examples and
evaluation outcomes.

In localnet, miner role mechanics can be demonstrated in isolation. Local miner examples reflect
local chain configuration rather than production subnet performance.

On testnet, miner work can be observed in a shared, non-production network. Testnet evaluation
outcomes are separate from mainnet subnet state.

On mainnet, subnet miner is the live work-producing role inside production subnet markets on the
connected Bittensor network.

The [Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks) reference
separates mainnet, testnet, and localnet. Miner examples from one environment should not be read as
evidence for production subnet performance in another environment.

## Distinction from Subnet Validator

Subnet miner names the work-producing role inside a subnet. Subnet validator names the evaluating
role that measures that work against the subnet's standards
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Glossary: Subnet Validator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-validator)).

Production and evaluation are complementary sides of the same subnet flow. A miner produces output;
a validator scores it before consensus and emissions processing.

- **Subnet miner** — the work-producing role whose output validators measure.
- **Subnet validator** — the evaluating role that scores miner work.

## Distinction from Subnet Task

Subnet task names the work a mechanism is meant to evaluate. Subnet miner names the role that
performs that work inside the subnet
([Glossary: Subnet Task](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-task),
[Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)).

The miner performs the work. The task defines what that work should be.

- **Subnet miner** — the work-producing role on a subnet.
- **Subnet task** — the work miners are asked to produce.

## Distinction from Subnet Protocol

Subnet protocol names the exchange rules for how validators request work and miners return
responses. Subnet miner names the work-producing role that returns responses within those rules
([Glossary: Subnet Protocol](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-protocol),
[Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)).

The protocol defines how work is exchanged. The miner produces responses within those rules.

- **Subnet miner** — the work-producing role returning responses.
- **Subnet protocol** — rules for requesting and returning work.

## Distinction from Rank

Subnet miner names who produces work on a subnet. Rank is the post-clip stake-weighted aggregate
standing Yuma assigns each miner before incentives are credited—not the normalized emission share
itself ([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Glossary: Rank](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rank),
[Yuma Consensus: Miner emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#miner-emissions)).

Producing useful work is the input. Rank names consensus standing after clipping; miner
[incentives](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentives) credit each miner's
normalized share after that standing is aggregated across the subnet
([Yuma Consensus: Miner emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#miner-emissions)).

- **Subnet miner** — the work-producing role on a subnet.
- **Rank** — post-clip aggregate standing before incentive normalization.

## Distinction from Trust

Subnet miner names who produces work on a subnet. Trust measures how much of a miner's pre-rank
support survived consensus clipping in the same Yuma pass—not the work-producing role itself
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Glossary: Trust](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#trust),
[Yuma Consensus: Clipping](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#clipping)).

- **Subnet miner** — the work-producing role on a subnet.
- **Trust** — clipping survival ratio comparing final rank to pre-rank support.

## Distinction from Incentives

Subnet miner names who produces work. Miner incentives are the credited emission shares normalized
from aggregate rank across the subnet—not the role that produces evaluated work
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Glossary: Incentives](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentives),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

- **Subnet miner** — the work-producing role on a subnet.
- **Incentives** — credited miner emission shares after rank is normalized.

## Distinction from Axon

Subnet miner names the work-producing role. Axon names the server-side endpoint that receives
incoming synapse objects from validator dendrite clients
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Glossary: Axon](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#axon),
[Understanding Neurons](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/neurons#neuron-to-neuron-communication)).

The miner produces work. Axon is the server-side endpoint that receives incoming requests.

- **Subnet miner** — the work-producing role on a subnet.
- **Axon** — server-side endpoint that receives miner-side requests.

## Distinction from Synapse

Subnet miner names the work-producing role. Synapse names the structured message object that carries
challenge and response fields through the axon-dendrite exchange
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Glossary: Synapse](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#synapse)).

The miner fills response fields in synapse objects. Synapse is the structured message for the
exchange.

- **Subnet miner** — the work-producing role on a subnet.
- **Synapse** — structured message object for the exchange.

## Distinction from Pruning Score

A subnet miner and pruning score vocabulary relate as the work-producing role and the
emissions-based displacement shorthand applied when a full subnet must make room for a new
registration. A subnet miner is the participant that performs the work validators evaluate, while
pruning score names documented replacement-order vocabulary used in that roster-exit comparison
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Glossary: Pruning Score](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#pruning-score),
[Mining: Miner deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-deregistration)).

Live deregistration applies that rule by comparing current emissions, immunity status, and
registration-block order on ties rather than relying on a separate maintained pruning-score table
alone. A UID can perform subnet miner work yet still face displacement when its emission standing is
weakest among eligible incumbents once immunity ends
([Mining: Miner deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-deregistration)).

Role vocabulary and displacement-ranking vocabulary also sit apart from Yuma rank and miner
incentives. Rank names post-clip aggregate consensus standing in each pass; pruning score names
roster-exit ordering when registration pressure forces one slot open—not the same layer as tempo
settlement ([Glossary: Rank](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rank),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

- **Subnet miner** — the work-producing role on a subnet.
- **Pruning score** — shorthand for emissions-based displacement ordering on a full subnet.

## Distinction from Mining and Validating

Mining and validating is overview vocabulary for both subnet roles, while subnet miner names the
participant that produces subnet work inside a selected netuid
([Miners](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners),
[Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction)).

- **Mining and validating** — paired role overview for subnet participation.
- **Subnet miner** — the work-producing participant role on one subnet.

## Distinction from Yuma Consensus

Subnet miner names the work-producing participant role on a selected netuid inside a subnet
incentive mechanism. It describes who supplies evaluated work, not the recurring settlement step
that converts validator weight submissions into emission shares
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

Yuma Consensus is the on-chain mechanism that runs at the epoch boundary on each subnet. It reads
the weight matrix from eligible validators, applies clipping and bonding, and converts the result
into miner incentives and validator dividend shares
([Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

Validators score miner work into weights; Yuma settles the consensus outcome from eligible
submissions—not the miner role definition itself
([Glossary: Weight Matrix](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#weight-matrix),
[Weight Copying Problem](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/weight-copying-in-bittensor)).

Readers should treat subnet miner as the work-producing role vocabulary, and Yuma as the
epoch-boundary mechanism that allocates rewards from consensus while validators remain active.

- **Subnet miner** — work-producing role on a selected netuid.
- **Yuma Consensus** — on-chain mechanism that uses validator weights to determine emission shares
  ([Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions)).

## Distinction from Subnet Scoring Model

A subnet miner and the subnet scoring model relate as the producer and the method that grades it. A
subnet miner is the participant that performs the subnet's task and returns work, while the subnet
scoring model is the subnet-defined method validators use to evaluate that work into the weights
that drive rewards
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Glossary: Subnet Scoring Model](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-scoring-model),
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)).

The scoring model belongs to subnet incentive design, not to an individual miner's registration
record. A miner can receive low weights from that grading method without ceasing to be a subnet
miner on the selected netuid
([Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)).

- **Subnet miner** — the participant that performs the task and returns work.
- **Subnet scoring model** — the subnet-defined method that grades that work into weights.

## Reader Boundary

Subnet miner names the work-producing role inside a subnet. It describes the work side of subnet
incentives rather than how-to steps, performance reports, account labels, or claims about a specific
miner's output
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

A specific miner's rank names its post-clip consensus standing; its incentive is the credited
emission share normalized from that standing. Both are consensus-and-network outcomes to read from
live subnet data, not something the subnet miner term itself reports.

Pruning score should not be read as proof of which UID would be displaced, as Yuma rank, or as miner
incentive share. Displacement eligibility comes from live emissions, immunity status, and
registration order on the relevant netuid at observation time on the relevant netuid at observation
time ([Glossary: Pruning Score](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#pruning-score),
[Mining: Miner deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-deregistration)).
