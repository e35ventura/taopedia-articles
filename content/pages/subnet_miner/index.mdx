# Subnet Miner

A subnet miner is the task-performing role inside a Bittensor subnet. Miners perform the work that
subnet validators evaluate
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

The term belongs to subnet incentive vocabulary. It names the role that produces work, not the
validator role that evaluates that work.

## Work Role

The miner role is tied to the subnet's work target. A miner in one subnet can produce a different
kind of output from a miner in another subnet because each subnet can define its own goals and
standards ([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Glossary: Subnet Task](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-task)).

This keeps miner vocabulary scoped to the subnet being discussed. The role is defined by the work a
subnet asks miners to perform.

The role is therefore subnet-specific. Subnet miner describes the producing side of a subnet's
incentive mechanism, while the exact work depends on the task and mechanism selected by that subnet.

## Work Product Versus Incentive Outcome

The subnet miner supplies work that validators later evaluate under the subnet's rules. The
incentive mechanism then connects task, protocol, and evaluation method into a scoring path
([Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism),
[Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner)).

For readers, miner names who produces requested work. It does not by itself define emissions,
weights, or final incentive outcomes.

That distinction keeps work production separate from reward results. Miner output can become part of
the evidence validators evaluate, but the final path depends on scoring, consensus, and emissions
logic.

## Relationship to Validators

Subnet miners and subnet validators form the core work-and-evaluation relationship inside a subnet.
Miners provide work, and validators measure that work against the subnet's standards
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Glossary: Subnet Validator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-validator)).

The distinction is role-based. Subnet miner names the producing side; subnet validator names the
evaluating side.

This work-and-evaluation split is the center of mining and validating language. The miner role
produces candidate outputs; the validator role measures those outputs and turns evaluation into
signals for consensus.

## Relationship to Subnet Task

A subnet miner is the role performing the work, while the subnet task is the work target itself. The
glossary connects subnet tasks to the work miners perform, and the incentive-mechanism documentation
places task design inside the broader subnet mechanism
([Glossary: Subnet Task](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-task),
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)).

Keeping those terms separate makes the article easier to read: the miner is the role, and the task
describes what that role is expected to produce.

Task language also explains why miner work is not interchangeable across subnets. A miner claim is
clearest when it identifies the task or mechanism that defines the work being evaluated.

## Multiple Mechanisms

Some subnets can run multiple incentive mechanisms in parallel. In that case, miner work and
validator evaluation belong to the mechanism being discussed
([Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms)).

This prevents a result in one mechanism from being treated as a complete statement about miner work
across every mechanism in the subnet.

Mechanism-specific language matters because a subnet can contain more than one path for work,
evaluation, and incentive allocation. A miner example from one mechanism belongs to that mechanism's
task and evaluation path.

## Network Examples

The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)
describes subnet development as moving from localnet to testnet and then mainnet. The subnet miner
role applies across the Bittensor lifecycle: miners can operate in localnet for isolated
development, testnet for shared non-production testing, and mainnet for live operation with real
emissions.

The [Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks) reference
separates mainnet, testnet, and localnet. Miner examples or emission outcomes from one environment
are not evidence for production subnet performance in another environment.

Localnet examples are isolated development examples. Testnet examples are shared non-production
examples. Mainnet miner interpretation concerns live subnet operation, production miner output, and
the incentive mechanism actually running on that subnet.

## Reading Miner Claims

Subnet miner names the work-producing role inside a subnet. It describes who produces requested
work, separate from how-to steps, performance reports, account labels, or claims about a specific
miner's output
([Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

The stable point is that a subnet miner performs the work a subnet asks validators to evaluate.
Subnet validator, subnet task, scoring model, incentive mechanism, and network-environment language
describe adjacent parts of the same system.
