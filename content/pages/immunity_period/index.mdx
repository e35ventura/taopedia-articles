# Immunity Period

An immunity period is a temporary protection window after a miner or validator enters a Bittensor
subnet. The glossary defines it as the period during which new subnet entrants are protected from
deregistration for poor performance
([Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)).

In plain language, immunity period answers: for how long after registration is a new subnet entrant
protected from being replaced by a stronger candidate? During that window, low performance does not
trigger automatic deregistration
([Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)).

For a reader, immunity period is not a grace period for reward collection. It protects against
removal, not against low emission outcomes. A new entrant with poor performance during immunity
still earns according to their subnet results; the protection is specifically against the
replacement mechanism that removes low performers to free up UID slots
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

The term is a timing concept, not a performance score. It says that ordinary low-performance removal
is temporarily limited for a new subnet entrant; it does not say the entrant has already produced
strong results.

Because immunity is measured in blocks while a subnet's consensus cycle length is its tempo, the
protection window usually spans several epochs rather than aligning to one epoch boundary. When
immunity lasts longer than one tempo, a new entrant stays shielded across multiple consensus cycles
before ordinary deregistration rules apply; when it is shorter than one tempo, protection may end
within a single epoch
([Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters),
[Glossary: Tempo](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tempo),
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)).

## Documented Default

The subnet hyperparameters reference lists `immunity_period` as a `u16` value with a documented
default of 5000 blocks. It is owner-settable through the `sudo_set_immunity_period` extrinsic
(`btcli sudo set --param immunity_period`). That figure is only the documented starting point: the
value actually in force is per-subnet chain state and can differ from the default
([Subnet Hyperparameters: ImmunityPeriod](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#immunityperiod)).

## Distinction from Activity Cutoff

Immunity period and activity cutoff address different timing problems. Immunity period is the grace
window that protects a newly registered neuron from deregistration for a number of blocks. Activity
cutoff vocabulary names how long a validator may remain without an on-chain update before its stake
is masked inactive for consensus—not a post-registration displacement shield
([Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period),
[Subnet Hyperparameters: Activity Cutoff](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#activitycutoff),
[Subnet Hyperparameters: Activity Cutoff Factor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#activitycutofffactor)).

On dynamic-tempo subnets the fixed block hyperparameter is deprecated; the live inactivity span
comes from activity cutoff factor and tempo instead
([Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)).

- **Immunity period** — blocks a new neuron is shielded from deregistration.
- **Activity cutoff** — inactivity span after which a stale validator is masked out of consensus.

## Distinction from Deregistration

Immunity period is the block-measured protection window after registration. Deregistration removes
an eligible participant when subnet capacity and replacement rules require it
([Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period),
[Glossary: Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#deregistration)).

Protection vocabulary delays ordinary removal for a new entrant; deregistration vocabulary names the
displacement event once eligibility rules apply after the window ends
([Mining: Miner deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-deregistration)).

## Distinction from Subnet Creator

Immunity period is an owner-settable hyperparameter that fixes how long a new UID is protected from
deregistration. The subnet creator is the role that defines a subnet and sets its hyperparameters
([Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period),
[Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator)).

Protection length is therefore a per-subnet setting at the creator's discretion rather than a fixed
network constant
([Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)).

## Distinction from Active UID

Immunity period is the block window that exempts a newly registered UID from deregistration. Active
UID names whether a slot currently satisfies activity conditions on the metagraph
([Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period),
[Glossary: Active UID](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#active-uid)).

The immunity window is block-counted and expires; active-UID status can change with participation
and scores without sharing that fixed grace duration.

## Distinction from Register

Register is the subnet-entry action that grants the UID slot where immunity timing begins. Immunity
period names how long ordinary deregistration protection lasts after that entry
([Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register),
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)).

Entry vocabulary names admission; immunity vocabulary names the post-entry protection window on the
same netuid.

## Distinction from Pruning Score

Pruning score ranks which UID is removed first when a full subnet accepts a new registration.
Immunity period temporarily exempts a newly registered UID from that removal ranking
([Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period),
[Mining: Miner deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-deregistration)).

During immunity, a low pruning score does not trigger displacement. After the configured block
window ends, ordinary ranking rules resume
([Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#immunityperiod)).

This exemption is conditional rather than absolute. On-chain pruning prefers the lowest-ranked
non-immune neuron, ordered by emission, then earliest registration block, then lowest UID, but it
does so only while the count of non-immune UIDs stays above the subnet's minimum non-immune floor.
When too few non-immune UIDs remain, selection falls back to the lowest-ranked immune neuron, so a
UID can be pruned inside its immunity window; subnet-owner immortal hotkeys are never selected
([`get_neuron_to_prune`](https://github.com/opentensor/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/subnets/registration.rs),
[`get_min_non_immune_uids`](https://github.com/opentensor/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/utils/misc.rs)).

## Distinction from Commit Reveal

Commit reveal guidance recommends keeping immunity period longer than the concealed-weight window
measured in blocks. Immunity period protects newly registered miners during that hidden-weight
interval on the same subnet
([Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal),
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)).

Weight-disclosure timing and post-entry removal protection are separate hyperparameter comparisons.
Both should be cited with the netuid whose settings are being read
([Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)).

## Distinction from UID Slot

A UID slot is the subnet position registration grants. Immunity period is the grace window that
begins after that slot is occupied
([Glossary: UID Slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot),
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)).

Slot vocabulary names the position; immunity vocabulary names how long ordinary deregistration
protection lasts once the entrant holds that position on the selected netuid.

## Distinction from Subnet Deregistration

Subnet deregistration is the removal mechanism, while an immunity period is the protection that
delays it. The documentation describes an immunity window that keeps a newly created subnet out of
the removal comparison while it establishes price history
([Subnet Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-deregistration),
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)).

- **Subnet deregistration** — the rule that frees a slot when the cap is full.
- **Immunity period** — the window that keeps a new subnet out of the removal comparison.

## Distinction from Last Update

Immunity period names post-registration protection from displacement. Last update measures whether a
neuron has updated recently enough to count as active
([Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period),
[Glossary: Last Update](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#last-update)).

- **Immunity period** — block-counted grace window after registration.
- **Last update** — recency signal for ongoing activity requirements.

Entry-timing vocabulary and activity-recency vocabulary answer different participation questions
([Understanding Neurons](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/neurons)).

## Distinction from Yuma Consensus

Immunity period names the post-registration block window that protects a new UID from displacement.
That lifecycle-protection vocabulary is separate from the tempo pass that converts validator weight
submissions into emission shares
([Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period),
[Mining: Immunity period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#immunity-period)).

Yuma Consensus still aggregates submitted weights and credits miner incentives and validator
dividends when the epoch closes on the netuid
([Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

Pruning and deregistration can displace miners after immunity ends. Those roster events are not the
same vocabulary as the recurring settlement pass that reads evaluation signals
([Mining: Miner deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-deregistration)).

- **Immunity period** — post-registration protection from pruning or displacement.
- **Yuma Consensus** — on-chain mechanism that uses validator weights to determine emission shares
  ([Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions)).

## Distinction from Miner Registration

Immunity period names post-registration protection from displacement. Miner registration names the
initial entry step into a subnet UID slot
([Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period),
[Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)).

- **Immunity period** — grace window after a UID is obtained.
- **Miner registration** — entry step that obtains the UID slot.

Protection-window vocabulary and entry-action vocabulary sit at different lifecycle stages
([Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register)).

## Distinction from Validator Deregistration

Immunity period and validator deregistration are opposite ends of a neuron's lifecycle. The immunity
period is the grace window after registration during which a UID cannot be pruned for a low score,
while validator deregistration is the removal that can occur once that protection lapses and the
neuron ranks below the deregistration threshold
([Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period),
[Validating: Validator deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators#validator-deregistration),
[Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#immunityperiod)).

Mining documentation applies the same block-counted immunity window to newly registered miners and
validators on a netuid. Validator deregistration vocabulary names the post-grace removal step once
that shared protection ends, not a separate immunity rule for validators alone
([Mining: Immunity period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#immunity-period),
[Mining: Miner deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-deregistration)).

- **Immunity period** — the post-registration window that blocks pruning.
- **Validator deregistration** — the removal that can follow once immunity lapses.

## Reader Boundary

Immunity period should not be read as permanent protection, validator immunity, or proof that a UID
remains active. It names a block-counted grace window after subnet registration
([Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period),
[Mining: Immunity period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#immunity-period)).
