# Nominator

A nominator is a TAO holder who stakes with a validator in Bittensor delegation vocabulary
([Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

The term names the supplying side of the staking relationship. It should be read separately from the
validator role that receives delegated stake and performs validation work.

## Delegation Role

In Bittensor staking vocabulary, a nominator supplies TAO support to a validator. A delegate is the
receiving validator role, while a nominator is the holder role supplying delegated stake
([Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate),
[Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator)).

This keeps the relationship clear: nominator names the TAO-holder role, nominate names the staking
action, and delegate names the receiving validator role.

The role boundary is important because a nominator does not become the validator by supplying TAO.
The validator remains the delegate-side actor, while the nominator remains the holder-side actor
whose stake supports that validator
([Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate),
[Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator)).

This also separates role vocabulary from amount vocabulary. Nominator identifies who supplied
support; stake and effective stake describe amounts or totals associated with the staking
relationship.

## Nominate and Stake Context

Nominate is the action vocabulary for staking TAO with a validator. Nominator is the role label for
the holder associated with that action
([Glossary: Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate),
[Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator)).

Stake is the related amount vocabulary. Delegated TAO contributes to the validator's stake context,
while the nominator term identifies who supplied that support
([Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

Effective stake is nearby but broader than the nominator label. It can describe the total staked TAO
associated with a delegate after self-stake and nominated support are considered, while nominator
continues to name the holder role on the support side
([Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake),
[Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator)).

That distinction keeps the article from turning a role word into a total. A nominator can supply
some of the stake context around a validator, but the nominator term itself is not the complete
stake total.

## Emissions and Dividends

Nominators sit in the broader staking-emissions picture through delegated stake. Emissions
documentation distinguishes validator dividends and staking roles from miner incentives
([Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

That does not make nominator a reward quote or validator ranking term. It names the role that
supplies delegated TAO support; dividends are an outcome term in the emission system.

Dividend language should therefore stay attached to the emission context. Nominator identifies the
supporting holder role, while dividends describe an outcome category in the staking-emission model
([Glossary: Dividends](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#dividends),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

This avoids implying that the word nominator alone proves a specific dividend amount, validator
result, or future reward path. Those statements need their own source and context.

## Scope Boundaries

Nominator should not be collapsed into delegate. The two terms describe opposite sides of a staking
relationship: the nominator supplies support, and the delegate receives support as the validator
role ([Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator),
[Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate)).

The role label also does not by itself identify a subnet, validator, balance, or reward outcome. A
more specific statement should include that context when it matters.

Nominator should also stay separate from delegate stake. Delegate stake is the delegate's
self-supplied TAO, while nominated stake comes from the supporting holder side
([Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake),
[Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator)).

For precise prose, use nominator when the focus is the supporting holder role, nominate when the
focus is the action of staking with a validator, and delegate when the focus is the receiving
validator role.

## Environment Scope

Bittensor separates localnet, testnet, and mainnet environments. Nominator examples from one
environment should not be treated as evidence for another because validators, balances, emissions,
and staking relationships are environment-specific
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).

Localnet examples are isolated development examples. Testnet examples are shared non-production
examples. Mainnet nominator examples concern production delegation relationships and production TAO.

The term can be explained across environments, but concrete validator sets and stake relationships
belong to the environment being discussed. A local example can illustrate the role boundary, while a
mainnet statement needs support from mainnet context
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).
