# Multiple Incentive Mechanisms

Multiple incentive mechanisms describe the case where one Bittensor subnet contains more than one
separate evaluation mechanism. Each mechanism has its own evaluation system and bond pool for Yuma
Consensus calculations
([Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms),
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)).

The term is narrower than incentive mechanism by itself. An incentive mechanism connects work,
evaluation, and rewards; multiple incentive mechanisms name the structure where several such systems
coexist inside one subnet
([Glossary: Incentive Mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentive-mechanism),
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)).

## Subnet Container

Multiple mechanisms still live inside one subnet. The subnet provides the shared market setting,
while each mechanism provides a separate evaluation frame within that subnet
([Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

This keeps the term from meaning multiple subnets. The phrase points to several evaluation systems
inside one subnet, not to a collection of independent subnet markets.

That container distinction matters for claims about work quality and rewards. A result belongs to
the mechanism that produced it, while the subnet remains the shared market around those mechanisms
([Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)).

That also separates multiple mechanisms from ordinary subnet comparison. The comparison is inside
one subnet, between mechanisms, rather than between independent subnet markets
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

## Evaluation Separation

The core idea is separation by mechanism. Documentation describes validators evaluating miners
separately for each mechanism, so an evaluation result belongs to the mechanism that produced it
([Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms),
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)).

That separation matters when reading ratings, weights, or work quality. A result from one mechanism
does not automatically describe another mechanism just because both mechanisms belong to the same
subnet.

## Bond Pool Context

The glossary ties multiple mechanisms to separate bond pools. Yuma Consensus uses validator weight
signals when calculating miner incentives and validator dividends, and the separate bond-pool frame
keeps those calculations divided by mechanism
([Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms)).

The bond-pool detail is structural. It explains why the mechanism split can affect how evaluation
signals are interpreted before rewards are assigned.

## Yuma Consensus

Yuma Consensus is the aggregation process that turns validator weight information into miner-side
and validator-side outcomes. Multiple incentive mechanisms do not replace that consensus process;
they separate the inputs and bond-pool frame used for each mechanism
([Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)).

The mechanism tells which evaluation frame is in use; Yuma Consensus describes the aggregation that
follows from the signals in that frame.

This keeps the shared consensus step visible without flattening the mechanism-specific inputs.

## Emission Flow

Emission documentation gives the broader reward flow for Bittensor, while the multiple-mechanism
references explain why evaluation and bond-pool frames can be separated inside a subnet
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)).

That makes emission claims more precise. The multiple-mechanism term supports structural claims
about separated evaluation systems, while emission documentation explains how reward flows are
allocated through the wider tokenomics process.

## Network Examples

Bittensor documentation separates localnet, testnet, and mainnet environments
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).
Examples of multiple mechanisms keep that environment attached because local testing, testnet
behavior, and mainnet behavior provide different kinds of evidence.

The concept is stable across those environments: one subnet can contain more than one evaluation
mechanism. A concrete result from one environment still belongs to that environment.

## Reading Multiple Mechanisms

Multiple incentive mechanisms are subnet-internal evaluation structure. The term does not by itself
say how many mechanisms any particular subnet has, which mechanism is preferable, or how rewards
were distributed in a specific case
([Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms),
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)).

The stable reference point is separation: several evaluation mechanisms can exist inside one subnet,
with their own evaluation systems and bond pools.

## Miner Emissions Combine Each Epoch

Official multiple-mechanism documentation states that each miner receives
[emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#emission) separately from each
[incentive mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentive-mechanism). A
miner's performance in one mechanism does not affect their rating in another, and their emissions
for each [epoch](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#epoch) are summed across all
mechanisms
([Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms)).

That payout shape separates per-mechanism evaluation from the miner-facing total. Strong results in
one mechanism contribute to one part of the sum without automatically lifting or lowering the
miner's standing in another mechanism's evaluation frame.

The same page adds a comparative note: a miner who excels in one mechanism but performs poorly in
others can still receive more total emissions than a miner who performs moderately across all
mechanisms, depending on emission proportions and relative performance. Miner-side totals therefore
reflect both per-mechanism scores and how the subnet divides emissions among its mechanisms.

References:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms)

## Validators Must Cover Every Mechanism

On a multi-mechanism subnet, the same
[subnet validators](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-validator) participate
in every incentive mechanism inside that subnet. Official guidance states that validators receive
validator-side rewards as a weighted sum of their performance across all incentive mechanisms, they
cannot choose which mechanisms to validate, and if they do not validate all mechanisms they receive
proportionally reduced [emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#emission)
([Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).

The same documentation also notes that all validators participate in all incentive mechanisms with
identical [stake weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight), rather
than requiring separate validator registration per mechanism. Validators must also set
[weight matrix](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#weight-matrix) inputs
independently for each mechanism, because each mechanism maintains its own validator-weight
collection for consensus calculations
([Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Weight Matrix](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#weight-matrix)).

That participation rule ties validator rewards to complete coverage of the subnet's evaluation
frames. A validator-side reading should account for whether validation work reached every mechanism,
not only the mechanism that produced the most visible weight signals.

References:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Weight Matrix](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#weight-matrix)

## Subnet Emission Total Stays Fixed

Multiple incentive mechanisms do not increase the total
[emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#emission) assigned to a
[subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet). Official documentation states
that they do not change the total emissions to a subnet, but create a way for
[subnet creators](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator) to distribute
those emissions to miners working on different tasks
([Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator)).

The
[Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms)
adds that the feature lets subnet creators distribute emissions across different types of work or
evaluation criteria, with transparent on-chain visibility of mechanism configurations and emission
flow
([Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

Emission proportions among mechanisms are set by the subnet creator and remain visible on-chain, so
the split is an internal allocation choice inside a fixed subnet total rather than an expansion of
network-wide reward supply.
