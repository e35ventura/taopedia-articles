# Multiple Incentive Mechanisms

Multiple incentive mechanisms let a Bittensor subnet distribute its emissions across more than one
independent evaluation system. Each mechanism runs its own [[Yuma Consensus]] path with separate
bond pools and weight matrices, while miners, validators, and stakers still participate through one
subnet registration. The official
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)
documentation describes this as a way for subnet creators to apportion emissions across different
tasks without registering separate subnets.

## Why Subnets Use Multiple Mechanisms

Historically, each subnet operated with a single [[Incentive Mechanism]]. Multiple mechanisms extend
that model so a subnet can reward different kinds of work inside one competitive market. The subnet
creator can set what share of miner and validator emissions each mechanism receives, and that
allocation is visible on-chain.

Reference:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)

## What Stays Shared

Several subnet roles do not split when mechanisms are added. The same miners and validators
participate across mechanisms, validators keep the same stake weight in each mechanism, and the
subnet's total emissions do not increase. Multiple mechanisms only redistribute emissions internally
among evaluation paths.

Reference:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)

## What Stays Separate

Each incentive mechanism maintains its own bond pools, [[Weight Matrix]], and incentive accounting.
A miner's performance in one mechanism does not change their rating in another, and total miner
emissions for an epoch are the sum across mechanisms where they participate. Validators must set
weights independently for each mechanism and evaluate miners separately under each mechanism's
criteria.

References:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms)

## Emission Split Control

The subnet creator controls how total subnet emissions are divided among mechanisms through on-chain
configuration. When the mechanism count changes, the distribution resets to an even split until the
creator sets custom proportions again. The official documentation explains how those proportions are
expressed and why they must total the full configured range when custom values are submitted.

Reference:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)

## Validator Dividends

Validator dividends become a weighted sum of performance across all mechanisms. A validator cannot
choose to validate only one mechanism inside the subnet: if they do not evaluate all mechanisms,
their emissions are reduced proportionally. This keeps subnet participation unified while still
requiring separate assessments per mechanism.

Reference:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)

## Capacity and Mechanism Limits

Before increasing the mechanism count, the product of the new mechanism count and the subnet's
maximum UID capacity must stay within the protocol limit described in the official documentation.
The same source also notes a current runtime cap on how many mechanisms a subnet may run, with plans
to raise that cap later. Readers should treat those limits as live protocol parameters rather than
fixed article constants.

References:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)

## Example on Subnet 109

[[Subnet 109: Academia]] is a published Taopedia example of a subnet that uses multiple incentive
mechanisms under netuid 109. Its article describes separate research-and-design and
prototype-and-arena tracks that compete inside the same subnet, with emissions flowing through
[[Yuma Consensus]] according to measured performance in each track. Live SN109 subnet data is
available on [TaoStats](https://taostats.io/subnets/109).

References: [Subnet 109 on TaoStats](https://taostats.io/subnets/109),
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)

## Metagraph Context

Multiple mechanisms extend [[Metagraph]] data with per-mechanism weight and incentive columns. Each
mechanism therefore has its own weight-matrix input to Yuma Consensus, rather than one shared matrix
for every task the subnet supports. Readers should treat mechanism-specific weights and incentives
as parallel accounting paths inside the same subnet.

References:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Weight Matrix](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#weight-matrix)

## Backward Compatibility

Existing subnets continue with a single mechanism by default. Unless a subnet owner adds more
mechanisms, all emissions stay on that default path, which preserves current subnet behavior while
allowing optional expansion.

Reference:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)

## Careful Interpretation

Multiple incentive mechanisms are a subnet-design tool, not a guarantee of better miner rewards or
validator performance. Exact mechanism counts, emission splits, evaluation rules, and operational
details belong in current official documentation and on-chain state for the subnet in question.

Reference:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)
