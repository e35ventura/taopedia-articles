# Emission

Emission is Bittensor's process for creating and allocating TAO and subnet-specific alpha tokens. It
covers value entering subnet economic settings and value reaching miners, validators, stakers, and
subnet creators through protocol allocation flow. It is the process around role-facing reward terms,
not a synonym for any single reward label.

References: [Emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Glossary: Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#emission)

## Injection and Distribution

Bittensor emission vocabulary separates injection from distribution. Injection concerns value
entering subnet economic settings, while distribution concerns how that value is allocated across
miners, validators, stakers, and subnet creators.

That distinction keeps emission from being read as one balance, one role outcome, or one subnet
ranking. A miner incentive, validator dividend, staking-related outcome, or creator allocation is a
narrower result inside the larger creation-and-allocation process.

References:
[Emission: Injection and distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#injection-and-distribution-two-stages-of-the-emissions-process),
[Glossary: Incentives](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentives),
[Glossary: Dividends](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#dividends)

## TAO and Alpha Context

Emission in Bittensor involves both TAO and subnet-specific alpha tokens. TAO reserve injection and
alpha reserve injection are separate parts of the model, and the alpha side belongs to the
subnet-specific token side.

This framing matters for tokenomics articles. A TAO-only explanation can miss the subnet pool side
of Dynamic TAO, while an alpha-only explanation can miss the network asset entering subnet liquidity
settings.

References:
[TAO reserve injection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#tao-reserve-injection),
[Alpha reserve injection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#alpha-reserve-injection),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## Consensus Context

Yuma Consensus turns validator evaluations into allocation outcomes. Validators submit weights,
consensus filters and aggregates those signals, and the result affects miner incentives and
validator dividends inside the subnet's emission flow.

Consensus score and validator-miner bonds belong near this process because they shape how
evaluations move through consensus. They do not replace the word emission: emission is the
value-creation and allocation process around those consensus signals.

References: [Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Glossary: Validator Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-weights),
[Glossary: Consensus Score](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#consensus-score),
[Glossary: Validator-Miner Bonds](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-miner-bonds)

## Supply Context

Emission is related to issuance and halving but is not the same term. Issuance concerns circulating
TAO supply, while halving concerns scheduled reductions in emission rate. Emission names the ongoing
process that creates and allocates value before those broader supply concepts are interpreted.

This keeps supply vocabulary and role-allocation vocabulary separate. Emission can affect the
broader tokenomics picture without becoming a substitute for issuance, halving, incentives, or
dividends.

References: [Glossary: Issuance](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#issuance),
[Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving)

## Reading Emission

Emission is a reference term for Bittensor token creation and allocation. It is not a staking
recommendation, subnet ranking table, market forecast, or role-specific operating guide. The direct
reading is process-first: emission names creation and allocation before a statement narrows to a
miner incentive, validator dividend, staking outcome, reserve injection, or supply metric.

Network context also matters. Bittensor separates localnet, testnet, and mainnet environments, so
example amounts and role outcomes need the selected subnet, environment, epoch, and relevant source.

References: [Emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Glossary: Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#emission),
[Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)

## Dynamic TAO Flow

In Dynamic TAO, emission connects network-level TAO allocation with subnet-level alpha distribution.
TAO reserve injection and alpha reserve injection describe pool-side growth, while distribution
describes role-facing rewards.

That creates two emission layers. The first layer is network-level: TAO is allocated into subnet
reserve settings. The second layer is subnet-level: subnet alpha tokens move through incentive,
dividend, and staking-related reward vocabulary.

Protocol alpha is one nearby category. During reserve-injection activity, the protocol can
accumulate alpha cached by subnet rather than placing all alpha into ordinary distribution. Protocol
alpha is therefore an emission-side category that sits alongside the standard distribution flow.

Dynamic TAO emission is not just about TAO supply. It involves the relationship between TAO reserve
flow and subnet alpha production, which is why emission vocabulary separates injection and
distribution stages rather than treating the process as a single token release.

References: [Emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Dynamic TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#dynamic-tao),
[Emission: Injection and distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#injection-and-distribution-two-stages-of-the-emissions-process),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)

## Net TAO Flow Compares Subnets Before Injection

Official [Emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions) documentation describes a
flow-based model for deciding how network-level TAO reserve injection is shared across subnets. The
comparison uses net TAO movement from staking minus unstaking into each subnet's pool, not a single
static ranking of subnet reserves.

That model belongs to the cross-subnet injection stage rather than to within-subnet reward tables.
Before miners, validators, or stakers receive role-facing outcomes, subnets are compared on
sustained demand signals drawn from those net flows. Short bursts therefore matter less than the
smoothed trend the documentation attaches to that comparison.

The same page notes that those flow signals are smoothed so one block of activity does not dominate
subnet share. Emission vocabulary here stays at the allocation-comparison layer: flow-based
emissions name how TAO injection shares are set across subnets, while
[Glossary: Incentives](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentives) and
[Glossary: Dividends](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#dividends) name narrower
results inside a subnet after consensus has run.

References:
[Emission: Distribution Across Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution-across-subnets),
[Flow-Based Emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#flow-based-emissions)

## Staker Rewards Follow Validator Allocation

Within-subnet distribution does not stop at miner and validator outcomes. Official
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution) documentation
states that after Yuma Consensus determines validator-side and miner-side shares,
[Glossary: TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao) and
[Glossary: Alpha Tokens](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-tokens) are also
emitted to stakers in proportion to their support of the relevant validator positions.

That makes staker-facing emission a separate distribution path from validator dividends. Dividends
name the validator-side consensus result tied to bonds and miner outcomes, while staker emission
names the portion credited to nominators through delegation after those validator shares are known
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

The documentation also separates root-subnet staking from alpha-subnet staking on the payout unit.
Root-subnet [Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake) receives
TAO-denominated staker emission, while stake on an alpha subnet receives alpha-denominated staker
emission
([Understanding Subnets: Root subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets#root-subnet)).
Emission therefore reaches stakers through delegation proportions, but the asset label follows the
subnet context where the stake is held.

References: [Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Subnet Creators Share the Distribution Stage

Distribution vocabulary covers more than miners, validators, and stakers. The same official
distribution section lists subnet owners alongside those roles when describing who can receive
emitted value after injection and consensus have progressed
([Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

That keeps the
[Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator) role
inside emission flow rather than outside it. A subnet creator still defines task and evaluation
context upstream, but emission documentation also treats the creator as a recipient category in the
distribution stage rather than as a separate tokenomics label.

Alpha-side accounting follows the same participant list. Official material describes alpha set aside
outside the pool as value pending distribution to miners, validators, stakers, and the subnet owner
before those balances reach role-facing outcomes
([Glossary: Alpha Outstanding](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-outstanding)).
Creator allocation therefore sits beside other role outcomes inside one distribution picture instead
of replacing miner incentives, validator dividends, or staker delegation vocabulary.

References:
[Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)
