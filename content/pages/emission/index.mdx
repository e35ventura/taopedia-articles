# Emission

Emission is Bittensor's term for protocol reward distribution. The official glossary defines
emission as newly distributed token rewards, and Bittensor emission documentation describes how TAO
is distributed through the network's incentive system.

## TAO Issuance Context

Emission is closely related to issuance, but the terms are not identical. Issuance describes supply
being created or brought into circulation. Emission focuses on how protocol rewards are distributed
after that reward supply is allocated. In Bittensor, readers most often encounter the term when
studying how TAO rewards flow through subnet and validator/miner incentive logic.

## Miner Incentives and Validator Dividends

Bittensor emission is not a simple wallet transfer. In subnet context, miner incentives and
validator dividends are reward outcomes. Validator evaluations, submitted weights, and consensus
outcomes help determine how those rewards are split among participants. This is why emission appears
near articles about incentives, dividends, validator weights, and Yuma Consensus.

## Difference from Transfers

A transfer moves existing TAO from one wallet to another. Emission describes protocol-level reward
distribution. Keeping those ideas separate helps readers understand why a reward event, a staking
event, and a user-initiated transfer are different token movements even though all may involve TAO.

## Source Notes

This article defines the term at a high level. Exact reward amounts, timing, and live subnet shares
depend on current chain state and should be checked from current Bittensor sources.
