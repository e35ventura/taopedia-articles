# Bonds Moving Average

A bonds moving average is the smoothing that Yuma Consensus applies to validator bonds so that a
bond reflects supported behavior over time rather than a single moment. It is a way of reading
bonds, not a separate quantity that sits beside them
([Yuma Consensus: Bonding mechanics](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#bonding-mechanics)).

Bittensor documentation describes the moving average of bonds and notes that higher bonds yield
higher dividends for validators. The smoothing is what connects a validator's recent consensus
alignment to the bond value that those dividends depend on
([Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#bondsmovingaverage),
[Yuma Consensus: Bonding mechanics](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#bonding-mechanics)).

## A Bond Is Not the Same as Its Smoothing

A bond is the weight a validator builds up toward a miner it has supported, and that accumulated
weight is what later contributes to the validator's dividends
([Glossary: Validator-Miner Bonds](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-miner-bonds),
[Yuma Consensus: Bonding mechanics](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#bonding-mechanics)).

Smoothing does not create that weight. It decides how quickly the bond value follows new consensus
outcomes instead of carrying forward what came before. The bond is the quantity; the moving average
describes how fast that quantity is allowed to move.

## Why Smoothing Bonds Helps

Consensus outcomes can shift from one round to the next, and an unsmoothed bond would swing with
every shift. Averaging a bond over a window lets it express a validator's sustained alignment rather
than a momentary one, which is why the bond that feeds dividends is read as a moving average rather
than a single latest value
([Yuma Consensus: Bonding mechanics](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#bonding-mechanics)).

The trade-off has two ends. Smoothing a bond over a longer span makes it steadier but slower to
react, while smoothing over a shorter span makes it quicker to react but more sensitive to
short-lived changes. A claim about bond smoothing is clearest when it says which of those two ends
it means.

## How Smoothing Reaches Dividends

The reason smoothing matters is that bonds feed validator rewards. Documentation ties higher bonds
to higher dividends, and the bonding mechanics explain that consensus-aligned support is what
accrues into a bond in the first place
([Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#bondsmovingaverage),
[Yuma Consensus: Bonding mechanics](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#bonding-mechanics)).

Because dividends follow bonds, the pace of bond smoothing also paces how dividends respond.
Steadier bonds let a validator's earned position persist through brief fluctuations; quicker bonds
let that position re-form sooner around new outcomes. In neither case does the smoothing change the
consensus rule that decided the outcome.

## Consensus Alignment Comes First

Smoothing works on top of consensus, not around it. Yuma Consensus penalizes bonds that fall out of
consensus, so what is being averaged is a value whose direction is already constrained by alignment
with the network's agreed result
([Yuma Consensus: Penalizing out-of-consensus bonds](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#penalizing-out-of-consensus-bonds)).

That ordering keeps bond smoothing in scope. It affects how fast an aligned bond moves, but it does
not let a validator avoid the consequences of weight copying or of submitting weights that sit
outside the consensus the network reaches
([Glossary: Weight Copying](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#weight-copying)).

## Reading Bonds Moving Average Correctly

The way bonds are smoothed can differ between subnets, so a bonds moving average should not be read
as one fixed behavior across all of Bittensor. The stable idea is that bonds are averaged over time;
how quickly a particular subnet's bonds respond belongs with that subnet's documentation
([Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)).

Read it, then, as the pace at which validator bonds track new consensus results inside Yuma
Consensus. It is not a bond, a dividend formula, or a substitute for consensus. It is what keeps a
bond a reflection of sustained behavior rather than a single moment
([Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#bondsmovingaverage)).
