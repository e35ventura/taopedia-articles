AdTAO is Bittensor Subnet 21, operated by [PPC Rebel](https://www.ppcrebel.com/), a Google Ads
management firm. The subnet's purpose is to turn counterfactual impact prediction for advertising
campaigns into a competitive, verifiable market: miners submit probabilistic forecasts about how a
given advertising intervention will affect a campaign's performance, and they are rewarded strictly
on the accuracy of those forecasts once real outcomes are measured. Full protocol details are
published in the
[AdTAO whitepaper](https://github.com/ippcteam/SN21-adtao/blob/main/docs/whitepaper.md).

The subnet was designed around two hard properties: predictions are cryptographically bound to the
miner that produced them before the outcome can possibly be known, and validator scoring is
independently reproducible by any third party with access to the chain. This architecture makes it
impossible to backdate a prediction or silently alter a score after the fact, since every step is
anchored by on-chain cryptographic commitments.

## How the Mechanism Works

The subnet operates in weekly cycles called episodes. At the start of each episode, the operator
publishes a batch of prediction problems drawn from real Google Ads account data. Each problem is a
structured snapshot of an ad account at a moment in time — including recent campaign performance,
the action being applied (such as a budget change, bid strategy switch, or target adjustment), and
60 days of prior history. The operator commits a cryptographic digest of this data on chain at the
moment of release, so the problem set is fixed and tamper-evident.

Miners receive these episodes and must submit probabilistic distributions — specifically P10, P50,
and P90 quantile estimates — over the campaign's performance change across 7-day and 14-day
horizons. Submissions are encrypted with AES-GCM and the encryption key is itself locked using
timelock encryption tied to a future [drand](https://drand.love/) randomness beacon round. The
ciphertext hash, the locked key, and the archive URL where the ciphertext lives are all committed on
chain before the outcome is knowable. This construction guarantees that a miner cannot observe the
outcome and then claim credit for predicting it.

After the timelock expires and outcomes are measured, validators fetch miners' encrypted
predictions, decrypt them using the released drand key, and run a standardized open-source scoring
procedure. Scoring combines four components: quantile accuracy, measured by pinball loss and the
Continuous Ranked Probability Score, makes up 50% of the score; calibration — whether the intervals
produced by the miner's P10/P50/P90 actually contain outcomes at the stated rates — makes up 20%;
directional accuracy (whether the miner correctly predicted the sign of the change in the primary
goal metric) contributes 15%; and goal accuracy, measured as a Brier score on the probability that
the campaign goal is met, contributes the final 15%. Predictions that are near-zero face a null
penalty of up to 60%, and any miner whose aggregate score fails to beat a conditional-prior baseline
receives zero emissions for that epoch.

Validators commit their scoring artifacts on chain using Bittensor's commit-reveal weight mechanism.
A shadow validator — a separate instance running identical code on a different hotkey —
independently scores every epoch and commits its own artifacts, providing a public audit trail.
Anyone can run the
[verify_epoch.py](https://github.com/ippcteam/SN21-adtao/blob/main/scripts/verify_epoch.py) script
to re-run the scoring against chain state and confirm or contradict the primary validator's claims.
[[Yuma Consensus]] then aggregates validator weights into final emissions for the miner set.

## Participating as a Miner

Miners on AdTAO are forecasters. The task is to build a model that takes a structured Google Ads
episode — account state, recent campaign history, and a description of the action being applied —
and outputs calibrated P10/P50/P90 distributions over the resulting performance change. The subnet
ships a reference baseline based on XGBoost, along with sample training episodes and helper scripts,
so participants can benchmark their approach against a known starting point before committing to the
network.

Because predictions must be submitted before the timelock reveals, timing discipline matters: late
submissions are detectable from chain state and treated as missing data. The miner quickstart guide
is available in the
[AdTAO repository](https://github.com/ippcteam/SN21-adtao/blob/main/docs/miner_quickstart.md). The
subnet's hardware requirements are defined in the repository's `min_compute.yml` file.

## Participating as a Validator

Validators on Subnet 21 are responsible for the full scoring pipeline each epoch: fetching miner
prediction commits from the three-tier archive system, verifying the cryptographic scoreability of
each submission against the eight-check rule, running the scoring components, and committing the
resulting weights via timelocked weight commits. Validators also publish pre-scoring and
post-scoring artifact roots on chain so that any observer can verify the scoring independently.

A tiered weight allocator is implemented in the codebase and governs how emissions flow between
Elite and Standard tier miners once it is activated after a review period. Validator setup details
are in the
[validator setup guide](https://github.com/ippcteam/SN21-adtao/blob/main/docs/validator_setup.md).
