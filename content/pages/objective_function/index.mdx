# Objective Function

In machine learning and subnet operations, an objective function is the goal that a system is
continuously optimizing for. The
[Glossary: Objective Function](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#objective-function)
entry frames it in Bittensor terms as the goal a subnet pursues through its incentive mechanism.

## The Goal Being Optimized

An objective function states what "better" means for a given problem. Optimization then pushes a
system toward higher objective values, so the objective is the target that gives direction to the
whole process. Without one, there is nothing for training or competition to move toward
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

## How Bittensor Uses It

In a subnet, the incentive mechanism encodes the objective. Validators score miner work against what
the subnet is trying to achieve, and rewards flow toward the contributions that advance that goal,
so the subnet as a whole is steered toward its objective
([Anatomy of Incentive Mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)).

This is why a subnet's design choices matter: the objective they encode is what miners are
ultimately pushed to optimize.

## Distinction from Loss Function

An objective function names the goal to optimize toward, while a loss function measures how far
current output sits from that goal. The objective sets the target; the loss quantifies the distance
to it, and reducing loss is how a system moves toward the objective
([Glossary: Loss Function](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#loss-function)).

- **Objective function** — the goal the subnet optimizes for.
- **Loss function** — the measured gap between output and that goal.

## Why the Distinction Matters

Keeping the two apart clarifies how subnet incentives work. The incentive mechanism expresses an
objective, and the scoring that approximates a loss is what tells validators which miners are closer
to it. Reading a subnet's rewards means asking what objective the mechanism encodes, not only how
scores are computed
([Anatomy of Incentive Mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)).
