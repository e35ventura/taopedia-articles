An objective function names the goal a Bittensor subnet is designed to optimize through its
incentive mechanism. The official
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#objective-function) defines the term
in the context of machine learning and subnet operations.

## What It Names

In Bittensor, a subnet is an incentive-based marketplace for a specific kind of digital commodity.
The official [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)
page frames subnets around useful work that is produced and measured inside that market.

The objective function is the target behind that design. It gives a short way to ask what outcome
the subnet is trying to make more likely, before looking at the details of any one scoring method or
implementation.

## Why It Matters

The term matters because Bittensor subnets do not reward work in the abstract. Their incentive
mechanisms are meant to guide participant behavior toward the kind of output the subnet wants to
produce. The official
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)
page describes incentive mechanisms as structures that drive participant behavior and production of
the subnet's digital commodity.

For readers, the objective function is the high-level purpose behind those incentives. It helps
separate the goal of a subnet from the details a project may use to measure progress toward that
goal.

## Reading the Term

When an article or source mentions an objective function, read it as a statement about intended
direction. It is not automatically a formula, a performance guarantee, a live chain value, or a
complete description of a subnet's evaluation process.

That distinction is useful because two sources can discuss the same subnet at different levels. One
source may describe the goal the subnet is optimizing for, while another may describe the tools,
tests, or measurements used by that subnet. The objective function belongs to the first level.

## Boundaries

An objective function should not be treated as a standalone explanation of rewards. A reward process
also depends on the broader incentive mechanism and on the subnet's own documentation. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#objective-function) gives the term's
concept-level meaning, while
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)
provides the wider context for how subnet goals are connected to incentives.

The safest use of the term is therefore narrow: it names what the subnet is trying to optimize. It
does not, by itself, say how any particular subnet calculates scores, updates its mechanism, or
allocates rewards.
