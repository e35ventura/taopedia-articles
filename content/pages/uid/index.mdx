# UID

A UID is the unique identifier assigned to each participant position in a Bittensor subnet. Each
miner or validator registered to a subnet holds a UID that identifies their position within that
subnet's participant set
([Glossary: UID slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

UID is identifier vocabulary. It names the number, not the position structure itself. UID slot is
the related term for the participant position that the UID identifies.

## Subnet-Specific Scope

UIDs are subnet-specific. The same UID number can appear in different subnets while referring to
different participants. A UID identifies a position within one subnet's participant set, not a
network-wide identity
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

Netuid identifies the subnet itself, while UID identifies a participant within that subnet
([Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid)). The two terms
answer different questions about the same subnet context.

## Registration and UIDs

A participant receives a UID by registering to a subnet. Registration assigns a UID to a hotkey,
placing that participant into a UID slot within the subnet's capacity
([Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register)).

That means a UID is earned through registration, not self-assigned. If the subnet is at capacity,
registration replaces the lowest-ranked existing participant through the immunity and deregistration
rules.

## Metagraph Context

The metagraph records each subnet's participant set. UIDs appear in metagraph data as identifiers
for the miners and validators indexed in that subnet's state
([Glossary: Metagraph](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#metagraph)).

Reading a UID from metagraph data gives a position identifier within the subnet's current
participant set. The position's associated state — stake, rank, trust — is indexed by that UID.

## Reader Boundary

UID is concept vocabulary for subnet participant identification. It should not be read as a network
address, a balance container, or a cross-subnet identity
([Glossary: UID slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot)).

UIDs can change through deregistration. A UID that identifies one participant today may identify a
different participant in the same subnet after a registration event
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

## Relationship to UID Slot

A UID and a UID slot are related but different parts of Bittensor subnet vocabulary. A UID slot
names a participant position inside a subnet, while a UID names the unique identifier assigned to
that position. The [Glossary: UID Slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot)
describes the participant position, and the
[Glossary: UID](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid) defines UID as the unique
identifier for each participant in a subnet.

For readers, a UID slot names the position structure, while a UID names the number identifying who
currently occupies that position after registration.

References: [Glossary: UID Slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot),
[Glossary: UID](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid)

## Relationship to Active UID

A UID and an active UID are related but different parts of Bittensor subnet vocabulary. A UID names
the identifier for a participant position, while an active UID names a slot whose participant is
considered active for subnet participation purposes. The
[Glossary: UID](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid) defines the identifier, and
the [Glossary: Active UID](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#active-uid) describes
activity status within a UID slot.

For readers, a UID names the position number, while an active UID names whether that position's
participant is currently treated as active inside the subnet.

References: [Glossary: UID](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid),
[Glossary: Active UID](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#active-uid)

## Relationship to Netuid

A UID and a netuid are related but different parts of Bittensor subnet vocabulary. A netuid
identifies a subnet on the network, while a UID identifies a participant position inside that
subnet's participant set. The
[Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid) places netuid at the
subnet level, and the [Glossary: UID](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid)
defines UID as subnet-specific rather than network-wide.

For readers, a netuid identifies which subnet is in scope, while a UID identifies which participant
position exists inside that subnet.

References: [Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid),
[Glossary: UID](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid)

## Relationship to Deregistration

A UID and deregistration are related but different parts of Bittensor subnet vocabulary.
Deregistration names removal of a miner or validator from a UID slot inside a subnet, while a UID
names the identifier for that participant position before or after such a change. The
[Glossary: Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#deregistration)
describes participant removal from a slot, and the
[Glossary: UID](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid) defines UID as the
identifier assigned to each participant in a subnet.

For readers, deregistration names a lifecycle event that can free or reassign a UID slot, while a
UID names the position identifier that may later identify a different participant after
registration.

References:
[Glossary: Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#deregistration),
[Glossary: UID](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid)
