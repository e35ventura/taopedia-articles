# Staking and Delegation (TAO)

Staking and delegation describe how TAO holders attach support to validators in Bittensor.
Delegation is the holder-to-validator path, staking is the action of attaching support, and stake is
the amount associated with the resulting validator position
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking),
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake)).

The term is broad. It can refer to the staking area, the validator support relationship, or the
stake position that results after TAO is attached. A precise use should keep the asset, validator,
and network context visible.

## Validator Support

Stake is the amount of TAO associated with a validator position. Staking is the act of attaching
that TAO to a validator. Delegation is the common holder-to-validator path: a TAO holder supports a
validator without becoming that validator
([Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation),
[Glossary: Validator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator)).

Related staking terms name different parts of that relationship. A nominator delegates to a
validator. Delegate stake names stake supplied by a delegate validator itself. Effective stake is
the combined stake measure used when self-stake and delegated support are considered
([Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator),
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake),
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake)).

## Rewards and Stake Measures

Staking does not promise a fixed return. Validator dividends depend on the network's emission and
consensus mechanics, validator behavior, and the stake relationship around a validator
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

Stake also differs from stake weight. Stake names the validator-associated amount, while stake
weight is the interpreted measure used for consensus and emission context
([Glossary: Stake Weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight),
[Understanding Subnets: Validator stake weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets#validator-stake-weight)).

The useful boundary is action versus amount versus weighting. Staking attaches support, stake names
the attached amount, and stake weight is the downstream consensus measure.

## Root and Alpha Paths

Staking and delegation can describe more than one staking path. Root Subnet, also called Subnet
Zero, is the subnet-agnostic staking venue for root validators and TAO holders. An alpha staker
belongs to the subnet-local alpha-token side of staking rather than the Root Subnet side
([Glossary: Root Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution),
[Glossary: Alpha Tokens](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-tokens)).

Root claims are downstream of Root Subnet staking. Alpha dividends earned through root stake can be
swapped into TAO or kept as subnet alpha, and that claim-handling step is separate from the initial
act of staking to a validator
([Root Claim Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

## Automatic Routing

Autostaking is related to staking and delegation but names a different route into a validator
position. Staking and delegation begins with a TAO holder who decides to commit TAO to support a
validator. Autostaking routes a miner's own emission proceeds to a validator position automatically
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Autostaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners/autostaking)).

Both paths can result in TAO staked to a validator, but the source differs. One names the holder's
staking path; the other names the miner's automatic emission routing.

## Wallet and Environment Scope

Staking actions are tied to wallet authority. The validator receives stake support, while the holder
controls the wallet authority used to add, move, or remove that support
([Wallets, Coldkeys and Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets),
[Managing Your Stakes](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/managing-stake-sdk)).

Bittensor examples should keep their environment visible. Localnet examples are isolated development
examples, testnet examples are shared non-production examples, and mainnet examples concern
production TAO and production validator relationships
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).

Choosing a validator, evaluating risk, and monitoring rewards remain separate decisions. They should
not be implied by the definition of staking and delegation itself.

## Delegation Involves Staking to Another Coldkey

Official
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
documentation describes
[Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation) as the act of
assigning your TAO stake to a validator's
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey). This action allows
a TAO holder to support a chosen validator without needing to operate a validator node themselves.
The coldkey owner retains full control over their staked TAO, including the ability to withdraw or
reassign it at any time, but delegates the operational management of that stake's influence to the
validator.

This contrasts with direct staking to one's own validator, where the coldkey owner is also the
operator managing the validator's performance. Delegation separates the ownership of stake from its
active use in validating, enabling broader participation in securing the network. A delegator makes
a strategic choice to align their capital with a validator they believe will perform well, thereby
contributing to the network's decentralized consensus while earning a share of the validator's
rewards. The relationship is always between the delegator's coldkey and the validator's coldkey,
ensuring that the fundamental asset ownership and control remain with the delegator.

For readers, delegation is a method to participate in the Bittensor ecosystem's incentive mechanism
without requiring the technical infrastructure or constant monitoring needed to run a validator. It
leverages the expertise of chosen validators to generate returns on staked TAO, fostering a
symbiotic relationship where validators gain increased stake weight and delegators earn passive
income. The decision to delegate requires careful consideration of a validator's reputation and
performance history.

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey)

## Validator Determines Delegator Reward Share

Official
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
documentation explains that validators set a commission fee, which is a percentage of the rewards
they earn before distributing the remainder to their delegators. This commission acts as the
validator's compensation for operating the node, maintaining high performance, and securing their
position in the subnet. The remaining rewards are then proportionalized among the delegators based
on their individual stake contributions.

The commission rate is a key factor for delegators when choosing which validator to support. A lower
commission might seem more attractive, but delegators also consider a validator's historical
performance, reliability, and total stake. A validator with higher total stake (from their own stake
and all delegations) has greater
[Glossary: Stake Weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight) in the
network's consensus, potentially leading to more consistent rewards. The system ensures that all
participants, both validators and delegators, are incentivized to contribute to the network's health
and performance.

The reward distribution mechanism encourages a competitive marketplace among validators. Validators
compete for delegated stake by offering attractive commission rates and demonstrating strong
performance. This competition ultimately benefits the network by encouraging validators to maintain
optimal operation and high security standards. Delegators regularly monitor their chosen validator's
performance and reward payouts to ensure their delegated TAO is generating expected returns, with
the flexibility to redelegate if performance declines.

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)

## Unstaking Involves a Timed Release Period

Official
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
documentation details that
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking) is the process
of removing previously staked TAO, and it typically involves a predefined timed release period. This
waiting period is a security measure designed to protect the network from sudden, large withdrawals
that could destabilize validator operations or negatively impact subnet dynamics. During this
period, the TAO is no longer actively contributing to stake weight or earning rewards, but it
remains locked.

The unstaking period ensures a predictable liquidity schedule for staked assets, allowing the
network to gracefully adjust to changes in stake distribution. Once the timed release period
concludes, the unstaked TAO becomes fully available to the coldkey owner, who can then transfer it,
restake it to a different validator, or use it for other purposes. This mechanism is crucial for
maintaining the stability and security of the Bittensor network, especially for managing large
amounts of staked capital.

The concept of a timed release for unstaking is common in many proof-of-stake blockchain systems. It
prevents "flash exits" during periods of network stress or market volatility, providing a buffer
that allows the system to react and maintain integrity. Understanding this waiting period is
essential for both validators and delegators when planning their capital allocation and withdrawal
strategies. The process is transparent, with the duration of the timed release period being a
well-documented parameter of the network.
