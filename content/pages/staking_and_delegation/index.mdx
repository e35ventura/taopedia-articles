# Staking and Delegation (TAO)

Staking and delegation describe how TAO holders attach support to validators in Bittensor. The
staking documentation presents delegation as the path by which a holder commits TAO to a validator,
while the glossary separates the action of staking from the stake amount attached to a validator
position
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking),
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake)).

The term is broad. It can refer to the user-facing staking area, the validator support relationship,
or the stake position that results after TAO is attached. A precise use should keep the asset,
validator, and network context visible.

## Stake, Delegation, and Validator Support

Stake is the amount of TAO associated with a validator position. Staking is the act of attaching
that TAO to a validator. Delegation is the common holder-to-validator path: a TAO holder supports a
validator without becoming that validator
([Glossary: Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation),
[Glossary: Validator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator)).

This distinction matters because Bittensor articles often use nearby terms together. A validator is
the network role that receives stake support. A nominator is a holder who delegates to a validator.
Delegate stake names stake supplied by a delegate validator itself, while effective stake is the
combined stake measure used when self-stake and delegated support are considered
([Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator),
[Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake),
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake)).

## Reward Relationship

Staking does not promise a fixed return. Validator dividends depend on the network's emission and
consensus mechanics, validator behavior, and the stake relationship around a validator. The
emissions documentation distinguishes miner incentives from validator dividends, and the staking
overview frames staking as support for validators rather than a separate token class
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

Because validator dividends are part of the emission system, staking language should not be read as
a reward quote, validator recommendation, or performance promise. It identifies the support
relationship that can participate in validator dividend distribution.

## Root Subnet Context

Root Subnet, also called Subnet Zero, gives staking language a subnet-agnostic venue. The glossary
describes Root Subnet as the special subnet where validators can register and TAO holders can stake
without choosing one ordinary subnet's alpha-token position
([Glossary: Root Subnet/Subnet Zero](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero),
[Glossary: Root Staker](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-staker)).

That root path is separate from subnet-local alpha staking. Emissions documentation distinguishes
Root Subnet stakers from stakers holding subnet alpha in a mining subnet
([Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)). Root
claims are downstream of that root staking path: they describe what can happen to alpha dividends
earned through root stake, not the original choice to attach TAO to a validator
([Root Claim Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims)).

## Wallet Authority Boundary

Staking actions are tied to wallet authority. Bittensor wallet documentation separates coldkeys and
keys, and staking documentation treats stake management as an action controlled through the holder's
wallet rather than through the validator receiving support
([Wallets, Coldkeys and Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets),
[Managing Your Stakes](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/managing-stake-sdk)).

That boundary keeps the roles clear. The validator receives stake support. The holder controls the
wallet authority used to add, move, or remove that support. Wallet custody, validator selection, and
reward monitoring are related decisions, but they are not part of the definition of staking and
delegation itself.

## Environment Scope

Bittensor documentation separates localnet, testnet, and mainnet environments. Staking examples from
one environment should stay scoped to that environment because balances, validators, emissions, and
chain state do not transfer across those contexts
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).

Localnet examples are isolated development examples. Testnet examples are shared non-production
examples. Mainnet staking concerns production TAO and production validator relationships. Keeping
the environment visible prevents a sample stake position or reward outcome from being mistaken for a
general Bittensor-wide fact.
