# Subnet 9: iota

iota is Bittensor Subnet 9 (SN9), operated by [Macrocosmos](https://www.macrocosmos.ai/). It
implements a pipeline-parallel architecture for pretraining large language models across a network
of heterogeneous, permissionless machines — each earning TAO in proportion to the compute work it
contributes. Macrocosmos describes iota as the world's first permissionless pipeline-parallel
training architecture and has published a
[technical primer on arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17766) detailing its design. Live training
metrics are available at
[iota.macrocosmos.ai/dashboard/mainnet](https://iota.macrocosmos.ai/dashboard/mainnet).

References: [macrocosm-os/iota (GitHub)](https://github.com/macrocosm-os/iota),
[iota.macrocosmos.ai](https://iota.macrocosmos.ai/)

## How the Mechanism Works

iota trains a single shared model by partitioning it into sequential layer groups and distributing
those groups across miners. An orchestrator assigns each registered miner a contiguous segment of
the model — a head, body, or tail stage — and streams activations forward through the pipeline one
training step at a time. The model currently trained is a 1.5B parameter Llama-inspired architecture
divided into three pipeline stages.

After each training stage, miners upload their local weight updates and merge them with other
participants via a variant of Butterfly All-Reduce, an algorithm for aggregating gradients across
distributed workers without any single node holding the full model. Miners compete on throughput:
those that process more activations per unit of time receive more emissions.

Validators do not score miners on model quality; they spot-check individual computations against
expected outputs to verify that claimed work was actually performed. Validated weight-sets flow into
[[Yuma Consensus]], which distributes per-block TAO emissions through [[Dynamic TAO]].

References: [macrocosm-os/iota (GitHub)](https://github.com/macrocosm-os/iota),
[iota technical primer (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17766)

## Participating as a Miner

Miners on iota contribute GPU compute to one pipeline stage of the shared training run. A miner
registers on netuid 9, receives a layer assignment from the orchestrator, and processes activations
forwarded from the previous stage. Earnings scale with throughput — the volume of activations
handled during each training step. Miners with faster, more reliable hardware process more
activations and earn a larger share of emissions.

Because the model is partitioned and activations are streamed, a miner does not need to hold the
entire model in memory; only the weights for its assigned layer segment are required. Macrocosmos
plans to scale subsequent training runs to 15B, 50B, and 100B parameter models as the system
matures, which will shift hardware requirements upward for each successive run.

References: [macrocosm-os/iota (GitHub)](https://github.com/macrocosm-os/iota)

## Participating as a Validator

Validators on iota maintain the integrity of the training run by spot-checking miners' computations.
A validator selects a subset of a miner's layer outputs and independently re-runs the same forward
pass to verify that the miner's reported result is correct. Miners whose outputs deviate from
expected values receive lower scores; miners who pass consistently earn higher weights.

These per-miner weights are submitted to the network each epoch. Validators that produce honest,
consistent weight-sets earn emissions through [[Yuma Consensus]]. Validators must remain online
throughout the training run to produce timely spot-checks, since a stale validator contributes
nothing to the scoring round.
