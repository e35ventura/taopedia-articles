# Subnet 9: iota

iota is Bittensor Subnet 9 (SN9), operated by [Macrocosmos](https://macrocosmos.ai/). The subnet
implements the **I**ncentivized **O**rchestrated **T**raining **A**rchitecture — a framework for
pretraining large language models across a network of heterogeneous, unreliable, permissionless GPU
miners. By splitting model layers across participants and streaming activations between them, iota
makes large-scale pretraining accessible to contributors who would otherwise lack the hardware to
run a full training job independently.

The technical design is described in the Macrocosmos research primer at
[macrocosmos.ai/research/iota_primer.pdf](https://www.macrocosmos.ai/research/iota_primer.pdf) and
on [ArXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17766).

## How the Mechanism Works

iota uses a data- and pipeline-parallel training approach. A central orchestrator partitions the
model into discrete layer segments — a head, one or more body layers, and a tail — and assigns each
segment to a different miner or group of miners. During a training step, the orchestrator streams
intermediate activations from one segment to the next, assembling a forward pass and backward pass
across the full model without any single participant needing to hold the entire model in memory.

Miners periodically upload their local layer weights and merge them with peers using a variant of
Butterfly All-Reduce, a communication pattern that balances the number of messages each node must
exchange. A shared object-store bucket holds activations and weight checkpoints between stages,
allowing asynchronous handoffs and making the pipeline resilient to individual dropouts.

Validators receive gradient and weight samples from miners and run spot-check verification to
confirm that each miner actually processed the activations assigned to them. The orchestrator
distributes validation work across all registered validators and logs the results, ensuring that
miners who skip or falsify computation receive no reward. Validated weight-sets flow into
[[Yuma Consensus]], which converts them into per-block TAO emissions via [[Dynamic TAO]].

A live view of the network — active miners, layer assignments, and training metrics — is available
at the [iota dashboard](https://iota.macrocosmos.ai/dashboard/mainnet).

## Participating as a Miner

Miners on iota contribute GPU compute to a specific layer segment of the current training run. After
registering on netuid 9, a miner connects to the orchestrator, which assigns it one or more model
layers based on available capacity. The miner then receives activation tensors from upstream
participants, runs forward and backward passes over its assigned layers, and forwards the outputs
downstream.

Reward depends on the volume of activations processed and the integrity of the resulting gradients.
Miners who drop activations, fail to upload weights on schedule, or produce incorrect gradient
values will be down-weighted or excluded by validators. Because training is continuous, consistent
uptime matters: a miner that disappears mid-run creates a gap in the pipeline.

The Macrocosmos team maintains official setup guides for miners at
[docs.macrocosmos.ai](https://docs.macrocosmos.ai/subnets/subnet-9-pre-training/subnet-9-iota-mining-setup-guide).

References: [macrocosm-os/iota (GitHub)](https://github.com/macrocosm-os/iota),
[Macrocosmos miner docs](https://docs.macrocosmos.ai/subnets/subnet-9-pre-training/subnet-9-iota-mining-setup-guide)

## Participating as a Validator

Validators on iota run gradient validation services that receive weight and activation samples from
the orchestrator and verify that miners computed them correctly. The orchestrator distributes
validation assignments across all registered validators; each validator tracks a subset of active
miners and reports pass/fail results back to the network.

Validators do not need to hold or train the full model. They receive only the samples required for
spot-checking, which keeps hardware requirements lower than running an equivalent training job.
Honest, high-availability validators earn TAO emissions through [[Yuma Consensus]] proportional to
the accuracy and consistency of their reported weight-sets.

Macrocosmos provides a validator setup guide at
[docs.macrocosmos.ai](https://docs.macrocosmos.ai/subnets/subnet-9-pre-training/subnet-9-validating).
