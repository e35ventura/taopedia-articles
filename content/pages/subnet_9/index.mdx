Subnet 9 hosts **iota** — short for Incentivized Orchestrated Training Architecture — a framework
developed by [Macrocosmos](https://www.macrocosmos.ai/) that enables large language model
pretraining across a decentralized network of heterogeneous machines. Macrocosmos describes it as
the world's first permissionless pipeline-parallel training architecture, allowing contributors with
varying hardware to collectively train models that would otherwise require a coordinated data-center
cluster. The project is documented in a technical primer available on
[ArXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17766).

## How the Mechanism Works

iota divides a model into discrete layers and assigns each layer segment to a different miner in the
network. An orchestrator coordinates the flow of activations — the intermediate numerical outputs of
each layer — passing them sequentially through the pipeline so that the full forward and backward
training pass is completed across multiple machines rather than on a single GPU.

Miners compete by processing as many activation batches as possible during each training round.
Periodically, each miner uploads its locally updated weights and merges them with the rest of the
network using a variant of Butterfly All-Reduce, a technique that reduces communication overhead in
distributed training. Validators then perform spot-checks on a random selection of miners to verify
that the reported work was actually performed correctly, scoring participants based on the volume
and integrity of activations processed.

This scoring feeds directly into [[Yuma Consensus]], which determines how emissions are allocated
across the 256 registered neurons. Miners who process more activations accurately receive higher
scores; validators who catch fraudulent submissions anchor the integrity of the system.

The current live training run targets a 1.5-billion-parameter, Llama-inspired architecture with
uninterrupted residual flow, split across three pipeline stages (head, body, and tail). Macrocosmos
has [published details](https://www.macrocosmos.ai/research/iota_primer.pdf) on the architecture and
plans to scale future runs to 15B, 50B, and 100B parameter models as the network matures.

## Participating as a Miner

Miners in iota are responsible for holding and processing one or more layers of the model being
trained. Because the pipeline distributes layers across participants, a single miner does not need
to fit the entire model into memory — only the segment it is assigned. Macrocosmos recommends a
CUDA-capable GPU with at least 16 GB of VRAM to run the current 1.5B-parameter training pass
comfortably.

Prospective miners should review
[Macrocosmos's official mining guide](https://docs.macrocosmos.ai/subnets/subnet-9-pre-training/subnet-9-iota-mining-setup-guide)
for hardware and software prerequisites before registering a hotkey on the subnet. Registration
competes with existing miners under the standard Bittensor pruning rules: the network allows up to
256 neurons, and low-scoring participants are deregistered to make room for new entrants.

## Participating as a Validator

Validators in iota spot-check miner outputs to ensure that activations are being computed correctly.
Rather than reprocessing every activation, validators sample a subset of miner work and compare
results against expected outputs, flagging miners that fail verification. This sampling strategy
keeps validation computationally tractable while still providing a meaningful deterrent against idle
or fraudulent miners.

Validators stake TAO on the subnet and receive emissions proportional to their stake under
[[Yuma Consensus]], provided they are actively checking miner work. Macrocosmos provides a
[dedicated validator guide](https://docs.macrocosmos.ai/subnets/subnet-9-pre-training/subnet-9-validating)
covering the hardware and configuration expectations for running a validator node on the current
training run.

A live [dashboard](https://iota.macrocosmos.ai/dashboard/mainnet) shows the current state of the
network in real time, including the number of active miners, layer assignments, and general training
metrics.
