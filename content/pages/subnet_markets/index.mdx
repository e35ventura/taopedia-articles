# Subnet Markets

Subnet markets are Bittensor subnets viewed as economic systems for specialized digital work. The
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets) documentation
describes each subnet as a market for a particular digital commodity, while the
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction) places subnets
inside the broader network.

A subnet market links work demand, evaluation rules, staking, tokenomics, and emissions. The market
view helps explain why a subnet is more than a topic label: it is an incentive system around a
specific class of work.

## Work Market

Each subnet specializes in a kind of digital work. The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction) documentation
describes subnets as specialized systems that can focus on different services or commodities.

In market terms, miners supply work and validators evaluate it. The subnet defines the work category
and the incentive mechanism that makes that work measurable.

## Evaluation Loop

Subnet markets depend on evaluation. The
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)
documentation explains how an incentive mechanism links miner behavior, validator evaluation, and
reward outcomes.

That evaluation loop is what turns a subnet from a simple service category into a market. Work is
produced, evaluated, and rewarded according to rules defined for that subnet.

## Consensus and Rewards

Validator signals still need a consensus process before rewards are assigned. The
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus) documentation describes how
validator rankings are combined for subnet reward outcomes.

This connects subnet markets to Bittensor's reward system. A market creates demand for work, but
Yuma Consensus is part of how evaluation becomes emissions inside the subnet.

## Tokenomics Side

Subnet markets also have a tokenomics side. The
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets) documentation
describes TAO reserves, alpha reserves, and alpha outstanding in subnet pools.

That means a subnet market combines two views: a work-and-evaluation view and a TAO-and-alpha view.
Dynamic TAO is the broader design that connects those views across subnets.

## Emission Link

The [Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions) documentation describes TAO and
subnet alpha moving through Bittensor's emission system. For subnet markets, emissions are the
reward side of the system: they connect evaluated work and stake context to token flows.

This keeps the market concept concrete. A subnet market is not only a description of demand; it is a
system where rewards give economic weight to the evaluated work.

## Multiple Mechanism Context

Some subnets can use more than one incentive mechanism. The
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)
documentation explains how multiple mechanisms can operate inside a subnet.

That does not change the market framing. It means one subnet market can contain several evaluation
paths for different kinds of work or scoring, all inside the same subnet.

## Term Placement

Subnet market is the broad phrase for the economic system around a subnet. Incentive mechanism
describes the scoring rules. Yuma Consensus describes how validator signals are combined. Dynamic
TAO describes the TAO-and-alpha tokenomics that support subnet markets
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

These terms overlap because they describe different layers of the same subnet economy.
