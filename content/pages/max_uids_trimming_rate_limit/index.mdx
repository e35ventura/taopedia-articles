# Max UIDs Trimming Rate Limit

Max UIDs trimming rate limit, named `MaxUidsTrimmingRateLimit` in the documentation, controls how
often a subnet owner can [trim UIDs](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming) on their
subnet. The rate-limits reference lists it at 216,000 blocks, about 30 days, on main net, and
explains that the cap exists to prevent disruptions in subnet stability and excessive network
reorganization ([Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits)).

## What Trimming Does

UID trimming lets a subnet owner reduce the number of registered neurons on their subnet, removing
the lowest-emission UIDs while preserving temporally immune and owner-controlled UIDs, then
compressing the remaining UIDs to keep consecutive indexing
([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming)). It is a forceful management
action: excess neurons are deregistered, so it is meant to be used deliberately rather than
routinely.

## How a Trim Selects and Preserves UIDs

The documented trim is not a blind cut. When it runs, the chain works through UIDs starting from the
lowest emitters and moving upward, deregistering the poorest performers first and stopping once the
subnet has been reduced to the owner's target count
([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming)).

Two categories of UID are skipped rather than removed. Temporally immune UIDs — those still inside
the subnet's post-registration immunity window — are preserved, as are owner-immune UIDs controlled
by the subnet creator. The documentation also caps total immunity so a trim cannot leave a subnet
made up almost entirely of protected slots: the number of immune UIDs may not exceed 80% of the
maximum UID count, the same ceiling the `maxImmuneUidsPercentage` constant fixes at `80`
([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming),
[Subtensor Constants: maxImmuneUidsPercentage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subtensor-api/constants#maximmuneuidspercentage),
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)).

After the low emitters are removed, the surviving UIDs are compressed to the left to keep
consecutive indexing. The documentation gives a worked example: if UIDs 5, 7, and 9 are what remain
once trimming finishes, they are reindexed to 0, 1, and 2. The trim also cannot cross the documented
floor, since the minimum count an owner can trim to is 64, so the action removes the weakest
non-protected neurons down to that floor rather than emptying the subnet
([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming),
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)).

## What the Rate Limit Controls

This parameter is the cooldown between those trims, not a limit on how far a single trim can go. The
UID trimming guide states the chain's trim rate limit is 216,000 blocks (~30 days), so subnet owners
can only change their UID count once roughly every 30 days
([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming),
[Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits)).

## When It Matters

A common misconception is that an owner can keep resizing their subnet to react to performance week
by week. The rate limit blocks that: once a trim lands, the UID count is locked for about 30 days
before it can be trimmed again
([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming)).

Concretely, suppose the owner of netuid 14 trims it down to 100 UIDs with
`btcli sudo trim --netuid 14 --max 100`. If a week later they decide 100 was still too many and want
to trim to the documented minimum of 64, the chain will reject the second trim until the roughly
216,000-block window elapses. Because trimming deregisters the excess neurons, that waiting period
is the safeguard: it forces owners to size their subnet carefully rather than churn participants
repeatedly ([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming),
[Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits)).

## Documented Nature

The [Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits) reference lists
`MaxUidsTrimmingRateLimit` as a block-counted limit that is not queryable from chain state, with the
interval at 216,000 blocks on main net and 1 block in fastblocks development mode. The trim itself
is a subnet-owner sudo action performed with `btcli sudo trim` or the
`sudo_trim_to_max_allowed_uids` extrinsic
([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming),
[Extrinsics](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subtensor-api/extrinsics)).

## Distinction from Min Allowed UIDs

The two interact but answer different questions. Min allowed UIDs is the floor a trim cannot drop
the subnet below, while the trimming rate limit is how long the owner must wait between trims
([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming)).

- **Min allowed UIDs** — the lowest UID count a trim may target.
- **Max UIDs trimming rate limit** — the cooldown between successive trims.
