# Coldkey

A coldkey is the ownership key in Bittensor wallet vocabulary. The official
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) describes a coldkey
as the wallet component responsible for securely storing funds and performing high-risk operations
such as transfers and staking.

## Ownership and Hotkey Context

Bittensor separates long-term ownership from operational network activity. Official wallet
documentation describes coldkeys as the security-critical key that controls TAO and stake, while
hotkeys handle day-to-day roles such as mining and validation. A coldkey therefore names the
ownership side of that split rather than the operational side.

For readers, coldkey should be read as a role label inside wallet vocabulary. It is not a subnet
miner UID, a validator permit, or a staking return figure.

References: [Working with Keys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/working-with-keys),
[Coldkey-Hotkey Security](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-hotkey-security)

## Staking and Delegation Context

Coldkey ownership also frames staking participation. The
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
describes how TAO holders delegate support to validators, and coldkey authority is what backs those
financial operations on chain. A
[nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator) supplies stake through
delegation vocabulary, but the coldkey remains the ownership key behind that participation.

This article does not describe how to create, import, rotate, or back up a coldkey. Those steps
belong in official wallet documentation.

## Contrast with Hotkey and Wallet

Three terms stay distinct for readers: **Bittensor wallet** names the identity container,
**coldkey** names ownership authority inside that container, and **hotkey** names operational
authority for network roles. The
[Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey) entry defines the
operational key separately from coldkey ownership.

A hotkey-coldkey pair then names how those two key roles are bound for staking and governance
authentication. Coldkey alone names only the ownership half of that relationship.

References:
[Glossary: Bittensor Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bittensor-wallet),
[Glossary: Hotkey-Coldkey Pair](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey-coldkey-pair)

## Senate and Governance Boundary

Coldkey authority also appears in governance participation requirements. The
[Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate) documentation describes Senate
membership in terms of registered hotkey-coldkey relationships and delegate stake context. Coldkey
is part of that identity picture because ownership authority must be established before operational
hotkeys act in governance roles.

This article does not list senators, proposal outcomes, or delegation recommendations.

## Relationship to Multiple Mechanisms

Coldkey names wallet ownership authority for participants who may later stake to validators inside
subnets. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms) notes
that subnets can run more than one incentive mechanism.

For readers, coldkey still names ownership-key vocabulary at the wallet layer. Mechanism count
belongs with subnet incentive design, not with the coldkey role itself.

References:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms)

## Reader Boundary

Coldkey should not be read as a command tutorial, a seed-phrase guide, or a recommendation to expose
ownership keys on online nodes. It is a concept term for the ownership key that secures TAO, stake,
and high-risk wallet operations in Bittensor vocabulary.
