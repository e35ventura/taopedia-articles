# Bicameral Legislature

Bicameral legislature describes the two-body governance structure used in Bittensor proposal review.
The [Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bicameral-legislature) defines it
as a two-tier system comprising the Triumvirate and the Senate for proposal approval.

## Two Governance Bodies

The Triumvirate and Senate have separate governance roles. The official
[Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance) describes the Triumvirate as the
body that creates proposals and the Senate as the body that approves or rejects them. The
[Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate) documentation provides the Senate-side
context for reviewing proposals.

## Relationship to Triumvirate

Bicameral legislature and the Triumvirate are related but different scopes in Bittensor governance
vocabulary. Bicameral legislature names the two-body review structure, while the Triumvirate names
the proposal-origin body inside that structure. The
[Glossary: Bicameral Legislature](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bicameral-legislature)
defines the two-tier Triumvirate-and-Senate pairing, and the
[Glossary: Triumvirate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#triumvirate) defines the
Triumvirate as the group responsible for creating proposals.

For readers, bicameral legislature names the overall two-body model, while the Triumvirate names the
first body in that model.

References:
[Glossary: Bicameral Legislature](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bicameral-legislature),
[Glossary: Triumvirate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#triumvirate)

## Relationship to Senate

Bicameral legislature and the Senate are related but different scopes in Bittensor governance
vocabulary. Bicameral legislature names the two-body structure, while the Senate names the
stake-based approval body inside that structure. The
[Glossary: Bicameral Legislature](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bicameral-legislature)
frames the Senate as the second tier in proposal approval, and the
[Glossary: Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#senate) describes the Senate
as the group of elected delegates that reviews Triumvirate proposals.

For readers, bicameral legislature names the paired review model, while the Senate names the
approval body that votes on proposals after Triumvirate creation.

References:
[Glossary: Bicameral Legislature](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bicameral-legislature),
[Glossary: Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#senate)

## Relationship to Proposal

Bicameral legislature and a proposal are related but different scopes in Bittensor governance
vocabulary. Bicameral legislature names the governance structure through which proposals move, while
a proposal is the specific governance item created by the Triumvirate and reviewed by the Senate.
The [Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance) describes that flow, and the
[Glossary: Proposal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#proposal) defines a proposal
as a suggestion or plan put forward by the Triumvirate for the Senate to vote on.

For readers, bicameral legislature names the review structure, while a proposal names the item
passing through that structure.

References: [Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance),
[Glossary: Proposal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#proposal)

## Relationship to Sudo

Bicameral legislature and Sudo are related but different scopes in Bittensor governance vocabulary.
Sudo names the legacy single-key administrative authority that privileged changes once depended on,
while bicameral legislature names the two-body review structure that governance uses instead. The
[Glossary: Sudo](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#sudo) notes that the privileged
sudo key has been replaced by the governance protocol, and the
[Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance) explains that privileged changes
now require Senate majority support and Triumvirate closure rather than direct sudo execution.

For readers, Sudo names the prior centralized authority model, while bicameral legislature names the
split review structure that replaced it.

References: [Glossary: Sudo](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#sudo),
[Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance)

## Relationship to Bittensor Governance

Bicameral legislature and Bittensor governance are related but different scopes in Bittensor
governance vocabulary. Bittensor governance names the full network process for reviewing and
executing privileged changes, while bicameral legislature names the Triumvirate-and-Senate structure
inside that process. The [Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance) describes
governance as moving network management away from centralized control through proposal review, and
the
[Glossary: Bicameral Legislature](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bicameral-legislature)
defines the two-tier proposal-approval system at the center of that model.

For readers, Bittensor governance names the overall privileged-change process, while bicameral
legislature names the two-body review structure within it.

References: [Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance),
[Glossary: Bicameral Legislature](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bicameral-legislature)

## Relationship to Senator

Bicameral legislature and a senator are related but different scopes in Bittensor governance
vocabulary. Bicameral legislature names the two-body governance structure, while a senator names the
delegate role that votes on proposals inside the Senate half of that structure. The
[Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate) documentation describes senators as
delegates who participate in proposal review, and the
[Glossary: Bicameral Legislature](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bicameral-legislature)
defines the Senate as the second tier in the bicameral approval model.

For readers, bicameral legislature names the governance structure, while senator names an individual
voting role within the Senate tier of that structure.

References: [Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate),
[Glossary: Bicameral Legislature](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bicameral-legislature)

## Reader Boundary

This term names the governance structure, not the status of any specific proposal or the membership
of either body. For proposal records, readers should use the official governance references rather
than treating a glossary article as live governance state.

## Relationship to Multiple Mechanisms

Bicameral legislature names Bittensor's two-body governance review for proposals. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator) notes that subnet
creators can configure multiple incentive mechanisms to distribute emissions across different types
of work or evaluation criteria.

For readers, bicameral legislature still names governance structure. Approved subnet design changes
may enable more than one incentive mechanism in a subnet market.

References:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator)
