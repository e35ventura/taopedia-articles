# Stake

Stake is the amount of currency tokens associated with a validator UID in a Bittensor subnet. The
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake) entry defines it as the
currency tokens delegated to a validator UID, including both the validator's own stake and stake
delegated from others.

## Validator Context

Stake is read in relation to validator support. The official
[Staking/delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
explains that TAO holders can stake liquidity to a validator and that the validator's total stake,
including delegated stake, helps determine consensus power and emissions. The same page also frames
staking and delegation as closely related terms.

This makes stake a position-linked amount rather than a generic wallet balance. It describes support
associated with a validator in a subnet context.

## Subnet Context

Stake is local to subnet state. The
[Staking into Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation#staking-into-subnets)
section explains that stake is always local to a subnet and is held in subnet alpha-token
denominations.
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets#liquidity-pools)
uses the term alpha outstanding for alpha held by subnet participants and describes it as total
stake in the subnet.

This subnet boundary matters because stake is not a single network-wide number that means the same
thing in every context. It depends on the subnet and the validator position being discussed.

## Own Stake and Delegated Stake

The glossary definition includes both a validator's own stake and stake delegated from others. That
makes stake a combined support amount around a validator UID rather than only the validator's
personal token position. The validator can contribute stake directly, and other TAO holders can add
delegated support through the staking and delegation system.

This combined view is important for staking articles because the same validator position can be
supported by multiple participants. A staker may not operate the validator, but the delegated amount
still contributes to the stake associated with that validator UID. Stake therefore describes the
position's support base, not just one account's holdings.

References: [Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Staking/delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Subnet-Local Alpha Context

Stake is also tied to subnet-local alpha accounting. The staking documentation explains that stake
inside a subnet is held in that subnet's alpha-token denomination, while the subnet overview uses
alpha outstanding to describe alpha held by subnet participants. This connects stake to the subnet's
own token context rather than to a single global staking pool.

Because each subnet has its own context, stake should be read with the subnet attached. A validator
position on one subnet does not automatically describe the same amount, influence, or token
denomination on another subnet. The local alpha context is part of what makes Bittensor staking
subnet-specific.

References:
[Staking into Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation#staking-into-subnets),
[Understanding Subnets: Liquidity pools](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets#liquidity-pools)

## Validator Influence Context

Stake contributes to validator influence, but it should stay separate from the final computed
influence measure. The staking overview says validator total stake helps determine consensus power
and emissions, while the stake-weight glossary defines stake weight as the computed total stake
value used for those outcomes.

That means stake is the token amount associated with validator support, and stake weight is the
measure the protocol uses when interpreting that support inside subnet consensus. A high-level
article can describe the connection without treating the two terms as interchangeable.

References:
[Staking/delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Stake Weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight)

## Balance and Position Context

Stake is different from an ordinary balance. A balance describes tokens held by an account, while
stake describes tokens associated with validator support in a subnet. The distinction matters
because staking changes how the tokens are positioned in the network: they are read through the
validator UID, subnet, and staking relationship.

This also separates stake from general ownership language. A participant can hold tokens without
staking them, and a validator position can include delegated support from others. The stake term is
therefore about network position and validator backing, not merely possession.

References: [Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Staking/delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Reward Context

Stake sits upstream of validator-side reward outcomes. The dividends glossary defines dividends as
the validator-side reward distributed from emissions, while the staking overview connects validator
stake to consensus power and emissions. Together, those references place stake in the support side
of the reward path and dividends in the return side.

Stake supports reward participation without making every staked position equivalent. This is why
stake appears in staking, validator, and emission articles: the amount associated with a validator
position helps determine how that position participates in consensus and reward distribution.

References: [Glossary: Dividends](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#dividends),
[Staking/delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Relationship to Stake Weight

Stake and stake weight are related but not identical. Stake names the associated token amount, while
[Glossary: Stake Weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight) defines
stake weight as the computed total stake value used for validator consensus power and emissions.
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets#validator-stake-weight)
places that computed measure in the validator stake-weight context.

For a reader, the distinction is simple: stake is the underlying associated amount; stake weight is
the subnet-specific measure derived from stake for consensus interpretation.

## Staking Process Context

Bittensor uses separate terms for validator support action and validator support amount.
[Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking) names the process of
attaching TAO to a validator hotkey, while stake names the token amount already associated with a
validator UID in a subnet. That distinction helps readers separate action language from amount
language when reading staking and consensus articles.

References: [Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking),
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake)

## Relationship to Multiple Mechanisms

Stake is usually discussed for one incentive mechanism at a time, but subnets can also run multiple
mechanisms in parallel. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentive-mechanism) notes that each
subnet has one or more incentive mechanisms.

For readers, stake still names validator-associated tokens inside one subnet, but the mechanism
context matters when reading how that subnet evaluates work and allocates consensus influence.

References:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Incentive Mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentive-mechanism)

## Relationship to Dividends

Stake and dividends are related but different parts of Bittensor vocabulary. Stake names the TAO
associated with a validator that gives the validator influence in a subnet, while dividends name the
validator-facing emission share that flows back to that stake. The
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake) describes stake as
backing a validator's weight, and the
[Glossary: Dividends](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#dividends) describes
dividends as the validator-side reward distributed from emissions.

For readers, stake names the committed input that gives a validator standing, while dividends name
the reward outcome that standing can earn. Stake is what is committed; dividends are what may be
returned.

References: [Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Glossary: Dividends](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#dividends)

## Relationship to Protocol Alpha

Stake and protocol alpha are related but different parts of subnet tokenomics. The
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake) describes stake as the
currency-token amount delegated to a validator UID, while the
[Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)
describes protocol alpha as alpha tokens accumulated by the Bittensor protocol itself from
chain-side TAO buys during reserve injection.

For readers, stake names the amount participants commit to validators, while protocol alpha names
the alpha the protocol holds on its own behalf. Stake belongs to participants; protocol alpha
belongs to the chain. The two should not be read as the same alpha-token concept.

References: [Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)

## Reader Boundary

Stake should not be read as a live balance report, a validator recommendation, or a complete
explanation of staking operations. It is a term for the amount associated with validator support
inside subnet staking and consensus. Current balances, staking actions, fee effects, and slippage
depend on current official tooling and subnet state.
