# Root Claims

Root claims handle alpha dividends earned through Root Subnet staking. They decide whether earned
alpha remains as subnet alpha or is converted into TAO and restaked on Root
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

The mechanism is narrow. It is about dividend treatment after rewards accrue, not about moving the
original root stake or explaining a general staking procedure.

## Root Subnet Context

Root Subnet is the subnet-agnostic staking environment in Bittensor. Root claims exist because stake
placed there can receive alpha dividends from other subnets while the underlying stake remains
root-routed
([Glossary: Root Subnet/Subnet Zero](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero),
[Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims)).

That separation gives root claims their role. The original stake remains tied to Root Subnet, while
earned alpha needs a handling rule once it is claimable.

## Keep and Swap Outcomes

Root claims have two outcomes. Keep leaves earned alpha as the alpha of the subnet that produced it.
Swap converts the earned alpha into TAO and restakes that dividend output on Root
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

Both outcomes apply to dividends, not to the original root stake. This keeps root claims separate
from unstaking, moving stake, or changing delegation.

That distinction is important when reading root-staking material: a claim changes the treatment of
earned dividend output, while the root stake remains the staking position that produced access to
those dividends.

## Claim Type and Processing

Root claim type is the preference that determines which outcome a root claim uses. Keep and Swap are
routing choices for dividend output, while the claim event processes accumulated dividends
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Glossary: Root Staker](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-staker)).

Root claims may be processed automatically or directly, but the same claim type governs the
resulting dividend treatment. The important concept is the dividend outcome: Keep preserves alpha
exposure from the producing subnet, while Swap routes the dividend into TAO on Root.

## Emission and Ownership Scope

Root-claim swaps sit inside Bittensor emission accounting. They handle dividend output, while
flow-based emissions track subnet inflows and outflows for emission distribution
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

Root claims should keep ownership and network context visible. Wallet authority remains separate
from the claim mechanism, and a root claim should not be read as a generic claim about every subnet
role or wallet action
([Wallets, Coldkeys and Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets),
[Glossary: Root Subnet/Subnet Zero](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero)).

## Environment Scope

Bittensor separates localnet, testnet, and mainnet environments. Root-claim examples from one
environment should not be treated as evidence for another because balances, dividend state, and
claim outcomes are environment-specific
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).

Localnet examples are isolated development examples. Testnet examples are shared non-production
examples. Mainnet root claims concern production Root Subnet staking and production dividends.
