# Root Claims

Root claims handle alpha dividends earned through Root Subnet staking. They decide whether earned
alpha remains as subnet alpha or is converted into TAO and restaked on Root
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

The mechanism is narrow. It is about dividend treatment after rewards accrue, not about moving the
original root stake or explaining a general staking procedure.

## Root Subnet Context

The Root Subnet, also called Subnet Zero, is the special subnet where validators can register and
TAO holders can stake in a subnet-agnostic way. Root claims exist because stake placed through that
Root Subnet path can receive alpha dividends from other subnets while the underlying stake remains
associated with the Root Subnet
([Glossary: Root Subnet/Subnet Zero](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero),
[Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims)).

That separation gives root claims their role. The original stake remains tied to Root Subnet, while
earned alpha needs a handling rule once it is claimable.

## Keep and Swap Outcomes

Root claims have two outcomes. Keep leaves earned alpha as the alpha of the subnet that produced it.
Swap converts the earned alpha into TAO and restakes that dividend output on Root
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

Both outcomes apply to dividends, not to the original root stake. This keeps root claims separate
from unstaking, moving stake, or changing delegation.

That distinction is important when reading root-staking material: a claim changes the treatment of
earned dividend output, while the root stake remains the staking position that produced access to
those dividends.

## Claim Type Context

Root claim type is the preference that determines which outcome a root claim uses. Keep and Swap are
routing choices for dividend output, while a root claim is the handling of accumulated dividends
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Glossary: Root Staker](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-staker)).

The important concept is the dividend outcome: Keep preserves alpha exposure from the producing
subnet, while Swap routes the dividend into TAO on Root.

## Claim Processing

Root claims can be processed automatically by the chain or manually through the root-claim extrinsic
path. In both cases, the configured Keep or Swap preference is what determines how the claim treats
accumulated alpha dividends
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims)).

This keeps the processing method separate from the outcome choice. Automatic and manual claims
describe how a claim is triggered, while Keep and Swap describe what happens to the dividend output.

## Emission Scope

Root-claim swaps sit inside Bittensor emission accounting. They handle dividend output, while
flow-based emissions track subnet inflows and outflows for emission distribution
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

This keeps root claims narrower than emissions overall. Root claims explain what happens to
dividends from Root Subnet staking after they accrue; they do not describe every reward distribution
mechanism in Bittensor.

## Reading Claim Outcome Evidence

Root-claim wording is safest when the original root stake, earned dividend, claim type, and final
output stay separate. The root-claims documentation describes Keep and Swap as treatments of earned
alpha dividends, while the Root Subnet glossary keeps the staking venue distinct from ordinary
subnet-local staking
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Glossary: Root Subnet/Subnet Zero](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero)).

| Evidence         | What it supports                                        | What it does not prove                              |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Root stake       | TAO is staked through the Root Subnet path              | The original stake was claimed or moved.            |
| Alpha dividend   | A subnet-produced reward became claimable               | The reward has already been kept or swapped.        |
| Keep outcome     | The dividend remains as the producing subnet's alpha    | It is not converted into TAO by that claim.         |
| Swap outcome     | The dividend is converted into TAO and restaked on Root | It is not an unstake of the original root position. |
| Root claim type  | A preference controls how dividends are handled         | It is not a validator recommendation.               |
| Emission context | The dividend came from Bittensor reward flow            | It does not describe every emission mechanism.      |

This comparison keeps root claims focused on dividend output. A claim changes how accumulated
dividends are handled after they accrue, while the underlying Root Subnet staking position remains
the source context for earning those dividends
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Glossary: Root Staker](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-staker)).

Keep and Swap should therefore be read as outcome labels, not as broad staking actions. Keep
preserves the subnet-alpha form of the dividend, while Swap routes that dividend through conversion
into TAO and restaking on Root
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

The claim type is nearby but not identical to the claim event. Claim type is the preference that
selects the outcome, while a root claim is the handling of accumulated dividend output according to
that preference ([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Glossary: Root Staker](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-staker)).

Emission context explains why the dividend exists, but it does not replace the root-claim boundary.
Flow-based emissions and subnet reward accounting describe how value moves through the network; root
claims describe the later treatment of dividends available to Root Subnet stakers
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims)).

For readers, the safest phrasing is outcome-specific. Use Keep when the claim preserves alpha
exposure, Swap when the claim converts dividend output into TAO on Root, and root claim when the
sentence is about dividend handling rather than the original stake itself.

## Reader Boundary

Root claims do not report current claimable balances, current subnet dividend amounts, or the
current preference of a particular wallet. Those are live-state questions that depend on the
selected network, subnet, account, and observation time
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims)).

The page also does not replace staking instructions. It preserves the concept boundary: Root Subnet
staking can earn alpha dividends, and root claims describe whether those dividends are kept as alpha
or swapped into TAO and restaked on Root
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Glossary: Root Subnet/Subnet Zero](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero)).

## Environment Scope

Bittensor separates localnet, testnet, and mainnet environments. Root-claim examples from one
environment should not be treated as evidence for another because balances, dividend state, and
claim outcomes are environment-specific
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).

Localnet examples are isolated development examples. Testnet examples are shared non-production
examples. Mainnet root claims concern production Root Subnet staking and production dividends.

## Reader Boundary

Root claims should be read as dividend-handling vocabulary for Root Subnet staking. The claim type
sets how accumulated alpha dividends are handled after they are claimable; it does not rename the
original root stake, guarantee future dividend amounts, or describe a general unstaking action
([Root Claims](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/root-claims),
[Glossary: Root Staker](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-staker)).
