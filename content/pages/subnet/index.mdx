# Subnet

A subnet is a specialized market inside Bittensor for a particular kind of digital work
([Glossary: Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

The term names the context where miners produce work, validators evaluate that work, and incentive
mechanisms turn evaluation into emission outcomes.

## Market Scope

A subnet narrows Bittensor's work into a particular market rather than treating all useful work as
one undifferentiated task. Subnet documentation describes each subnet as having its own goal and
standards ([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction)).

This makes subnet a root concept for reading Bittensor. A subnet names where a specific kind of work
is requested and evaluated.

## Miner and Validator Roles

Miners and validators give a subnet its work-and-evaluation structure. Miners produce the requested
digital commodity, and validators measure whether that work meets the subnet's standards
([Glossary: Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

For readers, a subnet is not only a topic label. It is the setting where work is produced,
evaluated, and interpreted through Bittensor incentives.

## Incentive Mechanism Context

A subnet runs one or more incentive mechanisms that define the task, protocol, and evaluation path
for its market. Incentive-mechanism documentation places those parts together inside subnet design
([Glossary: Incentive Mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentive-mechanism),
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)).

Readers comparing subnets should therefore ask what work the subnet requests and how that subnet
evaluates responses.

## Relationship to the Network

Subnets are part of Bittensor, not separate blockchains. The introduction places subnets alongside
the blockchain, TAO, and open-source tools as parts of the platform
([Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction),
[Glossary: Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet)).

That distinction keeps subnet from being used as a synonym for the whole Bittensor network. A subnet
is one work market context inside the network.

## Multiple-Mechanism Context

A subnet is often discussed through one mechanism at a time, but Bittensor also supports more than
one incentive mechanism inside the same subnet
([Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Incentive Mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentive-mechanism)).

When that happens, rules and standards for one mechanism should not automatically be treated as the
whole subnet's only evaluation path.

## Development Stage Context

The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)
describes subnet development as moving from localnet to testnet and then mainnet. The subnet concept
applies across the Bittensor lifecycle: subnets can be developed in localnet for isolated testing,
exercised on testnet for shared non-production evaluation, and deployed on mainnet for live
operation with real emissions.

The [Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks) reference
separates mainnet, testnet, and localnet. Subnet examples or incentive outcomes from one environment
should not be read as representing production subnet performance in another environment.

## Each Subnet Is Selected by netuid

The [Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid) defines netuid as
the subnet identifier used when reading subnet-specific state. Subnet vocabulary names the work
market concept; netuid names which instance of that market is in scope, such as netuid 1 on mainnet
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

Readers comparing subnets should attach the netuid whenever they cite participant sets,
hyperparameters, or staking pools. The same subnet concept can exist on different networks with
separate netuid registries
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).

## Subnet Markets Use Local Pool and Emission Context

[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets) places each
subnet inside Bittensor's Dynamic TAO design with local staking pools and emission routing. A subnet
therefore names a specialized market with its own participant evaluation path, not a generic label
detached from pool or incentive context
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

Subnet reading should stay tied to that local market frame. Yuma Consensus and validator weights
describe how evaluation inside one subnet becomes emission outcomes for that subnet's participants
([Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).

## Distinction from Digital Commodity

A subnet and a digital commodity name a market and the output it produces. A subnet is the
specialized incentive market where miners produce work and validators evaluate it under an incentive
mechanism, while a digital commodity is the specific kind of useful work that market is built to
deliver, so the subnet is the structure and the commodity is the product that comes out of it
([Glossary: Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

- **Subnet** — the specialized incentive market for a kind of digital work.
- **Digital commodity** — the useful output that market is built to produce.

## Distinction from Emissions

A subnet is the per-netuid incentive market that organizes miner and validator activity, while
emissions are the newly created TAO and subnet alpha tokens that flow into the market after
consensus runs. A subnet is the market; emissions are the supply that funds miner incentives and
validator dividends in that market. The two terms describe different layers of the same
participation cycle: one names the per-netuid market structure; the other names the token flow that
distributes into that structure.

The two operate at different stages of the participation cycle. A subnet is the live
incentive-market structure for a kind of digital work; emissions are the flow of new TAO and subnet
alpha that consensus reads to pay miners and validators in that structure. The subnet organizes who
can participate and what work is scored; emissions determine what miners and validators are paid for
the recorded weights. The subnet is the market; emissions are what that market distributes
([Glossary: Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Glossary: Miner Incentive](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#miner-incentive),
[Glossary: Dividends](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#dividends),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

## Reader Boundary

Subnet should be read as the general concept for a Bittensor work market. It is not a status report,
quality guarantee, launch guide, or complete description of a subnet's rules
([Glossary: Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

The stable article-level point is that a subnet organizes specialized digital work, miner
production, validator evaluation, and incentive context inside Bittensor.
