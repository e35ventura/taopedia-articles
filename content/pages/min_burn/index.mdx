# Min Burn

Min burn is a per-subnet hyperparameter that sets the lower bound on the dynamic TAO burn required
to register a neuron on a subnet. Official documentation describes it as the lower bound for that
registration burn, gives a default of 0.0005 TAO, and marks it owner-settable.

References: [Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)

## How the Floor Works

Registering a neuron burns TAO, and that cost is dynamic: it rises as registrations occur and eases
back during quieter periods, moving between a lower and an upper bound. Min burn fixes the bottom of
that range, so the registration cost can settle toward it but not below it, keeping a baseline cost
even when demand is low.

Reference: [Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)

## Per-Subnet Setting

Min burn is one of the subnet hyperparameters that configure a single subnet, for example netuid 1,
and a subnet owner can adjust it. The value in force is per-subnet chain state and can differ from
the documented default, so the real floor should be read for the subnet in question.

References:
[Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters),
[Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept. It does not report the min burn value or the current registration
cost on any particular subnet; those are live chain state to check for the relevant netuid.

## Max Burn Caps the Upper Registration Cost

Subnet [hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters) pair min
burn with max burn. Min burn fixes the lower bound on the dynamic registration burn, while max burn
fixes the upper bound on that same moving cost. Registration price can rise with demand and ease
during quieter periods, but it is kept inside the range set by those two hyperparameters on each
subnet.

That range gives subnet owners two levers on entry economics. Min burn prevents the registration
cost from collapsing to zero when activity is slow, while max burn prevents runaway registration
pricing during intense demand spikes. Both values are owner-settable per subnet and should be read
from live chain state for the relevant
[netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid).

Min burn vocabulary names only the floor side of that band. Readers who want the full dynamic
registration picture also need the max-burn ceiling and the current burn value on chain.

References:
[Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters),
[Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register)

## Registration Assigns a UID Slot to the Hotkey

The [Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register) describes
registration as purchasing a [UID slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot)
on a subnet. The burn paid at registration secures that slot for the registering hotkey; the TAO
used in that step is sunk rather than returned to the registrant.

When a subnet is already at capacity, registration can displace the lowest-ranked existing
participant through the subnet's immunity and deregistration rules rather than simply failing. Min
burn still sets the minimum price for attempting that entry, but it does not by itself decide
whether a slot is available.

That keeps min burn in the economics layer and UID assignment in the participant layer. The
hyperparameter names the cheapest the dynamic burn can fall; registration decides which hotkey
occupies a subnet position once the burn is paid.

References: [Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register),
[Glossary: UID Slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot)

## Burned Registration TAO Enters Recycling

Registration burns TAO as part of securing a neuron slot. Official tokenomics documentation treats
burned TAO as value removed from circulating supply through the network's recycling-and-burning
mechanisms rather than as a fee returned to the registrant.

Min burn therefore shapes how expensive the minimum entry attempt can be, while the actual TAO
consumed at registration follows the dynamic burn in force at that moment. A higher live burn can
sit well above the documented min-burn default even when the floor itself stays unchanged.

Readers comparing subnet entry costs should separate the configured floor from the live burn and
from the fact that paid registration TAO is burned. Min burn is a hyperparameter bound, not a refund
schedule or a stake deposit.
