# Min Burn

Min burn is a per-subnet hyperparameter that sets the lower bound on the dynamic TAO burn required
to register a neuron on a subnet. Official documentation describes it as the lower bound for that
registration burn, gives a default of 0.0005 TAO, and marks it owner-settable.

References: [Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)

## How the Floor Works

Registering a neuron burns TAO, and that cost is dynamic: it rises as registrations occur and eases
back during quieter periods, moving between a lower and an upper bound. Min burn fixes the bottom of
that range, so the registration cost can settle toward it but not below it, keeping a baseline cost
even when demand is low.

Reference: [Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)

## Per-Subnet Setting

Min burn is one of the subnet hyperparameters that configure a single subnet, for example netuid 1,
and a subnet owner can adjust it. The value in force is per-subnet chain state and can differ from
the documented default, so the real floor should be read for the subnet in question.

References:
[Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters),
[Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept. It does not report the min burn value or the current registration
cost on any particular subnet; those are live chain state to check for the relevant netuid.

## Max Burn Caps the Upper Registration Cost

Subnet [hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters) pair min
burn with max burn on each subnet, for example netuid 1. Min burn fixes the lower bound on the
dynamic registration burn, while max burn fixes the upper bound. Registration price can rise with
demand and ease during quieter periods, but official documentation keeps that moving cost inside the
range set by those two owner-settable values.

That band gives subnet owners separate floor and ceiling controls. Min burn prevents the
registration cost from collapsing to zero when activity is slow, while max burn limits how high the
burn can climb during intense registration demand. The glossary describes the neuron registration
burn as bounded by `MinBurn` and `MaxBurn` with additional adjustment parameters such as
`BurnHalfLife` and `BurnIncreaseMult`
([Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register)).

Min burn vocabulary names only the floor side of that range. A reader who wants the full dynamic
registration picture also needs the max-burn ceiling and the live burn value for the subnet in
question.

References:
[Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters),
[Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register)

## Registration Purchases a UID Slot

The [Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register) describes
registration as purchasing a [UID slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot)
on a subnet by paying the dynamic registration burn in
[TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao). Min burn shapes how low that burn can
fall, while registration itself decides which hotkey occupies the subnet position once the payment
is made.

Official [Mining](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration) documentation uses
miner registration as a concrete example: a participant must register with a subnet to receive a UID
on that subnet. When a subnet is already full, registration can displace the lowest-ranked existing
participant through immunity and deregistration rules rather than simply failing.

That keeps min burn in the economics layer and UID assignment in the participant layer. The
hyperparameter names the cheapest the dynamic burn can fall; register names the subnet-entry action
that consumes that burn to secure a slot.

References: [Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register),
[Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)

## Registration Fee Recycling Moves Pool Alpha

The [Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register) states that
alpha tokens worth the current swap value of the registration fee are taken from the subnet's alpha
liquidity pool and recycled when a hotkey registers on a subnet. That recycling step is separate
from the [UID slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot) assignment itself.

Min burn still names only the TAO floor on the dynamic burn. The registration payment is not a
refundable deposit or stake balance the registrant can reclaim later. Official wording treats the
TAO side as a registration burn while also describing pool recycling of equivalent alpha value
during subnet entry
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

Readers comparing entry costs should keep those layers separate. Min burn is a hyperparameter bound
on how low the dynamic burn can fall, while register vocabulary covers the sunk TAO payment and the
recycling step tied to subnet pool accounting.

References: [Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)
