# Chain Identity

Chain identity is human-readable information recorded on-chain for a key, so the participant behind
it can be recognized rather than seen only as an anonymous address. Official documentation describes
a coldkey or hotkey carrying an on-chain identity made up of details such as a chosen display name
and related links.

References:
[Staking and Delegation Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## From Address to Recognizable Participant

By default a key is just an SS58 address, which says nothing about who controls it. A chain identity
adds a layer of meaning on top of that address: a chosen name and accompanying details that let
others tell one participant apart from another. The underlying key does not change; the identity is
descriptive information attached to it.

References:
[Staking and Delegation Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Why It Matters for Delegation

Recognition is most useful when stake is involved. When a TAO holder decides which validator to
delegate to, the documentation notes that delegate identities, including a display name and a
description, are shown while browsing delegates. That lets a delegator choose based on a known,
named validator rather than guessing from a raw address, so chain identity supports informed
delegation.

References:
[Staking and Delegation Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Validating in Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators)

## Where It Appears

Chain identities are meant to be read by the tools people use. Explorers and staking interfaces look
up the identity attached to a key and present a validator by its chosen name alongside details like
its stake and take, turning a list of addresses into a readable set of participants. The identity is
the bridge between the on-chain address and a name a person recognizes.

References: [Validating in Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/validators)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept at a high level. It does not enumerate every field an identity can
hold or describe how one is registered, and the identity attached to any particular key is live
chain state that can change or be absent entirely. A key with no chain identity simply appears as
its address.
