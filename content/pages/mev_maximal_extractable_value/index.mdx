# MEV (Maximal Extractable Value)

MEV, short for Maximal Extractable Value, is the value that can be extracted by controlling or
observing the order of transactions before they are included in a block
([Glossary: MEV](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#mev-maximal-extractable-value)).

In Bittensor, MEV risk arises when pending transactions in the mempool expose enough information for
another party to profit by acting ahead of or around them. This is most relevant for subnet pool
operations where transaction contents can affect pool prices.

## Mempool Visibility

MEV opportunities begin with mempool visibility. When a transaction is pending but not yet included
in a block, its contents may be readable. A party observing those contents can act on that
information before the original transaction settles
([Glossary: MEV](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#mev-maximal-extractable-value)).

This is the root of MEV risk in Bittensor. The value is not created by the attacking party — it is
transferred from the original transaction's sender to whoever acts on the ordering opportunity.

## MEV Shield Relationship

MEV Shield is Bittensor's encrypted mempool-protection mechanism. It addresses MEV risk by hiding
sensitive transaction details before block inclusion, reducing the information available to parties
looking for ordering opportunities
([Glossary: MEV](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#mev-maximal-extractable-value)).

MEV names the problem; MEV Shield names one mechanism designed to reduce that problem. The two terms
are related but distinct: understanding MEV explains why MEV Shield exists.

## Pool Context

MEV risk is most discussed in the context of subnet pool operations such as staking and unstaking,
where transaction ordering can affect pool prices. A transaction that moves a pool price creates an
opportunity for someone observing the pending transaction to act first
([Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)).

Price protection is a related tool that allows callers to set bounds on acceptable execution rates
for pool operations ([Price Protection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/price-protection)).

## Reader Boundary

MEV is concept vocabulary for value extraction through transaction ordering. It should not be read
as a consensus attack, a validator scoring issue, or a description of normal pool slippage
([Glossary: MEV](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#mev-maximal-extractable-value)).

The MEV concept applies most precisely to contexts where transaction order is visible before
finalization. It is less relevant for operations that do not involve pool pricing or mempool
exposure.

## Front-Running Targets Visible Pending Stakes

Official
[Managing Your Stakes](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/managing-stake-sdk)
documentation describes MEV attacks as exploiting the fact that pending transactions are visible in
the [Glossary: Mempool](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#mempool) before they land
in a block. When a pending
[Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking) transaction exposes
enough detail, another party can act on that foreknowledge rather than on the same pool conditions
the original sender expected.

The [Glossary: Front-Running](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#front-running)
names one ordering pattern tied to that visibility window. An observer who sees a stake before it
settles can buy subnet
[Glossary: Alpha Tokens](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-tokens) first,
pushing the pool price up before the victim trade executes at a worse rate.

Front-running therefore names a concrete MEV shape around visible staking activity, not a generic
label for any unfavorable pool outcome. The value is transferred through early action made possible
by pending visibility, which is why front-running vocabulary belongs with ordering foreknowledge
rather than with every case where a conversion returns a different amount than a static quote.

References:
[Managing Your Stakes](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/managing-stake-sdk),
[Glossary: Front-Running](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#front-running)

## Sandwich Attacks Surround the Victim Trade

Official
[Managing Your Stakes](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/managing-stake-sdk)
documentation also describes a
[Glossary: Sandwich Attack](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#sandwich-attack) in
which an observer buys before a visible victim trade and sells after it. That two-sided sequence
extracts value from the victim's execution rather than from ordinary reserve movement alone.

Sandwich vocabulary adds a second ordering shape beside front-running. Front-running names getting
ahead of one pending trade; a sandwich attack names surrounding that trade with an entry and exit
that profit from the price move the victim causes when the pool absorbs the larger trade.

Both patterns depend on predictable pending details in the waiting period before
[Glossary: Block](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#block) inclusion. MEV language
for sandwich attacks stays with that foreknowledge-and-ordering path, not with slippage that appears
because a single conversion moved through subnet pool reserves without an adversary reordering
around it.

References:
[Managing Your Stakes](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/managing-stake-sdk),
[Glossary: Sandwich Attack](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#sandwich-attack)

## Slippage Reflects Pool Movement, Not Ordering Foreknowledge

Official [Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage) documentation
defines [Glossary: Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#slippage) as the gap
between an expected amount and the amount received when staking or unstaking moves value through a
subnet pool. That difference comes from how the pool's TAO and alpha reserves change as the
conversion executes, not from another party observing a pending transaction and acting ahead of it.

Slippage can widen when the conversion is large relative to available pool liquidity because the
reserve relationship moves more during the trade. MEV vocabulary names value extracted by
controlling or observing transaction order before inclusion
([Glossary: MEV](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#mev-maximal-extractable-value)).
The two labels therefore answer different questions even when both appear around staking activity.

A staker can face slippage from pool mechanics alone while no ordering attack occurs, and MEV risk
can appear when pending details let someone reorder around a trade. Keeping slippage with reserve
movement and MEV with pre-inclusion ordering foreknowledge avoids treating every worse-than-expected
fill as an MEV event.

References: [Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage),
[Glossary: Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#slippage)
