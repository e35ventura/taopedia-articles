# Proposal

A proposal is a Bittensor governance item put forward by the Triumvirate for Senate review. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#proposal) defines it as a suggestion
or plan put forward by the Triumvirate for the Senate to vote on.

## Governance Context

The [Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance) describes the same separation
of roles: the Triumvirate creates proposals, and the Senate approves or rejects them. In Taopedia,
the useful boundary is that a proposal is the item being reviewed, while the Triumvirate and Senate
are the governance bodies around it.

## Bicameral Context

The
[Glossary: Bicameral Legislature](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bicameral-legislature)
describes Bittensor governance as a two-tier system comprising the Triumvirate and Senate for
proposal approval. A proposal is the item that moves through that two-body structure.

This keeps the term narrower than governance itself. Governance names the broader protocol area,
bicameral legislature names the review design, and proposal names the specific item being submitted
for that review.

References:
[Glossary: Bicameral Legislature](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bicameral-legislature),
[Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance)

## Review Lifecycle

A proposal moves through governance in stages rather than taking effect when it first appears. The
[Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance) explains that a proposal must
receive Senate majority approval and then be closed by a Triumvirate member before it is executed.
This makes proposal review a two-body process instead of a single-party action.

Proposal creation, Senate approval, and final closure are separate moments. A proposal can exist for
review before it has enough Senate support, and Senate support still needs the closing step
described in the governance documentation.

Reference: [Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance)

## Senate Review Boundary

The [Senate documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate) says anyone can view
proposals currently before the Senate, while voting is limited to senators. That makes a proposal
publicly inspectable even though its approval path depends on a specific governance body.

This distinction helps avoid two common mixups. A visible proposal is not necessarily an approved
proposal, and a Senate vote is not the same thing as the proposal itself. The proposal is the item
under review; Senate voting records how the review body responds to it.

References: [Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate),
[Glossary: Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#senate)

## Proposal Hash and Reference Records

The proposal hash is the reference used to identify a proposal during voting. The
[Glossary: Proposal hash](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#proposal-hash) defines
it as a unique identifier for a proposal used in the voting process. The hash points to the proposal
being reviewed; it is not the proposal text, vote result, or governance body.

When reading governance pages, this means a proposal hash is useful for disambiguation. It can
identify which proposal is being discussed without turning that identifier into a claim that the
proposal passed, failed, or executed.

References:
[Glossary: Proposal hash](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#proposal-hash),
[Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate)

## Security Context

The governance overview frames proposals as part of Bittensor's move away from single-key
administration. Before governance, privileged administrative changes depended on sudo authority.
Under governance, the documentation describes a stronger approval path involving both a Triumvirate
member and a Senate majority.

A proposal should therefore be understood as the reviewable wrapper around a privileged change
request, not as proof that the change is safe or already active. The security value comes from the
review process around the proposal.

References: [Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance),
[Glossary: Sudo](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#sudo)

## Relationship to Proposal Hash

A proposal is referenced on chain by its proposal hash. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#proposal-hash) defines the proposal
hash as the unique identifier for a proposal used in the voting process, so it is the reference for
the proposal rather than the proposal body itself. For readers, the proposal is the governance item
being reviewed, while the proposal hash is the identifier used to point at it during Senate voting.

References: [Proposal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#proposal),
[Proposal Hash](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#proposal-hash)

## Reader Boundary

This article does not describe any live proposal, proposal record, or approval outcome. Readers
should use official governance references for current proposal details and Senate review context.
