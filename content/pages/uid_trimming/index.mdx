# UID Trimming

UID trimming is a Bittensor subnet-management feature that lets a subnet owner reduce the number of
neuron UIDs registered on their subnet. The official
[UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming) documentation describes it as a
way to lower a subnet's UID count while compressing the remaining UIDs so indexing stays
consecutive.

## Why Subnets Trim UIDs

A subnet's UID count shapes how many neuron slots exist for miners and validators. The UID trimming
documentation frames the feature as owner-side control over that capacity, letting an owner shrink
an oversized subnet rather than carry slots that are not earning their place.

Trimming is designed to be safe for strong participants. The documentation states that high-earning
neurons and immune neurons are preserved, so trimming targets weak slots rather than removing
contributors indiscriminately.

References: [UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## How Trimming Selects UIDs

Selection is emission-based. The documentation describes sorting UIDs by emission score and removing
from the lowest emitters upward, while skipping UIDs that are temporally immune or owned by the
subnet owner. After removal, the remaining UIDs are compressed to the left so the index stays
consecutive, and associated storage is migrated and cleaned up.

The on-chain behavior behind this lives in the Subtensor
[subnet UID source code](https://github.com/opentensor/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/subnets/uids.rs).
For readers, the key idea is that trimming is a ranked cull of the weakest non-immune slots, not a
random reduction.

References: [UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming),
[Subtensor subnet UID source code](https://github.com/opentensor/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/subnets/uids.rs)

## Limits and Rate Limiting

UID trimming is bounded. The documentation states a trim rate limit of 216,000 blocks, roughly 30
days, so an owner can change the UID count only once in that window. It also notes configurable
minimum and maximum UID counts, with a current minimum trim target of 64 and a cap that immune UIDs
must not exceed a large share of the maximum count.

These bounds connect UID trimming to the broader
[Rate Limits in Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits) model, which
spaces sensitive chain actions over time.

References: [UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming),
[Rate Limits in Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits)

## Reader Boundary

UID trimming should not be read as a step-by-step owner guide, a live parameter quote, or a
recommendation about any specific subnet's size. The minimum count, rate limit, and immune-share cap
can change on chain, so current values belong in official references and live chain state. This
article describes the concept of reducing and compacting a subnet's UID set.
