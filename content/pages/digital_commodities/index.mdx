# Digital Commodities

Digital commodities are the useful outputs that Bittensor subnets are designed to produce. A subnet
sets the work target, miners produce work for that target, and validators evaluate the results
([Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

The term keeps attention on the product of a subnet. It is not only a label for token incentives or
for the software that performs the work.

## Subnet Work Context

A digital commodity is subnet-specific. Different subnets can target different kinds of useful
digital output, while still using Bittensor's shared role and incentive vocabulary
([Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

That subnet boundary matters because the commodity is not a universal object across the entire
network. It is the work category a particular subnet asks miners to produce and validators to judge.

## Miner Production

Miners are the role family that produces the requested work. Their output is meaningful inside the
standards of the subnet that requested it
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Mining and Validating](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners)).

This makes digital commodities a work-output concept. The article topic is the useful product a
subnet asks for, not the miner software or operator process.

## Validator Evaluation

Digital commodities need evaluation as well as production. Validators assess miner outputs under the
subnet's incentive rules and express those evaluations through weights
([Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).

That evaluation layer separates a digital commodity from an arbitrary file, answer, or service. In
Bittensor, the output becomes economically meaningful because the subnet defines how it is scored.

## Incentive Mechanism Context

The incentive mechanism links the commodity to rewards. It specifies what useful work counts, how
that work is evaluated, and how scores feed the reward process
([Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

This is why digital commodities sit near subnet creation and incentive design. A subnet is a market
for a defined kind of useful digital work, backed by a scoring and emission process.

## Multiple-Mechanism Context

Some subnets can use multiple incentive mechanisms. In that case, each mechanism can define a
separate work-and-evaluation path, so the commodity context should stay tied to the mechanism being
discussed
([Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Incentive Mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentive-mechanism)).

The broader idea stays the same. A digital commodity is the useful output a subnet asks for, while
the mechanism defines how that output is assessed and rewarded.

## Reader Boundary

Digital commodities should be read as Bittensor subnet-output vocabulary
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)). The stable
point is the relationship among requested work, miner production, validator evaluation, and emission
incentives.

This article does not rank subnets, measure output quality, provide mining instructions, or claim
that every submitted output is useful. Commodity claims should be read through the relevant subnet
and incentive mechanism.

## Development Stage Context

The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)
describes subnet development as moving from localnet to testnet and then mainnet. The digital
commodities concept applies across the Bittensor lifecycle: subnet work outputs can be developed and
tested in localnet for isolated evaluation, exercised on testnet for shared non-production
conditions, and produced on mainnet for live operation with real emissions.

The [Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks) reference
separates mainnet, testnet, and localnet. Digital commodity examples or evaluation outcomes from one
environment should not be read as representing production subnet performance in another environment.

## Commodity Range Extends Beyond AI

Official Bittensor documentation describes the platform as a producer of digital commodities that
are not limited to artificial intelligence alone. The frequently asked questions page states that
Bittensor supports commodities including AI inference, training, and infrastructure, as well as work
unrelated to artificial intelligence
([Frequently asked questions (FAQ)](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/questions-and-answers)).

The docs home page lists additional commodity families such as compute power, storage space, protein
folding, and financial markets prediction alongside AI inference and training
([Docs Home](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/)). Those examples show that subnet work outputs can
span different categories while still using the same miner, validator, and
[emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#emission) vocabulary.

That breadth matters when reading a digital commodity claim. The term names a subnet's useful
output, but the output type is defined by that subnet's work target rather than by one network-wide
AI template.

References:
[Frequently asked questions (FAQ)](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/questions-and-answers),
[Docs Home](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/)

## Subnet Mining Names Commodity Production

Bittensor reuses the word mining, but subnet mining is not block mining. Official FAQ guidance
states that mining within subnets has nothing to do with adding blocks to the chain and instead
concerns production of digital commodities
([Frequently asked questions (FAQ)](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/questions-and-answers)).

The [Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner) places
that production role inside a [subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet)
market. A subnet miner performs tasks given by subnet validators and produces the commodity that
subnet incentives reward.

When this article discusses miner production, it follows that commodity-production meaning rather
than proof-of-work block validation vocabulary from other blockchains.

References:
[Frequently asked questions (FAQ)](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/questions-and-answers),
[Glossary: Subnet Miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner)

## Subnet Task Names the Work Target

The [Glossary: Subnet Task](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-task)
describes a key
[incentive mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentive-mechanism)
component that defines the work subnet miners will perform. Official guidance states the task should
be chosen to maximize subnet miner effectiveness at the subnet's intended use case.

That wording separates two related terms. A digital commodity names the useful output a subnet
market values, while a subnet task names the work assignment miners receive inside the mechanism.
The commodity is the product vocabulary; the task is the work-definition vocabulary.

An incentive mechanism still connects both sides through evaluation and rewards, but the task label
keeps prose anchored to what miners are asked to do rather than to the broader output category
alone.

References: [Glossary: Subnet Task](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-task),
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)
