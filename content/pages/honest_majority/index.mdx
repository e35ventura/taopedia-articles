# Honest Majority

An honest majority is the security assumption behind Bittensor's consensus: the network produces
trustworthy results as long as most of the stake behind a subnet's validators is honest. Because
consensus forms around the position supported by a majority of stake, honest validators holding that
majority means the agreed outcome reflects honest evaluation. On a subnet such as netuid 1, that is
what makes the consensus dependable rather than something every participant must be trusted to get
right.

References: [Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)

## Consensus Follows Majority Stake

The documentation describes the algorithm forming consensus around the weights supported by a
majority stake fraction and reducing those that exceed that benchmark, weighting more trusted
validators more heavily and discounting the less reliable part of the signal. A validator scoring
far from the majority-stake position has its outlying input pulled back, so the agreed result is, by
design, the majority-stake view of how miners performed.

References: [Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)

## Why a Dishonest Minority Is Tolerated

Because outliers are clipped toward the majority, a minority of dishonest or careless validators
cannot drag the result to their preferred outcome: their deviating signal is exactly the part that
gets reduced. The mechanism therefore absorbs some bad actors instead of failing when any single
validator misbehaves, which is what lets an open network reach a dependable result without trusting
every participant individually.

References: [Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Glossary: Validator Trust](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-trust)

## Where the Assumption Breaks

The security holds only while honest stake stays in the majority. If dishonest stake were to gain
the majority, the same machinery that clips a minority would instead serve the attackers, because
consensus follows whoever holds the majority of stake. This is why acquiring or coordinating
majority stake is the underlying threat that collusion and stake-concentration concerns describe,
and why the cost of acquiring stake is part of what keeps consensus secure in practice.

References: [Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)

## Reader Boundary

This page describes the security model at a high level. The exact majority point and how each
validator's stake is weighted are governed by subnet parameters and the consensus algorithm, and the
real distribution of honest versus dishonest stake is live network state. The durable point is the
assumption itself: honest results depend on honest stake holding the majority, and the design
tolerates a minority rather than guaranteeing every validator behaves.

References: [Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)
