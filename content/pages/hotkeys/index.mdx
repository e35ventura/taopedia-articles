# Hotkeys (Bittensor)

A hotkey is the operational key in the Bittensor wallet model. It is used by participants to perform
routine, on-chain actions needed for roles like miners and validators.

References:
[Wallets (learnbittensor.org)](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/getting-started/wallets/),
[Working with Keys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/working-with-keys/),
[BTCLI Reference](https://docs.bittensor.com/btcli)

## Responsibilities

- Identify and authorize operational actions by network participants (e.g., register a
  miner/validator, submit evaluation weights).
- Interact with subnet services according to published mechanisms.

## Security model

- Hotkeys are designed to be used on online infrastructure (e.g., validator and miner nodes).
- They should not control funds; if compromised, the coldkey can rotate or revoke authority.

## Operational practices

- Use separate hotkeys for distinct roles or nodes to bound blast radius.
- Monitor activity and rotate keys if suspicious behavior is detected.
- Keep node environments patched and restrict access to hotkey material.

## Tooling

- btcli and the Wallet SDK support generating and managing hotkeys under a wallet.
- Registration and day-to-day actions are performed with the hotkey.

## Relationship to Multiple Mechanisms

Hotkeys authorize operational actions such as weight submission for miners and validators. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#weight-matrix) notes that each
incentive mechanism has its own weight matrix for independent consensus calculations.

For readers, a hotkey still names the operational key for subnet actions. When a subnet runs more
than one incentive mechanism, weight-related actions should be read with that mechanism's matrix
context in mind.

References:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Weight Matrix](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#weight-matrix)

## Relationship to Coldkey

Hotkeys and coldkeys address related but different parts of the Bittensor wallet model. A coldkey
controls TAO, stake, and ownership authority, while a hotkey is the operational key used for subnet
roles such as registration and weight submission. The
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) describes the
ownership key, and the [Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey)
describes the operational key controlled by that coldkey.

For readers, a coldkey answers who owns funds and stake, while a hotkey answers which operational
key performs day-to-day subnet actions under that ownership.

References: [Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey),
[Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey)

## Relationship to Hotkey-Coldkey Pair

Hotkeys and a hotkey-coldkey pair address related but different parts of Bittensor key vocabulary. A
hotkey names the operational key itself, while a hotkey-coldkey pair names the authentication
binding between that operational key and its owning coldkey. The
[Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey) describes the
operational role, and the
[Glossary: Hotkey-Coldkey Pair](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey-coldkey-pair)
describes the pairing used in staking and governance authentication.

For readers, a hotkey names the operational key, while a hotkey-coldkey pair names how that key is
bound to ownership authority.

References: [Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey),
[Glossary: Hotkey-Coldkey Pair](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey-coldkey-pair)

## Relationship to Neuron

Hotkeys and a neuron address related but different parts of Bittensor subnet participation
vocabulary. A hotkey is the operational key used for subnet actions, while a neuron names a
registered participant record inside a subnet. The
[Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey) describes the
operational key, and the
[Glossary: Neuron](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#neuron) describes a registered
subnet participant.

For readers, a hotkey names the operational key that performs subnet actions, while a neuron names
the participant record those actions help maintain inside subnet state.

References: [Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey),
[Glossary: Neuron](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#neuron)

## Relationship to Netuid

Hotkeys and a netuid address related but different parts of Bittensor subnet vocabulary. A netuid
selects which subnet context is in scope, while a hotkey is the operational key used when
participating inside that selected subnet. The
[Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid) places netuid at the
subnet level, and the [Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey)
describes the operational key used for subnet registration and day-to-day actions.

For readers, a netuid answers which subnet is being discussed, while a hotkey answers which
operational key acts inside that subnet context.

References: [Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid),
[Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey)
