# Multisig Wallets

A multisig — short for "multiple signatories" — wallet is a way of distributing responsibility for a
Bittensor coldkey across a set of separate wallets, called signatories. Instead of one key
controlling the funds on its own, an action must be approved by a threshold of the signatories
before it can go ahead. The docs describe this as protection against single points of failure, such
as the loss of one key or one signatory acting irresponsibly
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

## What It Represents

A multisig is conventionally described as **M of N**: N is the total number of signatories, and M is
how many must approve before an action is authorized. Any single signatory can propose an action,
but it does not take effect until enough of the others add their approval
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

The runtime also places an upper bound on N. The Subtensor constants reference lists
`api.consts.multisig.maxSignatories` with a value of `100`, so a multisig can distribute authority
across many signatories but not an unbounded set
([Subtensor Constants: multisig](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subtensor-api/constants#multisig)).

That threshold removes the single point of failure an ordinary coldkey represents. The docs frame
multisig as protection against both accidental loss of one key and one signatory acting against the
group's interest. Signatories can be different people or separate keys held by one person on
different secure workstations
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

Multisig changes who must approve an action, not what the action is. Protection depends on
signatories being genuinely separate; holding every signatory key in one place recreates the single
point of failure the pattern is meant to remove. The guidance fits beside coldkey and hotkey
workstation security: multisig makes one lost or misused signatory less decisive, but each signer
still needs careful key custody
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig),
[Coldkey and Hotkey Workstation Security](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-hotkey-security)).

The pattern is especially useful when compromise would be costly, such as coldkeys with subnet
creator permissions or coldkeys that control validator hotkeys backing significant stake. Spreading
control across signatories buys protection but also requires coordination before actions proceed
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

A multisig action follows a proposal, approval, and execution lifecycle. The proposing signatory
must provide a deposit that is returned once the proposal is approved or rejected. Intermediate
signatories may approve with hash-only approvals; the final signatory supplies call-data approval
before the call becomes executable
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

The constants reference fixes that deposit through two values: `api.consts.multisig.depositBase`,
listed as `132000000` rao (about 0.132 TAO), plus `api.consts.multisig.depositFactor`, listed as
`32000000` rao (about 0.032 TAO) for each additional signatory. The reserved amount therefore has a
fixed base and a per-signatory component, and it is released back to the proposer once the call is
approved or rejected
([Subtensor Constants: multisig](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subtensor-api/constants#multisig),
[Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

The proposer does not count as their own approver. The multisig guide specifies that the approving
signatory cannot be the same signatory who proposed the transaction, so a single participant can
start the process but cannot satisfy the threshold alone
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

Readers should keep the threshold count separate from that lifecycle. M answers how many approvals
are needed; the deposit and approval steps answer how a proposed call becomes submittable. Multisig
also does not change whether the underlying action is a transfer, staking operation, governance
vote, or another extrinsic — it only changes who must co-sign
([Wallets, Coldkeys and Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets),
[Subtensor extrinsics](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subtensor-api/extrinsics)).

## Development Stage Context

The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)
describes subnet development as moving from localnet to testnet and then mainnet. Multisig approval
mechanics can be tested in localnet and testnet without representing production coldkey custody on
mainnet, where threshold signing protects actual TAO and subnet positions
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

The [Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks) reference
separates mainnet, testnet, and localnet. A multisig example from one environment should not be read
as representing coldkey control in another environment.

## Distinction from Coldkey

A multisig and a coldkey name a control arrangement and the key it governs. A coldkey is the single
owning key that authorizes high-value wallet actions such as transfers and staking, while a multisig
distributes authority over a coldkey-level account across N signatories and requires M of them to
approve before an action executes, so a multisig is not a new key type but a threshold rule layered
over coldkey authority
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig),
[Wallets, Coldkeys and Hotkeys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/wallets)).

- **Coldkey** — the single owning key that authorizes high-value wallet actions.
- **Multisig** — an M-of-N approval rule layered over coldkey-level authority.

## Distinction from Pure Proxies

Pure proxies and multisig are related but different parts of Bittensor wallet-security vocabulary.
Multisig is the wallet pattern that requires approval from multiple signatories before an action
proceeds, while pure proxies are keyless accounts used as stable stand-in members within multisig
structures, allowing the shared wallet address to remain unchanged even when one signer rotates off
the team and another joins
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig),
[Understanding Pure Proxies](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies/pure-proxies)).

Threshold vocabulary and stable-identity vocabulary answer different questions. Multisig names how
many signatories must approve; pure proxies name stand-in slots that keep the shared address fixed
when membership changes.

- **Multisig** — shared control requiring multiple signatory approvals.
- **Pure proxies** — keyless stand-in members that preserve multisig address continuity.

## Distinction from Proxy Wallets

Multisig and proxy wallets are related but different wallet-security tools. Proxy wallets grant a
separate account limited, scoped authority to act on behalf of a protected safe wallet, while
multisig changes the approval model for the protected wallet itself by requiring a threshold of
signatories before an action is authorized
([Proxies overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/proxies),
[Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

A proxy relationship can reduce how often a safe wallet is used directly; a multisig reduces
reliance on any single signatory. Both raise security, but they place the control boundary in
different places.

- **Proxy wallets** — scoped delegation from a protected wallet to a less sensitive account.
- **Multisig** — M-of-N approval required on the protected wallet itself.

## Distinction from Signatory

Multisig is the M-of-N approval model for shared coldkey custody. A
[signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory) is one participant who can
propose or approve actions under that threshold
([Multisig](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig),
[Glossary: Signatory](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#signatory)).

- **Multisig** — shared custody model requiring multiple approvals.
- **Signatory** — one participant in the proposal and approval path.

Model vocabulary names the threshold pattern; signatory vocabulary names each participant in it
([Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey)).

## Distinction from Existential Deposit

A multisig and the existential deposit operate at different layers of an account's life: a multisig
is a custody arrangement that decides who must approve before a coldkey-level action proceeds, while
the existential deposit is the minimum amount of TAO an account must hold simply to exist on the
Bittensor blockchain. One governs authorization over an account's actions; the other governs whether
the account remains present on chain at all
([Glossary: Existential Deposit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#existential-deposit)).

- **Multisig** — a threshold rule for who must approve a coldkey-level action.
- **Existential deposit** — the minimum TAO balance an account must hold to exist on chain.

## Distinction from Regenerating a Key

A multisig and key regeneration both relate to surviving a lost key, but they answer different
questions. A multisig can be configured so that losing one signatory does not block the group,
provided the approval threshold can still be met by the remaining signatories, while regenerating a
key is the recovery process of recreating a lost or deleted coldkey or hotkey from its associated
mnemonic
([Glossary: Regenerating a Key](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#regenerating-a-key)).

- **Multisig** — a threshold rule that, when enough signatories remain to meet it, keeps one lost
  signatory from blocking the group.
- **Regenerating a key** — recreates a lost coldkey or hotkey from its mnemonic.

## Distinction from Yuma Consensus

A multisig is a threshold rule for shared coldkey control that requires multiple signatory approvals
before an action proceeds. It names custody authorization, not the on-chain step that turns
validator weight submissions into emission shares
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).

Yuma Consensus is the on-chain process that converts submitted validator weights into emission
shares after evaluation completes. It settles scoring outcomes on a subnet; it does not by itself
define who must co-sign coldkey-level extrinsics
([Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

Readers should keep shared custody vocabulary separate from the settlement step. Multisig answers
how many signatories must approve a coldkey action; Yuma Consensus answers how validator weights
become emission shares in a subnet
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)).

- **Multisig** — threshold rule for shared coldkey approval.
- **Yuma Consensus** — on-chain weight aggregation that settles emissions.

## Reader Boundary

Multisig should not be read as a subnet role, a proxy delegation pattern, or proof that any one
signatory can act alone. It names shared coldkey control that requires a threshold of approvals
before an action proceeds
([Secure a Coldkey with a Multisig Wallet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/multisig)).
