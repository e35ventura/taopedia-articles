Quantum Compute is Bittensor Subnet 48, a subnet that makes access to physical quantum computers
available through Bittensor. Unlike subnets where miners run software models, here miners must bring
real quantum hardware: users submit quantum circuits, miners execute them on actual quantum
processors, and the results are returned over the network. The incentive-mechanism code is
maintained in the
[qbittensor-labs/quantum-compute](https://github.com/qbittensor-labs/quantum-compute) repository.

## What the Subnet Provides

The subnet's product is quantum execution capacity. Quantum computers are scarce and costly to use,
which puts experimentation out of reach for most people; routing access through Bittensor's
incentive mechanism is intended to make that capacity broadly available, in many cases free at a
base level. Users express work as gate-based quantum circuits in the OpenQASM format and broadcast
them to the network for execution.

The repository describes a free public tier alongside paid premium options such as priority
execution and fully private, off-chain execution. In the free tier, circuits and their results are
broadcast to the network and are effectively public, which also turns the activity into a dataset
about how quantum hardware is being used.

## Miner and Validator Roles

Miners on Subnet 48 are unusual: a miner must own or operate a physical quantum computer capable of
running gate-based circuits in OpenQASM 2.0/3.0, or have a documented partnership with an
owner-operator. This makes the miner population small and hardware-bound rather than open to anyone
with a GPU. Miners receive submitted circuits, run them on their quantum processors, and return the
results.

Validators distribute work to miners and check the returned results so that the quantum capacity is
used faithfully; the repository holds the validator setup and verification details. On the rewards
side, the repository notes that emissions start mostly burned — beginning at a 90% burn with 10%
used to pay for quantum compute — and scale up with demand, and that the incentive paid out is
directed to the operators who settle costs with the hardware providers. Validator weights feed into
[[Yuma Consensus]], which converts them into the incentive split across miners and validators.

## On-Chain Identity

Live SN48 data, including metagraph state and alpha token pool information, is available on
[TaoStats](https://taostats.io/subnets/48). Readers can reproduce the subnet's registered identity
fields with the Bittensor CLI command documented in the
[btcli reference](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/btcli/#btcli-subnets-get-identity):
`btcli subnets get-identity --netuid 48 --network finney`.

The Finney subnet identity for netuid 48 points to the
[qbittensor-labs/quantum-compute](https://github.com/qbittensor-labs/quantum-compute) repository,
which is the source of truth for the execution and reward mechanism described above.
