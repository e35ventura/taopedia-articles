# Moving Stake

Moving stake is the process of transferring existing stake from one hotkey to another without
withdrawing it to a coldkey first. The
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
describes staking as attaching TAO to a validator hotkey, and moving stake is the operation that
rearranges that attachment between hotkeys directly.

In Taopedia terms, moving stake names a stake-rearrangement process, not a new stake amount or a
withdrawal. It belongs alongside staking and unstaking as one of the three core stake operations.

## Why Moving Stake Exists

Stake can end up on a hotkey that a participant no longer wants to use — for example, after retiring
an old validator or consolidating stake onto a new delegate. The alternative to moving would be
unstaking the TAO back to the coldkey and then staking it again to the new hotkey. The
[Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage) documentation explains that
staking and unstaking interact with the subnet liquidity pool, so a round-trip through unstake and
restake can move the pool price and reduce the amount received.

For readers, moving stake exists to avoid that round-trip cost. It lets a participant change which
hotkey holds the stake without pulling TAO out of the subnet pool and pushing it back in.

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)

## What Moving Stake Does

Moving stake takes existing stake on one hotkey and places it on another hotkey within the same
subnet. The [Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake) describes
stake as the currency-token amount delegated to a validator UID, and moving stake changes which
validator UID that amount is delegated to without withdrawing it from the subnet.

For readers, the key behavior is that the stake stays inside the subnet's staking context throughout
the move. The total amount of stake in the subnet does not change; only the hotkey it is attributed
to changes. This is what makes moving stake distinct from unstaking, which removes stake from the
subnet.

References: [Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Relationship to Staking

Moving stake and staking are related but different stake operations. The
[Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking) describes staking as
attaching TAO to a validator hotkey to increase stake and consensus power, while moving stake
rearranges stake that is already attached.

For readers, staking names the act of adding new stake to a hotkey, while moving stake names the act
of changing which hotkey already-existing stake sits on. One increases total staked stake; the other
redistributes it.

References: [Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking),
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake)

## Relationship to Unstaking

Moving stake and unstaking are related but different ways to change where stake sits. Unstaking
withdraws stake from a subnet back to the coldkey, while moving stake transfers it to another hotkey
without withdrawing. The
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
frames both as stake-management operations within the broader delegation system.

For readers, unstaking names the removal of stake from the subnet, while moving stake names the
rearrangement of stake within the subnet. A participant who wants to change delegates without
withdrawing can move stake; a participant who wants TAO back in the coldkey must unstake.

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking)

## Relationship to Slippage

Moving stake and slippage are related because moving stake avoids the slippage that an
unstake-and-restake cycle would incur. The
[Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage) documentation explains that
staking and unstaking convert between TAO and alpha through the subnet pool, and each conversion
moves the pool price.

For readers, slippage names the price-impact cost of pool conversions, while moving stake names the
operation that sidesteps that cost by keeping stake inside the subnet. This is the main reason a
participant would move stake instead of unstaking and restaking.

References: [Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Relationship to Hotkey

Moving stake and the hotkey are related because the hotkey is the unit that stake moves between. The
[Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey) describes a hotkey as
the operational key that registers in subnets and performs network actions, and moving stake changes
which hotkey holds the stake.

For readers, the hotkey names the operational identity that stake is attributed to, while moving
stake names the operation that changes that attribution. A move has a source hotkey and a
destination hotkey, but the stake itself is the same value throughout.

References: [Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey),
[Glossary: Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake)

## Reader Boundary

This article defines moving stake as a concept. It does not describe step-by-step stake operations,
recommend when to move stake, or state current pool prices. Readers should use the official staking
documentation for operational detail and current conditions.
