# Nominate

To nominate is to stake TAO with a validator or delegate in Bittensor. The
[Glossary: Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate) describes
nomination as the staking process by which TAO holders participate through validators.

## What It Represents

Nominate names the on-chain action that attaches TAO support to a chosen validator hotkey. A
[nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator) is the holder who performs
that action; the [delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate) is the
receiving validator role on the other side of the same relationship
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

Readers should separate four terms that sound similar:

- **Nominate** — the action that stakes TAO behind a chosen validator hotkey.
- **Nominator** — the TAO-holder role that performs nomination.
- **Delegation** — the ongoing support relationship created by that action.
- **Delegate** — the subnet validator role that receives nominated stake.

Nomination increases or begins delegated support on a validator hotkey.
[Effective stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake) is the
combined total recorded behind that hotkey after nomination is included, counting delegate
self-stake and nominated support together
([Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake)).

Nomination and unstaking move support in opposite directions on the same delegation path. Unstaking
withdraws delegated stake back toward the nominator's coldkey and follows subnet and wallet rules
for the position; nomination is not proof that stake can immediately exit again
([Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking),
[Staking and Delegation: Unstaking back to TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation#unstaking-back-to-tao)).

[Delegation rewards](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation) follow
nomination rather than replacing it. Payout amounts still depend on validator performance, subnet
emission context, validator take, and the distribution pass on the relevant netuid
([Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution),
[Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-)).

The
[staking and delegation documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
uses delegation to describe staking TAO with validators. In Taopedia prose, nominate should be read
as delegation action vocabulary rather than as a subnet role, account count, or chain-state claim.

Nominate examples from one network environment should not be read as representing production staking
relationships on another
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).

## A Minimum Stake Is Required

Nominating is not free of a size requirement. The staking and delegation documentation sets a
minimum stake for nominators of 0.1 TAO, so a holder has to stake at least that amount behind a
validator for the nomination to take effect
([Staking and Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

## Default Validator Take Affects Returns

After nomination,
[delegation rewards](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegation) depend on
validator take at tempo distribution. The staking and delegation overview documents a default
validator take of 18% on delegated-stake emissions, configurable by the delegate, so nominators
should expect the delegate to retain that share by default before the proportional split reaches
stakers unless that validator has set a different take
([Staking and Delegation Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

## Distinction from Nominator

- **Nominate** — action that attaches TAO support to a validator hotkey.
- **Nominator** — TAO-holder role that performs that action.

## Distinction from Delegation

- **Nominate** — action that creates or increases delegated support.
- **Delegation** — ongoing support relationship behind a validator hotkey.

## Distinction from Staking

- **Nominate** — glossary action label for a nominator attaching TAO to a delegate.
- **Staking** — general process of attaching TAO support to a validator hotkey.

## Distinction from Delegate

- **Nominate** — support action on the supplying side of the relationship.
- **Delegate** — receiving validator role that performs validation work.

## Distinction from Effective Stake

- **Nominate** — action that attaches TAO support to a validator hotkey.
- **Effective stake** — combined total recorded behind that hotkey after nomination.

## Distinction from Unstaking

- **Nominate** — attach TAO support to a validator hotkey.
- **Unstaking** — withdraw delegated stake back toward the owner's coldkey.

## Distinction from Validator Hotkey

- **Nominate** — delegation action attaching TAO to a validator.
- **Validator hotkey** — operational key receiving delegated support.

## Distinction from Nomination Applies Within a Subnet

- **Netuid** — selector for which subnet market is in scope.
- **Nominate** — delegation action attaching TAO to a validator hotkey within that context.

## Distinction from Delegation Rewards

- **Nominate** — act of assigning stake to a validator's hotkey.
- **Delegation rewards** — emissions earned from that stake, net of validator take.

## Reader Boundary

Nominate should not be read as a subnet role, a completed governance appointment, or proof that
emissions were received. In staking vocabulary it names the delegation action of attaching TAO to a
validator hotkey ([Glossary: Nominate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominate),
[Staking and delegation documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).
