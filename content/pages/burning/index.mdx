# Burning

Burning is the removal of tokens from spendable circulation. In Bittensor tokenomics, the term helps
readers distinguish tokens that are destroyed or otherwise removed from normal circulation from
tokens that are distributed, staked, transferred, or recycled through another mechanism.

References: [Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Tokenomics Context

Burning is a supply-side concept. It describes what happens to tokens, not who earned them or
whether a subnet is valuable. That distinction matters because Bittensor articles may discuss
registration costs, emissions, staking, and recycling in the same tokenomics area, but each term
describes a different movement or accounting treatment of TAO.

References: [Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Relationship to Recycling

Burning and recycling are related but not identical. Burning emphasizes removal from spendable
circulation. Recycling emphasizes reuse or redistribution through a protocol process. Keeping the
terms separate helps readers avoid treating every tokenomics sink as the same event.

Reference:
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Effect on Issuance

The [Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning) explains
that burned tokens remain counted in the record of token issuance even though they no longer
effectively circulate. Because they stay in that record, they are not re-emitted.

## Circulation Removal Context

The
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)
describes removal from circulation as tokens that exist in neither wallet nor liquidity pool and
cannot be transacted. Burning names one form of that removal rather than recycling tokens back into
the record of issuance for future emission.

References: [Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Relationship to Issuance

Burned tokens remain counted in issuance records even though they no longer circulate, which is why
burning is not the same as recycling back into emit-able supply. The
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)
contrasts removal from circulation with reuse through issuance accounting adjustments.

References:
[Glossary: Issuance](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#issuance),
[Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning)

## Relationship to Emission

Emission schedules describe how TAO enters the network over time. Burning reduces effective
circulation but does not automatically rewrite emission formulas in reader-facing glossary language.
Use [Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions) for schedule and distribution context.

References: [Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning)

## Relationship to Registration

Registration flows may require TAO payments that tokenomics articles later discuss alongside burning
or recycling depending on protocol treatment. Burning emphasizes permanent removal from spendable
circulation; registration articles describe why the payment occurred.

References:
[Glossary: Registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#registration),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Relationship to Miner Deregistration

Deregistration removes active participation context for a miner UID. Any associated token treatment
should be read from official lifecycle and glossary references rather than assumed to be burning.
Burning remains a supply-side removal concept, not a lifecycle status label.

References:
[Glossary: Miner Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#miner-deregistration),
[Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning)

## Relationship to Transaction Fees

Fees charge transactions and are described separately from burn sinks that remove tokens from
circulation. Readers should not treat every TAO outflow as a burn; fees have their own glossary entry
and operational documentation.

References:
[Glossary: Transaction Fees](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#transaction-fees),
[Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning)

## Relationship to Dynamic TAO

Dynamic TAO introduces subnet alpha mechanics that coexist with TAO supply discussions. Burning
addresses TAO circulation removal at the glossary level and should not be confused with alpha pool
movements described in subnet tokenomics articles.

References:
[Dynamic TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/dynamic-tao),
[Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning)

## Relationship to Liquidity Pools

Tokens held in liquidity pools are explicitly not in the burned-circulation state described by the
glossary pairing for removal from wallets and pools. Burning articles should be read with that
boundary so pool balances are not mislabeled as burned supply.

References:
[Understanding Subnets: Liquidity pools](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets#liquidity-pools),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Relationship to Staking

Staked TAO remains in staking contexts rather than being described as burned merely because it is
illiquid to the owner. Burning removes tokens from circulation entirely per glossary definitions;
staking locks value in protocol participation contexts.

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning)

## Relationship to Incentive Mechanism

Incentive mechanisms rank and reward subnet work. Burning does not evaluate miners or validators; it
describes token removal. Keep incentive articles for task evaluation and burning articles for supply
removal vocabulary.

References:
[Glossary: Incentive Mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentive-mechanism),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)

## Relationship to Subnet Weights

Subnet weights compare subnets for emission injection. Burning is not an input to subnet weight
formulas in reader-facing emission documentation. Cross-subnet allocation still belongs in emission
articles.

References:
[Glossary: Subnet Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-weights),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)

## Relationship to Flow-Based Emissions

Flow-based emissions read staking and unstaking flows for subnet allocation signals. Burning sits in
supply removal vocabulary and does not replace net-flow signals used at the injection stage.

References:
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution-across-subnets),
[Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning)

## Relationship to Alpha Distribution Ratio

Alpha distribution ratio articles discuss subnet alpha emission share language. Burning concerns TAO
circulation removal and should be kept separate from alpha ratio terminology unless official docs
explicitly connect them.

References:
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning)

## Relationship to Recycling (Expanded)

Recycling reintroduces tokens into issuance accounting so they may be emitted again, while burning
removes spendable circulation without that reuse path. The glossary pair
[Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning) is
the authoritative contrast: readers should decide which supply treatment applies before interpreting
network dashboards or token movement charts.

References:
[Glossary: Recycling](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Relationship to Issuance Halving Threshold

Long-horizon issuance articles discuss halving thresholds and schedule changes. Burning affects how
much issued supply effectively circulates but does not replace halving schedule definitions. Use
issuance references for schedule thresholds and burning references for circulation removal.

References:
[Glossary: Issuance](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#issuance),
[Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning)

## Relationship to Dividends and Incentives

Participant rewards flow through Yuma Consensus and distribution stages after emissions are injected.
Burning is not a dividend, incentive, or staking yield term. It is supply removal language only.

References:
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning)

## Relationship to Conviction and Locked Stake

Locked stake keeps TAO in participation contexts with commitment parameters. Burned TAO is removed
from circulation per glossary definitions and should not be labeled as locked stake merely because
it is unavailable to spend.

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the term. It does not report live burn amounts, predict token price, or provide
investment advice. Readers should use current official sources when they need exact network values.
