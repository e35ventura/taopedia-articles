# Netuid

A netuid is the identifier assigned to a subnet within the Bittensor network. The official
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid) defines it as a unique
identifier for a subnet, and the official
[Subnet Metagraph](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/metagraph) page describes `netuid` as the
subnet's unique identifier within the network.

## Subnet Identifier

The useful reader-facing idea is that netuid names which subnet is being discussed. Bittensor has
many subnets, and each subnet represents a separate marketplace for a particular kind of work. The
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets) page explains
that subnets are distinct incentive-based marketplaces inside Bittensor.

In that setting, a netuid is not the subnet's brand name or description. It is the numeric subnet
identifier that lets documentation, tools, and data refer to a specific subnet without relying on a
human-readable name.

## Relationship to Subnet Data

Subnet data is interpreted in relation to a particular netuid. The
[Subnet Metagraph](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/metagraph) documentation lists `netuid`
as the field identifying which subnet the metagraph data belongs to. That makes the identifier part
of the context for reading subnet-specific information.

For Taopedia readers, the important boundary is that a netuid points to a subnet; it does not by
itself describe what the subnet does, who operates it, or how its participants are evaluated. Those
details require the subnet's own identity, documentation, and current source material.

## Reader Boundary

Netuid should be read as an identifier, not as a quality signal or a permanent project label. A
subnet's public name, website, repository, and description can change over time, while the netuid is
the network-specific slot being referenced. When precise current identity matters, readers should
check the subnet's current registered metadata rather than infer details from the number alone.
