# Trimming Would Exceed Max Immune Percentage

`TrimmingWouldExceedMaxImmunePercentage` means a subnet owner's UID trim on netuid 1 or another
named subnet would leave too large a share of protected immune neurons at the requested target
count. The Subtensor API errors reference summarizes it as "Trimming would exceed the max immune
neurons percentage"
([Subtensor API Errors: TrimmingWouldExceedMaxImmunePercentage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subtensor-api/errors#trimmingwouldexceedmaximmunepercentage)).

[UID trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming) lets a subnet owner shrink the
live neuron count toward a lower maximum, remove low emitters first, and compress survivors into
consecutive indices. Temporally immune UIDs and owner-controlled UIDs are skipped during that
process ([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming)).

## What It Means

UID trimming documentation states that the maximum number of immune UIDs must not exceed 80% of the
maximum UID count. The chain enforces that ceiling before low emitters are removed, so a trim cannot
proceed if the protected immune share at the requested target would violate the limit
([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming),
[Subtensor Constants: maxImmuneUidsPercentage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subtensor-api/constants#maximmuneuidspercentage-percent)).

`TrimmingWouldExceedMaxImmunePercentage` is that immune-share rejection. It means the owner-selected
target count would leave immune neurons at or above the documented ceiling once temporally immune
and owner-protected UIDs are counted, so the trim stops before any deregistrations run.

This is an immune-composition gate on the trim target. It does not, by itself, say anything about
trim timing cooldowns, whether the subnet exists, or whether the requested count is outside the
documented minimum and maximum UID bounds.

## Why It Appears

This error appears when a subnet owner submits a UID trim whose target maximum would still leave too
many protected immune slots relative to that target. That can happen when many neurons remain inside
the immunity window, when owner-protected UIDs already occupy a large share of the table, or when
the requested target is low enough that the surviving immune set would cross the 80% line
([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming)).

When debugging a failed trim on netuid 1, compare against other early trim rejections before
assuming a performance-selection problem. `InvalidValue` means the submitted target count falls
outside the allowed minimum or maximum UID bounds for the subnet. `HotKeySetTxRateLimitExceeded`
means a prior trim has not cleared the documented trimming cooldown yet
([Subtensor Standard Errors: InvalidValue](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subtensor-api/errors#invalidvalue),
[Chain Rate Limits: UID trimming rate limit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits#uid-trimming-rate-limit)).

The practical meaning is narrow: the owner must choose a different target count, wait for some
immune protection to expire, or otherwise reduce protected immune share before the trim can proceed.

## Distinction from Invalid Value

`TrimmingWouldExceedMaxImmunePercentage` and `InvalidValue` both appear on UID trimming calls, but
they test different inputs. `InvalidValue` means the submitted target UID count is outside the
subnet's allowed minimum or maximum bounds. `TrimmingWouldExceedMaxImmunePercentage` means the
target count is within range, but the protected immune share at that count would violate the 80%
ceiling
([Subtensor Standard Errors: InvalidValue](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subtensor-api/errors#invalidvalue)).

- **TrimmingWouldExceedMaxImmunePercentage** — immune neurons would exceed the allowed share at the
  target count.
- **InvalidValue** — the submitted target UID count is out of allowed bounds.

## Distinction from Hotkey Set Tx Rate Limit Exceeded

`TrimmingWouldExceedMaxImmunePercentage` and `HotKeySetTxRateLimitExceeded` both block subnet-owner
UID trims, but at different checks. `TrimmingWouldExceedMaxImmunePercentage` is the immune-share
rejection on the requested target count. `HotKeySetTxRateLimitExceeded` is the timing rejection when
another trim was attempted before the documented trimming cooldown cleared
([Chain Rate Limits: UID trimming rate limit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits#uid-trimming-rate-limit),
[Subtensor Standard Errors: HotKeySetTxRateLimitExceeded](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subtensor-api/errors#hotkeysettxratelimitexceeded)).

- **TrimmingWouldExceedMaxImmunePercentage** — too many immune UIDs would remain at the target.
- **HotKeySetTxRateLimitExceeded** — the trim was attempted too soon after the last trim.

## Distinction from Min Allowed UIDs

`TrimmingWouldExceedMaxImmunePercentage` and the documented minimum trim target both constrain how
far an owner can shrink a subnet, but they guard different properties. The minimum allowed UID count
is the floor below which a trim cannot target. `TrimmingWouldExceedMaxImmunePercentage` is the
separate immune-share ceiling that can block a target even when that target is above the documented
minimum of 64 UIDs ([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming),
[Min Allowed UIDs](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#minalloweduids)).

- **TrimmingWouldExceedMaxImmunePercentage** — immune share at the target would exceed 80%.
- **Min allowed UIDs** — the target count is below the subnet's documented trim floor.

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the meaning of the error label and the immune-share gate it represents. It does
not report how many immune UIDs a live subnet currently has or which specific neurons would survive
a trim on netuid 1; those depend on chain state at observation time
([UID Trimming](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/uid-trimming),
[Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).
