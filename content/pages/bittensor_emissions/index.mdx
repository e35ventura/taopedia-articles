# Bittensor Emissions

Bittensor emissions are the process by which new TAO enters the network and is allocated to useful
work. Emissions connect subnet demand, miner performance, validator evaluation, and staking into one
economic loop: subnets compete to attract value, miners compete to produce useful outputs,
validators evaluate that work, and stakers support validators.

References: [Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[[dynamic_tao|Dynamic TAO]]

## What emission means

Emission is not just a token release schedule. In Bittensor, it is also the reward process that
turns subnet activity into incentives for miners and validators. The broader system issues TAO, but
subnet-level evaluation helps decide where those rewards go once work has been measured.

References: [Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)

## Subnet allocation context

Subnets are specialized markets for different kinds of digital work. Their economic role matters
because emissions are not only a network-wide concept; they also move through subnet-level markets.
[[dynamic_tao|Dynamic TAO]] describes the broader tokenomics model that makes subnet demand part of
how Bittensor values useful work.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[[subnet_creation_mechanisms|Subnet Creation and Incentive Mechanisms]]

## Miners and validators

Within a subnet, miners produce the work the subnet asks for, while validators evaluate that work.
Emissions make those roles economically meaningful: miners can earn rewards for strong outputs, and
validators can earn rewards for evaluations that help the network allocate incentives.

References: [Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism),
[[mining_and_validating|Mining and Validating]]

## Stakers and validator rewards

TAO holders can stake to validators and take part in validator reward outcomes. This connects
staking decisions to the quality of validator work: a staker is not directly evaluating miners, but
their stake supports a validator whose evaluations affect subnet rewards.

References: [Staking/Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[[staking_and_delegation|Staking and Delegation]]

## Why emissions matter

Emissions are the bridge between Bittensor's technical work and its economic incentives. Without an
emission process, subnet work would not have a native reward loop. Without validator evaluation and
consensus, rewards would not be connected to measured miner performance. The usefulness of the
system depends on both pieces working together.

References: [Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## How to read emission claims

An emission claim should make clear whether it is describing network-wide issuance, subnet-level
allocation, miner incentives, validator rewards, or staking outcomes. These are related, but they
are not interchangeable. Exact reward calculations should be read next to the source that defines
the relevant process.

References: [Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)
