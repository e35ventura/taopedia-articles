# Sybil Attack

A Sybil attack is an attempt by a single actor to gain outsized influence over a network by creating
many separate identities that appear independent. In a Bittensor subnet, that would mean one party
registering many neurons or hotkeys, for example on a subnet such as netuid 1, hoping that
controlling more identities translates into controlling more of the rewards or consensus.

References:
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)

## What It Would Target

The goal of a Sybil attack is leverage from numbers. In Bittensor terms, an attacker holding many
UIDs might hope to sway the weights that feed consensus, or to capture a larger share of emissions
than an honest single participant. The attack only works if having more identities, on its own, buys
more influence.

References:
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)

## Why Identity Count Alone Does Not Win

Bittensor weights influence by stake rather than by headcount. The documentation describes Yuma
Consensus using the stake associated with each UID when it calculates emissions, so a crowd of
low-stake identities carries little weight. Gaining real influence still requires proportional
stake, which cannot be conjured simply by registering more keys. This stake weighting is a core
reason identity flooding does not pay off.

References:
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)

## The Cost of Each Identity

Numbers also are not free. Registering a neuron carries an economic cost, since registration
consumes TAO, so every additional identity an attacker creates has a real price. Combined with stake
weighting, this means a Sybil attempt pays a growing cost for identities that, without matching
stake, add little influence. The economics are arranged so that flooding a subnet with identities is
expensive and weak rather than cheap and powerful.

References:
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism),
[Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept and Bittensor's economic resistance at a high level. It does not claim
attacks are impossible, quantify any specific defense, or describe how to attempt one. The exact
registration cost and stake distribution that determine how strong the resistance is on any subnet
are live chain state and vary over time.

References:
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)

## Relationship to Collusion

A Sybil attack and collusion are related but different safety concerns in Bittensor vocabulary. A
Sybil attack names one actor creating many separate identities to seek outsized influence, while
collusion names coordinated validators trying to push consensus toward favored miners beyond honest
evaluation. The [Glossary: Collusion](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#collusion)
frames coordinated validator manipulation, and
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)
describes stake-weighted consensus that limits influence from identity count alone.

For readers, a Sybil attack names identity-flooding leverage, while collusion names coordinated
distortion of validator evaluation signals.

References:
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)

## Relationship to UID Slot

A Sybil attack and a UID slot are related but different parts of Bittensor subnet vocabulary. A UID
slot names a participant position inside a subnet, while a Sybil attack names an attempt to occupy
many such positions with identities that appear independent. The
[Glossary: UID Slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot) describes the
participant position, and
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)
describes registration cost and stake weighting that limit influence from headcount alone.

For readers, a UID slot names one subnet position, while a Sybil attack names flooding many
positions to seek leverage from numbers.

References: [Glossary: UID Slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot),
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)

## Relationship to Immunity Period

A Sybil attack and an immunity period are related but different parts of Bittensor subnet
vocabulary. An immunity period names a protected window after registration during which a new
participant cannot be immediately displaced, while a Sybil attack names identity flooding that would
try to exploit many registrations. The
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)
describes the protected registration window, and
[Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register) describes neuron
registration that consumes TAO for each new identity.

For readers, an immunity period names timing protection around new registrations, while a Sybil
attack names the economic motive to register many identities despite per-registration cost.

References:
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period),
[Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register)

## Relationship to Netuid

A Sybil attack and a netuid are related but different parts of Bittensor vocabulary. A netuid
identifies a subnet on the network, while a Sybil attack names an identity-flooding attempt that
would be discussed inside one subnet's participant and consensus context. The
[Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid) places netuid at the
subnet level, and
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)
frames subnet-specific registration and stake-weighted influence.

For readers, a netuid identifies which subnet market is in scope, while a Sybil attack names an
identity-flooding risk inside that subnet's participant set.

References: [Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid),
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)
