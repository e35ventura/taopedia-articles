# Recycle

Recycle is the burn-based way to obtain a UID slot on a Bittensor subnet: a registrant pays the
current registration burn in TAO rather than solving a proof-of-work puzzle. The glossary defines
register as the process of purchasing a UID slot by paying the dynamic neuron registration burn in
TAO ([Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register)). Mining
documentation labels this path burned registration with recycled TAO
([Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)).

The term names the payment path, not a subnet's task or a miner's later performance. It answers how
a hotkey buys entry into one subnet, and it is a single event: the mining docs state that once a UID
slot is secured on the main chain, the TAO is a sunk cost and cannot be recovered
([Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)).

## Recycle vs Proof-of-Work Registration

Bittensor exposes two neuron-registration extrinsics. `register` (call index 6) still takes
proof-of-work arguments (`block_number`, `nonce`, `work`), while `burned_register` (call index 7)
takes only the netuid and hotkey. In current Subtensor those proof-of-work arguments are unused
(underscore-prefixed) and `register` calls the same `do_register` as `burned_register`, so both
extrinsics now resolve to the same burn payment
([Subtensor `dispatches.rs`: register / burned_register](https://github.com/opentensor/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/macros/dispatches.rs)).

That matches the documentation, which lists `NetworkPowRegistrationAllowed` as a boolean defaulting
to `false` and marks it not used for neuron registration, because registration is burn-based and
always open
([Subnet Hyperparameters: NetworkPowRegistrationAllowed](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#networkpowregistrationallowed),
[Understanding Neurons](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/neurons)). On ordinary subnets today,
recycle is effectively the registration path, and the older proof-of-work flag is legacy vocabulary
rather than an active control ([[Network Pow Registration Allowed]]).

## Why It Is Called Recycle

The name comes from supply accounting. Recycling removes TAO from ordinary circulation but, unlike
burning, the amount is treated as re-emittable rather than permanently destroyed
([Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)).
That is a network-side accounting label, not a refund: from the registrant's side the payment is a
sunk cost that cannot be recovered
([Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)).

- **Recycle** — the deducted TAO can re-enter future protocol distribution rather than being removed
  for good ([[Recycling and Burning]]).
- **Burn** — the deducted amount stays unavailable for future emission ([[Burning]]).

## What Happens to the Payment

In the current implementation, `do_register` computes the current burn with `get_burn(netuid)`,
transfers it into the subnet, swaps it for alpha through `swap_tao_for_alpha`, and deducts the
resulting alpha from the subnet's outstanding alpha (`SubnetAlphaOut`). It then records the paid
amount per subnet through `increase_rao_recycled` and `record_tao_inflow`
([Subtensor `registration.rs`: do_register](https://github.com/opentensor/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/subnets/registration.rs)).
This alpha-swap treatment is Dynamic TAO behavior, so the recycle payment interacts with the
subnet's own alpha market rather than a single global pool ([[Dynamic TAO (dTAO)]]).

The per-subnet tally lives in `RAORecycledForRegistration`, which records the amount of RAO recycled
toward registration on a subnet and starts from the runtime constant
`initialRAORecycledForRegistration`, valued at `0`
([Subtensor Constants: initialRAORecycledForRegistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subtensor-api/constants#initialraorecycledforregistration-u64)).
The re-emittable accounting label above is a supply concept; the exact code path a specific netuid
follows is chain state, so live behavior belongs with the source and tooling rather than a fixed
article figure ([[Initial Rao Recycled For Registration]]).

## How the Burn Amount Moves

The recycle price is dynamic. The mining docs describe a burn that decays over time and increases
each time a registration succeeds, governed by `BurnHalfLife` (the decay horizon in blocks) and
`BurnIncreaseMult` (the per-registration step-up), with `MinBurn` and `MaxBurn` as the bounds
([Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)). In code,
`get_burn` is decayed each block in `on_initialize` and bumped immediately after each successful
registration by `bump_registration_price_after_registration`, so back-to-back registrations in one
block pay a rising price
([Subtensor `registration.rs`: do_register](https://github.com/opentensor/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/subnets/registration.rs)).

The hyperparameters reference lists `MinBurn` (default `500000` RAO, `0.0005 τ`) and `MaxBurn`
(default `100000000000` RAO, `100 τ`) as the lower and upper bounds of that dynamic burn
([Subnet Hyperparameters: MinBurn](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#minburn),
[Subnet Hyperparameters: MaxBurn](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#maxburn)).
Those figures are documented defaults; the live band is per-subnet chain state ([[Min Burn]],
[[Max Burn]], [[Burn Half Life]], [[Burn Increase Mult]]).

## Legacy Interval-Based Adjustment

An older model recalibrated a registration cost or difficulty each `AdjustmentInterval` by comparing
realized registrations to `TargetRegistrationsPerInterval`. The documentation now marks those
parameters, along with `Difficulty` and `MaxRegistrationsPerBlock`, as not used for neuron
registration and removed from the canonical `get_subnet_hyperparams_v3` API
([Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)). Recycle
demand response today is the continuous burn above, not interval difficulty, so those terms are
legacy vocabulary for readers encountering older material ([[Adjustment Interval]],
[[Target Registrations Per Interval]]).

## Root Subnet Excluded

Recycle applies to ordinary subnets, not the root network. `do_register` rejects the root netuid
with `RegistrationNotPermittedOnRootSubnet`
([Subtensor `registration.rs`: do_register](https://github.com/opentensor/subtensor/blob/main/pallets/subtensor/src/subnets/registration.rs)),
consistent with the accounting note that Subtensor rejects burn and recycle operations on the root
subnet
([Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)).

## Distinction from Burn Cost

Recycle names the neuron registration burn that buys a UID on a subnet that already exists. Burn
cost is a separate amount: the recycled TAO required to create a new subnet
([Glossary: Burn Cost](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burn-cost)).

- **Recycle** — dynamic TAO burn to claim a UID slot on an existing subnet.
- **Burn cost** — recycled TAO to open a new subnet market ([[Burn Cost]]).

## Distinction from Recycle or Burn

Recycle names how a registrant pays to enter a subnet. The `RecycleOrBurn` hyperparameter is a
different mechanism: it sets whether miner emission routed to a subnet's burn UIDs is recycled
through issuance accounting or burned as removed supply
([Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)).

- **Recycle** — the burn-based registration path into a UID slot.
- **Recycle or burn** — per-subnet supply treatment of burn-UID emission ([[Recycle Or Burn]]).

## Reading the Live Value

The mining docs document `btcli subnet register` as running in safe mode by default: it prompts for
a price tolerance and aborts if the burn exceeds it, and `--unsafe` bypasses that guard
([Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)). The live
burn in force on a subnet is per-subnet chain state and can be read for the documented example
netuid with `btcli subnet hyperparameters --netuid 1 --network finney`
([Subnet Hyperparameters: View hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters#view-hyperparameters)).

## After Registration

Recycle grants the UID slot; later lifecycle rules then apply to it. An immunity period protects a
newly registered UID from deregistration for a block-counted window
([Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)),
and deregistration can remove it once that protection lapses
([Mining: Miner deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-deregistration)). The
recycle payment starts participation; those terms describe what happens to the slot afterward
([[Immunity Period]], [[Deregistration]], [[Miner Registration]]).

## Reader Boundary

Recycle should be read as the burn-based registration path and the sunk-cost TAO payment that claims
a UID slot. It is not a refundable deposit, not proof-of-work, not a live price quote, and not the
subnet-creation burn cost. The live burn is per-subnet chain state that belongs with the relevant
source or tool at the time of observation
([Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration),
[Subnet Hyperparameters](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-hyperparameters)).
