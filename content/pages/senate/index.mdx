# Senate

The Senate is the Bittensor governance body responsible for approving or disapproving proposals made
by the Triumvirate. The [Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#senate)
describes it as a group of elected delegates that reviews Triumvirate proposals.

## Governance Role

The [Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance) places the Senate after the
Triumvirate in the proposal flow: the Triumvirate creates proposals, and the Senate approves or
rejects them. The [Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate) documentation provides
the Senate-side context for proposal review and voting.

## Voting Mechanism

The [Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate) documentation describes how senators
cast votes on Triumvirate proposals: approval or disapproval votes are submitted using the
`btcli sudo senate-vote` command, and each vote is included in the next block after it is cast. This
places Senate voting within Bittensor's block-based state update model rather than taking effect
instantly.

## Relationship to Triumvirate

The Senate and Triumvirate are paired governance bodies, but they do different work. The
[Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance) describes the Triumvirate as the
body that creates proposals and the Senate as the body that approves or rejects them. The glossary
also defines the Triumvirate as the proposal-creating group.

For readers, this means the Senate should not be described as the source of a proposal. Its role is
review: senators vote on proposals after the Triumvirate has put them forward. That separation is
the core governance distinction around this term.

References: [Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance),
[Glossary: Triumvirate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#triumvirate)

## Relationship to Proposal

A proposal is the item the Senate reviews. The
[Glossary: Proposal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#proposal) describes a
proposal as a suggestion or plan put forward by the Triumvirate for the Senate to vote on, while the
Senate article describes the Senate as the voting body.

For readers, the proposal is the governance object and the Senate is the review body. A statement
about Senate approval should therefore identify the proposal being reviewed rather than treating the
Senate itself as the proposed change.

References: [Glossary: Proposal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#proposal),
[Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate)

## Relationship to Proposal Hash

The proposal hash is the identifier used during Senate voting. The
[Glossary: Proposal hash](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#proposal-hash) defines
it as a unique identifier for a proposal used in the voting process, and the Senate documentation
places that identifier inside the voting flow.

For readers, a proposal hash points to the proposal under review. It is not a separate governance
body, a senator, or an approval result. This distinction helps keep proposal records, votes, and
governance actors separate.

References:
[Glossary: Proposal hash](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#proposal-hash),
[Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate)

## Relationship to Bicameral Legislature

Bicameral legislature is the structure that contains the Senate role. The
[Glossary: Bicameral Legislature](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bicameral-legislature)
describes Bittensor governance as a two-tier system comprising the Triumvirate and the Senate for
proposal approval. The Senate is therefore one side of that two-body review structure.

For readers, bicameral legislature names the governance design, while Senate names the proposal
approval body within that design. The terms are related, but the Senate article should stay focused
on Senate review rather than describing the entire governance system as if it were one body.

References:
[Glossary: Bicameral Legislature](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#bicameral-legislature),
[Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance)

## Relationship to Sudo

Sudo is the older privileged-administration model that governance reduces reliance on. The
[Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance) explains that administrative
actions previously depended on a single privileged key, while the governance protocol requires both
Triumvirate and Senate participation for proposal approval.

For readers, the Senate is part of the governance safeguard around privileged changes. It should not
be treated as the same thing as sudo access: sudo names privileged authority, while the Senate names
the approval side of the newer governance process.

References: [Governance Overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance),
[Glossary: Sudo](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#sudo)

## Reader Boundary

This article describes the Senate as a governance role. It does not list active senators, proposal
outcomes, or participation requirements. Readers should use official governance references for
current proposal records and detailed Senate context.
