# Hotkey-Coldkey Pair

A hotkey-coldkey pair is the authentication pairing that links a participant's operational hotkey to
the coldkey that owns stake and signing authority. The official
[Glossary: Hotkey-Coldkey Pair](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey-coldkey-pair)
describes it as the authentication mechanism for delegates and nominators and for delegates
participating in the Senate.

## Role in Staking Identity

Bittensor separates long-term ownership from day-to-day network operations. Wallet documentation
describes coldkeys as the key that controls TAO and stake, and hotkeys as operational keys used for
subnet roles. A hotkey-coldkey pair names how those two key roles are bound together for staking
participation rather than either key label on its own.

For readers, the pair is an identity construct: it says which operational hotkey acts under which
owning coldkey in delegation vocabulary. It is not a subnet miner UID, a validator permit label, or
a wallet backup file.

References: [Working with Keys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/working-with-keys),
[Coldkey-Hotkey Security](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/coldkey-hotkey-security)

## Delegation and Senate Context

The glossary ties hotkey-coldkey pairing directly to delegate and nominator roles. A
[nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator) supplies stake through
delegation vocabulary, while a
[delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate) receives that stake and
performs validation work. The pair is the authentication frame those roles use when hotkey activity
must remain accountable to the coldkey that holds stake.

The same pairing also appears in governance participation. The
[Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate) documentation describes Senate
membership requirements in terms of registered hotkey-coldkey relationships and delegate stake
context. Hotkey-coldkey pair therefore bridges staking identity vocabulary with Senate delegate
roles without naming a specific senator or live membership list.

## Contrast with Key Labels Alone

Coldkey and hotkey are individual key-role terms. The
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) and
[Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey) entries define each
role separately. Hotkey-coldkey pair adds the relationship term: which operational key is authorized
under which ownership key for staking and governance authentication.

This keeps three ideas distinct for readers: **coldkey** names ownership authority, **hotkey** names
operational authority, and **hotkey-coldkey pair** names their bound authentication relationship.

## Nominator Stake Bound to a Pair

When a nominator delegates stake, the delegation vocabulary ties economic participation to a
hotkey-coldkey authentication relationship rather than to a coldkey label alone. The glossary
describes the pair as the authentication mechanism for delegates and nominators, which means staking
actions that reference a validator hotkey still trace back to the owning coldkey that authorized the
relationship.

Readers should not collapse nominator identity into either key label separately. The coldkey holds
stake authority; the hotkey receives operational delegation context; the pair names how those roles
are bound for authentication in official staking documentation. Live delegation records show which
hotkey received stake under which coldkey-owned relationship. Changing validators therefore means
delegating toward a different hotkey identity, not merely renaming a coldkey.

References:
[Glossary: Hotkey-Coldkey Pair](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey-coldkey-pair),
[Glossary: Nominator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#nominator)

## Senate Delegate Pairings

Senate participation for delegates depends on registered hotkey-coldkey relationships rather than on
hotkey operation alone. The Senate documentation describes membership requirements in terms of
delegate stake and the registered pairing that links a delegate's operational hotkey to the coldkey
context that owns the staking relationship.

That keeps governance authentication aligned with staking identity. A reader discussing Senate
eligibility should treat the pair as part of delegate role vocabulary, while live senator lists and
stake thresholds belong in governance references and chain state rather than in this concept
article. The pair term does not replace reading current Senate membership rules in official
governance documentation.

References: [Senate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/governance/senate),
[Glossary: Hotkey-Coldkey Pair](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey-coldkey-pair)

## One Coldkey, Several Hotkeys

Wallet documentation allows multiple hotkeys under one coldkey for different subnet roles. A
hotkey-coldkey pair names one binding between a specific operational hotkey and its owning coldkey,
not a one-to-one wallet limit across the entire network. Each pair should be read as its own
authentication binding even when the coldkey owner is the same person or team.

Participants may hold several pairs under the same coldkey when they operate miners, validators, or
delegates on different hotkeys. Readers should specify which hotkey and which coldkey form the pair
under discussion, because delegation, Senate context, and subnet participation can differ per hotkey
even when ownership stays with one coldkey. Effective stake and delegation totals are evaluated per
hotkey within that pairing frame.

References: [Working with Keys](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/keys/working-with-keys),
[Glossary: Hotkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey)

## Relationship to Multiple Mechanisms

A hotkey-coldkey pair authenticates participants who stake to validators inside subnets. The
[Glossary](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms) notes
that subnets can run more than one incentive mechanism.

For readers, the pair still names staking identity at the key-relationship level. Mechanism count
belongs with subnet incentive design, not with the pairing term itself.

References:
[Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Multiple Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#multiple-incentive-mechanisms)

## Relationship to Effective Stake

Hotkey-coldkey pair and effective stake describe different parts of the same staking picture in
Bittensor. A hotkey-coldkey pair names the authentication relationship between the operational
hotkey and the ownership coldkey, establishing the identity under which a validator or nominator
acts. Effective stake is the aggregate stake that a validator's hotkey carries across its own stake
and all delegated stake. The
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake)
describes it as the total combined staking weight a validator position holds.

For readers, a hotkey-coldkey pair names the identity frame; effective stake names the quantity
associated with that identity in a subnet. A nominator delegates to the hotkey; that delegation adds
to the validator's effective stake in the subnet.

References:
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake),
[Glossary: Hotkey-Coldkey Pair](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#hotkey-coldkey-pair)

## Reader Boundary

Hotkey-coldkey pair should not be read as a wallet creation guide, a command sequence, or a
recommendation to register with any particular validator. It is a concept term for how operational
and ownership keys are paired in Bittensor staking and Senate authentication vocabulary.
