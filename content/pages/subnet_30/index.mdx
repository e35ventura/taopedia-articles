Endure Network is Bittensor Subnet 30. It runs a competitive market for financial risk intelligence,
where independent analysts submit risk parameters each epoch and validators score those submissions
against real market outcomes.

The first product built on the network is Forge, a Bittensor-native money market where users can
borrow TAO against their alpha tokens or lend TAO for yield. Forge consumes the network's consensus
risk output directly to set borrowing and lending terms.

## How the Mechanism Works

Each epoch, miners submit risk parameter recommendations covering how capital should be deployed in
financial markets. Validators compare those recommendations to what actually happened in those
markets during the epoch, measuring both safety and capital efficiency.

The scoring design creates competing pressures. Recommendations that are too conservative leave
capital idle and score poorly on efficiency. Recommendations that are too aggressive increase
protocol risk and score poorly on safety. The mechanism rewards miners who find the equilibrium
between the two, driving the network toward parameters that maximize utilization at an acceptable
risk level.

Weight vectors from validators are aggregated through [[Yuma Consensus]], and emissions flow to
miners in proportion to how accurately their submissions predicted real market behavior.

## Participating as a Miner

Miners on Endure act as independent risk analysts. They register on Subnet 30 and submit risk
parameter recommendations each epoch. Those recommendations are evaluated against real on-chain
capital behavior rather than a synthetic benchmark, so miners benefit from genuine domain expertise
in financial risk and lending market dynamics.

Because scoring rewards accuracy across multiple dimensions, the network is designed to accommodate
diverse methodologies. No single modeling approach dominates permanently; the network adapts as
market conditions shift.

## Participating as a Validator

Validators run the scoring logic that compares miner submissions to real market outcomes. Each
epoch, validators evaluate how accurately miners predicted the conditions that actually emerged,
score those predictions across the safety and efficiency dimensions, and submit weight vectors that
determine miner emissions through [[Yuma Consensus]].

Validators rely on the same real market data used to evaluate miners, which aligns validator
incentives with producing accurate assessments rather than favoring any particular methodology.
