# Impermanent Loss

Impermanent loss is the loss a liquidity provider can end up with when the price of the pooled
assets moves, measured against simply having held those assets instead. In Bittensor it applies
because each subnet runs as an automated market maker holding TAO and that subnet's alpha, and a
provider's position sits inside that pool while the alpha price moves around.

References: [Understand Price Protection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/price-protection),
[Liquidity Positions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#liquidity-positions)

## Why Providing Liquidity Carries the Risk

An automated market maker rebalances its pool as the price changes, which steadily leaves a provider
holding more of whichever asset is falling and less of whichever is rising. The documentation notes
that liquidity can be lost to organic price volatility, and this rebalancing is the mechanism behind
that: the provider's mix shifts against them precisely as the market moves.

References: [Understand Price Protection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/price-protection)

## Impermanent Until It Is Realized

The name points to the catch. While a provider stays in the pool the shortfall is only on paper, and
if the alpha price drifts back toward where it started, the gap can shrink or close. It turns into a
real loss when the provider withdraws while prices are still divergent, locking in the less
favorable mix. So the loss is potential rather than certain until exit.

References:
[Liquidity Positions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#liquidity-positions)

## How It Differs from Slippage

Impermanent loss is a provider's exposure to ongoing price drift, which sets it apart from slippage.
Slippage is the execution cost a trader pays on a single swap as their order moves the price,
whereas impermanent loss accrues to whoever is supplying the pool over time. One is a moment's cost
to a trader; the other is a drift risk carried by a provider.

References: [Understand Price Protection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/price-protection),
[Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept at a high level and is not financial advice. Whether any position is
in loss, and by how much, depends on live prices and on when a provider entered and exits, all of
which change over time. The durable point is the shape of the risk: supplying a pool trades exposure
to price drift for a share of pool activity.

References: [Understand Price Protection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/price-protection)

## Each Subnet Pool Prices Alpha Independently

[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets) describes
each subnet as operating its own automated market maker that holds
[TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao) and that subnet's
[alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha). The pool ratio sets the alpha
price inside that subnet, so a provider on netuid 1 is exposed to price drift in that subnet's
market rather than to a single network-wide alpha price.

That isolation explains why impermanent loss is subnet-specific vocabulary. A liquidity position
tracks how one subnet's pool rebalances as its own alpha moves against TAO, not how every subnet
token moves together.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Liquidity Positions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#liquidity-positions)

## Trading Fees Accrue to Pool Providers

The
[Glossary: Liquidity Positions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#liquidity-positions)
describes providers as earning a share of trading fees from swaps that pass through the pool. Those
fees are one documented counterweight to impermanent loss: ongoing pool activity can return income
even while the provider's token mix shifts with price movement.

Fee income and impermanent loss therefore belong to different sides of the same position. Drift risk
comes from how reserves rebalance; fee income comes from traders paying to move the pool price
through swaps ([User Liquidity Positions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/liquidity-positions)).

References:
[Liquidity Positions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#liquidity-positions),
[User Liquidity Positions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/liquidity-positions)

## Price Protection Governs Trader Tolerance Bands

Official [Price Protection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/price-protection) documentation
describes swap and stake actions that let a caller set bounds on acceptable execution movement. That
tool addresses how much adverse movement a single trade will accept, which is separate from a
provider's longer-running impermanent loss exposure.

A trader using price protection is choosing execution limits for one action, while a liquidity
provider is carrying reserve-mix drift until exit. The two risks overlap in pool mechanics but
answer different reader questions: tolerated execution for a swap versus hold-versus-exit outcome
for a position.

References: [Price Protection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/price-protection),
[Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)
