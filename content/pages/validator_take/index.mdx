# Validator Take

Validator take is the share of delegated-stake emissions that a validator keeps before the remaining
emissions flow to stakers
([Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

The term is a distribution concept. It describes a split inside delegated-stake emissions after the
validator-side amount already exists, not the consensus step that produced that amount.

## Emission Distribution Context

Validator take belongs inside Bittensor's emissions distribution flow. Validator emissions are
calculated through consensus first, and validator take applies later when the validator/staker
allocation is split
([Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

This makes validator take a distribution term rather than a separate reward source. Yuma Consensus
explains the validator-emission calculation; validator take explains how the delegated-stake share
is divided afterward.

## Delegated-Stake Boundary

Validator take concerns the portion of emissions tied to delegated stake. It should not be expanded
into a statement about every emission a validator may receive
([Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

For readers, the key relationship is between a validator and the stakers who delegated to it. The
take is the validator's kept share; the remaining delegated-stake emissions flow to those stakers
([Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

## Validator Emissions Boundary

Validator emissions and validator take describe different stages. Validator emissions come from the
Yuma Consensus path; validator take describes how the delegated-stake portion is shared after that
validator-side amount exists
([Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions),
[Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-)).

This keeps consensus outcomes separate from distribution splitting. Validator take is downstream of
the calculation that produced validator-side emissions, so it should not be used as shorthand for
validator quality or consensus performance.

## Multiple-Mechanism Context

When a subnet uses multiple incentive mechanisms, evaluation and bond context can be mechanism
specific. Validator take still names the later validator/staker fee share on delegated-stake
emissions
([Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

The mechanism rules decide subnet work and scoring. Validator take describes the later
validator/staker split after the relevant emissions context has already been established.

## Staking Context

Validator take belongs to the staking relationship between a validator and the stakers who support
that validator. Delegation describes the support relationship, while validator take describes the
validator-kept share of the delegated-stake emissions
([Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-)).

Delegate stake and effective stake describe stake context around a validator. Validator take
describes how the delegated-stake emission share is split later
([Glossary: Delegate Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate-stake),
[Glossary: Effective Stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#effective-stake)).

## Reader Boundary

Validator take should be read as reference vocabulary for emission distribution. It is not a
recommendation, validator quality score, or complete explanation of staking outcomes
([Glossary: Validator Take %](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-),
[Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions)).

The narrow meaning is the validator-kept share before remaining delegated-stake emissions flow to
stakers.

## Network Scope

The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)
describes subnet development as moving from localnet to testnet and then mainnet. Validator-take
examples should keep that environment attached because localnet, testnet, and mainnet have separate
chain contexts.

The [Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks) reference
separates those environments. A validator-take example from one network should not be treated as a
general result for another network.

## Validator Take Sits Inside the Forty-One Percent Slice

Official [Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)
documentation describes tempo-end distribution as splitting accumulated subnet rewards among
participants in fixed proportions. Miners receive 41% of that distribution, validators and their
stakers receive another 41%, and the subnet owner receives 18%.

[Validator take](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-) therefore
applies inside the validator-and-staker slice rather than across the full subnet emission total.
Yuma Consensus first determines how much of that 41% slice each validator earns from bonds and miner
outcomes
([Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions)).
Validator take then splits the delegated-stake portion of that validator-side amount before the
remainder moves toward stakers.

That placement keeps miner-side and owner-side shares separate from the validator-delegator fee
split. A change in validator take adjusts how one validator-side allocation is shared with its
delegators; it does not by itself redefine miner incentives or the subnet-owner share described in
the same distribution table.

References: [Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution),
[Yuma Consensus: Validator emissions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus#validator-emissions)

## Staker Payouts Follow Root and Alpha Paths

After validator take is applied, official emission documentation states that remaining
[TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao) and
[alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-tokens) are emitted to stakers in
proportion to each validator's holdings in each token
([Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution)).

On the [Root Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero),
stakers who delegated in TAO receive TAO emissions tied to the validator's TAO
[stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake) weight on that route. On a mining
subnet, alpha stakers receive alpha emissions tied to the validator's alpha stake weight there. A
validator with both root TAO and subnet alpha positions can receive both token types through that
downstream staker-emission step.

That token split is separate from the take percentage itself. Validator take names the
validator-kept share from delegated-stake emissions; the root-versus-alpha paths name which token
stakers receive after that share is removed
([Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)).

References: [Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution),
[Glossary: Alpha Tokens](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-tokens)

## Delegate Take Changes Are Rate Limited

The
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
states that validators and delegates can configure their
[validator take](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take-), with a default
of 18%. The glossary frames that rate as the fee percentage validators charge delegators for
validation services on the delegated-stake portion of emissions.

Configured take is therefore a validator-side parameter rather than a single fixed network constant.
Official [rate limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits) documentation adds a
separate pacing rule: delegate take changes are limited to once every 216,000 blocks, described as
roughly thirty days on mainnet. That cooldown applies to changing the take setting, not to the
periodic emission distribution where an existing take rate is already applied.

The limit matters when comparing live take rates across validators. Two validators may advertise
different take percentages today, but a recently lowered rate may still reflect an earlier setting
until the chain accepts another take change after the cooldown window.

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Rate Limits](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/chain-rate-limits)
