# Alpha Tokens

Alpha tokens are subnet-specific assets in Bittensor. They are separate from TAO and give each
subnet its own tokenomics context for emissions, staking, and liquidity pools
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Glossary: Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha),
[Glossary: TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

The term is broad alpha-side vocabulary. It names the subnet-specific asset family rather than one
pool reserve, one price, or one staking action.

## Subnet Asset

An alpha token represents value inside a specific subnet rather than across the whole Bittensor
network. TAO remains the network asset, while alpha tokens belong to subnet-level tokenomics
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

TAO and alpha-token language stay separate. A TAO supply or emission statement is not automatically
a statement about a subnet's alpha-token context.

The subnet label is part of the asset context. Two subnets can both use alpha-token vocabulary
without sharing the same pool, reserve, staking, or emission setting.

That subnet label is the main reader cue. Alpha-token statements need the relevant subnet because
the asset is not a single network-wide pool token
([Glossary: Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

## Emissions and Pools

Bittensor emission uses both TAO and subnet-specific alpha tokens. TAO enters subnet contexts, while
alpha tokens appear in subnet-level reward and reserve flows
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Emission: Alpha reserve injection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#alpha-reserve-injection)).

Subnets use paired TAO and alpha reserve contexts. Each subnet can be read as an automated market
maker with TAO and subnet-specific alpha reserves
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

That means alpha tokens are not only reward vocabulary. They are also part of the subnet pool design
that supports Dynamic TAO tokenomics. TAO is network-scoped; alpha tokens are subnet-scoped assets
used inside each subnet's emission and pool context.

## Reserve and Outstanding Context

Alpha reserve is related but narrower than alpha tokens. It points to the pool-held side of a
subnet's alpha context, while alpha tokens refer to the subnet-specific asset more broadly
([Emission: Alpha reserve injection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#alpha-reserve-injection),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

Alpha outstanding is another narrower term. It refers to alpha outside the reserve pool, while alpha
reserve refers to alpha inside the pool
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

Together, reserve and outstanding vocabulary split where subnet alpha sits. Alpha-token vocabulary
stays wider: it names the asset that can appear in either context.

Protocol alpha is another neighboring phrase. It describes the protocol-side alpha vocabulary used
in tokenomics references, while alpha tokens remain the subnet-specific asset frame for readers
([Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

It does not replace the reserve, outstanding, price, or halving terms.

## Staking and Delegation

Outside the root subnet, stake can be represented in a subnet's alpha units. Delegation places
subnet alpha in staking context, while slippage explains why pool conditions matter when TAO and
alpha interact ([Delegation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)).

Alpha-token staking language stays scoped. It describes subnet-denominated stake context, not TAO
itself.

When TAO and alpha interact through a pool, the pool context matters for how the relationship is
read. Alpha-token vocabulary belongs with the subnet and pool setting being discussed.

## Halving and Price Context

Alpha tokens also have subnet-level halving context. Halving mechanisms distinguish network-level
TAO halving from subnet-level alpha halving
([Halving Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/halving),
[Glossary: Alpha Halving](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-halving)).

That distinction matters because the word "halving" can apply at different asset scopes. A TAO
halving statement and an alpha halving statement identify different asset scopes.

Alpha price is another narrower alpha-side term. It is the TAO-denominated quote derived from a
subnet's TAO reserve and alpha reserve, while alpha tokens refer to the asset being priced
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

## Reading the Term

Alpha tokens are the right term when the focus is the subnet-specific asset. If the focus is
network-scoped TAO, TAO is more precise. If the focus is the pool-held side of the subnet asset,
alpha reserve is more precise. If the focus is cross-subnet emission share, subnet weights are more
precise
([Glossary: Subnet Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-weights),
[Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

Those distinctions keep alpha tokens focused on subnet asset vocabulary, not a staking walkthrough,
pool calculation, or full explanation of Dynamic TAO.

The term is not a shortcut for every alpha-side concept. Alpha price, alpha reserve, alpha
outstanding, and alpha halving each name a more specific part of the alpha-token system.

## Network Reading

Bittensor separates mainnet, testnet, and localnet environments. Alpha-token examples from one
environment do not automatically describe another because subnet pools, reserve values, and staking
conditions can differ
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Introduction to Bittensor: Subnet development](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)).

Localnet examples are isolated development examples. Testnet examples are shared non-production
examples. Mainnet alpha-token interpretation concerns production Dynamic TAO behavior.

The concise reading is: alpha tokens are subnet-specific assets used across emissions, staking, and
liquidity-pool contexts. TAO, alpha reserve, alpha outstanding, alpha price, alpha halving, and
subnet weights name narrower or neighboring parts of the tokenomics model.

## Root Subnet Has No Alpha Currency

The [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)
documentation describes the root subnet, also called Subnet Zero, as the only Bittensor subnet
without its own alpha currency. Validators can still register there, and TAO holders can stake to
those validators, but that stake remains in TAO rather than converting into a subnet-specific alpha
token
([Glossary: Root Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

That exception sets the outer boundary for alpha-token vocabulary. Alpha tokens name subnet-specific
assets tied to alpha subnets that run paired TAO-and-alpha pool context. Root staking belongs to
subnet-agnostic TAO language instead
([Glossary: Root Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero)).

When prose moves from root staking to a mining subnet, the alpha-token label should be reattached to
the specific subnet market being discussed rather than treated as universal across every subnet
index.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Glossary: Root Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#root-subnetsubnet-zero)

## Each Subnet Names Its Own Currency

Official subnet documentation explains that each subnet can carry its own currency name while still
sharing abstract alpha-token vocabulary across the network
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Glossary: Alpha Tokens](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-tokens)).

In concrete terms, one subnet may use a branded token label in interfaces and docs, while general
Bittensor prose still refers to that subnet's asset as its alpha token. When several subnets are
compared, documentation can attach symbolic labels to keep each subnet index tied to its own token
name ([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

That naming pattern keeps alpha tokens plural in practice. The term names a family of
subnet-specific assets, not one interchangeable network token like TAO
([Glossary: TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao),
[Glossary: Alpha Tokens](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-tokens)).

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Glossary: Alpha Tokens](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-tokens)

## Alpha Emissions Can Fund TAO Dividends

The [Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions) documentation describes a
distribution step in which TAO paid to stakers can be sourced by swapping part of a subnet's alpha
emissions back through that subnet's liquidity pool. Alpha therefore acts as both emitted subnet
reward and the input side for some TAO dividend flows
([Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

That swap path keeps alpha-token and TAO-emission vocabulary linked without merging the assets.
Subnet alpha still names the subnet-specific token being emitted, while the resulting TAO dividend
belongs to network-token distribution context
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Glossary: Alpha Tokens](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-tokens)).

Official emission prose also notes that validators holding both root TAO stake and subnet alpha can
receive both dividend types from the same broader distribution picture, so alpha-token statements in
reward prose should name which side of the payout is being discussed.

References: [Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)
