# Miner Registration

Miner registration is how a participant enters a Bittensor subnet as a miner. The official
[Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)
documentation describes registration as the step that gives a miner a UID on a subnet so the miner
can take part in that subnet's work and reward flow.

## Why Registration Matters

Subnets organize participants around UID slots. The
[Glossary: UID Slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot) defines a slot as
the position a subnet miner or validator occupies inside a subnet. Without registration, a hotkey is
not participating in that subnet's miner set.

For a reader, miner registration is therefore the entry point into subnet mining vocabulary. It does
not describe how well a miner performs, how much it earns, or which subnet is best to join.

## Miner Role After Registration

A [subnet miner](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-miner) produces work for
the subnet's task and receives evaluation from validators. Registration makes that role possible by
placing the miner inside the subnet's participant set. Official mining documentation treats
registration as a prerequisite before a miner can be evaluated and considered for emissions inside
that subnet.

This article does not describe mining setup, scoring formulas, or ranking tables. Those details
belong in official mining references and live subnet data.

References: [Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## Registration Cost Context

Registering a miner uses the same broad register vocabulary as other subnet participants. The
[Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register) describes subnet
entry as purchasing a UID slot through a dynamic registration burn in TAO. Official mining
documentation also notes that this cost changes over time.

Miner registration should not be read as a fixed price quote. It names the paid entry step that
connects a miner hotkey to a subnet UID slot.

## Immunity and Deregistration Context

After registration, a miner is not immediately subject to every low-performance removal path. The
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)
describes a grace window for newly registered participants, and
[Miner Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-deregistration) describes how a
full subnet can later replace a low-performing miner when a new registration arrives.

For readers, registration starts participation; immunity period and deregistration describe later
subnet lifecycle rules around that slot.

## Relationship to Register

Miner registration and register are related but different scopes in Bittensor subnet vocabulary. The
[Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register) describes subnet
entry as purchasing a UID slot through a dynamic registration burn in TAO, while
[Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration) describes
that entry process applied to a subnet miner who will take part in mining work and evaluation.

For readers, register is the general subnet-entry term, while miner registration names the
miner-specific case of that process.

References: [Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register),
[Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)

## Relationship to UID Slot

Miner registration and a UID slot are related but different parts of the subnet participation
lifecycle. The [Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register)
describes the subnet-entry process, while the
[Glossary: UID Slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot) describes the
participant position inside a subnet that entry grants.

For readers, miner registration names the miner-specific entry process, while a UID slot names the
position a miner occupies after that entry succeeds.

References: [Glossary: UID Slot](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#uid-slot),
[Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)

## Relationship to Netuid

Miner registration and a netuid are related but different parts of Bittensor subnet vocabulary.
Register describes the subnet-entry process that grants a UID slot, while a netuid selects which
subnet that entry targets. The
[Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid) places netuid at the
subnet-selection level, and
[Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration) describes
miner entry into a selected subnet.

For readers, netuid names which subnet market the miner is entering, while miner registration names
the miner-specific entry process inside that subnet context.

References: [Glossary: Netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid),
[Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)

## Relationship to Immunity Period

Miner registration and an immunity period are related but different parts of the subnet
participation lifecycle. The
[Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)
documentation describes miner entry into a subnet UID slot, while the
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)
describes a temporary protection window that begins after a participant registers into a subnet.

For readers, miner registration names the entry event, while immunity period names the grace window
that can follow that entry before ordinary low-performance removal rules apply.

References: [Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration),
[Glossary: Immunity Period](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#immunity-period)

## Reader Boundary

Miner registration should not be read as a recommendation to join any particular subnet, as a
command tutorial, or as a guarantee of emissions. It is the concept term for how a miner enters a
subnet UID slot and begins participating in subnet mining vocabulary.

## Relationship to Subtensor

Miner registration and Subtensor are related but different parts of Bittensor chain vocabulary.
Subtensor names Bittensor's layer 1 blockchain and system of record, while miner registration names
the subnet-entry process that places a miner inside a UID slot on that chain layer. The
[Glossary: Subtensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subtensor) describes the
blockchain layer, and
[Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration) describes
miner entry into a subnet UID slot.

For readers, Subtensor names the chain layer, while miner registration names the miner-specific
entry process recorded on that layer.

References: [Glossary: Subtensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subtensor),
[Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)

## Relationship to Extrinsics

Miner registration and extrinsics are related but different parts of Bittensor chain action
vocabulary. Extrinsics name signed transactions and calls submitted to Subtensor, while miner
registration names the subnet-entry outcome that a successful register action can grant to a miner.
The [Glossary: Extrinsics](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#extrinsics) describes
signed transactions submitted to the chain, and the
[Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register) describes subnet
entry as purchasing a UID slot through a dynamic registration burn in TAO.

For readers, extrinsics name the submitted chain actions, while miner registration names the
miner-specific entry process those register actions implement.

References: [Glossary: Extrinsics](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#extrinsics),
[Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register)

## Relationship to Mempool

Miner registration and the mempool are related but different stages of Bittensor chain activity. The
mempool names pending transactions before block inclusion, while miner registration names the
subnet-entry outcome after a register action is accepted and processed on chain. The
[Glossary: Mempool](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#mempool) describes the
pending-transaction context, and
[Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration) describes
miner entry into a subnet UID slot.

For readers, the mempool belongs to the pre-inclusion waiting stage, while miner registration names
the entry result once a register action succeeds on chain.

References: [Glossary: Mempool](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#mempool),
[Mining: Miner registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/miners#miner-registration)

## Relationship to Transaction Fees

Miner registration and transaction fees are related but different parts of Bittensor chain
vocabulary. Transaction fees name the costs attached to state-changing actions on Subtensor, while
miner registration names the subnet-entry process that includes a dynamic registration burn in TAO
under the register vocabulary. The
[Transaction Fees in Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/fees) documentation describes
fee-bearing actions, and the
[Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register) describes subnet
entry as purchasing a UID slot through a dynamic registration burn in TAO.

For readers, transaction fees belong to the cost model for chain actions, while miner registration
names the paid entry process that can grant a miner a subnet UID slot.

References: [Transaction Fees in Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/fees),
[Glossary: Register](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#register)
