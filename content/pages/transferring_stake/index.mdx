# Transferring Stake

Transferring stake is the operation of changing which coldkey owns staked tokens while keeping the
stake on the same validator. The
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
describes it as an operation that changes ownership of staked tokens from one coldkey to another
while the stake stays with the validator it was originally delegated to.

In Taopedia terms, transferring stake names an ownership-change operation, not a new stake amount or
a withdrawal. It belongs alongside staking, unstaking, and moving stake as one of the core
stake-management operations a TAO holder can perform.

## Why Transferring Stake Exists

A participant may need to change which coldkey holds their staked position — for example, when
migrating to a new coldkey, reorganizing wallet custody, or transferring a staked position to
another account. The
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
places transferring stake alongside moving stake and direct unstaking as the participant-facing ways
to rearrange an existing staked position.

For readers, transferring stake exists so that ownership of a staked position can change without the
participant having to unstake, receive TAO, and then stake again from the new coldkey. The stake
stays delegated to the same validator throughout the ownership change.

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## What Transferring Stake Does

Transferring stake changes the coldkey that owns a staked position while the stake remains with the
same validator. The
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
describes this as a single participant-facing operation: the staked tokens move to a new coldkey,
and the validator receiving the delegation does not change.

For readers, the key behavior is that the destination is a different coldkey, not a different
validator or a different subnet. The operation changes who owns the stake, not where the stake is
delegated. This is what makes transferring stake distinct from both unstaking (which withdraws the
stake) and moving stake (which changes the hotkey the stake sits on).

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Relationship to Staking

Transferring stake and staking are related but different stake operations. The
[Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking) describes staking as
attaching TAO to a validator hotkey to increase stake and consensus power, while transferring stake
changes the coldkey that owns stake that is already attached.

For readers, staking names the act of adding new stake to a validator, while transferring stake
names the act of changing who owns stake that is already there. One adds stake; the other reassigns
its ownership.

References: [Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Relationship to Unstaking

Transferring stake and unstaking are related but different ways to change a staked position.
Unstaking withdraws stake back to the coldkey so the TAO is no longer delegated, while transferring
stake moves the ownership of the staked position to a different coldkey without withdrawing it.

For readers, unstaking names the removal of stake from the validator, while transferring stake names
the reassignment of that stake to a new owner. A participant who wants TAO back in their coldkey
must unstake; a participant who wants a different coldkey to own the same staked position can
transfer.

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Staking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#staking)

## Relationship to Coldkey

Transferring stake and the coldkey are related because the coldkey is the unit whose ownership
changes during a transfer. The
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) describes a coldkey
as the security-critical key that controls TAO and stake, and transferring stake changes which
coldkey holds that control over a particular staked position.

For readers, the coldkey names the ownership identity for staked tokens, while transferring stake
names the operation that changes that identity. A transfer has a source coldkey and a destination
coldkey, but the validator and the stake amount stay the same.

References: [Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Relationship to Delegate

Transferring stake and a delegate are related because the delegate — the validator receiving the
staked TAO — stays the same during a transfer. The
[Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate) describes a
delegate as a subnet validator that receives staked TAO from delegators, and transferring stake does
not change which delegate receives that stake.

For readers, the delegate names the validator-side recipient of the stake, while transferring stake
names the operation that changes the coldkey-side owner. The delegate is unchanged; only the owning
coldkey changes.

References: [Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate),
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Relationship to Moving Stake

Transferring stake and moving stake are related but operate at different levels of the wallet model.
The
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)
describes both as stake-rearrangement operations: transferring stake changes which coldkey owns the
stake, while moving stake changes which hotkey the stake sits on.

For readers, transferring stake is a coldkey-level ownership change, while moving stake is a
hotkey-level delegation change. One changes who owns the stake; the other changes where the stake is
delegated. The two operations address different participant needs and should not be read as the same
rearrangement.

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation)

## Reader Boundary

This article defines transferring stake as a concept. It does not describe step-by-step transfer
procedures, recommend when to transfer, or state current stake amounts or fees. Readers should use
the official staking documentation for operational detail and current conditions.
