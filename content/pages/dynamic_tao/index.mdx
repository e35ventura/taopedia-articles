# Dynamic TAO (dTAO) in Bittensor

Dynamic TAO is Bittensor's subnet tokenomics model. TAO remains the network asset, while each
dynamic subnet has its own alpha token and liquidity relationship with TAO. That design lets subnet
demand, staking activity, and validator evaluation influence how emission context is allocated
across the network.

The official [Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions) documentation describes
Bittensor emission as distribution of newly created TAO and subnet-specific alpha tokens. The
[Staking explainer](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/navigating-subtensor/swap-stake) describes each
subnet as maintaining a pool with TAO and alpha reserves, where staking into a dynamic subnet
converts TAO into that subnet's alpha-denominated stake.

## What Dynamic TAO Changes

Dynamic TAO makes subnet-level assets part of the way Bittensor represents demand and rewards useful
work. A subnet is not only a topic area for miners and validators; it is also a tokenomics context
with its own alpha token, liquidity pool, staking flow, and emission path.

- **TAO remains the network asset.** TAO is the common asset used across Bittensor, including root
  subnet staking context and TAO-side liquidity in subnet pools.
- **Alpha tokens are subnet-specific.** Each dynamic subnet has its own alpha token, so a claim
  about emission, stake, or pool reserves should say whether it is talking about TAO, alpha, or
  both.
- **Subnet pools connect staking and liquidity.** Staking into a dynamic subnet moves TAO into that
  subnet's TAO reserve and credits alpha-denominated stake for that subnet.
- **Emission has subnet context.** Emission involves both network-level TAO and subnet-level alpha,
  then later participant distribution to miners, validators, stakers, and subnet creators.

## Participants and Roles

Dynamic TAO ties token flows to the same roles that make subnets useful.

- **Subnet creators** define the work a subnet is trying to measure and the incentive mechanism used
  to evaluate it.
- **Miners** produce the digital commodity requested by a subnet.
- **Validators** evaluate miners and submit weights that feed subnet reward distribution.
- **Stakers** move TAO into validator and subnet contexts and receive exposure to the reward
  outcomes of those choices.
- **Subnet owners** participate in the subnet-level emission picture described by the official
  emissions documentation.

## Emissions under Dynamic TAO

Bittensor emissions have two useful reading levels: subnet-level injection and participant-level
distribution. Injection describes how newly created liquidity enters subnet contexts. Distribution
describes how accumulated subnet rewards reach miners, validators, stakers, and subnet creators.

The current official emissions docs describe a flow-based model for determining subnet-level TAO
reserve injection. Under that model, subnet emission share is based on net TAO movement into and out
of subnet staking contexts rather than only on subnet token price. The
[Coinbase explainer](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/navigating-subtensor/emissions-coinbase)
describes this flow as part of the process that adds liquidity to subnet contexts before rewards are
distributed.

After injection, subnet rewards still have to be distributed. Validator weights and consensus remain
part of the participant-facing reward path, so flow-based emissions should not be read as a
replacement for miner scoring, validator evaluation, or staking outcomes.

## Concept Boundaries

Dynamic TAO combines several related tokenomics ideas. A clear explanation separates:

- network-level TAO from subnet-specific alpha tokens;
- TAO reserve injection from alpha reserve and alpha outstanding changes;
- subnet-level emission allocation from participant-level reward distribution;
- staking and unstaking flows from final miner, validator, and staker rewards.

## Why Dynamic TAO Matters

- **Subnets get their own economic context.** Alpha tokens and subnet pools let Bittensor
  distinguish network-level TAO from subnet-specific exposure.
- **Staking becomes a demand signal.** Flow-based emissions connect subnet emission context to net
  TAO movement from staking and unstaking activity.
- **Work and tokenomics stay linked.** Miners and validators still operate inside subnet incentive
  mechanisms, while Dynamic TAO supplies the token context in which those outcomes are distributed.

## Common Questions

- **Is dTAO a separate token?** No. Dynamic TAO is the model. TAO remains the network asset, while
  each dynamic subnet has its own alpha token.
- **Are alpha tokens the same as TAO?** No. Alpha tokens are subnet-specific assets used in that
  subnet's staking, liquidity, and emission context.
- **Are flow-based emissions final payouts?** No. Flow-based emissions describe subnet-level
  injection context. Participant rewards are distributed later through subnet reward mechanics.
