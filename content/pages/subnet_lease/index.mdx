# Subnet Lease

A subnet lease is the Bittensor arrangement that defines split profit ownership of a leased subnet.
The [Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans) describes leasing
as tightly coupled to crowdloans — a lease is created when a crowdloan successfully registers a
leased subnet, and the crowdloan contributors become the lease shareholders while the crowdloan
creator becomes the lease beneficiary.

In Taopedia terms, a subnet lease names a profit-sharing arrangement, not a separate chain action.
It belongs alongside staking (adding stake), unstaking (withdrawing stake), moving stake
(re-pointing delegation), and transferring stake (reassigning coldkey ownership) as one of the
participant-facing concepts that shape how value moves through a subnet.

## Why Subnet Leasing Exists

A participant who wants to launch a new subnet needs to fund its registration. A participant who
wants exposure to a new subnet may want to support its registration in exchange for a share of its
owner emissions. The [Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)
places leasing as the arrangement that lets a group of contributors collectively fund subnet
registration and then share in the resulting owner emissions, instead of a single sponsor paying the
registration cost alone.

## What a Subnet Lease Defines

A subnet lease defines two roles in a single profit-sharing arrangement:

- **Lease shareholders** are the contributors to the crowdloan that registered the leased subnet.
  Their share is proportional to their contribution.
- **Lease beneficiary** is the crowdloan creator. The beneficiary receives the lease's configured
  share of the subnet owner cut and any remainder after shareholder distribution.

The [Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans) describes the
distribution as pro-rata by recorded share, with any remainder going to the beneficiary, and the
emissions share configurable between 0% and 100% at crowdloan creation.

## Relationship to Crowdloans

A subnet lease and a crowdloan are related but different parts of the Bittensor subnet-creation
vocabulary. A crowdloan is the funding mechanism — a campaign with contributors, a cap, and an end
block. A subnet lease is the resulting profit-sharing arrangement once the crowdloan finalizes and
registers the subnet.

The [Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans) explicitly
distinguishes the two: crowdloans enable the funding, and leasing describes the split-profit
ownership that the crowdloan produces.

For readers, a crowdloan names the funding mechanism that produces a leased subnet, while a subnet
lease names the profit-sharing arrangement that the funding produces.

Reference: [Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)

## Relationship to Subnet Creator

A subnet lease is the alternative ownership model to a single subnet creator. The
[Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator)
describes a subnet creator as the role that defines a subnet's work and evaluation, and the
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans) places the lease
beneficiary in that creator-style position for a leased subnet.

For readers, a subnet creator names the single-owner model, while a subnet lease names the
split-owner model where the creator is the designated beneficiary among many shareholders.

References:
[Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator),
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)

## Relationship to Subnet Emissions

Subnet lease distributions are drawn from the subnet owner cut of the subnet's emissions. The
[Glossary: Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#emission) describes emission
as the process of generating and allocating currency to participants, and the
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans) explains that lease
distributions happen during coinbase distribution when owner rewards are paid.

For readers, subnet emissions name the broader allocation flow, while a subnet lease names the
specific profit-sharing arrangement applied to the owner-cut portion of that flow.

References: [Glossary: Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#emission),
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)

## Relationship to TAO

Subnet lease distributions are paid in TAO, not subnet alpha. The
[Glossary: TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao) describes TAO as the
network-wide cryptocurrency, and the
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans) explains that lease
emissions are swapped for TAO and distributed to shareholders in TAO form, not as subnet alpha.

For readers, TAO names the asset that flows to subnet lease shareholders, while a subnet lease names
the arrangement that converts owner emissions into that TAO flow.

References: [Glossary: TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tao),
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)

## Relationship to Coldkey

Subnet lease shareholders and beneficiaries are identified by coldkey. The
[Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) describes a coldkey
as the security-critical key that controls TAO and stake, and the
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans) explains that
contributions are recorded by coldkey and that lease distributions are paid to each shareholder's
coldkey during the lease.

For readers, a coldkey names the wallet identity that holds a lease share or beneficiary role, while
a subnet lease names the profit-sharing arrangement recorded against those coldkeys.

References: [Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey),
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)

## Reader Boundary

This article defines a subnet lease as a concept. It does not describe how to start a crowdloan,
quote current lease shares, or list live leased subnets. Readers should use the official crowdloans
documentation and the live chain state for operational detail.

Reference: [Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)
