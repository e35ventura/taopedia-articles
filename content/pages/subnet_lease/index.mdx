# Subnet Lease

A subnet lease is the split-profit-ownership arrangement that ties a leased subnet to a beneficiary
and a set of contributor shareholders. The
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans) describes leasing as
tightly coupled to crowdloans — a lease is the arrangement produced when a crowdloan registers a
leased subnet — and names "split profit ownership of a subnet" as its defining concept.

In Taopedia terms, a subnet lease names the profit-sharing side of the arrangement, not a separate
chain action. It belongs alongside staking (adding stake), unstaking (withdrawing stake), moving
stake (re-pointing delegation), and transferring stake (reassigning coldkey ownership) as one of the
participant-facing concepts that shape how value is attributed inside a subnet.

## Why Subnet Leasing Exists

A participant who wants to launch a new subnet needs to fund its registration. A participant who
wants exposure to a new subnet may want to support its registration in exchange for a place in the
resulting profit-sharing arrangement. The
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans) places leasing as the
conceptual arrangement that lets a group of contributors collectively fund subnet registration and
then share in the resulting emissions, instead of a single sponsor paying the registration cost
alone.

## What a Subnet Lease Names

A subnet lease names the two roles a leased subnet is tied to:

- **Beneficiary** — the participant identified by the lease as the principal owner-side party. The
  crowdloan creator fills this role when the lease is created through a crowdloan.
- **Shareholders** — the participants identified by the lease as contributors to the funding that
  produced the leased subnet.

These two roles, taken together, are what the source calls "split profit ownership of a subnet." The
arrangement itself is the lease; the two roles are its named parties.

Reference: [Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)

## Relationship to Crowdloans

A subnet lease and a crowdloan are related but different parts of the Bittensor subnet-creation
vocabulary. A crowdloan is the funding mechanism — a campaign with contributors, a cap, and an end
block. A subnet lease is the profit-sharing arrangement that a successful crowdloan produces.

The [Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans) explicitly
distinguishes the two: crowdloans enable the funding, and leasing describes the profit-sharing
arrangement the funding produces.

For readers, a crowdloan names the funding mechanism that creates a leased subnet, while a subnet
lease names the profit-sharing arrangement that the funding produces.

Reference: [Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)

## Relationship to Subnet Creator

A subnet lease and a subnet creator role are related but different parts of the Bittensor
subnet-ownership vocabulary. The
[Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator)
describes a subnet creator as the participant that defines a subnet's work and evaluation in the
ordinary single-owner model. A subnet lease names the split-owner model where many contributors and
a designated beneficiary collectively fill the owner-side roles.

For readers, a subnet creator names the single-owner model, while a subnet lease names the
split-owner model that a crowdloan-produced subnet lives under.

References:
[Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator),
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)

## Relationship to Subnet

A subnet lease and a subnet are related but different parts of the Bittensor vocabulary. The
[Glossary: Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet) describes a subnet as
the incentive-based competition market that produces a specific digital commodity. A subnet lease
names the profit-sharing arrangement that is tied to a leased subnet, but a lease is not the subnet
itself.

For readers, a subnet names the production market, while a subnet lease names the profit-sharing
arrangement that the subnet's owner emissions pass through.

References: [Glossary: Subnet](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet),
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)

## Relationship to Emission

A subnet lease and emission are related but different parts of the Bittensor reward vocabulary. The
[Glossary: Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#emission) describes emission
as the process of generating and allocating currency to participants. The
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans) places a subnet lease
as the arrangement through which the owner-side portion of a leased subnet's emissions is attributed
to the beneficiary and the shareholders.

For readers, emission names the broader allocation flow, while a subnet lease names the specific
profit-sharing arrangement the owner side of that flow passes through on a leased subnet.

References: [Glossary: Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#emission),
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)

## Relationship to Coldkey

A subnet lease and a coldkey are related but different parts of the Bittensor identity vocabulary.
The [Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey) describes a
coldkey as the security-critical key that controls TAO and stake. The
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans) names coldkey as the
identity that records a contributor's role in a lease and that receives lease distributions.

For readers, a coldkey names the wallet identity that holds a lease role (beneficiary or
shareholder), while a subnet lease names the profit-sharing arrangement recorded against those
coldkey identities.

References: [Glossary: Coldkey](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#coldkey),
[Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)

## Reader Boundary

This article defines a subnet lease as a concept. It does not describe how to start a crowdloan,
quote current emission shares, or describe the current state of any specific leased subnet. Readers
should use the official crowdloans documentation and the live chain state for operational detail.

Reference: [Crowdloans documentation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/crowdloans)
