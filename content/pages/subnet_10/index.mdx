# Subnet 10: Swap

Swap is Bittensor Subnet 10, maintained by the
[Swap-Subnet team](https://github.com/Swap-Subnet/swap-subnet). Its purpose is to channel on-chain
incentives toward participants who provide liquidity to [TaoFi](https://www.taofi.com/pool), a
decentralized exchange built on Bittensor's EVM layer that hosts a concentrated-liquidity TAO/USDC
pool. Miners earn SN10 alpha in proportion to the trading fees their pool positions collect, turning
passive market-making into an active participation layer settled by [[Yuma Consensus]].

The subnet's stated goal is to onboard users to Bittensor by giving TAO holders a financially
meaningful reason to interact with decentralized infrastructure built around the TAO token.[^1]

## How the Mechanism Works

TaoFi's TAO/USDC pool uses a concentrated-liquidity design: participants choose a price range and
supply both tokens within it. When trades execute at a price that falls inside a position's range,
that position earns a share of the swap fee. Positions whose range no longer brackets the current
price are inactive and collect nothing.

Validators measure each registered miner's contribution by pulling historical fee data from the pool
and summing the fees that each position earned during the preceding 24 hours. A position earns a
higher score by staying in range and capturing a larger share of trade flow. Validators emit weights
proportional to those 24-hour fee totals, which flow into [[Yuma Consensus]] to determine SN10 alpha
distributions for that epoch.

Because the scoring window resets daily, miners whose positions drift out of range lose standing
quickly. This creates continuous pressure to monitor market conditions and keep capital deployed at
the active price rather than sitting idle outside the spread.[^1]

## Participating as a Miner

Participation starts on the liquidity side. A miner needs an EVM-compatible wallet — the Swap team
recommends MetaMask — and enough capital to supply both TAO and USDC to the pool. The TaoFi
interface at [taofi.com/pool](https://www.taofi.com/pool) walks through connecting a wallet,
approving token spend, and depositing into the pool across a chosen price range.[^2]

Selecting a narrow price range around the current market price tends to earn more fees per unit of
capital when trades pass through it, but it also means the position falls out of range more often as
the price moves. Wider ranges sacrifice fee efficiency for more consistent in-range time. Miners
should note the numeric position ID assigned after deposit, as it is needed to claim rewards and, if
running the optional miner software, to associate the position with a registered hotkey.

Daily rewards from fee income are distributed automatically to the depositing wallet address without
any additional steps. Running the full miner software and registering a Bittensor hotkey is
optional; doing so routes SN10 alpha emissions to the hotkey instead of direct pool payouts. If a
hotkey is registered, the miner must submit a miner-type commitment on-chain to link the LP position
to that hotkey before the validator can attribute fees to the correct participant.[^2]

## Participating as a Validator

Validators connect to an archive subtensor node to retrieve historical pool state and calculate, for
each miner's registered position, the cumulative fees earned over the past 24 hours. That fee total
becomes the weight submitted to the subnet for that miner. Higher-fee positions receive
proportionally larger weights, directly linking liquidity productivity to SN10 emissions.

The Swap team strongly recommends running validators against a locally operated subtensor node
rather than the public finney endpoint, because the scoring logic requires reliable access to
historical blockchain data that the public endpoint may not serve consistently. Validators may also
connect a Weights and Biases project for monitoring validator performance over time.[^3]
