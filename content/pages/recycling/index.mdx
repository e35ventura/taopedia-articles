# Recycling

Recycling describes protocol reuse or redistribution of tokens rather than simple transfer between
ordinary wallets. In Bittensor tokenomics, the term is useful because some token flows are better
understood as protocol-level accounting than as user-to-user payments.

References: [Glossary: Recycling](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Tokenomics Context

Recycling is a supply-flow concept. It helps readers describe tokens that pass through a protocol
process and are reused or redistributed. That is different from burning, which emphasizes removing
tokens from spendable circulation.

References: [Glossary: Recycling](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling),
[Glossary: Burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burning)

## Why the Distinction Matters

Bittensor articles often discuss emissions, staking, registration, burning, and recycling near each
other. Keeping recycling separate from burning prevents readers from treating every tokenomics sink
as destruction. Recycling is about what the protocol does with a token flow after it enters that
process.

Reference:
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Total Issuance Context

The
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)
explains that recycled tokens are subtracted from the chain's record of token issuance, so the same
quantity can be emitted again. For readers, recycling names a supply-flow adjustment rather than a
permanent removal from spendable circulation.

References: [Glossary: Recycling](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Relationship to Issuance

Issuance records how much TAO the chain has emitted over time. Recycling adjusts that record by
subtracting recycled quantities so the same tokens can be emitted again according to official
glossary language. Burning, by contrast, leaves burned amounts in the issuance record. Readers
comparing supply terms should start from
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning).

References:
[Glossary: Issuance](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#issuance),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Relationship to Emission

Emission describes how TAO enters participant and subnet contexts over time. Recycling is a
tokenomics sink/recirculation concept that interacts with issuance accounting rather than replacing
the emission schedule itself. The
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions) overview remains the primary reference
for how emissions are described; recycling explains one supply-flow adjustment path.

References: [Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Glossary: Recycling](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling)

## Relationship to Registration

Subnet and neuron registration can involve TAO flows that later appear in recycling or burning
discussions depending on protocol treatment. Recycling vocabulary helps readers interpret those flows
as protocol accounting events rather than ordinary wallet transfers. Consult registration articles
for the operational steps and official glossary entries for supply treatment.

References:
[Glossary: Registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#registration),
[Glossary: Recycling](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling)

## Relationship to Miner Deregistration

Miner deregistration removes a UID from active subnet participation and can involve token flows
described in lifecycle articles. Recycling terminology may apply when tokens re-enter issuance
accounting rather than remaining destroyed. Keep deregistration as the lifecycle event and recycling
as the supply-flow interpretation.

References:
[Glossary: Miner Deregistration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#miner-deregistration),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Relationship to Transaction Fees

Transaction fees can move TAO through protocol processes that tokenomics articles discuss near
recycling and burning. Recycling names reuse through issuance accounting; fees are operational charges
on transactions. Readers should use fee references for tariff mechanics and recycling references for
supply reuse language.

References:
[Glossary: Transaction Fees](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#transaction-fees),
[Glossary: Recycling](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling)

## Relationship to Dynamic TAO

Dynamic TAO articles describe subnet alpha and liquidity mechanics that coexist with TAO supply
concepts. Recycling remains a chain-level issuance accounting term and should not be confused with
alpha pool conversions or subnet token pricing.

References:
[Dynamic TAO](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/dynamic-tao),
[Glossary: Recycling](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling)

## Relationship to Liquidity Pools

Liquidity pools hold subnet alpha in staking contexts described in subnet documentation. Recycling
addresses TAO issuance record adjustments at the protocol tokenomics layer. Pool balances and
recycling accounting answer different reader questions.

References:
[Understanding Subnets: Liquidity pools](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets#liquidity-pools),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Relationship to Alpha Tokens

Alpha tokens name subnet-local staking denominations in many articles. Recycling, as defined in the
official glossary pairing with burning, concerns TAO issuance accounting rather than renaming alpha
balances. Readers should keep subnet alpha vocabulary separate from chain-level recycling.

References:
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Glossary: Recycling](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling)

## Relationship to Incentive Mechanism

Incentive mechanisms describe how subnets evaluate and reward work. Recycling does not select miners
or validators; it describes a token supply treatment. Use incentive mechanism articles for task
evaluation and recycling articles for supply reuse terminology.

References:
[Glossary: Incentive Mechanism](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#incentive-mechanism),
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus)

## Relationship to Subnet Weights

Subnet weights allocate emissions across subnets using flow signals. Recycling is not a subnet
weight input; it is an issuance accounting concept. Comparing subnets for emission share still
belongs in emission and subnet weight articles.

References:
[Glossary: Subnet Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-weights),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)

## Relationship to Halving and Issuance Schedules

Issuance schedules and halving thresholds describe how emission limits change over long horizons.
Recycling adjusts recorded issuance when tokens are reused rather than defining the schedule itself.
Readers should consult issuance articles for schedule mechanics.

References:
[Glossary: Issuance](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#issuance),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Relationship to Existential Deposit

Existential deposit concepts describe minimum balances that prevent account reaping in substrate-style
wallet discussions. Recycling is unrelated to per-account minimum balances; it is a tokenomics reuse
term at the protocol supply layer.

References:
[Glossary: Existential Deposit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#existential-deposit),
[Glossary: Recycling](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling)

## Relationship to Staking and Unstaking

Staking moves TAO into validator-associated subnet contexts; unstaking reverses that movement. Neither
term is interchangeable with recycling, which adjusts issuance accounting for reused supply. Readers
tracing TAO from wallet to stake should use staking articles; readers asking whether tokens re-enter
emit-able supply should use recycling and burning glossary entries.

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Unstaking](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#unstaking)

## Relationship to Conviction and Locked Stake

Conviction and locked-stake articles describe time-bounded or commitment-sensitive staking behavior.
Recycling remains a chain-level supply reuse concept and does not describe lock duration or reward
multipliers on stake.

References:
[Staking and delegation overview](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/delegation),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)

## Relationship to Validator Take

Validator take is a distribution-stage fee concept on delegated emissions. Recycling does not set
validator take rates; it names token reuse through issuance accounting. Distribution articles are the
correct reference for take percentages.

References:
[Emission: Distribution](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#distribution),
[Glossary: Validator Take](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-take)

## Relationship to Dividends and Incentives

Dividends and incentives are Yuma Consensus outcome terms for validators and miners. Recycling does
not rank participants or set consensus weights; it belongs in tokenomics supply vocabulary alongside
burning and issuance.

References:
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus),
[Glossary: Recycling](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the term. It does not report live recycled amounts, predict token price, or give
investment advice. Readers should use current official sources when they need exact network values.
