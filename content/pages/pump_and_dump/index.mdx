# Pump and Dump

A pump and dump is a manipulation where someone drives an asset's price up to attract buyers, then
sells into that demand, leaving latecomers holding the decline. In Bittensor the asset would be a
subnet's alpha, whose price is set inside its pool, so the scheme plays out through ordinary buying
and selling rather than any special privilege.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## How a Pool Price Moves

A subnet's alpha price comes from the ratio of TAO to alpha in its automated market maker pool.
Buying alpha removes it from the pool and pushes the price up; selling does the reverse. The "pump"
is simply a holder spending to buy the price upward, hoping others read the rising price as a signal
and follow it.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## The Dump and Its Cost

The "dump" is the exit. The manipulator sells the position built up earlier, and because the pool
moves the price against large sells through slippage, unloading a big holding drives the price back
down, often onto whoever bought near the top. Any profit comes from the gap between a low entry and
a higher exit, reduced by the slippage paid on the way out.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)

## Why It Is Neither Free nor Certain

This is not costless. The pump ties up capital and pushes the price against the buyer as they go;
the dump pays slippage on the way back; and nothing guarantees that others follow the move. That mix
of capital, cost, and uncertainty sets a pump and dump apart from wash trading, where the same actor
fakes activity without taking a real directional position. Here the manipulator genuinely holds, and
must find buyers to sell onto.

References: [Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)

## Reader Boundary

This page defines the concept at a high level. It is not a claim that any particular subnet is being
manipulated, and it is not financial advice. Whether a move is even possible, let alone profitable
after costs, depends on live pool depth, prices, and how others act, all of which change over time.
The durable point is the shape of the scheme: inflate, lure, and exit, against real costs.
