# Alpha Reserve

Alpha reserve is subnet alpha held inside a subnet liquidity pool. In Dynamic TAO, the pool pairs
TAO reserve with a subnet-specific alpha reserve, so alpha reserve names the pool-held alpha side of
that pair rather than every alpha token associated with the subnet.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Emission: Alpha reserve injection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#alpha-reserve-injection)

## Pool-Side Meaning

Each subnet pool has two reserve sides: TAO reserve and alpha reserve. Alpha reserve is the
subnet-alpha side of that pair. It belongs to the automated-market-maker pool context, while other
alpha vocabulary can describe alpha outside the pool.

That location keeps reserve vocabulary precise. Alpha reserve is not total subnet alpha supply and
not the broad alpha-token category. It is the alpha quantity held in the paired subnet pool.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Emission: Alpha reserve injection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#alpha-reserve-injection)

## Accounting Role

The subnet economy can be read through TAO reserves, alpha reserves, and alpha outstanding. Alpha
reserve is the pool-held alpha in that grouping
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

A subnet can have alpha in reserve and alpha outside reserve at the same time. The reserve figure
names the pool side, while alpha outstanding names alpha outside the reserve context. That makes
alpha reserve narrower than total alpha supply.

The pool location matters because subnet staking and unstaking interact with the paired reserve
structure. Reserve language is therefore pool-accounting language, not a general statement about all
subnet alpha.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)

## Emissions and Injection

Alpha reserve injection adds alpha to the reserve side of the pool
([Emission: Alpha reserve injection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#alpha-reserve-injection)).

In the emissions overview, the injection stage adds TAO to a subnet's TAO reserve, adds alpha to the
subnet's alpha reserve, and sets aside alpha for alpha outstanding
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

That split separates reserve-side alpha from alpha allocated outside the pool. Alpha reserve
injection supports the pool side of subnet tokenomics, while outside-pool alpha belongs to other
emission contexts.

References:
[Emission: Alpha reserve injection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#alpha-reserve-injection),
[Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)

## Reserve Vocabulary

Alpha reserve and alpha outstanding describe subnet alpha by location. Alpha reserve is pool-held
alpha; alpha outstanding is alpha outside the reserve context
([Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

Protocol alpha is related to reserve injection, but it is not the same term. Protocol alpha refers
to alpha gathered by the protocol during reserve-injection activity, while alpha reserve is the
pool-held alpha side itself
([Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)).

These distinctions prevent pool-reserve statements from being read as total-alpha statements. The
same subnet asset can appear in reserve-side, outside-reserve, and protocol-held contexts, but those
contexts are not interchangeable.

References: [Emission](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Glossary: Protocol Alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#protocol-alpha)

## Liquidity Context

Alpha reserve names the pool-held alpha side, not an individual user's liquidity position. User
liquidity positions describe how liquidity is placed around the subnet pool, while alpha reserve is
the reserve quantity inside the paired TAO-and-alpha pool
([User Liquidity Positions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/liquidity-positions),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

A liquidity position can interact with the pool, but alpha reserve still refers to the pool-side
alpha amount rather than to one user's chosen range or position. This keeps pool accounting separate
from participation strategy.

References: [User Liquidity Positions](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/liquidity-positions),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## Price Context

Alpha-token price is the ratio of TAO in reserve to alpha in reserve. Alpha reserve is the alpha
side of that relationship
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

That does not make alpha reserve the whole economic value of a subnet. It means reserve-side alpha
is the pool quantity used when explaining the subnet's alpha price. When a statement is about
market-cap-style supply or total stake, alpha outstanding also matters.

References: [Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)

## Reading the Term

Alpha reserve is a tokenomics term, not a separate token, staking guide, or pool strategy. It is the
precise term when the focus is subnet alpha inside the pool
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Emission: Alpha reserve injection](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions#alpha-reserve-injection)).

When the focus is the paired TAO side, TAO reserve is more precise. When the focus is alpha outside
the pool, alpha outstanding is more precise. When the focus is the broader tokenomics system,
Dynamic TAO is more precise.

Bittensor separates mainnet, testnet, and localnet environments. Alpha-reserve examples from one
environment do not carry over automatically to another because subnet pools, reserve values, and
emission conditions can differ.
