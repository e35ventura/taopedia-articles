# Subnet Stake Burn

Subnet stake burn is a Bittensor mechanism that adds TAO to a subnet reserve while permanently
removing alpha from circulation. The
[Subnet Stake Burn](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/stake-burn) page describes it as a
voluntary subnet buyback mechanism.

The term belongs to subnet tokenomics. It should be read as a pool and supply-accounting mechanism,
not as a general staking tutorial or a complete explanation of every reserve update that can happen
inside a subnet.

## Reserve Flow

The source describes a flow where TAO is added to the subnet's reserve in its automated market maker
pool. The pool then determines the corresponding alpha amount, removes that alpha from its reserve,
and burns it within the same chain action
([Subnet Stake Burn](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/stake-burn)).

That reserve flow is the core article-level point. TAO enters the subnet reserve context, while
alpha leaves circulation. The mechanism changes pool composition and alpha supply at the same time.

## Supply Accounting

Subnet stake burn is one concrete use of burning vocabulary. The recycling-and-burning glossary
separates re-emittable recycled tokens from burned tokens, while the stake-burn documentation
describes a mechanism that burns alpha
([Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning),
[Subnet Stake Burn](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/stake-burn)).

For readers, the useful distinction is accounting scope. Subnet stake burn names the mechanism;
burning names the supply outcome for the removed alpha.

## Alpha Outstanding Boundary

Alpha outstanding describes alpha in circulation for a subnet. Subnet stake burn describes a
mechanism that can reduce that circulating alpha by burning a calculated amount
([Glossary: Alpha Outstanding](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-outstanding),
[Subnet Stake Burn](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/stake-burn)).

The concepts are connected but not identical. Alpha outstanding is a supply quantity, while subnet
stake burn is a mechanism that changes supply through a reserve action.

## Pool Context

Subnet stake burn belongs beside subnet-pool vocabulary because it updates reserve context. A subnet
liquidity pool names the paired TAO and alpha reserve structure, while subnet stake burn names one
mechanism that can add TAO to that structure and remove alpha from circulation
([Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage),
[Subnet Stake Burn](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/stake-burn)).

That distinction prevents pool state and burn mechanism from being collapsed into one term. The pool
is the reserve structure; stake burn is one mechanism that can change it.

## Conviction Boundary

Subnet stake burn and conviction both involve subnet alpha, but they describe different outcomes.
Stake burn removes alpha from circulation, while conviction and locked stake describe locking subnet
alpha so that a staked balance cannot be reduced below the locked amount
([Subnet Stake Burn](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/stake-burn),
[Conviction and locked stake](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/staking-and-delegation/conviction-staking)).

This boundary matters because burning and locking are not interchangeable. A burn reduces
circulating alpha; a lock constrains movement of alpha that remains staked.

## Coinbase and Emission Context

Coinbase is another adjacent mechanism. The coinbase documentation describes a per-block protocol
process for TAO emission, pending-emission accounting, and epoch-boundary activity
([Coinbase Implementation](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/navigating-subtensor/emissions-coinbase)).

Both coinbase and subnet stake burn can be discussed near subnet reserves, but they move through
different mechanism paths. Coinbase is part of recurring emission flow; subnet stake burn is a
separate alpha-burning mechanism that changes pool reserves without re-emitting the removed alpha.

## Registration Burn, Stake Burn, and Recycling Pipeline

Bittensor uses several burn-adjacent flows that share vocabulary but differ in supply outcome.
[Subnet registration](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/subnet-registration) recycles TAO
through dynamic burn bounds when a subnet is created, returning deducted TAO toward future issuance
rather than permanently removing it
([Glossary: Burn Cost](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#burn-cost),
[Glossary: Recycling and burning](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#recycling-and-burning)).

Subnet stake burn sits on the opposite side of that split. Submitted TAO enters the subnet reserve
while an equivalent [alpha](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha) amount is
removed from circulation within the same transaction on a subnet such as netuid 1
([Subnet Stake Burn](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/stake-burn)). The burn is permanent
for the alpha side: it reduces
[alpha outstanding](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#alpha-outstanding) and
updates the pool ratio rather than recycling tokens toward later emission.

The useful pipeline order on a selected netuid is therefore registration or recycled TAO flows at
subnet creation, ongoing coinbase and
[Yuma Consensus](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/yuma-consensus) emission distribution at
[tempo](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#tempo) boundaries, and optional subnet
stake burn as a separate owner-initiated reserve action that removes alpha from circulation. Stake
burn is rate-limited per subnet to one `add_stake_burn` extrinsic each tempo, about 360 blocks; a
second call inside the same tempo on netuid 1 returns a rate-limit error
([Subnet Stake Burn](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/stake-burn)).

That cap keeps stake burn tied to subnet-owner timing rather than to unlimited repeated burns on the
same [netuid](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#netuid).

## Slippage Limits Apply to the Stake Step

The stake-burn reference warns that the staking step inside stake burn executes against the subnet
automated market maker and can move the alpha price, especially when liquidity is thin or the TAO
amount is large on a netuid such as netuid 1. Documentation recommends limit-price or slippage
tolerance controls on the operation.

Slippage vocabulary therefore belongs to stake-burn reading. The burn removes alpha from
circulation, but the amount acquired for burning can differ from a pre-trade estimate when the pool
moves during execution ([Understanding Slippage](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/slippage)).

## Development Stage Context

The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)
describes subnet development as moving from localnet to testnet and then mainnet. Subnet stake burn
examples on netuid 1 need that context because reserve state, tempo limits, and burn outcomes belong
to the network where they were observed
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).

In localnet, stake-burn examples are isolated development records. Local pool reserves and tempo
readings for netuid 1 reflect local chain configuration rather than production subnet behavior.

On testnet, stake burn can be exercised in a shared non-production network. Testnet reserve updates
and rate-limit behavior for netuid 1 are separate from mainnet subnet state
([Subnet Stake Burn](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/stake-burn)).

On mainnet, subnet stake burn concerns live production reserve actions on the active Bittensor
network, including alpha removal and pool ratio updates on subnets such as netuid 1.

A stake-burn example from one environment should not be treated as proof about another environment.
They use the same mechanism vocabulary, but not the same reserve state.

## Reader Boundary

Subnet stake burn should be read as a subnet tokenomics mechanism. It adds TAO to subnet reserve
context, removes alpha from circulation, and is bounded by the stake-burn rules described in
[Subnet Stake Burn](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/stake-burn).

The stable article-level point is that subnet stake burn connects reserve adjustment with permanent
alpha removal. Exact execution behavior, rejected attempts, and troubleshooting details belong in
the stake-burn documentation and the relevant network context at observation time.
