# Subnet Creator

A subnet creator is the role that defines what a Bittensor subnet is meant to produce and how that
work is evaluated
([Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

The term belongs to subnet design vocabulary. It identifies the role that gives a subnet its work
context before miners and validators operate inside that context.

## Design Role

The creator role sits upstream of miner work and validator evaluation. It gives the subnet a work
target, an interaction context, and an evaluation frame so subnet roles are not working against a
generic network-wide task
([Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator),
[Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)).

For readers, the creator shapes the context. Miners produce work inside that context, and validators
evaluate work inside that context.

Because subnets organize specialized markets for useful work, the creator role is easiest to read as
the design source for the subnet's work loop. The creator establishes what kind of work the subnet
is trying to elicit, while the operating roles turn that design into ongoing production and
evaluation ([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

The design role also explains why creator vocabulary often appears near incentive-mechanism
language. A useful subnet needs not only a task, but also a protocol for interaction and a scoring
model for evaluating submissions
([Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)).

## Task Context

A subnet creator defines the digital task miners are asked to perform. The subnet task names the
work target, while the creator role names who establishes that target for the subnet
([Glossary: Subnet Task](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-task),
[Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator)).

The role and the task answer different questions. The creator names the design role; the subnet task
names the work target created for miners to satisfy and validators to evaluate.

This makes the task context central to the article. A subnet creator is not just associated with a
subnet label; the role is tied to the work definition that gives the subnet its purpose
([Glossary: Subnet Task](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-task),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

## Protocol and Scoring Context

Incentive-mechanism documentation separates task, protocol, and scoring model as related parts of
subnet design. A creator's design context can include each of those pieces without making them the
same term
([Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism),
[Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator)).

The subnet protocol describes how miners and validators interact. The scoring model describes how
returned work is evaluated. The creator role sits around those design choices rather than replacing
them.

That structure gives creator vocabulary a practical interpretation. A creator-facing explanation can
describe the intended task, the interaction rules, and the evaluation method without treating those
pieces as interchangeable
([Understanding Incentive Mechanisms](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/anatomy-of-incentive-mechanism)).

Task, protocol, and scoring model also help distinguish design from operation. Once the design is in
place, miners and validators operate through those rules; the creator role describes how the rules
come into scope for the subnet.

## Operating Roles

Subnet creators are not the same role as miners or validators. Miners produce work for the subnet,
and validators evaluate that work against subnet standards
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets),
[Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator)).

This distinction matters because creator vocabulary is about design authority and context. Miner and
validator vocabulary is about operating inside the resulting subnet.

In the standard role picture, miners and validators are downstream of the creator's design context.
The creator defines what the market is trying to evaluate; miners and validators participate in the
work and evaluation process that follows
([Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

## Mechanism Context

A subnet creator can shape more than one incentive mechanism inside a subnet. Multiple-mechanism
documentation describes separate mechanism contexts that can operate in parallel inside one subnet
([Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets),
[Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator)).

For readers, one mechanism's task, protocol, or scoring model is mechanism-level context. The whole
subnet can contain more than one such context when multiple mechanisms are present.

That makes mechanism context important for interpreting creator work. A creator can define the
subnet-level design while separate mechanisms define narrower work and scoring paths inside that
subnet
([Multiple Incentive Mechanisms Within Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-multiple-mech-subnets)).

## Network Context

Subnet-creator examples belong to a network environment. The Bittensor Networks reference separates
mainnet, testnet, and localnet as different environments
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks)).

The creator concept is stable, but observed subnet behavior belongs to the environment where it was
observed. A localnet design test, a testnet trial, and a mainnet subnet can all involve creator
work, but they do not provide the same kind of evidence.

## Reading the Role

Subnet creator names the design role behind a subnet's work and evaluation context. It is not a
subnet launch guide, operations checklist, funding explainer, or quality rating for a specific
subnet
([Glossary: Subnet Creator](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#subnet-creator),
[Understanding Subnets](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets)).

The stable article-level point is that a subnet creator defines a subnet's work and evaluation
context, while miners and validators operate inside that created context.

A precise use of the term keeps the design role attached to the subnet's task, protocol, scoring
model, and network environment. That is enough to distinguish creator context from miner work,
validator evaluation, observed outcomes, and funding activity.

## Development Stage Context

The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)
describes subnet development as moving from localnet to testnet and then mainnet. The subnet creator
role applies across the Bittensor lifecycle: creators can design and validate work targets and
evaluation rules in localnet, test them in a shared non-production environment on testnet, and
deploy the resulting mechanism on mainnet for live operation with real emissions.

The [Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks) reference
separates mainnet, testnet, and localnet. Creator design examples or mechanism outcomes from one
environment are separate from production subnet performance in another environment.
