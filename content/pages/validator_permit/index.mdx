# Validator Permit

A validator permit is a Bittensor permission flag for validation rights inside a subnet. It answers
whether validation rights exist in that subnet context
([Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit),
[Glossary: VPermit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#vpermit)).

The term is scoped. It should not be read as a universal validator label across every subnet.

## Subnet Permission Flag

Validator permit is a one-subnet permission concept. It tells readers whether validation rights
exist for the relevant subnet, not whether the same validator has rights everywhere else
([Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit),
[Glossary: VPermit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#vpermit)).

This makes validator permit useful as a local rights term. The surrounding subnet context is part of
the meaning.

The subnet boundary is the main reading rule. A validator permit statement should keep the subnet
attached because validation rights are meaningful in that local context, not as a universal label
for every subnet a validator might touch
([Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit),
[Glossary: VPermit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#vpermit)).

That keeps permit language narrower than general validator role language. A validator can be
discussed as a role, while validator permit answers whether that role has validation rights in the
subnet being considered.

## VPermit Scope

Validator permit and VPermit describe nearby ideas at different scopes. A validator permit is the
one-subnet flag. VPermit is the broader view of where validation is authorized
([Glossary: VPermit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#vpermit),
[Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit)).

The distinction keeps the vocabulary readable. Validator permit answers a subnet-level permission
question, while VPermit summarizes validator authorization across subnet contexts.

VPermit should therefore not erase the subnet-level reading of validator permit. When the source is
about one subnet, validator permit is the more precise term. When the source is about authorization
across subnet contexts, VPermit is the broader term
([Glossary: VPermit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#vpermit),
[Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit)).

## Stake-Weight Context

Validator permit is connected to stake-weight position. Stake weight is the computed stake value
used for validator consensus power and emissions inside a subnet
([Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit),
[Glossary: Stake Weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight)).

This means a validator permit is not a reputation score by itself. It is a validation permission
flag connected to stake-weight eligibility inside the subnet context.

The stake-weight connection should stay eligibility-focused. Stake weight helps explain the
surrounding validator influence and eligibility setting, but the permit itself is still the rights
flag for validation in context
([Glossary: Stake Weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight),
[Understanding Subnets: Validator stake weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/subnets/understanding-subnets#validator-stake-weight)).

This prevents a permit statement from being read as a full stake-weight calculation. Stake weight
and validator permit are related inputs to the validation picture, but they answer different
questions.

## Stake Weight Boundary

Stake weight and validator permit describe different parts of the validation picture. Stake weight
is a computed value used for consensus influence, while validator permit records whether validation
rights exist for the subnet being discussed
([Glossary: Stake Weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight),
[Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit)).

The two ideas are related but not interchangeable. Stake weight helps explain the eligibility and
influence setting; the permit term names the rights state.

Delegate is another nearby term with a different scope. Delegate names the validator-side role that
can receive staked TAO from nominators, while validator permit names the subnet validation-rights
state ([Glossary: Delegate](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#delegate),
[Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit)).

Keeping the role and rights state separate avoids overloading one term. Delegate says what role is
being discussed; validator permit says whether validation rights exist for that subnet context.

## Reader Boundary

Use validator permit when the focus is permission to validate inside a subnet. Use stake weight when
the focus is stake-derived validator influence, and use VPermit when the focus is a broader
authorization view
([Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit),
[Glossary: Stake Weight](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#stake-weight),
[Glossary: VPermit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#vpermit)).

This page is a reference article for the permission flag. It is not a full validator guide, a
general ranking, or a description of every subnet role.

It should also not be used as a performance statement. A permit answers a rights question, while
validator weights, rank, trust, and dividends belong to different parts of consensus and emission
vocabulary
([Glossary: Validator Weights](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-weights),
[Glossary: Rank](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#rank)).

## Development Stage Context

The
[Introduction to Bittensor](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/learn/introduction#subnet-development)
describes subnet development as moving from localnet to testnet and then mainnet. The validator
permit concept applies across the Bittensor lifecycle: validation rights can be tested in localnet
for isolated subnet testing, verified on testnet for shared non-production conditions, and relied
upon on mainnet for production operation with real emissions.

The [Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks) reference
separates mainnet, testnet, and localnet. Validator permit observations or validation-rights
outcomes from one environment should not be read as representing production subnet performance in
another environment.

The concept can be explained across environments, but a concrete permit observation belongs to the
network, subnet, validator, and block context where it was read
([Bittensor Networks](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/bittensor-networks),
[Glossary: Validator Permit](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/resources/glossary#validator-permit)).
